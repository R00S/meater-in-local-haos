# Short Ribs × Oven Broil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Broiling is radiant heat from above, and on this cut it does two opposite jobs depending on which format is on the tray. Thin flanken- or LA-cut slices — the Korean *galbi* geometry, bone cross-sections every centimetre or so — are thin enough that a domestic broiler cooks them through in the time it takes to caramelise the marinade, five to ten minutes total, and the broiler is then the whole method. Thick English-cut blocks are the reverse: the broiler cannot cook them through before the surface carbonises, so it is used as a finish after a long moist cook, five minutes at maximum heat to set a glaze.

That split is the central honesty point of this leaf. A broiler heats by radiation, so the surface reaches caramelisation temperature long before the interior approaches the collagen band; on a thick rib the interior is still in the 60s when the sugar in a marinade starts to blacken. Everything the sources say about timing therefore depends on thickness, and none of them states an internal temperature — the cooking signal in all three is visual: the surface caramelises, the edges crisp.

Sugar is the other constraint. Two of the three sources use a sweetened soy marinade, and sugar burns before beef browns; the Korean source in this leaf says plainly that a thin cut needs roughly half the time of a thick one and that the marinade goes on after the first sear for exactly that reason. A broiled short rib is a glazed surface over either a very thin slice or an already-tender block, never a thick rib cooked from raw.

## Styckesprofil

Grillning ovanifrån är strålningsvärme, och på den här styckdelen gör den två motsatta saker beroende på vilket format som ligger på plåten. Tunt skurna flanken- eller LA-skivor — den koreanska *galbi*-geometrin, med ben i tvärsnitt varje centimeter — är så tunna att en hushållsgrill tillagar dem på den tid det tar att karamellisera marinaden, fem till tio minuter totalt, och grillen är då hela metoden. Tjocka engelskt snittade block är det omvända: grillen kan inte tillaga dem genom innan ytan förkolnar, så den används som avslutning efter en lång fuktig tillagning, fem minuter på högsta värme för att sätta en glasyr.

Den uppdelningen är filens centrala ärlighetspunkt. En grill värmer genom strålning, så ytan når karamelliseringstemperatur långt innan innertemperaturen närmar sig kollagenintervallet; på ett tjockt revben ligger innertemperaturen fortfarande i 60-talen när sockret i marinaden börjar svartna. Allt källorna säger om tid beror därför på tjocklek, och ingen av dem anger någon innertemperatur — tillagningssignalen i alla tre är visuell: ytan karamelliseras, kanterna knaprar.

Socker är den andra begränsningen. Två av de tre källorna använder en sötad sojamarinad, och socker bränns före nötköttets yta; den koreanska källan i filen säger rakt ut att ett tunt snitt behöver ungefär halva tiden mot ett tjockt och att marinaden penslas på efter den första ytstekningen just därför. Ett grillat revbensspjäll är en glaserad yta över antingen en mycket tunn skiva eller ett redan mört block, aldrig ett tjockt revben tillagat från rå.

## Source recipes

### 1. American (Serious Eats / Max and Eli Sussman, *This is a Cookbook*) — Korean-Style Short Ribs
**Inspired by**: Serious Eats, "Cook the Book" column (US); https://www.seriouseats.com/max-and-eli-sussmans-korean-style-short-ribs-recipe (fetched 2026-11-01 via the Wayback Machine capture of 2024-11-18; the live page returns 403). *Three pounds of Korean-style (LA) short ribs, an overnight soy–vinegar–sriracha marinade, then straight under the broiler: 5–7 minutes on one side, turn, about 5 minutes more.*
**Serves**: 2–4 · **Prep**: 20 min + 12–24 h marinade · **Cook**: 10–12 min under the broiler · **Rest**: up to 1 h to come to room temperature before broiling

**Ingredients**
- 1 cup soy sauce, 1 cup water, ¼ cup rice vinegar, 2 tbsp white vinegar
- ⅓ cup tightly packed dark brown sugar, 3 tbsp sriracha, 1 tbsp ground black pepper
- ½ large yellow onion, chopped; 1 bunch green onions, white and tender green parts; 8–10 garlic cloves, chopped
- 3 lb (1.4 kg) Korean-style short ribs
- Steamed white rice and hoisin sauce, to serve

**Method**
1. Whisk the soy sauce, water, both vinegars, brown sugar, sriracha and pepper; stir in the onion, green onions and garlic. Add the ribs, turn to coat, cover and marinate **12–24 hours** in the refrigerator.
2. Line a heavy baking sheet with foil. Lift the ribs out, shake off the aromatics, lay them in a single layer and let them come to room temperature (up to an hour).
3. Preheat the broiler. **Broil until the surface begins to caramelise, 5–7 minutes.** Turn, slide back under the broiler and broil until cooked through, **about 5 minutes longer**. Serve at once.

Notes: the test-kitchen commentary records that ribs crowded on the pan steam instead of caramelising and recommends two batches and patting the meat dry — a practical statement that surface drying, not time, decides whether the glaze forms. No internal temperature is given.

### 2. Chinese (下厨房 / 艾大金子, transcribing 小高姐) — 烤牛排骨 (oven-baked beef short ribs)
**Inspired by**: Xiachufang recipe 104745002 (China); https://m.xiachufang.com/recipe/104745002/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *A transcription of the well-known video cook 小高姐's beef short ribs: a dry spice rub, overnight, then 150 °C covered for 2½ hours, and finally a brush of barbecue sauce and five minutes under the oven's maximum grill setting.*
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: overnight rub · **Cook**: 2½ h at 150 °C + 5 min grill · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 900 g beef short ribs
- 4 g salt (½ tsp), 30 g brown sugar (2 tbsp), 7 g chilli powder (1 tbsp), 10 g garlic powder (1 tbsp), 3 g ginger powder (1 tsp)
- 15 g (1 tbsp) barbecue sauce, for glazing

**Method**
1. Mix the rub ingredients and coat the ribs; refrigerate overnight.
2. Soak the ribs first to draw out blood, drain, then rub — the transcription puts the soaking before the rub.
3. Put the ribs on a tray, **cover with foil and bake at 300 °F / 150 °C for 2½ hours.**
4. Remove, raise the oven to its grill/broil setting (maximum), brush both sides with barbecue sauce.
5. **Grill uncovered 5 minutes**, then brush with the rendered fat for shine.

Notes: the broiler step here is a five-minute glaze on an already-cooked rib — the two-stage structure in its purest form. No internal temperature is stated at either stage.

### 3. Korean (Naver blog, 오타와에서 적는 편지) — LA갈비 오븐구이
**Inspired by**: Naver Blog (Korean, written from Ottawa); https://m.blog.naver.com/soyustar/221163641977 (fetched 2026-11-01). *Five thin LA-cut slices, olive oil and herb salt, 220 °C (430 °F) for 20 minutes, then galbi marinade brushed on and 10 minutes more. The author notes that thinner slices need about 10 minutes and then 5 with the marinade.*
**Serves**: not stated (two adults and two children, with the author noting five slices is not enough) · **Prep**: 30 min, including oven preheat · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: 2–3 min in the switched-off oven

**Ingredients**
- LA-cut beef short ribs (5 thin slices used)
- Olive oil, herb salt
- Prepared galbi marinade (the author uses a bought or home-made 갈비양념)
- Vegetables to roast alongside: bean sprouts, mushrooms, carrots, onion, garlic

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to **430 °F (about 220 °C)**; take the meat out of the refrigerator at least 30 minutes ahead and rub with olive oil. Salt just before cooking — the author notes that salt draws juice out, so it goes on last.
2. Score or pierce the meat well; the author says this tenderises it noticeably.
3. **Bake 20 minutes**, then brush with galbi marinade and **bake 10 minutes more.**
4. Switch the oven off and wait 2–3 minutes before removing, so the glaze does not catch.
5. For thinner slices: about 10 minutes, then 5 minutes with the marinade.

Notes: a home-cook blog with no ingredient quantities beyond "galbi marinade"; recorded as such rather than filled in. It is the only source here that gives an oven temperature in °C and the only one that describes the timing difference between thick and thin cuts from experience. No internal temperature.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Geometry and heat | Internal temperature stated |
|---|---|---|
| Serious Eats / Sussman | Thin LA slices, broiler, 5–7 min + ~5 min | **none stated** |
| Xiachufang / 小高姐 | Thick ribs, 150 °C covered 2½ h, then max grill 5 min | **none stated** |
| Naver blog (Korean) | Thin LA slices, 220 °C, 20 min + 10 min glazed | **none stated** |

Three sources, no interior numbers at all. The broiler's own heat is not a temperature the recipes control — all three describe settings (broil, grill maximum, 220 °C) and visual endpoints (caramelised surface, crisp edges) instead. This leaf therefore proposes no method-specific band and no numeric pull point, and any figure placed here would be invented.

What the sources do agree on is structure: the glaze goes on late, and thin slices are cooked fast while thick ribs are cooked through first and glazed second. The two Korean-derived recipes both use a sweet soy glaze and both put it on after the first heat, which is the same conclusion reached from opposite directions — a bought marinade brushed at 20 minutes, and a dry rub followed by sauce at the very end.

## What makes this method special

The broiler is the only dry method in this cut's set that works on the thin galbi geometry at all: a flanken slice is too thin to braise usefully and would dry out in an oven roast, but under radiant heat it cooks through and caramelises in ten minutes. On the thick English cut the same element is demoted to a finisher, and that two-role nature is why the leaf's sources look so unlike each other — one is a weeknight marinade, one is a two-and-a-half-hour bake with a five-minute finish, and one is a home cook's oven adaptation of a grill. The common skill is judging the surface, because the interior gives no usable signal at these timescales.

## Summary

Three fetched broiler recipes show the method in both of its roles: thin LA-cut slices cooked through and glazed in ten minutes under radiant heat (Serious Eats, a Korean home cook's 220 °C oven version), and thick ribs baked through first and finished for five minutes under the grill (Xiachufang's transcription of 小高姐). None of the three states an internal temperature, so the leaf carries no numeric target, and the timing divergence between them is thickness, not disagreement.
