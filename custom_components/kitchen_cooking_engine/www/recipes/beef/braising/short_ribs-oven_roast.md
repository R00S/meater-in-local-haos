# Short Ribs × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven roasting is the answer to a real problem: short ribs need the internal temperature of a braise
(88–96 °C) but not every kitchen has a smoker, a grill or a heavy braising pot. The oven solves it by
supplying the same sustained heat from still air and a covered pan. The distinction from
[braise](./short_ribs-braise.md) is mechanical, not temperamental: here the ribs sit on the pan floor,
usually bone-side down and often on a rack, with at most a splash of stock, and the moisture that would
normally come from a braising liquid is trapped by foil or a lid. The meat is therefore roasted in its own
rendered fat and steam — dry heat at the surface, saturated air around it.

That combination is what the temperatures in this leaf measure. Because collagen hydrolysis needs time
above roughly 70 °C and completes near 90 °C, every source that publishes a number converges on **90 °C or
above**, and the oven's air temperature becomes a time control rather than a doneness control: 120 °C gives
three to five hours, 140 °C about three and a half, 275 °F (135 °C) three and a half to four and a half. The
finishing stage is the opposite — a short, hot, uncovered pass at 250–425 °F to caramelise a glaze or build
a crust, which is a surface event and must not be used to judge doneness.

Probe placement: insert from the flat cut end into the geometric centre of the thickest muscle section,
clear of the bone and of the fat pocket that sits over the plate ribs. Both the bone and a large fat pocket
read low and lag the lean fibres that actually determine doneness.

## Styckesprofil

Ugnsrostning löser ett verkligt problem: revbensspjäll behöver en innertemperatur som vid brässering
(88–96 °C), men alla kök har inte rök, grill eller en tung gryta. Ugnen ger samma ihållande värme från
stilla luft och en täckt form. Skillnaden mot [brässering](./short_ribs-braise.md) är mekanisk, inte
temperament: här ligger revbenen på formens botten, oftast med benet nedåt och ibland på galler, med högst
en skvätt buljong, och den fukt som annars kom från bräseringsvätskan stängs inne av folie eller lock.
Köttet rostas i sitt eget fett och sin egen ånga — torr värme vid ytan, mättad luft runt om.

Därför samlas alla källor som publicerar en siffra kring **90 °C eller mer**, och ugnstemperaturen blir en
tidskontroll snarare än en mörhetskontroll: 120 °C ger tre till fem timmar, 140 °C cirka tre och en halv,
275 °F (135 °C) tre och en halv till fyra och en halv timme. Avslutningen är det motsatta — en kort, het,
otäckt passage vid 250–425 °F för att karamellisera en glasyr eller bygga skorpa, vilket är en ythändelse
och aldrig får användas för att bedöma mjukhet.

Sond: sticks in från den skurna ändan mot det geometriska centret i den tjockaste muskeldelen, fritt från
benet och från fettfickan ovanför tallriksrevbenen. Både ben och fett läser lågt och släpar efter de magra
fibrer som avgör mörheten.

## Source recipes

### 1. Oven-Baked Short Ribs with a 425 °F Crust (inspired by 44 Farms)
**Inspired by**: 44 Farms, 44steaks.com/blogs/all-recipes/how-to-cook-beef-short-ribs-in-the-oven-fall-off-the-bone-tender-every-time (fetched 2026-10-27; US, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 3 hr 30 min–4 hr 30 min + 20–30 min · **Rest**: 30 min

**Ingredients**
- 1.4 kg (3 lb) bone-in Black Angus short ribs (plate or chuck end)
- 10 g (2 tsp) black-pepper infused salt
- 2 g (1 tsp) garlic powder (optional)
- 2 g (1 tsp) onion powder (optional)
- 2 g (1 tsp) freshly ground black pepper
- 120–240 ml (½–1 cup) beef broth (optional, for a moist pan)
- 15 g (1 tbsp) beef tallow, for the finish (optional)

**Method**
1. Take the ribs out of the refrigerator 30–45 minutes before cooking and pat them dry.
2. Season all sides with the salt, pepper and optional powders.
3. Optional: sear in a cast-iron pan in tallow, 2–3 minutes a side, then transfer to a roasting pan.
4. Heat the oven to 275 °F (135 °C). Arrange the ribs bone-side down and add the broth to the pan floor if using.
5. Cover tightly with foil or a lid and roast 3½–4½ hours.
6. Check for doneness by probe tenderness, not by the clock: a skewer slides in with almost no resistance and the meat pulls back from the bones. **Internal temperature is generally 200–205 °F (93–96 °C).**
7. Uncover, raise the oven to 425 °F (220 °C) and return the ribs for 20–30 minutes to build a crust, brushing with melted tallow if you like.
8. Rest 30 minutes before serving. **Pull at 93–96 °C (200–205 °F).**

### 2. Foil-Covered BBQ Short Ribs (inspired by Beckie, Eat Simple Food)
**Inspired by**: Beckie, eatsimplefood.com/easy-beef-short-ribs/ (fetched 2026-10-27; US, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 3 hr 30 min–4 hr · **Rest**: 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 8 bone-in beef short ribs
- 5 g (1 tsp) salt
- 1 g (¼ tsp) black pepper
- 80 g (⅓ cup) barbecue sauce

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 275 °F (135 °C).
2. Salt and pepper the ribs, lay them bone-side down and fat-side up, snuggled together in a baking dish, and cover tightly with two sheets of foil.
3. Bake 3½–4 hours, until the bone starts to separate from the meat and the meat is fork tender.
4. Uncover, drain the rendered fat and liquid from the dish, and baste with barbecue sauce.
5. Broil on low for 2–4 minutes until caramelised, watching closely.
6. **No internal temperature is published.** Doneness is judged by the meat shrinking away from the bone and by fork tenderness; the page's own instruction is that 3½ hours may be enough but most ovens need 4.

### 3. Short Ribs im Backofen mit Rauchsalz (inspired by Die Frau am Grill)
**Inspired by**: die-frau-am-grill.de/beef-short-ribs-im-backofen-rezept/ (fetched 2026-10-27; German, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 3–5 hr + 30 min · **Rest**: 15 min

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) beef short ribs
- 30 g (2 tbsp) sriracha
- 8 g (1½ tsp) salt
- 4 g (1 tsp) smoked salt
- 4 g (2 tsp) smoked paprika
- 2 g (1 tsp) ground cumin
- 2 g (1 tsp) black pepper
- 3 g (1 tsp) sugar
- 100 g (⅓ cup) smoky barbecue sauce
- 2 onions, sliced
- 2 red peppers, in strips

**Method**
1. Rub the ribs all over with sriracha, then with the mixed spices, and let them sit 15 minutes.
2. Heat the oven to 120 °C (250 °F). Put the ribs on the rack with a cast-iron pan underneath to catch the dripping fat and juices.
3. Roast until the core temperature reaches 90 °C — three to five hours depending on the size of the ribs. The author's explicit instruction is to work with a core-temperature thermometer rather than a clock.
4. Brush with the barbecue sauce and return to the oven at 120 °C for 30 minutes, with the onions and peppers in the pan underneath.
5. Rest briefly in the switched-off oven. **Pull at 90 °C (194 °F).**

### 4. Gobyazhi rebra v dukhovke (inspired by NV.ua)
**Inspired by**: nv.ua/food/eat/rebra-v-duhovke-kak-prigotovit-i-kakuyu-temperaturu-vystavit-chtoby-poluchilos-vkusno-50503258.html (fetched 2026-10-27; Ukrainian, Group D)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 3 hr 30 min · **Rest**: 15 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 rack beef ribs, at room temperature
- 240 g (1 cup) dry spice rub
- 240 g (1 cup) barbecue sauce
- 75 g (5 tbsp) honey dijon mustard
- 15 g (1 tbsp) liquid smoke (recommended for beef)

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 140 °C (285 °F) and line a tray with foil or baking paper.
2. Coat both sides of the rack with mustard and liquid smoke, then with the spice rub.
3. Bake 2 hours, then brush barbecue sauce on both sides and bake 1 hour more.
4. Brush again, check the internal temperature, and give it a final 30 minutes — or take it out as soon as it reads 90 °C or above.
5. The article gives the general oven rules for beef ribs as 4 hours at 120 °C, 3 hours at 150 °C or 2 hours at 175 °C, notes 62 °C as the bare minimum and 87 °C as the point where fat and collagen begin to melt, and states that the ribs are right at **90 °C (194 °F)**.

### 5. Costine di manzo al forno (inspired by Smeg)
**Inspired by**: smeg.com/it/ricette/costine-di-manzo.html (fetched 2026-10-27; Italian, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Rest**: 30 min (dry marinade) · **Cook**: 2 hr 57 min

**Ingredients**
- 1.3 kg (2.9 lb) beef short ribs
- 5 g (1 tsp) salt
- 5 g (1 tsp) black pepper
- 5 g (2 tsp) rosemary
- 3 g (1 tsp) dried garlic
- 3 g (1 tsp) dried oregano
- 3 g (1 tsp) dried thyme

**Method**
1. Dry-marinate the ribs with the salt and herbs for 30 minutes.
2. Lay them in a roasting tin, cover well with baking paper and then aluminium foil.
3. Bake on the third shelf with the circular (fan) function at 135 °C (275 °F) for 162 minutes.
4. Switch to the turbo function at 250 °C (480 °F) for 15 minutes to caramelise the surface.
5. **No internal temperature is published.** The recipe is written as a time-and-function programme for a domestic oven; the 15-minute turbo pass is a surface treatment, not a doneness test.

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Source | Oven | Published internal target | Oven-to-plate strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US (Texas) | 44 Farms / 44steaks.com | 275 °F → 425 °F | **200–205 °F (93–96 °C)** | covered roast, uncovered crust |
| US (home cooking) | Beckie / Eat Simple Food | 275 °F | none published | covered roast, broiled glaze |
| German | Die Frau am Grill | 120 °C | **90 °C (194 °F)** | roast on rack, glazed at 120 °C |
| Ukrainian | NV.ua | 140 °C | **≥90 °C (194 °F)**; 87 °C named as collagen melt point | sauce layers, 30-minute finish |
| Italian | Smeg | 135 °C → 250 °C | none published | baking-paper cover, turbo finish |

Four of the five sources are Group B (US ×2, German, Italian) and one is Group D (Ukrainian); nothing in
the fetched set is Group A or Group C, and the US pair is the same tradition twice. **This leaf is
therefore below the distinct-tradition gate and is marked partial rather than conformant** — the numbers
are solid, the diversity is not. The obvious missing records are a Korean oven galbi (a real oven method,
found but not yet written up as `oven_broil`) and a Chinese *hongshao niurou* oven variant.

The published numbers cluster tightly at **90–96 °C (194–205 °F)** — the same band as the braise leaf,
which is the useful finding of this pass: the method changes the oven air temperature by 15–100 °C and the
time by hours, and it does not move the target at all. Two sources publish no number and both judge by
time and appearance, which is a property of the source set rather than evidence against the band.

## What this method does to this cut

Oven roasting gives short ribs the collagen window without a braising pot: a covered pan traps the moisture
the meat releases, so the surface never desiccates while the core spends three to five hours in the 70–95 °C
band where collagen hydrolyses. The uncovered finish is the only dry-heat moment, and it acts on the crust
and the glaze, not on the centre. Compared with braising, the flavour is more concentrated and the sauce is
built separately, because there is no liquid for the meat to exchange with.

## Summary

Five oven-roasted short rib records were fetched: two American, one German, one Ukrainian and one Italian.
Four publish an internal temperature and all four land at 90–96 °C, identical to the braise leaf's band, and
one of them states the 87 °C collagen-melt point explicitly. Two publish no number and judge by time. The
leaf is marked partial because four of the five records are Group B and two of those are the same tradition.
