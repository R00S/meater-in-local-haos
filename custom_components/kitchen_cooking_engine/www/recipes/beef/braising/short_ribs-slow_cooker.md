# Short Ribs × Slow Cooker — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

A slow cooker does one thing to short ribs: it holds them in a moist, barely moving environment just above the collagen-melting point for hours, without the evaporative loss of an open oven. The ceramic crock heats slowly and the liquid settles somewhere under a simmer, so the meat spends its time between roughly 80 °C and 95 °C — the band where the collagen sheath around each intercostal fibre bundle hydrolyses into gelatin and the bundles separate. That is why every source in this leaf judges the rib by fork-tenderness and time, not by a probe: the appliance gives no control fine enough for a 3 °C target, and none of the four recipes states an internal temperature at all.

The cut divides into two formats here. Thick English-cut ribs on the bone need the full 6–10 hours and are done when the meat pulls back from the bone. Thin flanken/LA-cut slices are not slow-cooker material in any of these sources — they would disintegrate — so a slow-cooker leaf on this cut is a bone-in leaf.

Two structural consequences follow from the appliance. First, nothing browns: the Korean sources explicitly skip the traditional par-boil-and-sear and get a paler, softer result, while the Western sources sear first in a skillet and say the browned flavour is the reason. Second, the sauce does not reduce, so every source either strains and skims the cooking liquid or thickens it with starch; the liquid the ribs give up is the sauce.

## Styckesprofil

En slow cooker gör en enda sak med revbensspjäll: den håller dem i en fuktig, knappt rörlig miljö strax över kollagenets smältpunkt i timmar, utan den avdunstning en öppen ugn ger. Keramikgrytan värms långsamt och vätskan lägger sig en bit under kokpunkten, så köttet tillbringar tiden mellan ungefär 80 °C och 95 °C — det intervall där kollagenhöljet runt varje fiberknippa hydrolyseras till gelatin och knipporna separeras. Det är därför varje källa i den här filen bedömer revbenet efter gaffelmörhet och tid, inte med sond: apparaten ger ingen kontroll som är fin nog för ett mål på 3 °C, och ingen av de fyra recepten anger någon innertemperatur alls.

Styckdelen delas i två format här. Tjocka engelskt snittade revben med ben behöver de fulla 6–10 timmarna och är klara när köttet släpper från benet. Tunt skurna flanken-/LA-skivor är inte slow cooker-material i någon av dessa källor — de skulle falla sönder — så en slow cooker-fil på den här styckdelen är en benfast fil.

Två strukturella följder kommer av apparaten. För det första brunas ingenting: de koreanska källorna hoppar uttryckligen över den traditionella förvällningen och bryningen och får ett blekare, mjukare resultat, medan de västerländska källorna bryner först i stekpanna och anger den brynta smaken som skälet. För det andra reduceras såsen inte, så varje källa silar och skummar antingen kokvätskan eller redigerar den med stärkelse; vätskan revbenen avger är såsen.

## Source recipes

### 1. Korean (Korean Bapsang, Hyosun Ro) — Slow Cooker Galbijjim
**Inspired by**: Korean Bapsang (US, Korean home cooking); https://www.koreanbapsang.com/slow-cooker-galbijjim-korean-braised/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *A slow-cooker adaptation of the site's traditional galbijjim: no par-boiling, no browning, no grating, everything into the crock in the morning. The page notes that traditional galbijjim does not brown the ribs either, and that searing is optional and gives a slightly different result.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 30 min soaking · **Cook**: 6–7 h on high or 9–10 h on low · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3–4 lb (1.4–1.8 kg) beef short ribs
- ½ medium onion, 1–2 carrots, ½ Korean/Asian pear (or a Bosc pear or apple), 7–8 garlic cloves, 3–4 thin ginger slices
- 6 oz (170 g) Korean radish, 3–4 dried shiitake mushrooms (both optional)
- Sauce: ½ cup soy sauce, ¼ cup rice wine or dry white wine, 4 tbsp honey or sugar, black pepper, 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 2–3 scallions, roughly chopped

**Method**
1. Trim excess fat, rinse the ribs and soak in cold water about 30 min to draw out blood; drain and pat dry.
2. Cut the vegetables and pear into 1½-inch chunks and put them in the slow cooker with the ribs.
3. Mix the soy sauce, rice wine, honey and pepper, pour over, and toss to coat.
4. Cover and cook **6–7 h on high or 9–10 h on low**, flipping the ribs once midway if you are home.
5. Stir in the scallions and sesame oil. Lift out the ribs and vegetables, strain the liquid and skim the fat, then pour the sauce back over to serve.

Notes: doneness is described as fork-tender and falling off the bone; the page states no internal temperature and no target temperature for the appliance.

### 2. Korean (Instant Pot Culinary Team) — Slow Cooked Korean Style Ribs (Galbijjim)
**Inspired by**: Instant Pot recipe library (US appliance manufacturer, Korean cuisine); https://instantpot.com/blogs/recipes/slow-cooked-korean-style-ribs-galbijjim (fetched 2026-11-01). *The appliance maker's own galbijjim: a 5–10 minute blanch in the inner pot with aromatics and skimming, then a 6-hour SLOW COOK-Beef programme with the braise ingredients.*
**Serves**: 6–8 · **Prep**: 10 min + 20 min soaking · **Cook**: 6 h slow cook · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2.5 lb (1.1 kg) beef short ribs
- Blanching: 3 green onions, 6 garlic cloves, 1 yellow onion, 1 tsp ginger, 6 cups cold water
- Braise: 1 Asian pear, 1 yellow onion, 6 Korean radish chunks, 1 carrot, 5 shiitake mushrooms, 3 green onions, 6 red dates
- 1 cup soy sauce, 4 tbsp aji-mirin, 2 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp garlic, ½ tbsp ginger, ½ tbsp chilli flakes, ½ tsp black pepper

**Method**
1. Soak the ribs in cold water about 20 min; crush the garlic into the inner pot with the onions, ginger and drained ribs, add the water.
2. Sauté on high 10 min, then boil 5–10 min and skim the scum.
3. Lift out the ribs, strain the liquid and discard the fat from its surface.
4. Mix the braise ingredients in the pot, return the ribs and spoon the mixture over them.
5. **SLOW COOK-Beef for 6 hours.** Serve with rice; sesame oil optional.

Notes: the manufacturer gives no internal temperature and no tenderness descriptor beyond the programme name. The first seven steps can be done a day ahead.

### 3. American South (Paula Deen) — Slow Cooker Beef Short Ribs
**Inspired by**: Paula Deen (US, Southern home cooking); https://www.pauladeen.com/recipe/slow-cooker-beef-short-ribs/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *Sear in a skillet over high heat first, then 8 hours on low with chicken stock and root vegetables added in the last 4–6 hours; finished with a cornstarch slurry.*
**Serves**: 10 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 8 h on low · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 lb (1.4 kg) beef short ribs
- 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp pepper, 1 tbsp garlic powder
- 48 oz (1.4 l) chicken stock
- 5 ribs celery, 5 large carrots, 8 red potatoes, 1 large onion — all cut into 2-inch pieces
- 1 tbsp cornstarch

**Method**
1. Rub the ribs with salt, pepper and garlic powder.
2. Brown on all sides in a skillet over high heat, then transfer to the slow cooker with the pan juices and brown bits.
3. Add the stock, cover and **cook on low 8 hours**.
4. Add the potatoes, carrots, onions and celery during the last 4–6 hours.
5. Whisk the cornstarch into a cup of the cooking liquid, return it to the pot to thicken, and serve in bowls.

Notes: no internal temperature and no tenderness temperature stated; the recipe's only doneness statement is the cooking time.

### 4. Russian (Gastronom.ru) — Нежные говяжьи ребра в мультиварке
**Inspired by**: Gastronom.ru (Russia); https://www.gastronom.ru/text/nezhnye-govjazhi-rebra-v-multivarke-bez-hlopot-i-vsego-za-chas-1011794 (fetched 2026-11-01). *A multicooker article rather than a fixed formula: sear 20 minutes in the "fry" programme with a spoon of honey to caramelise, then stew one hour under a tomato-mustard dressing with water to cover. The article argues that a sealed multicooker imitates a Russian stove and cooks ribs faster than an oven or a pot.*
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 10 min salting · **Cook**: 20 min fry + 1 h stew · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Beef short ribs (the article names *short rib* and *asado* steak), membrane removed
- Salt and black pepper
- 1 tbsp honey, oil for the pot
- Tomato-mustard dressing (tomato note plus mustard heat; quantities not given)
- Water to cover; 1 onion, 1 garlic clove, optionally a couple of potatoes

**Method**
1. Pull off the tough membrane. Season both sides with salt and pepper and leave 10 minutes.
2. Fry programme, 20 minutes, a little oil, ribs in one layer; turn as they colour, add the honey and caramelise.
3. Add the tomato-mustard dressing, stir to coat, then add water to cover.
4. Stew programme, 1 hour; after 20 minutes add the sliced onion and crushed garlic.
5. The article's doneness statement: the meat **slides off the bone and can be cut with a spoon while keeping its shape** — not a temperature.

Notes: quantities are partly editorial ("to taste", "some"), which is recorded rather than guessed. No internal temperature is given.

### 5. South African (Foodle Club) — Slow-cooker short ribs in monkey gland sauce
**Inspired by**: Foodle Club (South African recipe, published from a UK-based site); https://www.foodleclub.com/slow-cooker-short-ribs-monkey-gland-sauce/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *Short ribs browned in sunflower oil, then 8 hours on LO in a monkey gland sauce — the South African tomato, chutney, Worcestershire and ketchup sauce served with steak — finished by blending the gravy with a stick blender. The site's own cuisine label reads "British"; the sauce and the dish are South African, which is the tradition recorded here.*
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 8 h on low · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 700 g (1½ lb) beef short rib on the bone, about 350 g per person including the bone
- ½ tsp salt, 2 tbsp sunflower oil
- 1 large onion (1 cm pieces), 2–3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 cup (240 ml) passata, 1 cup water
- 4 tbsp tomato ketchup, 4 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 4 tbsp fruit chutney (Mrs Balls in South Africa)
- 1 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tsp Tabasco, cornflour to thicken if needed

**Method**
1. Brown the salted ribs on all sides in the oil and set aside.
2. Soften the onion and garlic in the same pan until translucent and just colouring.
3. Put everything except the meat into the slow cooker and stir to dissolve the sugar, then add the meat.
4. **Slow cooker on LO for 8 hours**, steam valve open on a multi-cooker.
5. Lift out the meat, blend the gravy with a stick blender, thicken with cornflour if it is thin, and pour over.

Notes: no internal temperature and no tenderness temperature. The recipe's reason for the slow cooker is stated directly: the long time tenderises the cut to "fall-off-the-bone succulence" while the sauce penetrates it.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Setting and time | Internal temperature stated |
|---|---|---|
| Korean Bapsang | 6–7 h high / 9–10 h low | **none stated** — fork-tender, falling off the bone |
| Instant Pot Culinary Team | 6 h SLOW COOK-Beef after a 5–10 min blanch | **none stated** |
| Paula Deen | 8 h on low | **none stated** |
| Gastronom.ru | 20 min fry + 1 h stew | **none stated** — slides off the bone, spoon-tender |
| Foodle Club (South African) | 8 h on low | **none stated** — "fall-off-the-bone succulence" |

No source in this leaf states an internal temperature, so this leaf proposes no method-specific band and no numeric pull point. The consensus is entirely qualitative and consistent: the rib is done when the meat releases from the bone and yields to a fork, which for this cut means the collagen has converted — in practice the 88–96 °C range the cut file already records as `pulled`, but that is the cut's band, not a number these five sources measured.

The one genuine divergence is browning. The two Korean sources deliberately skip it (one says the traditional dish does not brown either, and that searing is optional), while the American Southern recipe sears in a skillet first, the Russian one caramelises in the appliance's own fry programme, and the South African one browns the ribs and softens the onions in oil before they go in. That changes flavour and sauce colour, not the target.

## What makes this method special

The slow cooker is the only method in this cut's set that cannot overshoot: with no open flame and a crock that responds over tens of minutes, the ribs sit in the collagen band and stay there, so the failure mode is blandness from lack of browning and an un-reduced sauce rather than a dried-out rib. That makes it the most forgiving route to pulled short ribs and the least able to produce bark or a glossy glaze. It also splits the cut's traditions cleanly: the Korean sources treat the appliance as a shortcut to a braise that never browned in the first place, while the Western sources treat it as a braise with the browning moved to a skillet.

## Summary

Five fetched slow-cooker short rib recipes — two Korean galbijjim adaptations, one American Southern braise, one Russian multicooker article and one South African monkey gland braise — agree on a long, gentle, moist cook and on a qualitative endpoint: the meat slides off the bone. Not one of them states an internal temperature, so the leaf carries no numeric target; the appliance's value on this cut is that it holds the collagen band without the risk of overshoot, at the cost of browning and sauce reduction.
