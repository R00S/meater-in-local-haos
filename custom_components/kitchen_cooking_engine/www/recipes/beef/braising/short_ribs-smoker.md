# Short Ribs × Smoker — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

The smoker's job on this cut is not to cook the meat but to hold it inside the collagen-melting band long enough for the intercostal muscle and the serratus ventralis to give up. At a 107 °C pit the surface dehydrates slowly and takes on smoke while the interior climbs from 60 °C towards 80 °C; through that stretch the meat stays tight and the seam fat has not run. The window that decides the result is 85–95 °C: the collagen sheath around each fibre bundle hydrolyses into gelatin, the bundles separate, and the rib goes from "tough but safe" to "slides off the bone". Below 85 °C the rib is safe — 63 °C is the whole-muscle safe temperature — but still chewy, which is the trap this cut sets for anyone who cooks it like a steak. Above 98 °C the muscle dries and the bark can turn bitter, so the sources pull inside a band rather than at one number.

Fat rendering follows the same curve with a lag. The seam fat softens at 70–80 °C but only runs freely above 85 °C, which is why a rib pulled at 75 °C tastes greasy and one pulled at 95 °C tastes rich. Carryover is small, because the mass is large and the surface is already dry, but a 10–30 minute rest is standard: the bark firms and the juices redistribute.

Two formats diverge and must not be pooled. English-cut plate ribs are cooked as a block to 93–98 °C. Flanken-cut ribs, sliced thin across the bones, are smoked only to 52 °C and then seared — a different geometry with a different target.

## Styckesprofil

Rökens uppgift på den här styckdelen är inte att tillaga köttet utan att hålla det inom det kollagenlösande intervallet tillräckligt länge för att mellangärdesmuskeln och serratus ventralis ska ge upp. Vid 107 °C rök torkar ytan långsamt och tar upp rök medan innertemperaturen stiger från 60 °C mot 80 °C; under den sträckan är köttet spänt och fettet mellan musklerna har inte smält ut. Fönstret som avgör resultatet är 85–95 °C: kollagenhöljet runt varje fiberknippa hydrolyseras till gelatin, knipporna separeras och revbenet går från "segt men säkert" till att lossna från benet. Under 85 °C är revbenet säkert — 63 °C är den säkra temperaturen för hel muskel — men fortfarande segt, vilket är fällan för den som tillagar det som en stek. Över 98 °C torkar köttet och skorpan kan bli bitter, så källorna tar ut inom ett intervall snarare än vid ett enda tal.

Fettrenderingen följer samma kurva med eftersläpning. Fettet mellan musklerna mjuknar vid 70–80 °C men rinner först fritt över 85 °C, vilket är varför ett revben taget vid 75 °C smakar fett och ett taget vid 95 °C smakar rikt. Eftervärmen är liten eftersom massan är stor och ytan redan torr, men 10–30 minuters vila är standard: skorpan sätter sig och saften fördelas.

Två format skiljer sig och får inte slås ihop. Engelskt snittade revbensspjäll tillagas som block till 93–98 °C. Flanken-snittade revben, tunt skurna tvärs benen, röks bara till 52 °C och grillas sedan — en annan geometri med ett annat mål.

## Source recipes

### 1. American (AmazingRibs.com, Meathead) — Texas-Style Smoked BBQ Beef Ribs
**Inspired by**: Meathead, AmazingRibs.com (US); https://amazingribs.com/tested-recipes/beef-and-bison-recipes/smoked-texas-style-bbq-beef-ribs-recipe/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *Plate short ribs cut into two-bone sections, dry-brined up to 24 h, oak smoke at 225 °F, pulled at 203 °F. The page states 1-inch meat reaches 203 °F in about 5 h, 1.5-inch in about 7 h and 2-inch in about 10 h, and advises against wrapping ("skip the Texas crutch").*
**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 20 min + up to 24 h dry brine · **Cook**: ca 10 h · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 5 lb (2.3 kg) beef short ribs from the plate
- 2½ tsp Morton coarse kosher salt (about ½ tsp per pound)
- 3 tsp Big Bad Beef Rub (the site's own rub)

**Method**
1. Trim the fat cap and all silverskin from the top of the meat; leave the membrane on the bone side or the meat can fall off. Cut into single or two-bone sections.
2. Salt up to 24 h ahead. Just before cooking, wet the surface and coat generously with the rub.
3. Set the cooker for indirect heat at 225 °F (107 °C).
4. Place bone side down, add 2–4 oz (57–113 g) of oak or cherry wood, close the lid. Do not turn it and do not add more wood.
5. Cook until the thickest part reads 203 °F (95 °C).
6. **Pull at 95 °C / 203 °F.** Serve at once or hold in a cooler.

Notes: the page states that at 165 °F (74 °C) the meat is pasteurised through, and that this cut is taken past 200 °F (93 °C) in any case.

### 2. American (Texas Beef Company) — Best Smoked Beef Short Ribs
**Inspired by**: Texas Beef Company (US); https://texasbeef.com/best-smoked-beef-short-ribs/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *Mustard binder with a chilli-heavy rub, pecan or oak, 225 °F and an hourly beef-stock and cider-vinegar spritz, pulled at 200 °F and rested 30 min.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 8 h · **Rest**: at least 30 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 lb (1.8 kg) beef short ribs (2–3 six-inch bones)
- 4 tbsp dry rub, or equal parts salt, black pepper, garlic powder and chilli powder
- 3 tbsp yellow mustard
- ½ cup beef stock + ½ cup cider vinegar, for spritzing

**Method**
1. Trim the fat cap and silverskin. Coat evenly with mustard, then season liberally.
2. Preheat the smoker to 225 °F (107 °C) with pecan or oak.
3. Smoke 3 h without opening the lid.
4. Spritz with the stock and vinegar every hour after the third.
5. **Pull at 93 °C / 200 °F**, when the probe slides in "like a soft stick of butter".
6. Rest loosely covered at least 30 min.

### 3. Austrian (Fitmeat) — Beef Ribs vom Smoker
**Inspired by**: Fitmeat (Austria); https://www.fitmeat.at/blog/fitmeat-beef-ribs-vom-smoker (fetched 2026-11-01). *Salt–pepper–paprika rub, 110–120 °C smoke for 3 h with a mop every 30–45 min, then a 2 h foil wrap with butter, brown sugar and beef stock at 120 °C, then 30 min unwrapped to reset the bark. States a core temperature of 94–98 °C.*
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 4 h to overnight rub · **Cook**: about 5½ h · **Rest**: 10–15 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 kg beef ribs (short ribs or plate ribs)
- 2 tbsp coarse sea salt, 2 tbsp coarsely ground black pepper, 1 tbsp paprika, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp onion powder
- Mop: 200 ml apple juice, 50 ml cider vinegar, 50 ml beef stock, 1 tbsp honey (optional)
- Wrap: 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp brown sugar, 50 ml beef stock

**Method**
1. Remove the silverskin and trim excess fat lightly.
2. Rub with the spice mix, cover and refrigerate at least 4 h, ideally overnight.
3. Preheat the smoker to 110–120 °C with a water pan.
4. Smoke meat side up for 3 h, mopping every 30–45 min.
5. Wrap with butter, sugar and stock; return to the smoker for 2 h at 120 °C.
6. **Target core 94–98 °C**, until butter tender.
7. Unwrap and smoke about 30 min to firm the bark. Rest 10–15 min.

### 4. South African (Food I Love) — Beef Ribs Smoked on the Braai
**Inspired by**: Food I Love (South Africa); https://foodilove.info/blog/2013/3/1/lekker-beef-ribs-on-the-braai (fetched 2026-11-01). *A Salt Lick–derived pepper rub, hickory chips, a water pan, 200 °F for 1–2 h, then the braai raised to 356 °F with a baste every 40 min for 4 h. No internal temperature is stated anywhere.*
**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 15 min + 1 h marinade · **Cook**: 4–6 h · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 6 kg bone-in beef ribs, cut 3–5 inches thick
- Rub: 7.5 tbsp black pepper, 1.5 tbsp cayenne pepper, 7.5 tbsp sea salt
- 1 bottle braai marinade or sauce; hickory chips, soaked 1 h

**Method**
1. Preheat the braai or Big Green Egg to 200 °F (94 °C); bring the meat to room temperature.
2. Grind the rub coarsely and rub generously; rest 20 min.
3. Scatter the drained hickory over the charcoal and set a water pan under the ribs.
4. Smoke 1–2 h with the lid closed, without opening it.
5. Raise the braai to 356 °F (180 °C) and baste every 40 min for 4 h.
6. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** Doneness is given by time and basting only.

### 5. Norwegian (Bradley Smoker Europe, recipe by Cory Walby) — Flaken Cut Røkt Short Ribs
**Inspired by**: Bradley Smoker Europe, recipe by Cory Walby (Norway); https://bradleysmokers.no/blogs/recipes-beef/flaken-cut-rokt-short-ribs (fetched 2026-11-01). *Flanken-cut (cross-cut) short ribs in a soy–ginger–sesame marinade, smoked 30 min at 225 °F to 125 °F internal, then seared 2–3 min per side. A different cut geometry from the plate ribs above — recorded for the divergence, not as an endpoint for English-cut.*
**Serves**: 1–2 lb of ribs · **Prep**: 4 h to overnight marinade · **Cook**: about 35 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Flanken-cut beef short ribs
- 1 inch fresh ginger, grated; 2 garlic cloves, grated; 2 spring onions, chopped
- ¾ cup soy sauce, ½ cup rice vinegar, 1 tbsp sesame oil, ⅓ cup brown sugar, ¼ cup white wine, 2 tsp hot sauce
- Sesame seeds and spring onion to finish

**Method**
1. Mix the marinade, bag the ribs and marinate at least 4 h or overnight.
2. Start the smoker at 225 °F (107 °C) with hickory.
3. Smoke 30 min, **or until the internal temperature is 125 °F (52 °C)**.
4. Optional: sear over the hottest possible grill, 2–3 min on the first side, turn, then 30 s and off.
5. Simmer the reserved marinade 4–5 min and use it to glaze.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Group | Pit / oven | Stated endpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| AmazingRibs (US) | B | 225 °F (107 °C) | 203 °F (95 °C) |
| Texas Beef Co. (US) | B | 225 °F (107 °C) | 200 °F (93 °C) |
| Fitmeat (AT) | B | 110–120 °C | 94–98 °C |
| Food I Love (ZA) | C | 200 °F, then 356 °F | not stated |
| Bradley (NO), flanken | D | 225 °F (107 °C) | 125 °F (52 °C) |

Three of the five sources state a numeric endpoint for English-cut plate ribs, and they agree inside a 5 °C band: **93–98 °C (199–208 °F)**. The flanken source's 52 °C belongs to a different geometry and is not comparable. The South African source contributes time and basting but no number.

That band sits inside what the cut frontmatter recommends for `pulled` (88–96 °C) at its lower end and slightly above it at the top. The divergence is recorded rather than smoothed: the sources that go highest are the ones cooking the thickest plate ribs.
