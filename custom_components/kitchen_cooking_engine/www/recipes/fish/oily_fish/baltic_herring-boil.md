# Baltic Herring × Boil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Boiling/simmering Baltic herring in liquid is the documented everyday method on both sides of the Gulf of Bothnia: Swedish tradition boils *salt strömming* (barrel-salted fish) in its own brine-spiked liquid and never wastes the broth — often mixed with milk and drunk to the meal, or thickened with barley flour and milk into a *redning* ("smältning"); Finnish tradition cooks fresh *silakka* into *silakkakeitto*, a clear or milk-thickened soup where potatoes, swede or vegetables boil in the same pot and the fish is laid in near the end. The salt-first step in the Swedish practice (and in some Finnish soups) firms the flesh so the small fish hold together in liquid; the cooking liquid becomes sauce, soup and drink. No fetched source states an internal pull temperature; none is inferred.

## Styckesprofil

Kokning/sjudning av strömming i vätska är den dokumenterade vardagsmetoden på båda sidor om Bottenviken: svensk tradition kokar *salt strömming* (fatlagd fisk) i spadet, som aldrig slängs — ofta blandas mjölk i spadet och dracks till maten, eller så görs en redning av kornmjöl och mjölk ("smältning"); finländsk tradition lagar färsk *silakka* till *silakkakeitto*, en klar eller mjölksjuden soppa där potatis, lanttu eller grönsaker kokar i samma kastrull och fisken läggs i mot slutet. Salt-steget (i svensk praxis och i vissa finska soppor) ger köttet konsistens så det håller ihop i vätskan. Ingen källa anger innertemperatur.

## Source recipes

### 1. Matkult.se (Institutet för språk och folkminnen material) — Tillagning: kokt salt fisk
**Inspired by**: https://www.matkult.se/fisk/konservering-och-tillagning.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish food-culture documentation ("Matkult"), with archival recipe titles from the Swedish Women's Educational Association recipe archive (e.g. "Kokt saltströmming med löksås från Ed", "Kokt salt sik från Nordmaling"). Historical practice rather than a quantified modern recipe.
**Serves**: — (method documentation) · **Prep**: salting beforehand (days to months on the barrel) · **Cook**: until cooked; duration not stated

**Ingredients** (as documented)
- Salt fish, usually herring or strömming (*salt fisk, vanligen sill eller strömming*), from the barrel
- Water or the brine-cooking liquid (*spad*)
- Optionally milk, mixed into the broth for drinking
- Root vegetables and/or potatoes, boiled in the same liquid
- For the thickening (*redning / smältning*): barley flour (*kornmjöl*) and milk (or water if milk was scarce); wheat flour in later periods
- Onion sauce (*löksås*), the documented accompaniment in the archived "Kokt saltströmming" recipe title

**Method** (as documented)
1. Salted fish could be eaten raw from the barrel (*spicken*), but was usually boiled (*kokades vanligen fisken*).
2. Boil the fish in liquid; the broth (*spadet*) was always used afterwards — often mixed with milk and drunk with the meal.
3. Potatoes and root-vegetable pieces could boil in the broth; the salted herring or strömming could be laid on top of the potatoes as they boiled.
4. Lift the fish from the broth and make a thickening (*redning, "smältning"*) of barley flour and milk (or water) for the sauce.
5. The source gives no boiling time, liquid temperature, internal temperature, or explicit pull point; do not infer any of them. Serve with the onion sauce noted in the archived recipe title. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 2. Martat.fi (Finnish Martha Association) — Kirkas silakkakeitto
**Inspired by**: https://www.martat.fi/reseptit/kirkas-silakkakeitto/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Recipe from the Finnish Martha Association (Martat), given to them by teacher Karri Rautsola of Ravintolakoulu Perho; originally published in Martat-lehti 02/2019. Clear Baltic herring soup; the ingredient list names *silakka, tuore, itämeri* and *silakka, savustettu, itämeri* (fresh and smoked Baltic herring).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: broth 10 min, then steep · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 100 g smoked silakka (Baltic herring)
- 200 g silakka fillets, fresh
- 200 g leek
- 200 g fennel
- 2 tbsp butter
- 2 dl white wine (or lemon-flavoured water)
- 2 tsp fennel seeds
- 2 tsp whole allspice
- 0.5 tsp salt
- 8 dl water
- 150 g frozen peas
- Dill

**Method**
1. Halve and rinse the leek, cut into strips; dice the fennel. Sweat the leek and fennel in the butter in a pot.
2. Add the white wine and let the alcohol evaporate (or use lemon water).
3. Wrap the fennel seeds and allspice in muslin (or a tea infuser) and add with salt and water. Boil about 10 minutes.
4. Skin the fresh silakka fillets and cut into pieces; fillet the smoked silakka and cut into generous pieces.
5. Add the fish pieces and peas to the pot, **turn off the heat** and let the soup steep a moment. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; the fish is cooked in the hot broth by standing, not by a stated target — do not infer one.
6. Remove the spice bundle; finish with chopped dill on top. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 3. Isoäidin reseptillä (1909 cookbook reproduction) — Silakkakeitto
**Inspired by**: http://isoaidinreseptilla.blogspot.com/2016/09/silakkakeitto.html (fetched 2026-10-24). Finnish heritage-cooking blog reproducing a recipe from a 1909 cookbook (original recipes © Kustannusosakeyhtiö Otava) in both the original wording and a modernised version; blog by "anna hildan tyttärentytär". One of the two forms is used below (the modernised quantities).
**Serves**: not stated (3/4 kg silakoita in the 1909 original) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: simmer until potatoes and fish are soft · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients** (modernised version as published)
- 350 g potatoes (small, in the original)
- 150 g swede (*lanttu*)
- Sea salt
- 80 g onion
- 400 g cleaned silakka
- 4 whole allspice
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 dl milk
- 2 tbsp wheat flour
- A knob of butter (or a splash of olive oil)
- Chives, to garnish

**Method**
1. Peel and chop the potatoes and swede; boil the cubes in salted water until al dente.
2. Cut the silakka and onion into bite-sized pieces; lay them on top of the potatoes. Add the allspice and bay leaf.
3. Let the soup simmer on gentle heat (*hautua miedolla lämmöllä*) with the lid on until the potatoes and fish soften — the 1909 wording: *keitto saa kiehua hiljasella tulella kannen alla, kunnes perunat ja kalat ovat pehmeät*. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one.
4. Whisk the milk and flour together and thicken the soup; season, finish with butter/oil and chives. The source states no final serving temperature. (The 1909 original: potatoes boiled half-soft first, cleaned fish laid on top, everything boiled gently under the lid, thickened with milk and flour.)

### 4. Pro Kala (Finnish fish-industry recipe site) — Silakka-pastakeitto
**Inspired by**: https://prokala.fi/reseptit/silakka-pastakeitto/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Recipe from Pro Kala, a Finnish fish recipe/industry site; house recipe, no individual author named.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: a few minutes for the fish · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 300 g silakka fillets
- ½ leek
- approx. 1 l water
- 2–3 tbsp fish or lobster fond
- 100 g flavoured fresh or frozen cheese (prawn, tomato-olive or similar)
- 300–400 g frozen or fresh vegetables in pieces
- 1–1½ dl small macaroni (e.g. shell shape)
- Salt (optional)
- Black or white pepper
- Chopped dill

**Method**
1. Rinse the leek and cut into rings. Bring the water to a boil; add the fond, leek strips and cheese in knobs; boil a few minutes.
2. Add the vegetables and pasta; boil 6–10 minutes (check the package times).
3. Cut the silakka fillets into 2 cm pieces and fold them carefully into the soup. Cook on gentle heat a few minutes — **do not boil**, so the fish does not fall apart (*älä keitä, ettei kala hajoa*). The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one.
4. Taste, season, add plenty of dill. Serve e.g. with toasted rye bread. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 5. Kalanduse teabekeskus — Räime-klimbisupp (Baltic herring and dumpling soup)
**Inspired by**: https://www.kalateave.ee/et/kala-tarbimine/retseptid (fetched 2026-10-25). Estonian Fisheries Information Centre (Kalanduse teabekeskus) recipe collection. Species note: *räim* = Baltic herring — the Estonian name in the cut file's verified name pool.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: broth about 50 min + 10 min + a few min for the fish · **Rest**: soup stands 30–60 min at 60–70 °C before serving

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg uncleaned fresh herring or herring fillets
- 1 kg stock fish (bullhead or perch) or fish-soup assortment
- 6 medium potatoes
- 1 medium carrot
- 1 onion
- Chopped dill/herbs, bay leaf, pepper, salt (amounts not stated)
- Dumplings: 2 eggs, 1 tbsp butter, flour and milk (amounts not stated), salt and sugar (amounts not stated)

**Method**
1. If using whole stock fish, remove gills (and the head from bullhead or perch); rinse. Start the fish or soup assortment in cold water. Skim, add quartered onion, bay leaf, pepper and salt; simmer about 50 minutes, skimming occasionally.
2. Clean the herring, remove the backbones while keeping each fish intact along the back, and cut into pieces.
3. Grate the carrot and dice the potatoes.
4. Make the dumpling batter by mixing beaten egg with softened butter, then adding milk and flour gradually until the batter holds on a spoon. Season with salt and sugar; herbs optional.
5. Strain the stock, add carrot and potato, and boil another 10 minutes.
6. Shape small dumplings with two wet teaspoons and add them to the hot stock.
7. Add the herring pieces and chopped herbs. The page says the soup is ready when the dumplings rise to the surface — the stated doneness cue. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point for the fish; do not infer one.
8. Leave the soup on a warm stove 30–60 minutes at 60–70 °C for the flavours to meld (this is a soup-holding step, not a fish endpoint).

### 6. nra.lv — Reņģu zupa (Baltic herring soup)
**Inspired by**: https://nra.lv/maja/170041-gardeziem-smalkas-renges.htm (fetched 2026-10-25). Latvian newspaper Neatkarīgā Rīta Avīze (nra.lv) food article "Garšīgām smalkas reņģes". Species note: *reņģe* = Baltic herring, *Clupea harengus membras* — verified via University of Latvia natural-history source priede.bf.lu.lv (fetched 2026-10-25).
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 200 g herring
- 1 carrot
- 1 onion
- 1 tbsp butter
- 400 g potatoes
- 1 bay leaf
- 100 g sour cream
- Salt, pepper, herbs and lemon (amounts not stated)
- 1 l water

**Method**
1. Chop the onion and fry it in butter in a pot. Add 1 litre water and bring to a boil. Add grated carrot, peeled and diced potatoes, and salt; continue cooking.
2. Shortly before the vegetables are done, add the remaining seasonings and the cleaned herring.
3. Cook briefly, taking care not to let the fish fall apart. Remove the fish, debone it, return it to the soup with chopped herbs, and heat through again. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one.
4. Serve with sour cream and a lemon slice. The source states no final serving temperature.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Matkult.se (SE, archival) | Boil salted fish; broth reused; thickening added | Not stated |
| Martat.fi (FI) | Broth boiled ~10 min; fish steeped in hot broth after heat off | Not stated |
| Isoäidin reseptillä (FI, 1909) | Simmer gently under lid until potatoes and fish soften | Not stated |
| Pro Kala (FI) | Fish added last, cooked on gentle heat a few min, "do not boil" | Not stated |
| Kalanduse teabekeskus (EE) | Fish added near the end; ready when dumplings rise; soup held 60–70 °C | Not stated |
| nra.lv (LV) | Fish cooked briefly in the vegetable broth; "take care not to let it fall apart" | Not stated |

No fetched source states an internal or final serving temperature. The observed process window is qualitative: the fish goes into liquid that is at or near boiling and is cooked "until soft", "a few minutes on gentle heat", or by standing off the heat — every source explicitly warns against vigorous boiling once the fish is in (Pro Kala verbatim: *älä keitä, ettei kala hajoa*; nra.lv: take care not to let the fish fall apart). No temperature range or `recommended_doneness` key can be derived. The 60–70 °C figure in the Estonian source is a soup-holding step after cooking, not a fish doneness target. Tradition coverage: Swedish archival ×1, Finnish ×3 (documented same-tradition duplication), Estonian ×1, Latvian ×1 — four distinct traditions of the Baltic name pool. ## What makes this method special

Boiling/simmering strömming is the zero-waste one-pot method: the fish seasons the broth, the broth feeds the soup, the sauce or the drink, and potatoes or vegetables cook alongside. The salt-first step — barrel salting in the Swedish practice, a short coarse-salt soak in some Finnish soups — firms the flesh so the small fish survive immersion without disintegration, and it shifts seasoning into the fish before cooking. The shared rule across all four sources is gentle heat after the fish enters the liquid: boiling breaks the flaking flesh apart, so the endpoint is texture ("soft"), not temperature. Unlike pan-frying or smoking, boiling produces no crust or smoke layer; the payoff is the broth economy and the soft, fully cooked texture suited to everyday and smörgåsbord-era boiled-fish courses.

## Summary

Boiled or simmered strömming is the everyday preparation across the Baltic: barrel-salted fish or fresh silakka/räim/reņģe cooked gently in broth with potatoes and vegetables, the liquid reused as soup, sauce or drink. The six fetched sources agree that the fish must never boil hard once it is in the liquid and that the endpoint is soft flesh or dumplings rising, but none publishes a fish temperature, so this leaf adds method coverage and documented traditions, not a temperature recommendation.
