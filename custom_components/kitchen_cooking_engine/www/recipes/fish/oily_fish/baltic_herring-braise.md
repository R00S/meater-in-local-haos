# Baltic Herring × Braise — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Braising/stewing Baltic herring (Russian *salaka* тушёная, Latvian *reņģes sautētas*) is the moist-heat method of the eastern Baltic: cleaned fish are laid over a bed of slowly stewed vegetables or onion, a small amount of liquid or cream is added, and the fish cooks covered or in sauce at gentle heat until the flesh flakes. The method suits this small fish precisely because it is forgiving — no crust to protect, no fillet to tear (the pan-fry failure mode), and the sauce buffers the lean flesh from overshooting. The two Russian sources are stovetop stews with the lid on; the Latvian source names its dish *sautētas* (stewed) and executes the braise in the oven at 200 °C in cream — recorded below exactly as published. None of the fetched sources states an internal pull temperature; every one uses time and texture ("until the fish is done", "do not stir") as the endpoint, and none is inferred here.

## Styckesprofil

Bräsering/stuvning av strömming (ryska *салака тушёная*, lettiska *reņģes sautētas*) är Östersjöns östra fuktmetod: rensad fisk läggs över en säng av stuvade grönsaker eller lök, lite vätska eller grädde tillsätts och fisken tillagas täckt eller i sås på mild värme tills köttet lossnar. Metoden passar den lilla fisken eftersom den är förlåtande — ingen yta att skydda, ingen filé att riva sönder, och såsen skyddar det magra köttet. De två ryska källorna är stuvningar i kastrull med lock; den lettiska källan namnger sin rätt *sautētas* (stuvad) och utför bräseringen i ugn vid 200 °C i grädde. Ingen källa anger innertemperatur; alla använder tid och textur som slutpunkt.

## Source recipes

### 1. Povar.ru — Салака тушеная (Stewed Baltic herring)
**Inspired by**: https://m.povar.ru/recipes/salaka_tushenaya-16614.html (fetched 2026-10-25). Russian recipe platform Повар.ру; unattributed house recipe.
**Serves**: 3 · **Prep**: part of 30 min total · **Cook**: ~20 min + 5 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 400 g salaka (Baltic herring)
- 3 carrots
- 2–3 tomatoes
- 1–2 celery roots
- ½ onion
- Vegetable oil, to taste
- Parsley, to taste
- Dill, to taste
- Salt, to taste

**Method**
1. Wash and peel the vegetables. Coarsely grate the carrots and celery; chop the onion. Cut the tomatoes into wedges and set aside. Put the onion, celery and carrot in an oiled pan and stew over medium heat.
2. Wash and gut the fish; the author recommends removing the heads. Add it to the vegetables.
3. Add tomatoes and salt to taste, cover. Stew until the fish is done, about **20 minutes**. Uncover, add fresh herbs and stew another **5 minutes**. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 2. iamcook.ru — Тушеная салака с морковью и луком (Stewed Baltic herring with carrot and onion)
**Inspired by**: https://www.iamcook.ru/showrecipe/23308 (fetched 2026-10-25). Russian recipe community Аймкук (iamcook.ru); page identifies the fish as frozen salaka. Species note: *салака* = *Clupea harengus membras* (Baltic herring), verified via ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Салака (fetched 2026-10-25).
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: part of 40 min total · **Cook**: ~10 min covered · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 600 g frozen salaka
- 1 onion
- 1–2 carrots
- 2 tbsp tomato sauce
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- 2 bay leaves
- Sugar, optional
- Water, amount not specified

**Method**
1. Thaw the fish, initially in its packaging. Heat the oil in a pan and fry the sliced onion for a couple of minutes, until soft.
2. Add finely sliced carrot and stew over low heat for **3–4 minutes**.
3. Add tomato sauce, bay leaves and water; bring the sauce to a boil. Season with salt and pepper; add a little sugar if needed. Simmer the vegetables for **2–3 minutes**.
4. Clean the fish; remove head and innards, and remove the backbone and dorsal fin. Lay the fillets over the vegetables, pressing them slightly into the sauce.
5. Cover and stew over low heat for about **10 minutes**. The recipe advises not stirring during this step. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one.

### 3. nra.lv — Krējuma mērcē sautētas reņģes (Baltic herring stewed in cream sauce)
**Inspired by**: https://nra.lv/maja/170041-gardeziem-smalkas-renges.htm (fetched 2026-10-25). Latvian newspaper Neatkarīgā Rīta Avīze (nra.lv) food article "Garšīgām smalkas reņģes"; recipe given within the article. Species note: *reņģe* = Baltic herring, *Clupea harengus membras* — verified via University of Latvia natural-history source priede.bf.lu.lv (fetched 2026-10-25).
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated (until done) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg herring or herring fillets
- 250 ml sweet cream
- Salt and pepper, amounts not stated
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 large onion

**Method**
1. Wash and clean the fish. Arrange in a baking dish, placing a layer of onion rings between the layers of fish.
2. Pour over cream; season with salt and pepper and add the bay leaf.
3. Cook uncovered in a preheated oven at **200 °C** until the fish is done. The page gives no duration and no internal temperature; do not infer either. (The same article notes the house rule that reņģes should not stay in the oven longer than 20–30 minutes — stated as advice, not as a timed step.)
4. Serve with boiled potatoes. The source states no final serving temperature.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Povar.ru (RU) | Covered stew ~20 min + 5 min uncovered, medium/low heat | Not stated |

No fetched source states an internal or final serving temperature. The observed cook window is **10–25 minutes** of moist heat after the fish enters the sauce (the Latvian oven form is un-timed in its own step text), driven by whether the fish is layered whole (Russian, ~20 min) or as fillets laid into sauce (Russian, ~10 min) or cream-covered in the oven (Latvian). All three sources share the same endpoint logic: time plus "the fish is done", never a probe. No temperature range or `recommended_doneness` key can be derived from this leaf. Tradition coverage: Russian ×2 (documented same-tradition duplication) and Latvian ×1 — Culinary Group D throughout; Groups A, B and C are not represented.

## What makes this method special

Braising is the only method in this cut's inventory that protects the fish with a liquid medium instead of exposing it: the sauce (vegetable-and-tomato in the Russian stews, cream in the Latvian) buffers the lean flesh from overshooting and carries flavour into it at the same time. That makes it the most forgiving method for a fish whose pan-fried failure mode is mechanical (sticking, tearing) and whose grilled failure mode is exposure — here the cook window is measured in tens of minutes and the endpoint is a texture. The eastern-Baltic specificity is notable: both Russian sources work from a vegetable bed with the fish laid on top and never stirred (iamcook verbatim: *не помешивая*), a technique that keeps the small fish intact in the sauce.

## Summary

Braised Baltic herring is the eastern-Baltic gentle method: fish laid over stewed vegetables or onion, cooked covered or in cream sauce until flaking, with a 10–25 minute window and no probe anywhere in the sources. The three fetched recipes (Russian ×2, Latvian) confirm the method and document the shared technique of never stirring the fish once it is in the sauce, but publish no temperatures, so this leaf adds method coverage and documented traditions rather than a temperature recommendation.
