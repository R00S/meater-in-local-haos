# Baltic Herring × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling Baltic herring (strömming, Finnish *silakka*) is documented in Swedish food culture as two archival techniques: *glödstekt* — the fish laid directly on glowing embers (sometimes wrapped in newspaper in later times) — and *halstrad*, small fresh fish skewered on a stick over an open fire. Modern recipes keep the same geometry: whole, gutted fish with the backbone left in, salted briefly, laid on an oiled grill rack (*halster*) over high heat or embers and turned once. The tiny fish (40–80 g) tolerates intense radiant heat only because the cook time is minutes; the historic methods used no thermometer and no stated endpoint beyond the fish being cooked through. This method was not in the auto-populated `methods:` list for baltic_herring; it is added on the basis of the fetched documentation below. No fetched source states an internal pull temperature; none is inferred.

## Styckesprofil

Moderna recept behåller samma geometri: hel rensad fisk med ryggbenet kvar, kort saltad, på oljad halster över hög värme eller glöd, vänd en gång. Den lilla fisken (40–80 g) tål den heta strålningen bara för att tiden mäts i minuter; de historiska metoderna använde ingen termometer. ## Source recipes

### 1. Matkult.se (Institutet för språk och folkminnen material) — Glödstekt eller halstrad
**Inspired by**: https://www.matkult.se/fisk/konservering-och-tillagning.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish food-culture documentation ("Matkult"), archival photographs credited to Isof (Institute for Language and Folklore); documents historical Swedish fish cooking. Historical practice rather than a quantified modern recipe.
**Serves**: — (method documentation) · **Prep**: catch cleaning only · **Cook**: minutes over embers/open fire; duration not stated

**Ingredients** (as documented)
- Fresh fish, especially herring or strömming (*särskilt sill och strömming*); small freshly caught fish
- (Later period: newspaper, for wrapping before laying on the embers)

**Method** (as documented)
1. For *glödstekt*: lay the fish directly on the glowing embers (*man la den helt enkelt på glöden*). In later times it could first be rolled in newspaper.
2. For *halstrad*: skewer the freshly caught small fish on a stick over the open fire (*halstrad på en pinne över öppen eld*). Spits (*halster*) became common in the late 19th century; earlier the fire tongs (*eldtången*) served the purpose.
3. Cook until done. The source gives no time, internal temperature, or explicit pull point; do not infer any of them. The stated context is that this was an immediate meal when fishing (*en bra måltid att äta när man var ute och fiskade*).
4. Serve immediately. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 2. Köket.se — Sotig strömming på grillen
**Inspired by**: https://www.koket.se/sotig-stromming-pa-grillen (fetched 2026-10-24). Swedish recipe platform (TV4's Köket.se); house recipe, no individual author named. "Sotare" is the sooty, hard-grilled strömming of the outdoor tradition.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: a few hours' salting (cold) · **Cook**: 3–4 min per side · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg strömming with the backbone left in
- 1.5 dl coarse salt
- 1 bunch dill, chopped
- Oil, for the grill rack

**Method**
1. Layer the strömming and salt in a bowl; leave cold for a couple of hours.
2. Brush a halster (grill rack) with oil, lay in the fish and grill 3–4 minutes per side. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.
3. Layer the finished strömming generously with dill.
4. Serve with boiled new potatoes. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 3. Lisa Elmqvist (Stockholm fish merchant) — Halstrad strömming
**Inspired by**: https://shop.lisaelmqvist.se/recepies/recepie/239/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Recipe from the long-running Stockholm fishmonger Lisa Elmqvist (Östermalmshallen); house recipe.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: about 1 h salting · **Cook**: 4–5 min per side over embers · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg whole fresh strömming (*pinfärsk*)
- 1 dl coarse salt (or ½ dl fine salt)
- For the potato bed: 800 g cold boiled new potatoes, 1 pepper, 250 g fresh spinach, 1½ tsp white pepper, 1½ tbsp white wine vinegar, 5 tbsp neutral oil, ½ tsp salt, 1 dl olives

**Method**
1. Clean the strömming but leave the backbone in. Rinse and put it in a plastic bag with the salt; shake. Refrigerate about 1 hour, turning a few times so the fish salts evenly.
2. Rinse off the salt and let the fish drain on kitchen paper. Lay the strömming tightly in a row on a halster and grill over embers 4–5 minutes per side. The source also states it works to halstra in a hot frying pan with a little oil or on a rack. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one.
3. Prepare the bed: slice the cold potatoes; the pepper is grilled cut-side up at 200 °C for 15 minutes, then peeled and cut into strips; wash and tear the spinach. Whisk salt, pepper and vinegar, then the oil, into a vinaigrette and dress the bed.
4. Serve the grilled strömming on the potato bed with boiled new potatoes, dill or chives, sour cream or cold butter. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 4. What's Up Yukon (Sydney Keddy) — Rosemary grilled herring
**Inspired by**: https://whatsupyukon.com/recipes/entrees/rosemary-grilled-herring/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Canadian outdoor/lifestyle publication (Yukon); recipe by Sydney Keddy (Oland). Species note: the source says *herring* without specifying the subspecies; included as the North American (Group B) whole-fish grilling tradition for the same species.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min marinating · **Cook**: 3 min + 3 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 8 whole herring, gutted
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp dried rosemary
- Pinch red pepper flakes
- Juice of ½ lemon
- Salt and pepper to taste

**Method**
1. Toss the herring with olive oil, dried rosemary, red pepper flakes, lemon juice, salt and pepper. Cover and refrigerate 20 minutes while heating the grill.
2. Heat the grill to high. Place the herring on the grill and cook 3 minutes; flip and cook 3 more minutes. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.
3. Remove and serve drizzled with lemon juice and olive oil. The source states no final serving temperature. (Source tips: rinse the herring before seasoning; season inside the body cavity too.)

### 5. Balti Jaama Turg — Grillitud räimed gremolata ja sibula-paprikasalatiga (Grilled Baltic herring with gremolata and onion-pepper salad)
**Inspired by**: https://www.astri.ee/bjt/retseptid/retsept/grillitud-raimed-gremolata-ja-sibula-paprikasalatiga/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Recipe page of Balti Jaama Turg (Baltic Station Market, Tallinn). Species note: *räim* = Baltic herring — the Estonian name in the cut file's verified name pool.
**Serves**: 4–5 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 5 min per side · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g herring fillets
- Salt, amount not stated
- For the gremolata: large handful parsley (2 dl), 5 tbsp olive oil, zest and juice of ½ lemon, 1 garlic clove, salt and black pepper
- For the salad: 250 g small peppers, 10–15 green onion stalks, 2 tbsp olive oil, 1 head lettuce

**Method**
1. Finely chop the parsley. Mix with olive oil, lemon zest and juice, and grated garlic; season with salt and pepper. Set aside.
2. Heat the grill. Halve and seed the peppers; cut the onion stalks in half. Toss with olive oil.
3. Grill the peppers over medium heat, turning occasionally, about 8 minutes. Add the onions and grill a few minutes until golden.
4. Tear the lettuce and mix with the grilled vegetables; season and dress with half the gremolata.
5. Sprinkle the herring with salt, place it in a fish-grill basket and grill **about 5 minutes on each side**. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.
6. Serve with the salad and the remaining gremolata. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 6. EdimDoma — Салака на гриле (Baltic herring on the grill)
**Inspired by**: https://www.edimdoma.ru/retsepty/75659-salaka-gril-sladkaya-rybka (fetched 2026-10-25). Russian recipe community Едим Дома (EdimDoma). Species note: *салака* = *Clupea harengus membras* (Baltic herring) — verified via ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Салака (fetched 2026-10-25). Note: the fish is marinated 1–1½ hours before grilling; this is a short fresh-fish marinade, not a pickled product.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 1–1½ h marinating · **Cook**: "a few minutes" (page total 1 h 30 min incl. marinade) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg salaka
- 2 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 ml unrefined vegetable oil, as printed on the page
- Salt, to taste
- Mixed peppercorns, to taste

**Method**
1. Clean, rinse, gut, and remove the head and dorsal fin. Add salt, spices, lemon juice and oil. Marinate 1–1½ hours.
2. Put the fish on a well-heated, lightly oiled grill grate and close it.
3. Wait until the coals are covered with grey ash and there is no open flame. The page gives no numerical heat setting or cooking duration.
4. Turn the fish frequently and brush with vegetable oil while cooking.
5. Check the thickest part with a knife: the fish is done when the meat separates easily from the bone. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one. Serve hot. The source states no final serving temperature.

All six sources agree on the endpoint being visual or tactile — skin crisped, flesh opaque, meat separating from the bone — and none states an internal or final serving temperature, so no temperature range or `recommended_doneness` key can be derived from this leaf. Culinary Groups A and C are not represented.

## What makes this method special

Cooking directly on embers or over a high grill is the most intense heat application for this fish: radiant heat sears the skin and cooks the small body in minutes, with smoke from dripping fat contributing flavour that no indoor method reproduces. The Estonian source's fish-grill basket is the modern answer to the archival stick — both hold the small fish together over the fire. The salt-first step in both modern Swedish recipes (1–2 h in coarse salt) firms the small fish so it survives the grill rack without tearing — the same function the archival stick served. The technique's elegance is its zero equipment; the trade-off is total exposure: no lid, no sauce blanket, no margin, which is why every fetched source pairs the method with small, fresh fish cooked and eaten at once.

## Summary

Grilled strömming is the fisherman's preparation: whole small fish salted briefly, then straight onto embers or a hot grill for three to five minutes a side and eaten immediately. The six fetched sources — archival Swedish practice, two modern Swedish recipes, Canadian, Estonian and Russian — agree on the technique and the visual endpoint but publish no measured data, so this leaf adds method coverage and documented traditions, not a temperature recommendation.
