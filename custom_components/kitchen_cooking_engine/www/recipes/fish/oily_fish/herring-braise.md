# Herring × Braise — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

This Caribbean preparation braises already-smoked, salted herring flakes briefly in a tomato, onion, pepper and herb sauce after rehydration. The source uses the traditional *red herring* form, so this leaf records a cooked smoked-fish preparation rather than implying that it applies to fresh herring. The fish needs only a short simmer to take on the sauce; the recipe does not state a core temperature or a measured endpoint. The title method is supported by an award-winning Caribbean food writer's published recipe, though more sources are needed before making broader claims.

## Source recipes

### 1. Chris De La Rosa / CaribbeanPot.com — Stewed Red Herrings (smoked herring)
**Inspired by**: https://caribbeanpot.com/stewed-red-herrings-smoked-herring/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Caribbean culinary curator Chris De La Rosa; the story connects the dish to Barbados street-vendor breadfruit stuffed with stewed red herring.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: rehydrate and debone (time not stated) · **Cook**: about 15 minutes for sauce and 3–5 minute simmer · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb red herring fillets (smoked herring)
- 2 medium onions, sliced
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 3–5 thyme sprigs
- 3 garlic cloves, smashed
- 3 scallions, chopped
- 10 cherry tomatoes or 1 large tomato, diced
- 1 1/2 tbsp tomato purée
- 2 tbsp finely chopped parsley
- 1 Scotch bonnet, sliced
- 3/4 cup water
- Juice of 1/2 lime

**Method**
1. Pour boiling water over the smoked herring to remove excess salt and smoke and rehydrate it. Cool, drain, rinse with warm water, drain and squeeze dry; remove large bones and break into small pieces.
2. Heat olive oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Sweat the onions over low heat for about 3 minutes.
3. Add black pepper, Scotch bonnet, garlic and thyme; stir and cook 2 minutes. Add bell pepper (included in the source method although not listed in its ingredient list) and cook another 2 minutes.
4. Add tomato purée in the centre of the pan and cook 2 minutes, stirring.
5. Add the prepared herring, scallions, tomato and water. Bring to a boil, cover, then lower heat and simmer uncovered for **3–5 minutes**.
6. Turn off the heat; stir in lime juice and parsley. Source does not state internal temperature or final serving temperature.

## What makes this method special

The braise begins with smoked herring that is rehydrated and desalted, then adds it late to a liquid already carrying softened onion, pepper, herbs and tomato. The short simmer lets the fish lend smoke and salt to the sauce without pretending it needs a long cook as a fresh cut would. Scotch bonnet and lime add heat and acidity, while the smoked fish's strong flavour stands up to the aromatic vegetable base. The source provides no probe endpoint, so this leaf records method evidence rather than temperature guidance.
