# Herring × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling herring exploits the fish's naturally high fat content — the oil self-bastes the flesh over direct heat and drips to create aromatic smoke that flavours the skin. Herring are typically grilled whole (gutted, head on or off), scored 2–3 times per flank to prevent curling and let heat penetrate, or grilled as butterflied fillets. The fetched sources document four traditions: the British hot-grill (broiler) with compound mustard butter, Japanese whole-fish salt-grilling (*shioyaki*, 塩焼き), Russian Don-region charcoal-grilling of fresh or thawed fish, and the Egyptian *rangah* — hot-smoked herring re-grilled over a dry pan or under the oven grill, an Eid dish. The Egyptian source is the only Group C representative, and it starts from already-smoked fish rather than fresh; that difference is recorded here rather than smoothed over. All four use qualitative endpoints; none states an internal pull temperature, and none is inferred here.

## Source recipes

### 1. Grilled herrings with mustard butter — BBC Good Food
**Inspired by**: https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/grilled-herrings-mustard-butter (recipe page verified 2026-05-26 by earlier session; magazine recipe, September 2005).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: 2 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 whole herring, gutted and cleaned
- 25 g softened butter
- 1 tbsp wholegrain mustard
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- Lemon wedges, to serve

**Method**
1. Using a sharp knife, slash each herring 2–3 times on each side.
2. Mix together the butter, mustard and parsley to make a compound butter.
3. Season the fish inside and out with salt and pepper.
4. Cook under a preheated hot grill (or over charcoal) for 3–4 minutes each side until the skin is blistered and charred in places.
5. The stated endpoint is opaque flesh and crisp skin. The source does not specify an internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one.
6. Rest 2 minutes. Top with a slice of compound mustard butter and serve with lemon wedges.

### 2. cocomin23 / Cookpad JP — ニシンの姿焼き（塩焼き）(Whole grilled salted herring)
**Inspired by**: https://cookpad.com/jp/recipes/19484914 (fetched 2026-10-23). Japanese community recipe platform; contributor recipe by cocomin23 (whole 35 cm seasonal herring, salt-grilled).
**Serves**: 2 (2 herring) · **Prep**: 10 min+ salting · **Cook**: appliance grill, auto setting · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 herring (*nishiin*)
- Coarse salt (*arajio*), to taste
- 2 shiso leaves
- Grated daikon radish, to serve

**Method**
1. Scale the herring (a bottle cap works well), tail toward head; rinse and pat dry.
2. Salt the fish and leave 10+ minutes; wipe off the moisture that rises. Score both flesh sides, salt the fins ("cosmetic salt"), and salt the whole fish; grill on the appliance grill (*grill* function, auto setting).
3. Serve with shiso and grated daikon.
4. The source notes grill time varies by appliance, and gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one. Placing the head toward the back of the grill and the tail toward the front colours the fish evenly.

### 3. ЮсяЮся / EdimDoma — Сельдь гриль (charcoal-grilled fresh fish; herring variant)

**Ingredients**

**Method**
1. Wash and clean the fish; marinate if desired, or leave un-marinated.
2. Place on the grill grate over coals and grill. The source gives no duration or measured doneness endpoint.

### 4. طريقة عمل الرنجة المشوية — Cairo24 (chef Nona, "البلدي يوكل", PNC Food)
**Inspired by**: https://www.cairo24.com/1988296 (fetched 2026-10-25). Cairo24 (القاهرة 24), Egyptian news outlet; the grilled-herring methods are attributed to chef Nona's segment on the PNC Food programme "البلدي يوكل", with a further variant credited to chef Hala Fahmy. Egyptian tradition (Culinary Group C). The fish is *rangah* (رنجة), a hot-smoked herring, and the page treats grilling as the step that opens the fish and loosens the skin from the flesh.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: a few minutes per side on the hob; 10 min foil-wrapped in a very hot oven; ~4 min on a rack under a fully heated grill · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- *Rangah* (smoked herring), quantity not stated — no ingredient list is printed for the grilled methods themselves
- Onion slices, tomato slices and a few drops of corn oil (foil-wrapped oven variant)
- Tahini, vinegar, lemon juice and olive oil, for the finished salad the page dresses the flaked fish with

**Method** (dry-pan variant, the one the page presents first)
1. Put a flat pan on the hob over medium heat and let it get very hot, adding no fat of any kind.
2. Lay the *rangah* side by side on the hot pan, leave it a few minutes, then turn it to the other side.
3. The fish opens and the skin separates from the flesh — that separation, not a time, is the endpoint the source gives.
4. Clean away skin, head, tail and bones, flake the fish, and dress it with oil, lemon juice, vinegar and tahini. The page warns that this method fills the kitchen with a strong smell.

**Oven-grill variants** on the same page: wrap each fish with onion and tomato slices and a few drops of corn oil in two layers of foil and put it into a fully preheated very hot oven for **10 minutes**; or set the fish on a rack over a water-filled tray in an oven heated top and bottom and grill for about **4 minutes**, which the page says leaves the fish opened and puffed.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The endpoint is the skin lifting away from the flesh, with time given only as "a few minutes" on the hob.

## What makes this method special

Direct radiant heat renders herring's abundant subcutaneous fat as it cooks, effectively basting the flesh while dripping oil flares into the smoke that flavours the skin — a self-reinforcing cycle that suits this oily fish better than lean species. Scoring is structural: without it the skin contracts and curls the fish away from the heat. The Japanese salt-grill variant uses pre-salting and standing time to draw surface moisture before grilling; the Don-region recipe accepts optional marinading or plain fish and uses coals. The Egyptian *rangah* shows the opposite case: the fish arrives already smoked, so grilling is purely a surface step — a dry pan with no added fat, a few minutes a side, until the skin lifts — which makes the grill double as the way the fish is opened and cleaned for the salad.

## Summary

Grilling blisters herring's skin and lets its own rendered fat baste the flesh, so a very fatty fish comes out crisp outside and moist inside within minutes — a strong match for this cut, and the reason scoring the flanks matters more here than on a lean fish. The British, Japanese, Russian and Egyptian sources reach that same physical result on entirely different equipment, from a domestic hot grill to a bare dry pan, which suggests the method tolerates substitution well. What none of them offers is a probe target: every cook judges by char, opacity, and the skin lifting away from the flesh. So grill herring by watching the surface and pulling early rather than waiting for a number that no tradition supplies.
