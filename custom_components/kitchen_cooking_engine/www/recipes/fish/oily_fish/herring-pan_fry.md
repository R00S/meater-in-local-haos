# Herring × Pan Fry — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Pan-frying herring spans several forms: rye- or breadcrumb-coated paired fillets in Swedish cooking, whole fresh Don-region herring dusted with flour, and whole salted or fresh fish in Japanese home recipes. The small, oily fish cooks rapidly on a hot pan; the fetched recipes describe browned or crisp skin and fully cooked flesh without reporting probe temperatures. ### 2. Stekt sill med potatismos, lingon och brynt smör — Köket.se / Tareq Taylor
**Inspired by**: https://www.koket.se/stekt-sill-med-potatismos-lingon-och-brynt-smor (fetched 2026-10-24). Recipe from Tareq Taylor’s cookbook *Favoriter*; full recipe page read.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg sill fillets, dorsal fin removed
- 2 dl rye flour
- 2 dl breadcrumbs
- Salt
- 100 g butter
- 1 dl sunflower oil
- For serving: potato purée, browned butter, lingonberries

**Method**
1. Combine the rye flour and breadcrumbs.
2. Place fillets flesh-side together in pairs and coat them in the flour mixture; season with salt.
3. Fry in butter and sunflower oil. The source gives no time, explicit doneness cue, or internal temperature.
4. Serve with potato purée, browned butter and lingonberries.

### 3. М. Е. / EdimDoma — Донская селедка жареная (Don-region pan-fried herring)
**Inspired by**: https://www.edimdoma.ru/retsepty/66201-donskaya-seledka-zharenaya.amp (fetched 2026-10-24). Home recipe explaining the Don spring-herring tradition.
**Serves**: metadata says 1; recipe uses 3 fish · **Prep**: clean and season · **Cook**: duration not stated · **Rest**: briefly with onion

**Ingredients**
- 3 fresh Don herring, about 27–28 cm each
- 150 g flour
- 1 bunch green onion
- 5 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 tsp salt

**Method**
1. Clean and rinse the fish; slit the belly and remove gills and viscera, taking care not to damage the roe. Salt inside and out.
2. Coat in flour and fry in hot vegetable oil over medium heat, first on both sides, then brown the back and belly so the roe also cooks through.
3. Transfer to a dish, sprinkle with chopped green onion, and let the fish absorb its aroma and juices before serving.
4. The source gives no cooking duration or internal-temperature endpoint.

### 5. HIROGARU / Cookpad JP — にしんのムニエル・マスタードソースのせ
**Inspired by**: https://cookpad.com/jp/recipes/19774426 (fetched 2026-10-24). Contributor recipe with fresh herring browned in butter and served with mustard sauce.
**Serves**: 2–4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: duration not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 herring
- Flour and butter, as needed
- Mustard sauce: 3 tbsp each mayonnaise and wholegrain mustard; 1 tbsp each ponzu and honey or syrup; 1/4 onion, minced

**Method**
1. Fillet the herring into three pieces, rinse and dry thoroughly; halve the flesh and coat in flour.
2. Heat a generous amount of butter in a frying pan. Place fish flesh-side down and cook over medium heat until browned.
3. Turn and continue until browned on both sides and cooked through; no duration or internal temperature is given.
4. Mix the sauce ingredients, warm minced onion for 30 seconds in a microwave, combine and serve over the fish.

## What makes this method special

Pan-frying exposes the herring skin directly to a hot cooking surface, browning the exterior while the small, oily fillets cook quickly. The Swedish source pairs rye flour and breadcrumbs with butter and oil, the Don-region recipe fries whole fish in flour, and the Japanese recipe uses a butter-browned floured fillet. Their equipment and endpoints differ, and none publishes probe temperatures; these observations describe the collected methods, not a completed consensus.
