## Cut profile

Air-frying mackerel is a modern simplification of the pan-fry: the basket's high-velocity hot
air renders the skin and crisps it without the sticking and splattering that mackerel's very
high fat content causes in a pan. Four fetched traditions now document it: a
Japanese-style breakfast preparation (Tiffy Cooks), a British fillet guide (Feast Glorious
Feast), a Nigerian/American spiced-steak marinade (Cooking With Claudy) and a South Indian
masala whole-fish fry (Kannamma Cooks). The three fish forms differ — skin-on pieces, fillets,
cut steaks and whole gashed fish — and so do the basket temperatures (390–400 °F / 199–204 °C)
and times (8–20 min). Only the Nigerian/American source states an internal temperature
(145 °F / 63 °C); the others trust time and appearance, and that absence is recorded here
rather than filled in.

### 1. 3 Ingredient Mess-Free CRISPY Air Fryer Mackerel — Tiffy / Tiffy Cooks
**Inspired by**: https://tiffycooks.com/crispiest-3-ingredient-air-fryer-mackerel-mess-free/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Tiffy, Tiffy Cooks; the page labels the cuisine "Asian, Japanese" (served as part of a Japanese-style breakfast). 2 skin-on mackerel pieces; the page does not state whole-fish vs fillet, species, or fresh vs frozen.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 pieces mackerel, skin-on
- Salt and pepper, to taste

**Method**
1. Make several cuts in the skin side of the mackerel.
2. Season both sides with salt and pepper.
3. **Air fry at 400°F for 10 minutes, flipping halfway through.**

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is the 10-minute timed cook at 400 °F.

### 2. How to Air Fry Mackerel Fillets — Feast Glorious Feast
**Inspired by**: https://feastgloriousfeast.com/air-fry-mackerel-fillets/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Feast Glorious Feast; British home-cooking guide, written around UK basket air fryers (Cosori models named). Mackerel fillets, boned; fresh or from frozen (frozen method also given).

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated (fillet and de-bone; dry thoroughly) · **Cook**: ~8 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Mackerel fillets, quantity not stated
- Salted butter, quantity not stated (oil may be substituted)
- Sea salt flakes, to season

**Method**
1. Check the fillets and remove any bones. Pat the fish extremely dry.
2. Coat the fillets in butter and season with sea salt flakes.
3. Preheat the air fryer (the author's own model is preheated at 205 °C / 400 °F for 4 minutes).
4. Air fry **skin side up at 200 °C / 390 °F for around 8 minutes**; do not turn the fillets.
5. From frozen: run the fillets under cold water to remove surface ice, dry them, then proceed as above and add about a minute to the cooking time.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is ~8 minutes at 200 °C with browned skin and plump flesh.

### 3. Air Fryer Mackerel — Claudy / Cooking With Claudy
**Inspired by**: https://cookingwithclaudy.com/air-fryer-mackerel/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Claudy, Cooking With Claudy; the page labels the cuisine "African, American". 2 whole mackerel, scaled, gutted and cut into steaks (or 4 fillets).

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 20 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 whole mackerel, scaled, gutted and cut into slices (steak sizes), or 4 mackerel fillets
- 1 tsp paprika
- ½ tsp curry powder
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 2 tsp ginger and garlic paste (or 1 tsp of each powder)
- 1 tsp seasoned salt
- 1 tsp bouillon powder
- ½ tsp white pepper
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 1 tsp dried parsley
- ¼ cup cooking oil
- 1 Tbsp lemon juice

**Method**
1. Preheat the air fryer to **400 °F for 3–5 minutes**, depending on the model.
2. Combine all the ingredients except the fish into a marinade.
3. Pat the fish dry, pour the marinade over it and coat every piece by hand. (Optional: marinate 2–3 hours, covered, in the fridge.)
4. Arrange the mackerel steaks in the basket in a single layer without overlapping.
5. **Air fry at 400 °F for about 20 minutes, flipping at the 10-minute mark.**
6. Remove, garnish if wished, and serve immediately. The FAQ gives the working range as 18–22 minutes, the flesh opaque and flaking.

**Probe and endpoint**: the recipe card states **Pull at 63°C** (145°F) — "The internal temperature should be around 145°F (63°C)"; the FAQ repeats "if it reads 145 degrees or more, the fish is done" and offers the fork-flake test as the thermometer-free fallback.
Final serving temperature: **63°C / 145°F** (as stated by the source).

### 4. Air Fried Mackerel Fish Fry – South Indian Style — Suguna Vinodh / Kannamma Cooks
**Inspired by**: https://www.kannammacooks.com/air-fried-mackerel-fish-fry/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Suguna Vinodh, Kannamma Cooks; South Indian (Tamil) tradition. Whole ayala/ayila meen (a mackerel); the printed ingredient list covers only the marinade and gives no fish quantity — recorded as published.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated (+1 h refrigeration) · **Cook**: 12–13 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Whole mackerel (ayala meen), quantity not stated in the ingredient list
- 1.5 tsp thick ginger-garlic paste
- ½ tsp turmeric powder (½ tsp more for a spicier fry)
- 1 Tbsp red chilli powder (1 Tbsp more for a spicier fry)
- 1 tsp red chilli flakes
- 1 tsp salt
- 3 sprigs curry leaves, finely chopped
- ½ tsp plain vinegar
- 3 Tbsp coconut oil (or peanut oil)

**Method**
1. Wash, clean and prep the fish. Make small, even gashes on it with a sharp knife so the masala penetrates; do not cut too deep.
2. Mix the marinade ingredients with the oil only — no water.
3. Coat the fish evenly, working the masala into the gashes.
4. Refrigerate and marinate **1 hour**.
5. **Air fry at 200 °C for 12–13 minutes**, the time varying with fish size.
6. Serve hot with sliced onions and lemon or lime wedges.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is 12–13 minutes at 200 °C.

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Japanese-labelled (Tiffy Cooks) | Not stated — 400 °F, 10 min, flip halfway | Not stated |
| British (Feast Glorious Feast, fillets) | Not stated — 200 °C / 390 °F, ~8 min, skin up | Not stated |
| African / American (Cooking With Claudy, steaks) | 145 °F internal (FAQ: "145 degrees or more") | **63 °C / 145 °F** |
| South Indian (Kannamma Cooks, whole gashed fish) | Not stated — 200 °C, 12–13 min | Not stated |

Where temperatures are stated the observed range is a single value, **63 °C / 145 °F**, which is
exactly the USDA safe minimum for fish and this cut's `well_done` target. The other three
sources publish no internal temperature at all, so no range can be constructed from them and
none is inferred. What does vary and is published is the appliance setting (390–400 °F /
199–204 °C) and the time (8–20 min) — a spread explained by the fish's form rather than by
doneness philosophy: thin fillets finish in ~8 minutes, whole cut steaks in ~20, and the
gashed whole fish in 12–13. All four land on the same well-done outcome (opaque, flaking,
crisp skin); nothing in the collection supports a lower-doneness air-fryer preparation.

## What makes this method special

The air fryer does for mackerel what it does for chicken skin: it concentrates fast-moving hot
air on the surface so the skin renders and crisps while the interior stays moist, with none of
the pan's sticking and none of the deep-fryer's oil. For this oily cut the practical gain is
twofold — much of the rendered fat drops into the basket instead of splattering, and the
scored/gashed skin in the Japanese and Indian sources serves the same anti-buckling role it
does on the grill, keeping the skin attached and crisp. The British butter coating and the
Indian/Nigerian oil-based spice pastes show the two ways cooks exploit the same physics: fat on
the surface as a heat-transfer and flavour vehicle. The cost is the butter basting and pan
deglazing that the Swedish pan-fry tradition relies on.

## Summary

Air-frying is the clean, modern stand-in for the pan-fry: hot circulated air crisps the skin and drops most of the rendered fat away, in 8–20 minutes depending on whether the fish is filleted, cut into steaks or left whole. It suits this oily cut well for weeknight cooking, and the spiced Indian and Nigerian marinades show it takes strong seasoning without losing the fish. The one measured source pulls at 145 °F, the safe floor, so the method's target is identical to every other mackerel method collected so far.
