# Mackerel × Boil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Published recipes use the label “boiled mackerel,” but their actual instructions span slowly boiling a whole fish, gently bringing liquid near boiling, and bringing a fish stew to a boil before reducing it to a lively simmer. The modern Korean mackerel stew explicitly uses the last sequence, so it is classified as braise/simmer rather than sustained boiling. Historical recipes establish boiled mackerel as a culinary method, though they do not identify Atlantic or Indian species or specify an internal temperature.

## Source recipes and method evidence

### 1. Eliza Leslie / Directions for Cookery (1851) — Boiled Mackerel
**Inspired by**: https://www.seafoodfish.com/html/boiledmackerel1851.html (fetched 2026-10-24), transcription of the 1851 cookbook. Tradition: US cookbook. The source says “Clean the mackerel well”; species and more specific cut are not stated.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: brief vinegar-water soak · **Cook**: 20 min for small fish · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- Whole cleaned mackerel (quantity/size not stated); vinegar and water for brief soak
- Cold water and a handful of salt
- Parsley sauce and minced parsley garnish; mustard

**Method**
1. Clean fish and let it lie briefly in vinegar and water.
2. Put it in a fish kettle with cold water and a handful of salt.
3. “Boil them slowly”; the source says small fish are sufficiently cooked in 20 minutes. The eye starting and tail splitting are the doneness cues.
4. Lift immediately when cooked; serve with parsley sauce and mustard.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The source relies on eye and tail cues.

**Ingredients**
- 2 large mackerel; hot water; salt
- Parsley sauce (recipe title; ingredient quantities not captured in the available source excerpt)

**Method**
1. Put fish in hot water and bring gently to near boiling point.
2. Reduce heat and cook very gently about 10 minutes.
3. Serve with parsley sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The page says the skin becoming loose is a doneness sign.

### 3. Kyu Jeong Jeon and Duncan Robertson / The Guardian — Korean-Style “Hard-Boiled” Fish with Korean Radish
**Inspired by**: https://www.theguardian.com/food/2025/jun/11/korean-style-boiled-fish-stew-asparagus-salad-recipes-duncan-robertson-kyu-jeong-jun-dongnae-bokman-bristol (fetched 2026-10-24). Korean tradition. Ingredient line: “2 large sustainably-sourced mackerel, or herring,” heads and guts removed and cut into large chunks (800 g–1 kg net); species not stated.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Seasoning sauce: 120 g soy sauce; 120 g mirin; 40 g garlic; 10 g ginger; 15 g doenjang; 15 g gochujang; 30 g fish sauce; 15 g gochugaru
- 1 mooli (about 500 g); 2 small onions (about 250 g); 2 large mackerel or herring (800 g–1 kg net); 10 g kelp; sliced spring onions; steamed rice

**Method**
1. Blitz sauce ingredients smooth.
2. Layer mooli, onion, fish and kelp in a wide shallow pan; pour sauce over and add enough water to cover fish.
3. Bring to a boil, then turn down to a lively simmer; cook 20–25 minutes, regularly basting, until fish is cooked through, mooli tender and sauce reduced.
4. Serve with spring onions and rice.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. Although the article cites “hard-boiled mackerel,” the recipe explicitly reduces to a lively simmer after the initial boil; this is not counted as a sustained-boil source.

## Method disposition

The historical cookbook sources establish the named boiled-mackerel tradition, but their exact species is unspecified and one source cooks gently near boiling. The Guardian recipe calls its dish “hard-boiled” but its method is initial boil followed by lively simmer. This confirms a real boiling-category lead and clearly documents overlap with simmering; it does not justify claiming that modern Atlantic or Indian mackerel recipes routinely use a sustained rolling boil. No internal temperatures are stated.

## Summary

Boiled mackerel is evidenced in historical cookery, while contemporary fish stews commonly transition from an initial boil to simmer. `boil` is a credible cut-method lead, but the currently fetched evidence is not sufficient for a robust modern, species-specific method leaf or temperature analysis. **PARTIAL RESEARCH**.
