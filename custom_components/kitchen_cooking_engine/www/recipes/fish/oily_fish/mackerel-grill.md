# Mackerel × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling is one of mackerel's finest preparations: the high fat content renders dramatically
over direct heat, the skin blisters and crisps, and the flesh cooks in minutes while basting
itself. The fetched sources handle the fillet or whole fish the same three ways — salt first
(and, in the Korean and Swedish traditions, rest 20–30 minutes to firm the flesh), score the
skin to stop buckling, and grill skin-side first over medium-high to grey-coal direct heat
with a single turn. All three fetched sources use visual endpoints (opaque flesh, golden or
charred skin); none states an internal temperature, and none is inferred here.

## Styckesprofil

Grillning är en av makrillens finaste beredningar: det höga fettinnehållet renderar kraftigt
över direktvärme, skinnet blisterar och krispar, och köttet tillagas på minuter medan det
självbastar. De hämtade källorna saltar först, snittar skinnet och grillar med skinnsidan
nedåt över medelhög direktvärme med en enda vändning. Alla tre använder visuella slutpunkter;
ingen anger innertemperatur och ingen sådan härleds här.

## Source recipes

### 1. Grillad makrill — Köket.se
**Inspired by**: https://koket.se/grillad-makrill (fetched 2026-05-31). Köket.se; Swedish tradition.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min (plus potato cooking) · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: 2 min

**Ingredients**
- 700 g makrillfilé (mackerel fillet — skin on, pin bones removed)
- 600 g färskpotatis (new potatoes)
- 12 schalottenlökar (shallots)
- 4 salladslökar (spring onions)
- 1 ask körsbärstomater (punnet cherry tomatoes)
- 2 msk olivolja
- 0,5 limefrukt, pressad saft (lime, juiced)
- 0,5 dl pinjenötter (pine nuts)
- 1 rödlök, finhackad (red onion, finely chopped)
- Salt, peppar

**Method**
1. Skär bort benraden från makrillfiléerna och snitta ett par gånger genom skinnet (remove pin bones; score skin 2–3 times).
2. Skrubba eller skala potatisen. Koka den nästan färdig. Skala schalottenlökarna och lägg ner dem när ca 5 minuter återstår. Lägg efter någon minut ner salladslökarna. Slå av vattnet och låt ånga av.
3. Dela tomaterna. Vänd samman dem med potatis och lök. Tillsätt olivolja och smaka av med pressad limesaft, salt och peppar.
4. Rosta pinjenötterna gyllenbruna i torr panna (toast pine nuts in dry pan until golden).
5. Grilla fisken vackert rutig (grill fish to an attractive crosshatch pattern). Krydda.
6. Dra av när skinnet är grillat i rutigt mönster och köttet är ogenomskinligt rakt igenom.
7. Lägg upp och dekorera med pinjenötter och rödlök. Serve with the warm potato-tomato salad.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is visual (crosshatched skin, opaque flesh).

### 2. Receta de caballa a la barbacoa — Pakus / Directo al Paladar
**Inspired by**: https://www.directoalpaladar.com/recetas-de-pescados-y-mariscos/receta-caballa-a-barbacoa-ideal-para-disfrutar-pescado-a-brasas-verano (fetched 2026-10-24). Pakus, Directo al Paladar; Spanish tradition. Whole mackerel (~450 g), skin on.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 3 min · **Cook**: 12 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Whole mackerel — approximately 450 g
- Salt — to taste (plus salt crystals at the table)
- Black pepper — to taste
- Extra-virgin olive oil — for brushing

**Method**
1. Clean the mackerel and remove the innards if the fishmonger has not done so. Season lightly inside and brush with a few drops of olive oil, spreading it with a silicone brush.
2. Prepare the barbecue coals. When they are grey and ready, place the mackerel on the grill bars about **10 cm above the coals**.
3. Cook about **5–6 minutes on each side**. Turn with barbecue tongs to avoid breaking the fish.
4. Add salt crystals at the table.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is time and coal state ("grey and ready").

### 3. Godeungeo gui (Grilled Mackerel) — Korean Bapsang
**Inspired by**: https://www.koreanbapsang.com/godengeo-gui-grilled-mackerel/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Korean Bapsang; Korean tradition. Fresh mackerel, filleted into 2 or 4 pieces, or butterflied whole; skin-on.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min (20–30 min salt rest) · **Cook**: 6 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Fresh mackerel — 1, filleted into 2 or 4 pieces, or butterflied whole
- Salt, preferably sea salt — quantity not stated
- Lemon juice — 1 tablespoon, optional
- Ginger juice — 1 teaspoon, optional

**Method**
1. Cut crosswise slashes in the skin of each piece and pat the fish dry. Drizzle with the optional lemon and ginger juice. Salt both sides liberally and leave **20–30 minutes**.
2. Shake off the excess salt. Clean and lightly oil the grill; preheat over **medium-high heat**.
3. Place the fish **skin-side down**. Cook until the lower edges are golden and the flesh turns opaque, **2–3 minutes**.
4. Turn once and cook the other side for about **2 minutes**.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is visual (golden edges, opaque flesh). The page also gives pan-frying (medium-high) and broiling (high, 4–6 inches from the element) as alternatives.

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Swedish (Köket.se) | crosshatched skin, opaque throughout | Not stated |
| Spanish (Directo al Paladar, whole fish over coals) | Not stated — 5–6 min per side | Not stated |
| Korean (Korean Bapsang, godeungeo gui) | golden edges, flesh opaque | Not stated |

No fetched grill source states an internal temperature, so no numeric range can be observed
and none is invented (the previous version of this leaf's "approximately 63–70 °C" was an
inference, removed 2026-10-24). The three sources converge on **full doneness** — opaque
flesh throughout — in three unrelated traditions, which supports the cut file's `well_done`
key qualitatively but supplies no measured value. The divergence worth noting is structural,
not thermal: the Korean source rests the salted fish 20–30 minutes to firm it, the Spanish
source grills a whole ungutted-scored fish over coals, and the Swedish source grills scored
fillets — three different answers to the same flare-up-and-breakage risk.

## What makes this method special

Grilling is the only method where mackerel's rendering fat drips onto the heat source and
returns as flavour-carrying smoke — the direct-heat equivalent of hot smoking. The skin takes
the full brunt of the fire, so scoring and skin-side-first cooking are near-universal in the
fetched sources; the whole-fish Spanish form additionally shields the flesh behind the skin and
cavity. Mackerel's small size means the cook lasts 4–8 minutes total, which is why every
source works in visual cues rather than thermometers: by the time a probe read settles, the
fish is over.

## Summary

Grilling suits mackerel superbly: the fish's own fat fuels the crisping and self-bastes the flesh while the fire adds smoke. Swedish, Spanish and Korean sources all cook it completely in minutes and none measures the result. The method earns its place on technique and tradition; its temperature record is still visual-only.
