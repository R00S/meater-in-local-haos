# Mackerel × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling mackerel puts its oily skin and fat layer directly against radiant heat, so the
surface crisps and renders within minutes while the flesh behind it steams in its own fat.
That speed is the method's defining constraint: mackerel is thin, so an extra two minutes per
side dries it rather than improving it, and every fetched source answers with short, counted
times (2–3 minutes a side for fillets, 5–7 for whole fish) and a visual test at the bone. The
fat also means the grill needs no oil on the fish itself beyond a light brush, and it means
the skin is the deliverable — the sources consistently grill skin-side down first to protect
it. Charcoal is a fuel/setup variant of this method, not a separate one: two of the five
sources specify coals and the rest use gas or a grill pan.

## Source recipes

### 1. Imma / Immaculate Bites — Whole Grilled Mackerel
**Inspired by**: https://www.africanbites.com/whole-grilled-mackerel/ (fetched 2026-10-24). African-diaspora home cooking; species not stated, "2–3 whole mackerel, about 1.5–2 lb each".

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min (+30 min minimum marinating) · **Cook**: about 14+ min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2–3 whole mackerel, about 1.5–2 lb each
- Salt and pepper; 3–4 fresh lemons
- 2 tbsp minced garlic; 1–1.5 tbsp minced ginger; 1–2 tsp minced basil
- Optional: 2 tsp or more chicken bouillon powder or Maggi

**Method**
1. Rinse and dry the fish. Make 3–4 diagonal cuts on each side through to the bone; season inside and out with salt, pepper and lemon juice.
2. Mix garlic, ginger and basil; coat the fish inside and out. Marinate at least 30 minutes or up to 24 hours.
3. Oil the grill basket. For charcoal, open the bottom vents, add charcoal about 1/4 full, and wait until the flames die down and the ash turns white.
4. Grill about **7 or more minutes per side**, flipping as needed. Baste with unused marinade during the final 5 minutes if desired.
5. Check doneness by confirming the flesh nearest the bone is fully cooked; the author slashes with a knife to inspect.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. Endpoint is fully cooked flesh closest to the bone.

### 2. Pakus / Directo al Paladar — Caballa a la Barbacoa
**Inspired by**: https://www.directoalpaladar.com/recetas-de-pescados-y-mariscos/receta-caballa-a-barbacoa-ideal-para-disfrutar-pescado-a-brasas-verano (fetched 2026-10-24). Spanish tradition; *caballa* is the Spanish market name for Atlantic mackerel (*Scomber scombrus*) — the species this cut describes. Whole fish about 450 g.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 3 min · **Cook**: 12 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole mackerel, about 450 g
- Salt and black pepper
- Olive oil for brushing

**Method**
1. Clean the fish, remove the innards if needed, season the inside and brush with a little olive oil.
2. Prepare the barbecue coals; once they are grey and ready, place the fish about 10 cm above the coals.
3. Cook about **5–6 minutes per side**, turning carefully with barbecue tongs.
4. Add salt crystals at the table.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**.

### 3. Korean Bapsang — Godeungeo Gui (Grilled Mackerel)
**Inspired by**: https://www.koreanbapsang.com/godengeo-gui-grilled-mackerel/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Korean tradition; *godeungeo* is chub mackerel (*Scomber japonicus*), the Pacific counterpart of this cut. Fresh mackerel filleted or butterflied whole.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min (+20–30 min salting) · **Cook**: 6 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 fresh mackerel
- Salt
- Optional: 1 tbsp lemon juice and 1 tsp ginger juice

**Method**
1. Score the skin, pat dry and add the optional lemon and ginger juices. Salt both sides and rest 20–30 minutes.
2. Shake off the excess salt; clean and oil the grill, preheat to medium-high.
3. Grill skin-side down **2–3 minutes** until the edges are golden and the flesh opaque; turn and cook about 2 minutes more.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**.

### 4. Sophie Pham / Delightful Plate — Grilled Mackerel Fillets with Asian Spices
**Inspired by**: https://delightfulplate.com/easy-grilled-mackerel-fillets/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Vietnamese home cooking (author's recipe index is Vietnamese); *saba* fillets, about 340 g total for two. Written for outdoor grill, grill pan or air-fryer.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 min (+10–15 min marinating) · **Cook**: 10 min (about 3 min per side) · **Rest**: none

**Ingredients**
- 2 mackerel fillets, about 12 oz / 340 g
- 1 tbsp sake or rice wine
- **Marinade**: 1 tbsp finely minced ginger; 2 tsp finely minced garlic; 1/4 tsp five-spice powder; 1/8–1/4 tsp red pepper flakes (optional); 1/8 tsp turmeric; 1/4 tsp black pepper; 1 tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp cooking oil; 2–3 tsp chopped cilantro

**Method**
1. Rub the fillets with the sake or rice wine; set aside 4–5 minutes, then pat dry.
2. Mix all the marinade ingredients except the cilantro. Rub over the fillets and leave at room temperature 10–15 minutes — the author warns longer makes them too salty.
3. Preheat the grill pan or outdoor grill over **medium** heat; once hot, oil the grates.
4. Shake off the excess aromatics and grill until **just cooked through, about 3 minutes per side**.
5. Scatter the cilantro over the flesh side, remove from the grill and serve at once with lime or lemon.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated** — the stated test is "just cooked through", with an explicit warning not to overcook. For the air-fryer variant the author gives **180 °C / 350 °F for 5–6 minutes**.

### 5. Epicurious — Grilled Whole Mackerel with Lemon, Oregano, and Olives
**Inspired by**: https://www.epicurious.com/recipes/food/views/grilled-whole-mackerel-with-lemon-oregano-and-olives-109523 (fetched 2026-10-24). US magazine test-kitchen recipe; the fish is **1 whole 3 1/4–3 1/2 lb Spanish mackerel** (*Scomberomorus*, a different, much larger genus) with bluefish as the stated substitute. Recorded as a **method lead only** — its temperatures and times are not transferable to Atlantic/Indian mackerel.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 45 min active · **Cook**: about 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1/2 tsp lemon zest; 1 1/2 tbsp lemon juice; 1/3 cup olive oil
- 1/4 cup pitted Kalamata olives; 3 tbsp chopped oregano plus 6 large sprigs
- 1 whole 3 1/4–3 1/2 lb Spanish mackerel or bluefish, cleaned
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil; 6 lemon slices

**Method**
1. Whisk the lemon zest and juice with salt and pepper; whisk in the olive oil, olives and chopped oregano.
2. Slash the fish at 2-inch intervals, oil and season it; place lemon slices and oregano in the cavity and tie closed.
3. For charcoal, open the bottom vents and light the coals; medium-hot is defined as holding a hand 5 inches above the rack for 3–4 seconds.
4. Grill covered (gas grills only) for 15 minutes; turn the fish and grill about 15 minutes more until just cooked through.
5. Serve with the lemon-olive sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The page's endpoint is "just cooked through".

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| African-diaspora (Immaculate Bites, whole fish over charcoal) | fully cooked at the bone | Not stated |
| Spanish (Directo al Paladar, caballa over coals) | seasoned, cooked through | Not stated |
| Korean (Korean Bapsang, godeungeo gui) | opaque flesh, golden edges | Not stated |
| Vietnamese (Delightful Plate, saba fillets) | "just cooked through" | Not stated |
| US test kitchen (Epicurious, Spanish mackerel lead) | "just cooked through" | Not stated |

**The observed temperature range across the fetched sources is empty: no grilled-mackerel source
states a final internal temperature.** What the sources do converge on is a *texture* endpoint —
flesh opaque to the bone, flaking at the spine, skin golden — which corresponds to the
`well_done` band of the cut file, but the mapping is by practice, not by measurement. Two
distinct conventions are visible in the times rather than the temperatures: whole fish are given
long counted times (7+ min/side for 1.5–2 lb fish; 5–6 min/side for a 450 g fish; 15 + 15 min for
a 3.5 lb fish) while fillets are given short ones (3 min/side), because the fillet has no bone
mass to buffer the radiant heat. The Vietnamese source is the only one of the five that warns
explicitly against overcooking, and its remedy is the shortest time of the set — a fillet-level
expression of the same fat-rendering constraint that pushes every source to "just through".
`recommended_doneness: well_done` therefore stands, with no measured support from this method.

## What makes this method special

Grilling is the only dry-heat method here where the heat arrives mostly as radiation through a
very short air gap, so the skin's fat renders before the interior has time to dry: the surface
temperature soars past the boiling point while the flesh 10 mm below is still far cooler. That
gradient is what produces the split mackerel texture cooks prize — crisp, blistered, slightly
bitter skin over moist, softly flaking flesh. It also makes the method unforgiving: because the
fillet is thin and the fat is already at the surface, there is almost no thermal buffer, so the
window between "just through" and "dry" is a minute or two, which is why four of five sources
give per-side counts rather than a range. The slashing and scoring that three sources specify is
not decoration: it lets rendered fat and marinade reach the interior and gives the heat a path
past the insulating skin.

## Summary

Grilling is mackerel's fastest and most characteristic dry-heat treatment: hot radiant heat crisps
the oily skin in minutes while the flesh underneath stays moist, and the fat that renders out is
the whole point of the dish. It suits this cut better than almost anything else, precisely because
a fish this oily needs no added fat and tolerates fierce heat. What the cook sacrifices is margin —
the fillet is thin enough that a minute too long turns crisp skin into dry flesh, so short counted
times and a visual check at the bone matter more than any timer. The takeaway: grill mackerel hard
and brief, skin-side down first, and take it off the moment the flesh at the spine turns opaque.
