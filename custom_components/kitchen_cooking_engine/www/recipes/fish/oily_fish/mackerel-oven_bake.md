# Mackerel × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven-baking mackerel suits both the Japanese salt-grill-adjacent preparation (saba shioyaki
finished under high oven heat) and the European tray bake — Mediterranean chermoula, Turkish
fish-on-potatoes, Swedish fillets under a breadcrumb-and-seed crust: the dry convective heat
renders the fillet's fat in place, crisping skin against a rack or vegetables while the flesh
steams inside its own oil. Four fetched sources bake at 175–200 °C for 15–30 minutes depending on
form (rack-mounted fillets, fillets under a crust, or whole fish on a potato bed). **One of them
states a measured internal endpoint — 52 °C (Swedish)** — and the other three publish none. That
single figure sits well below the USDA fish minimum and below this cut's `well_done` band, and the
divergence is recorded rather than smoothed over.

## Source recipes

### 1. Saba Shioyaki (Japanese salt-grilled mackerel) — Just One Cookbook
**Inspired by**: https://www.justonecookbook.com/saba-shioyaki/ (fetched 2026-05-26). Namiko Hirasawa Chen, Just One Cookbook; Japanese tradition. The oven/grill setting at high heat is the home adaptation of the charcoal original.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 20 min (inc. salting rest) · **Cook**: 15–20 min at 200 °C · **Rest**: 2 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 saba (mackerel) fillets, skin on (about 150–200 g each)
- 1 tsp salt (kosher or sea salt)
- 1 tbsp sake
- Grated daikon radish (*daikon oroshi*), to serve
- Soy sauce, to serve
- Lemon wedge, to serve

**Method**
1. Pat the mackerel fillets dry. Sprinkle both sides generously with salt. Let rest 15–20 minutes — the salt draws moisture to the surface.
2. Pat dry again — removing the drawn-out moisture is key to crinkled, blistered skin.
3. Brush the flesh side lightly with sake.
4. Preheat the oven to **200 °C (400 °F)**, grill/broil setting or high heat.
5. Place the fillets skin-side up on a wire rack over a foil-lined tray.
6. Bake (or broil) **15–20 minutes** until the skin is blistered and the flesh is fully opaque.
7. Serve immediately with grated daikon, a drop of soy sauce and a wedge of lemon.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is visual (blistered skin, opaque flesh).

### 2. Mackerel baked with chermoula — BBC Good Food
**Inspired by**: https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/baked-mackerel-chermoula (fetched 2026-05-26). BBC Good Food; British tradition cooking North-African-spiced mackerel (the page is a UK adaptation of chermoula, **not** a Group C primary source — recorded as Group B). Note: the site now serves subscribers-only boilerplate to fresh fetches (checked 2026-10-25); the recipe below is the version recorded at the 2026-05-26 fetch.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 20–25 min at 180 °C · **Rest**: 3 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 mackerel fillets (skin on) or 2 whole mackerel, scored
- **Chermoula marinade**: 1 small bunch flat-leaf parsley; 1 small bunch coriander; 3 garlic cloves; 1 tsp ground cumin; 1 tsp paprika; pinch of chilli flakes; juice of 1 lemon; 3 tbsp olive oil; salt
- Lemon wedges, to serve
- Couscous or flatbread, to serve

**Method**
1. Blend all chermoula ingredients in a food processor to a rough paste. Season.
2. Rub the chermoula paste all over the mackerel fillets (or inside and outside the scored whole fish).
3. Preheat the oven to **180 °C / 160 °C fan / gas 4**.
4. Place the mackerel in a single layer in an ovenproof dish. Spoon any remaining chermoula over the top.
5. Bake **20–25 minutes** until the fish is cooked through and the chermoula is beginning to char at the edges.
6. Rest 3 minutes. Serve with couscous or flatbread and lemon wedges.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is visual (opaque, flaking, chermoula charred at the edges).

### 3. Fırında Uskumru Tarifi — Yemek.com
**Inspired by**: https://yemek.com/tarif/firinda-uskumru/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Yemek.com (editor "mrs.sunlock"); Turkish tradition. Whole mackerel baked on a potato bed — a genuine Group C primary source (uskumru = mackerel).

**Serves**: 4 pieces · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 40 min listed; method says about 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 medium mackerel
- 1 medium onion
- 2 medium potatoes
- 1 medium lemon
- 5 cloves garlic
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon ground red pepper
- 2 teaspoons salt
- 1 bay leaf
- (the method also calls for thyme, not listed in the ingredients and not quantified)

**Method**
1. Slice the onion, potatoes and lemon into rounds.
2. Finely chop the garlic. Mix it with the juice of half a lemon, olive oil, salt, thyme (mentioned in the method but not quantified in the ingredient list) and ground red pepper.
3. Coat the fish all over with the sauce.
4. Put the potatoes in the baking tray, drizzle with some sauce, and place the coated fish on top.
5. Add the bay leaf and onion rings to the tray; place the lemon slices on the fish.
6. Bake at **200 derece** for about **30 minutes**, until the potatoes are cooked. No rack position is stated.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated** — the page's endpoint is "until the potatoes are cooked"; its time field (40 min) and method (30 min) disagree, both recorded as published.

### 4. Ugnsbakad makrill med ärtkräm och dillslungad potatis — Allt om Mat (Anna Wendt)
**Inspired by**: https://alltommat.expressen.se/recept/ugnsbakad-makrill-med-artkram-och-dillslungad-potatis/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Anna Wendt, Allt om Mat (Expressen); Swedish tradition. Frozen-then-thawed mackerel fillets, 400 g total, under a breadcrumb-and-seed crust.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: about 30 min active · **Cook**: 15 min in the oven (30 min total cooking) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 400 g mackerel fillets, frozen then thawed
- 1/2 tsp salt
- **Bread and seed crust**: 1 small garlic clove; 2 slices light bread, preferably sourdough; 3/4 dl olive oil; 2 tbsp pumpkin seeds; 1 tbsp sesame seeds; 1 tsp frozen chopped dill; 1 tsp salt; 1 pinch freshly ground black pepper
- **Dill-tossed potatoes**: 800 g potatoes; 1 small garlic clove; 1/2 dl olive oil; 1 tbsp frozen chopped dill; 1/2 tsp salt; 1 pinch black pepper
- **Pea cream**: 1 garlic clove; 250 g frozen green peas, thawed; 1 dl crème fraîche; 2 tbsp olive oil; 1 tsp dried mint; 1 tsp lemon juice; 1/2 tsp salt; 1 pinch black pepper
- **Brown butter**: 75 g butter

**Method**
1. Set the oven to **175 °C**. Put the mackerel fillets in an oiled ovenproof dish, **skin-side down**, and season with salt.
2. For the crust, peel the garlic and blitz all the crust ingredients together; spread the mixture over the fillets.
3. Boil the potatoes until soft, drain and let them steam dry. Grate the garlic and mix with the oil and dill; season. Halve the potatoes, turn them in the dill oil and keep warm.
4. Bake the mackerel in the middle of the oven for about **15 minutes, until the fish's internal temperature is 52 °C**.
5. For the pea cream, grate the garlic and blend all its ingredients with a stick blender. For the brown butter, melt the butter and brown it over medium-high heat until golden.
6. Take the mackerel out of the oven and serve at once with the dill potatoes, pea cream and brown butter.

**Probe and endpoint**: the method **states a measured internal endpoint — "tills fiskens innertemperatur är 52°"** (until the fish's internal temperature is 52 °C) — so a probe is implied even though no insertion point is named.
Final serving temperature: **52 °C** (as stated by the source).

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Japanese (Just One Cookbook, saba shioyaki) | blistered skin, fully opaque | Not stated |
| British (BBC Good Food, chermoula) | "cooked through", flaking | Not stated |
| Turkish (Yemek.com, fırında uskumru) | potatoes cooked; fish baked through | Not stated |
| Swedish (Allt om Mat, crusted fillets) | internal temperature 52 °C | **52 °C** |

The observed range across the fetched sources where a temperature is stated is a **single point:
52 °C** — and it is a genuine outlier in this collection, not a rounding of one. It sits **11 °C
below the USDA fish minimum (63 °C / 145 °F)** and below every band the cut file defines
(`well_done` is 60–68 °C), so it cannot be mapped onto a doneness key at all: it describes a
Nordic preference for barely-set, jelly-soft oily fish, taken out of the oven while the crust is
still pale and the flesh just holds. The three other sources express the same bake as a visual
"opaque/flaking/until the potatoes are cooked", which is consistent with full doneness but measures
nothing. The variation is driven by two different questions — the Swedish recipe is optimising
texture at the moment of serving with no hold time, while the Turkish whole-fish bake is timed by a
companion (potatoes) that must also be done, and the Japanese and British recipes are timed by
surface appearance. No source supports the cut's `medium` band either (57 °C was retired on
2026-10-24 and this 52 °C source would not have justified it: it is lower still).

## What makes this method special

Oven baking is the gentlest dry-heat method for mackerel: instead of the pan's or grill's direct
contact, the fillet roasts in circulating hot air, and its abundant fat keeps the flesh self-basted
even at 200 °C. The rack-based Japanese form chases crisp skin; the dish-based Turkish and British
forms trade skin texture for sauce and vegetable integration, effectively steaming the fish's
underside in its own juices; the Swedish crust adds a third mechanism, insulating the top surface so
the bread and seeds brown in the same 15 minutes the fish needs to reach 52 °C. For this cut the oven
is the forgiving option — a wide tray and slower heat give a several-minute margin the pan does not —
but the Swedish figure is a reminder that "forgiving" is a texture choice, not a safety guarantee:
the same recipe would read 63 °C after another two or three minutes without the crust burning.

## Summary

Oven-baking mackerel trades the pan's crust for evenness and capacity: skin crisps on a rack, or
softens under a sauce, crust or bed of vegetables while the fat keeps the flesh moist. It is the
most forgiving method here and the only one in this collection where a source cooks the fish by
thermometer rather than by eye — and that recipe pulls at 52 °C, far softer than the safe minimum
the rest of the cut is built around. The takeaway for a cook: the oven gives you margin, but decide
first whether you want the Swedish barely-set result or a fully set, flaking fillet, because those
are two different temperatures, not two descriptions of the same one.
