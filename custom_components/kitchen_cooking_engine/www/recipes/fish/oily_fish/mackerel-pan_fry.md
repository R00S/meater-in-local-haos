# Mackerel × Pan Fry — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Pan-frying mackerel is fast and unforgiving of hesitation: the fillet's very high fat content
(up to 20 g/100 g at autumn peak) renders on contact with a hot pan, self-basting the flesh,
while the skin crisps within minutes. All three fetched sources score or salt the fish first
(Swedish scoring, Japanese salting, Turkish flouring) and cook skin-side first over
medium-to-high heat with a brief second-side finish. None of the fetched sources states an
internal temperature; their endpoints are visual — deep golden skin, opaque flesh — and those
endpoints are recorded here without converting them into invented numbers.

## Styckesprofil

Pannstekning av makrill är snabb och oförlåtande: filéns mycket höga fettinnehåll (upp till
20 g/100 g vid hösttopp) renderar vid kontakt med het panna och bastar köttet, medan skinnet
krispar på minuter. Alla tre hämtade källor snittar eller saltar fisken först och steker med
skinnsidan nedåt över medelhög–hög värme. Ingen av de hämtade källorna anger
innertemperatur; deras slutpunkter är visuella och återges här utan härledda siffror.

## Source recipes

### 1. Smörstekt makrill med dillstuvad potatis — Köket.se (Alexandra Zazzi)
**Inspired by**: https://koket.se/smörstekt-makrill-med-dillstuvad-potatis (fetched 2026-05-31). Alexandra Zazzi, Köket.se; Swedish west-coast tradition (smörstekt makrill).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: 2 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 makrillfiléer (mackerel fillets, skin on, pin bones removed)
- Salt, peppar
- 50 g smör (butter)
- **Dillstuvad potatis**: 600 g ny potatis; 3 dl mjölk; 1 dl hackad dill; 1 msk majsstärkelse; salt

**Method**
1. Skär bort eventuella ben från makrillfiléerna. Snitta skinnet ett par gånger med kniv.
2. Krydda filéerna med salt och peppar.
3. Hetta upp smör i stekpanna på hög värme tills det slutar att fräsa.
4. Lägg i filéerna med skinnsidan nedåt. Pressa lätt med en stekspade för att hålla skinnet i kontakt med pannan.
5. Stek 4–5 minuter tills skinnet är djupt gyllent och krispigt. Häll över det smälta smöret i pannan med jämna mellanrum.
6. Vänd filéerna. Stek 1–2 minuter på köttsidan.
7. Dra av när köttet är ogenomskinligt rakt igenom och skinnet är djupt gyllent.
8. Vila 2 minuter. Servera med dillstuvad potatis.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is visual (deep golden skin, opaque flesh).

### 2. ふっくらツヤツヤ！サバの照り焼き (Saba no teriyaki) — DELISH KITCHEN (Asuka Sawada)
**Inspired by**: https://delishkitchen.tv/recipes/216477197584565407 (fetched 2026-10-24). Asuka Sawada, DELISH KITCHEN; Japanese tradition.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated (5 min salt rest) · **Cook**: not stated (overall ~30 min) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Mackerel, filleted — 2 pieces (200 g)
- Long green onion — 1 (165 g)
- Salt — 1/3 teaspoon
- Salad oil — 1/2 tablespoon
- Sake — 1 tablespoon
- Mirin — 2 tablespoons
- Soy sauce — 1 tablespoon
- Grated ginger — 1/2 teaspoon

**Method**
1. Cut the mackerel into three pieces. Sprinkle with salt, leave about 5 minutes, then pat dry with kitchen paper.
2. Cut the green onion into 4 cm lengths.
3. Heat the salad oil in a frying pan. Put in the mackerel **skin-side down** with the onion. Cook over **medium heat** until browned; turn and cook over **low heat 3–4 minutes**, until the fish is cooked through.
4. Add the sake, mirin, soy sauce and grated ginger. Bring to a boil over medium heat, then simmer, coating the fish and onion, until the sauce thickens.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is "cooked through" after the low-heat stage.

### 3. Tavada Uskumru Tarifi — Yemekte Balık Var
**Inspired by**: https://yemektebalikvar.com/tarif/tavada-uskumru-tarifi (fetched 2026-10-24). Yemekte Balık Var; Turkish tradition (uskumru = mackerel). The recipe pan-fries the fillets and finishes them in the oven.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 10 min listed (+ un-timed oven finish) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Mackerel fillets — 4
- Corn flour — 2 tablespoons
- Flour — 2 tablespoons
- Salt — quantity not stated
- Olive oil — quantity not stated

**Method**
1. Wash the fillets and salt them. Mix the flour and corn flour in a bowl.
2. Coat the salted fish in the flour mixture.
3. Put a thick-bottomed pan over **medium heat**; add a thin layer of olive oil and swirl to coat the pan.
4. Arrange the fish in the pan and cook both sides over **high heat for 2–3 minutes per side**.
5. Transfer the pan-fried fish to an oven tray and continue cooking in the oven (**180–200 degrees**, the page does not specify the unit), adjusting for the size of the fish.
6. If needed, brush the fish with olive oil before serving.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the page gives only pan heat levels and the oven range.

## What makes this method special

Pan-frying is the fastest route to mackerel's signature contrast — shattering crisp skin over
barely-set, fat-laced flesh — because the fillet's own rendered oil conducts heat into the
flesh side while the skin fries against the metal. The fat content means no basting is needed,
but it also means the window between done and dry is measured in tens of seconds, which is why
every fetched source uses a two-stage heat (high to colour, then low/medium through) or a
short oven finish. The Turkish source's flour coating and the Japanese source's soy-mirin
glaze both exploit the same physics: anything added to the surface must be applied in the last
moments or it burns before the flesh sets.

## Summary

Pan-frying gives mackerel its crispest skin and shortest cook, in Swedish, Japanese and Turkish forms that all finish the fish completely. It suits the oily cut perfectly, though the margin between rendered and dried is thin. None of the fetched recipes measures the endpoint, so this method contributes technique evidence and full-doneness practice, not temperatures.
