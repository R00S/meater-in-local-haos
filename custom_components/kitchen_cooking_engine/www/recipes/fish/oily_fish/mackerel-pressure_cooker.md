# Mackerel × Pressure Cooker — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Pressure cooking is documented for mackerel from quick fillet cooking to long pressure processing intended to soften bones. Unlike a conventional simmer, the cooker is sealed and the method pages explicitly use pressure-cook settings or pressure-pan whistles; their cook times therefore cannot be treated as one texture target. Most do not state an internal fish temperature or pressure level. Species is often unspecified; the Big Fat Tummy source explicitly names Bangda/Bangde, Indian mackerel.

## Source recipes

### 1. Noemie / Pressure Cooker Recipes — Mackerel Mix Up
**Inspired by**: https://www.pressure-cooker-recipes.com/recipes/mackerel-mix-up/ (fetched 2026-10-24). French, as labelled by the site; ingredient line: “300 g mackerel fillet.” Species not stated.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 1 min pressure time (page metadata: 10 min) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 300 g mackerel fillet; 60 g minced shallots; 60 g thick fresh cream; 100 ml white wine
- 100 g semi-salted butter; 2 tbsp capers; salt and pepper
- 200 ml water for the cooker

**Method**
1. Set Instant Pot to browning; cook shallots and wine until wine evaporates.
2. Add butter, salt and pepper; reserve and clean the pot.
3. Put mackerel in steamer basket with 200 ml water in the cooker.
4. Select pressure cooking for **1 minute**. Remove bones by crumbling the cooked fish; mix with other ingredients.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. Pressure time is 1 minute; the page gives no pressure level.

### 2. tomo_ya / Cookpad — Miso Poached Mackerel (Japanese Pressure Cooker)
**Inspired by**: https://cookpad.com/eng/recipes/16121912 (fetched 2026-10-24). Japanese recipe by tomo_ya (@tomo); ingredient line: “1 mackerel.” Species and cut form beyond whole mackerel not specified. The author describes it as a pressure-cooker adaptation of everyday miso-poached mackerel.

**Serves**: 1 · **Prep**: 10 min salting · **Cook**: 8 min high pressure + about 2 min after adding miso · **Rest**: rapid cooling by water

**Ingredients**
- 1 mackerel; salt and sake for rubbing (quantities not stated)
- Leek, quantity not stated; 1 finger of ginger
- 2 tbsp soy sauce; 1/3 cup water; 1/2 cup sake (or white wine); 2 tbsp sugar; 1 tsp vinegar
- 4 tbsp miso, added at the last stage

**Method**
1. Rub mackerel with salt and sake; leave 10 minutes to reduce fishy smell.
2. Mix the cooking seasonings other than miso.
3. Add chopped leek and fish to the pot.
4. Cook at **high pressure for 8 minutes**, then cool quickly with water.
5. Add miso directly to pot and cook about **2 minutes**.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. “High pressure” is the cooker setting; no numeric pressure is supplied.

### 3. 바겨사 / 10K Recipe — Steamed Mackerel Kimchi Cooked in a Pressure Cooker
**Inspired by**: https://www.10000recipe.com/en/6923440/Steamed_mackerel_kimchi_cooked_in_a_pressure_cooker (fetched 2026-10-24). Korean home recipe. Ingredient line: “Mackerel 5pack”; the author says each pack is 70 g and cuts the fish into pieces. Species not stated. English page translation has an inconsistent ingredient rendered as “a green house”; the method calls for plum, so the translation is retained as uncertain, not silently corrected.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 5 min to steam release + 20 min on low · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 5 packs mackerel (70 g each, per author); 1/4 piece kimchi; radish, amount described as “little”
- 2 tbsp red pepper powder; 3 tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp cooking wine; 1 tbsp crushed garlic
- 2 ginger pieces; leek, “little”; water to cover; ground pepper, “suitably”
- 1 tbsp plum is specified in the method, although the translated ingredient list says “a green house”

**Method**
1. Open the mackerel packs, trim fins, wash, cut into pieces and remove bones that can be pulled by hand.
2. Slice radish and place in the pressure cooker; wrap fish pieces in kimchi and arrange over it.
3. Mix red pepper powder, soy sauce, plum (as named in the method), garlic and pepper; add sauce, ginger and enough water to cover.
4. Heat on medium until steam appears, about 5 minutes; lower heat and cook in the pressure cooker for **20 minutes**.
5. Finish with sliced green onions.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. Page gives heat settings and time only; pressure level is not stated.

### 4. HomeCook / Big Fat Tummy — Mackerels in Tomato
**Inspired by**: https://www.bigfattummy.com/2017/09/mackerels-in-tomato/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Goan/Indian home-style; ingredient line: “Mackerels [Bangda/Bangde] – 8 to 10 big,” identifying Indian mackerel.

**Yield**: about 1 kg · **Prep**: 20 min listed (plus 45–60 min lime/salt rest) · **Cook**: 30 min active + 50 min passive · **Rest**: natural pressure release

**Ingredients**
- 8–10 large Bangda/Bangde mackerels; 1 large lime; 1–2 tbsp palm vinegar; 1/3 cup tomato ketchup
- 5 large ripe tomatoes, deseeded; 6 small garlic cloves; 1/2 inch ginger; 3–4 light green chillies
- 1/3 cup oil; 1 tsp salt or to taste; 3/4 cup warm water; optional 1/2 tsp sugar

**Method**
1. Clean fish, cut each into 2–3 pieces, wash and drain. Apply salt and lime juice; leave 45–60 minutes.
2. Grind tomatoes, garlic, ginger and chillies to a paste.
3. Heat oil in pressure cooker and cook masala over medium heat 10–12 minutes until raw smell disappears and oil separates.
4. Add warm water, vinegar and ketchup; mix. Add mackerel and gently coat; simmer 2 minutes and adjust salt.
5. Cover and cook on medium until first whistle; reduce to low and pressure-cook **50 minutes**.
6. Turn off heat and allow pressure to release naturally. The author says the long cook softens the fish bones.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. This is a long pressure-processing preparation, not a short-cooked fillet recipe.

## Method scope and evidence limits

Four fetched recipe pages explicitly use a pressure cooker for mackerel, so pressure cooking cannot be ruled out as unsupported. They range from a one-minute fillet cook to a 50-minute bone-softening preserve-like preparation; species is unstated in three, and none reports an internal temperature or numeric pressure. This is a source inventory, not a unified temperature consensus. A generic Instant Pot fish guide that does not name mackerel, and a mackerel recipe whose instructions merely suggest an untested pressure-cooker time, were excluded.

## Summary

Mackerel pressure cooking is real but heterogeneous: French and Japanese pages pressure-cook fish briefly, a Korean home recipe cooks pieces with kimchi, and a Goan/Indian recipe pressure-processes Bangda for soft bones.
