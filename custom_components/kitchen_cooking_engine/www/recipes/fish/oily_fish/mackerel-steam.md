## Cut profile

Steaming whole spiced mackerel in a banana-leaf parcel (*pepes*) is the Sundanese West-Javanese
answer to the fish's fragility: the parcel traps the aromatics against the flesh and the steam
cooks it in 25 minutes without any risk of sticking or breaking. The fetched source then gives
the parcel a final grill or pan-heat blast to char the leaves — a two-stage steam-then-char
structure typical of pepes. Indian mackerel (ikan kembung) is the canonical fish for this
preparation; the high fat content keeps the steamed flesh succulent, and no source states an
internal temperature.

## Source recipes

### 1. Pepes Ikan Kembung (Steamed & Grilled Mackerel in Banana Leaves) — Nunuk Sri Rahayu / Cook Me Indonesian
**Inspired by**: https://www.cookmeindonesian.com/pepes-ikan-kembung-steamed-grilled-mackerel-in-banana-leaves/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Nunuk Sri Rahayu, Cook Me Indonesian; Indonesian tradition (pepes, associated with Sundanese West Java). 2 whole ikan kembung (Indian mackerel), 500 g total. **Note**: this is a steam-then-grill two-stage method, recorded as `steam` for its dominant cooking stage.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated (15 min marination) · **Cook**: 25 min steaming (+ un-timed grilling finish) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g (2 pieces) kembung fish (Indian mackerel)
- 7 shallots, peeled and chopped
- 3 cloves garlic, peeled and chopped
- 4 roasted candlenuts, chopped
- 4 cm fresh turmeric, peeled and chopped
- 4 cm fresh ginger, peeled and chopped
- Salt, to taste
- 1 tsp tamarind juice
- 2 stalks lemongrass, bruised and cut into 4 cm pieces
- 2 fresh or dried salam leaves
- 12 bird's-eye chillies
- 1 bunch kemangi (Indonesian basil), around 50 g, stems discarded
- Fresh banana leaves for wrapping, quantity not stated
- Toothpick(s) to secure the wrapping, quantity not stated
- (The cleaning instruction calls for lime juice; it is not in the ingredient list and no amount is given.)

**Method**
1. Clean and wash the fish. Sprinkle with salt and lime juice to reduce the fishy smell; set aside.
2. Blend the shallots, garlic, candlenuts, turmeric and ginger into a smooth paste.
3. Transfer the paste to a bowl, add salt and tamarind juice, and mix.
4. Pat the fish dry. Spread half the spice paste over it.
5. Lay two banana-leaf pieces in a cross. Put about 10 g kemangi leaves, 3 chillies, 1 salam leaf and 1 piece of lemongrass on the leaves.
6. Place the fish on top, then add another layer of 10–15 g kemangi and 3 chillies.
7. Fold the leaves into a parcel with no gaps and secure with a toothpick. Repeat with the other fish.
8. Refrigerate the parcels **15 minutes** to marinate.
9. **Steam over high heat for 25 minutes.**
10. Before serving, grill each parcel in a non-stick pan or oven until the leaves are brittle and nicely browned. No time or numeric temperature is given for the grilling finish.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is 25 minutes of steaming plus leaf char.

spiced juices.

## What makes this method special

Steaming is the gentlest moist-heat method for mackerel: the banana-leaf parcel seals the fish
with its aromatics so none of the fat-soluble flavour escapes, and the flesh cooks in a pure
100 °C steam atmosphere with zero direct heat until the final char. For an oily, fragile fish
this is close to an ideal structural match — no sticking, no breaking, no drying — and the
two-stage finish recovers some of the aroma that plain steaming lacks.

## Summary

Steaming spiced whole mackerel in banana leaves keeps the fragile fish intact and moist while the parcel concentrates the aromatics. The Indonesian pepes form adds a final leaf-char for smoky aroma, making it the mildest yet most aromatic method in this collection. One source supports the method; temperature claims must wait for sources that measure.
