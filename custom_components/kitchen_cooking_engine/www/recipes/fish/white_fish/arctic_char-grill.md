# Arctic Char × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Arctic char is a small-bodied salmonid — a market whole fish runs 400 g to 1 kg, a fillet 150–200 g —
and grilling it splits cleanly into two different problems depending on which form is put on the grate.
A **fillet** is thin, moderate in fat (5–10 g/100 g) and has a skin that crisps well over direct heat; it
needs two or three minutes a side and nothing else. A **whole fish** has to survive long enough for the
cavity and the backbone to heat through, so the four Nordic records here all cook it stuffed, in a
basket or *halster*, over **indirect** heat with the lid down, and only finish it over the coals.

That structural difference is what the temperature spread in this leaf reflects, and it is the useful
finding: the records are not disagreeing about char, they are measuring two different objects. The
Swedish fillet method (Tasteline) pulls at **45 °C**, the whole-fish methods cluster at **50–54 °C**, and
the German whole-fish record names **55 °C as still glassy and 60 °C as done**. A single grilled-char
band that averaged those numbers would describe no actual recipe, so the band below is stated as two
rows rather than one.

Char also carries a real risk that salmon does not: it is small enough that a whole fish reaches serving
temperature in about fifteen minutes, which leaves no window to correct a mistake. The probe goes into
the thickest part of the back, beside the backbone — every whole-fish record here that names a probe
placement names that spot.

## Styckesprofil

Röding är en liten laxfisk — en hel marknadsfisk väger 400 g till 1 kg, en filé 150–200 g — och grillning
delar sig därför i två olika problem beroende på form. En **filé** är tunn, måttligt fet (5–10 g/100 g) och
har ett skinn som blir krispigt över direkt värme; den behöver två–tre minuter per sida och inget mer. En
**hel fisk** måste hinna bli genomvarm ända in till benet, så de fyra nordiska källorna här tillagar den
fylld, i halster, över **indirekt** värme med lock och avslutar först över kolen.

Det är den skillnaden temperaturerna i det här bladet speglar: filémetoden (Tasteline) drar vid **45 °C**,
helfiskmetoderna ligger på **50–54 °C**, och den tyska helfiskkällan anger **55 °C som fortfarande glasig
och 60 °C som färdig**. Sonden sätts i den tjockaste delen av ryggen, intill ryggbenet.

## Source recipes

### 1. Grillad röding med Västerbottenspesto — Tasteline (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.tasteline.com/recept/grillad-roding/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Tasteline (Swedish recipe site), 4/5 from 21 votes. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 1–2 h tempering · **Cook**: 4–6 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 800 g arctic char fillet
- 2 dl rapeseed oil
- ½ dl roasted hazelnuts, coarsely chopped
- 1 dl fresh parsley, chopped
- 1 garlic clove, chopped
- 1 dl Västerbotten cheese, coarsely grated
- salt, white pepper
- 1 lemon half, for grilling

**Method**
1. Chop the hazelnuts, parsley and garlic and fold them into the grated cheese and rapeseed oil; serve the pesto at room temperature. *(paraphrase)*
2. Take the fillets out of the fridge 1–2 hours before grilling so they are at room temperature. *(verbatim)*
3. Brush the fillets with a little oil, salt and pepper them. *(verbatim)*
4. Grill directly over the grill — an oiled fish basket is recommended — for about 2–3 minutes per side, to an internal temperature of **45 °C**. *(verbatim)*
5. Serve at once with a grilled lemon half and a creamy potato salad. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: 45 °C / 113 °F. The source states this as the finishing point, not a pull-and-rest point, and gives no rest. Note that 45 °C sits *below* this cut's `medium_rare` minimum (46 °C), so this record is the rarest single data point in the whole cut, from a method where the fillet is thin enough that carryover is almost nothing.

### 2. Grillad röding med somriga smaker och hollandaise — Köket.se / Jessica Ortman (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.koket.se/grillad-roding-med-somriga-smaker-och-hollandaise (fetched 2026-10-26). Recipe by Jessica Ortman from the TV4 show *Grillmästarna*, published by Köket.se. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 45 min total · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole arctic char, cleaned
- caster sugar and salt, about 50/50, for rubbing
- 1 lemon, 1 shallot, 1 garlic clove, dill, parsley, butter, black pepper
- rapeseed oil, for the basket
- **Hollandaise**: 250 g butter, 2 egg yolks, 1 lemon, 2 tbsp water, salt, white pepper
- 1 bunch asparagus (about 250 g), flaked salt

**Method**
1. Prepare a kettle grill for 50/50 grilling — coals on one side, nothing on the other. *(verbatim)*
2. Clean the fish, remove fins and innards, and rub it inside and out with a roughly 50/50 sugar-and-salt mixture. *(verbatim)*
3. Slice the lemon thinly, chop the shallot, garlic, dill and parsley, and put it all in the belly with a few knobs of butter and pepper. *(verbatim)*
4. Put the fish in a fish basket rubbed with rapeseed oil and set it over **indirect heat with the lid closed**. *(verbatim)*
5. Watch the temperature: at about **50–51 °C** move the fish — still in the basket — over **direct heat** to crisp the skin and colour the surface, then turn and do the same on the other side. *(verbatim)*
6. Lift the fish out of the basket, plate it, add the asparagus and flaked salt, and serve the hollandaise separately. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **50–51 °C** at the switch from indirect to direct. This is the only record in the leaf that uses a temperature as a *stage boundary* rather than an end point — the direct finish adds crust and a few degrees after the probe reading.

### 3. Grillad röding med syrad romgräddsås och primörer — Allt om Mat / Petra Lindstrand (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://alltommat.expressen.se/recept/grillad-roding-med-syrad-romgraddsas-och-primorer/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Allt om Mat (Expressen's food title), recipe by Petra Lindstrand, photography Helén Pe. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 45 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 whole arctic char, about 1 kg each, cleaned
- 1 tbsp flaked salt + 1 tsp, 2 pinches ground black pepper + 1 pinch
- 1 lemon, 150 g spring onion, ½ bunch dill, 1 tbsp olive oil + 1 tbsp
- 500 g bunch carrots, 250 g green asparagus
- **Roe sauce**: 1 banana shallot, 1 bunch dill, ½ bunch chives, 1 small lemon, 2 dl soured cream, 2 dl crème fraîche, 80 g whitefish roe, 1 tbsp Dijon mustard, 1 tsp salt, 2 pinches black pepper

**Method**
1. Dry the fish inside and out with kitchen paper and season with 1 tbsp flaked salt and 2 pinches pepper. *(verbatim)*
2. Rinse the lemon in warm water, slice it, coarse-shred the spring onion, and fill the bellies with lemon slices, onion and dill sprigs; tie with string so the filling stays put. *(verbatim)*
3. Brush the fish and the grill grate with 1 tbsp oil and lay the fish in the grate. *(verbatim)*
4. Grill the fish over **indirect heat with the lid on for about 15 minutes at 175–200 °C, until the internal temperature is 52–54 °C**; check with a thermometer in the thickest part of the back, near the backbone. *(verbatim)*
5. Grill the carrots about 5 minutes and the asparagus about 2 minutes, season with 1 tsp flaked salt and 1 pinch pepper. *(verbatim)*
6. Serve with the roe sauce, new potatoes and the grilled vegetables; garnish with dill. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **52–54 °C**, with the probe placement stated explicitly. The printed "175–200 °C" is the *grill/air* temperature, not the fish — recorded here so the two are not confused later.

### 4. Saibling grillen — eat.de / Michael (recipe) and Nadine (text) (Germany)
**Inspired by**: https://eat.de/rezept/saibling-grillen/ (fetched 2026-10-26). eat.de, 5/5 from 26 ratings; "Rezept entwickelt und gekocht von Michael. Mit Liebe verfasst von Nadine", updated 17 August 2026. **Culinary Group B**.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 20 min + 20–30 min tempering · **Cook**: ~10 min · **Rest**: 15 min stated as *Ruhezeit* (see note)

**Ingredients**
- 2 saibling (arctic char), 400–500 g each
- 1 organic lemon, 2 thyme sprigs, 4 sage leaves, ½ bunch flat-leaf parsley
- salt and pepper
- a fish grill basket, oil for the grate

**Method**
1. Descale the char, rinse it inside and out, dry it, and cut out the gills. *(paraphrase)*
2. Wash the lemon and slice it; wash the herbs. Salt and pepper the fish inside and out, fill it with the lemon slices and herbs, and clamp it into a grill basket. *(paraphrase)*
3. Heat the grill to **220–240 °C direct heat**. *(verbatim)*
4. Grill the char for about **5 minutes per side**. *(verbatim)*
5. Their stated check: at a core temperature of **55 °C the fish is still glassy ("glasig"); from 60 °C it is cooked through ("durchgegart")**. *(verbatim)*
6. Tempering tip: take the fish out of the fridge 20–30 minutes before grilling, because a cold fish steams and sticks. *(paraphrase)*

**Recorded temperature**: **55 °C glassy / 60 °C done** — the only record in the leaf that describes a doneness *spectrum* rather than a single target. The page's "Ruhezeit 15 Min." in the header block is the sum of the tempering and herb-resting steps, not a post-grill rest; no rest after grilling is stated.

### 5. いわなの塩焼き (iwana salt-grill) — MAFF うちの郷土料理, 鈴木正文 (Japan)
**Inspired by**: https://www.maff.go.jp/j/keikaku/syokubunka/k_ryouri/search_menu/menu/iwanano_shioyaki_miyagi.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Japan's Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries regional-cuisine archive; Miyagi prefecture entry, image and recipe provider 鈴木正文氏 (Kurihara City). **Culinary Group A**.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not timed · **Rest**: none

**Method** (as published)
1. Split the belly, remove the innards and gills, and clean the fish. *(verbatim)*
2. Skewer it, salt it, and grill it slowly over charcoal (*炭火でじゅっくり焼く*). *(verbatim)*
3. Turn the skewer while the fish is still hot, so it comes off cleanly; serve with *hajikami* (pickled ginger bud). *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **none — this source publishes no internal temperature and no timing.** It records that the technique exists and what it consists of: whole fish, salt, skewer, charcoal, judged by eye and hand.

**Species note, recorded rather than glossed over**: イワナ (*iwana*, *Salvelinus leucomaenis*) is not *Salvelinus alpinus* — it is a char of the same genus, and it is the fish a Japanese cook would actually use for this preparation. This is therefore **evidence of a Group A char-grilling tradition, not a temperature datum for arctic char**. It is counted as a source record because it is a named human's published recipe, and it is explicitly silent on temperature.

## Temperature summary

Grilled arctic char, as published:

| Form | Pull / target | Source | Notes |
|------|---------------|--------|-------|
| Fillet, direct | **45 °C** | Tasteline (SE) | served straight off the grill; below this cut's medium_rare floor |
| Whole, basket | **50–51 °C** | Köket.se / Ortman (SE) | temperature used as the switch point to direct heat |
| Whole, indirect lid | **52–54 °C** | Allt om Mat / Lindstrand (SE) | probe in the thickest part of the back |
| Whole, basket | **55 °C glassy / 60 °C done** | eat.de (DE) | a spectrum, not a point |
| Whole, salt-grill | *no temperature published* | MAFF / 鈴木 (JP) | charcoal, by eye |

**What this supports.** Four of five records publish a number, and they are not one band but two: **fillets pull around 45 °C and whole fish around 50–54 °C**, with the German record treating 60 °C as the point where char is unambiguously done. The cut's `medium` recommendation (54 °C target, 52–57 °C range) sits at the top of the whole-fish cluster and above every fillet record, which is consistent: a 1 kg stuffed fish carries more heat into its rest than a 200 g fillet does.

**What it does not support.** There is no record here of a grilled char pulled at 60–63 °C, so the `well_done` band is not evidenced for this method at all. Nor is there a North American or Southern European grilled-char record — the numbered evidence in this leaf is Swedish (×3) and German (×1), with the Japanese record silent.

## What makes this method special

Grilling is where char's size becomes an asset: the fish is small enough to cook over live fire without the skin burning before the centre sets, which is why the Nordic records can afford indirect heat and a lid. It is also the least forgiving method in this cut. The gap between 45 °C and 60 °C is fifteen degrees and roughly three or four minutes on a whole fish, so a char on a grill has less margin than a salmon fillet or a steak, and the two Swedish whole-fish records both use the probe as a decision point mid-cook rather than at the end.

## Summary

Five records, four of them publishing a temperature: **45 °C for a direct-grilled fillet, 50–54 °C for a stuffed whole fish over indirect heat, and 55–60 °C named as the glassy-to-done transition** by the one German source that describes a spectrum. The Japanese entry documents a charcoal salt-grill tradition in the same genus with no temperature at all — evidence for Group A coverage, not a datum. Pull placement is consistent wherever it is stated: thickest part of the back, beside the backbone.
