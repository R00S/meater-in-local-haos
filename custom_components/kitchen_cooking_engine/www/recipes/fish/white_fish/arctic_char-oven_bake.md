# Arctic Char × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven baking is the gentlest thing that can happen to an arctic char fillet, and the five records here
agree on that more than they agree on any number: **four of the five bake at or below 175 °C**, and two
of them at 75 °C and 100 °C. That is not a stylistic coincidence. Char flesh is fine-grained and low in
connective tissue, so there is nothing to break down and no benefit from a hard oven; what an oven can do
that a pan cannot is hold a wide, even temperature while the fillet sits still, which lets a 150 g fillet
reach serving temperature with almost no gradient from edge to centre.

The method therefore turns on one variable the pan and grill leaves do not have: **oven air temperature,
not just the fish's target**. At 75 °C a fillet needs about 40 minutes and cannot overshoot quickly; at
175 °C it needs 12–15 minutes and will. Both practices are represented here, and the recorded pull
temperatures land in a tight 42–52 °C whichever oven setting is used — which is the leaf's real finding:
**Nordic oven baking is a low-temperature method with a low-temperature target, and the oven setting is
the control, not the timer.**

## Styckesprofil

Ugnsbakning är det mildaste som kan hända en rödingfilé, och de fem källorna här är mer eniga om det än
om någon siffra: **fyra av fem gräddar vid 175 °C eller lägre**, två av dem vid 75 °C och 100 °C. Det är
ingen slump. Rödingens kött är finkornigt och fattigt på bindväv, så det finns inget att bryta ner och
ingen vinst med het ugn; det ugnen kan som pannan inte kan är att hålla en jämn temperatur medan filén
ligger stilla. Metoden styrs därför av **ugnstemperaturen, inte bara av fiskens måltemperatur**: vid 75 °C
tar filén cirka 40 minuter och kan inte skena, vid 175 °C tar den 12–15 minuter och kan det.

## Source recipes

### 1. Ugnsbakad röding med "sauce au trois caviars" — Arla (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.arla.se/recept/ugnsbakad-roding-med-sauce-au-trois-caviars-och-glacerade-gronsaker/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Arla (Swedish dairy company test kitchen), 3.9/5 from 41 votes. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 30 min salting · **Cook**: ~15 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 arctic char fillets, 1 tsp salt, ½ finely grated lemon zest
- **Sauce**: 2 dl white wine, 1 tbsp white wine vinegar, 3 tbsp finely chopped shallot, 1 chopped garlic clove, 2 tbsp soured cream, 150 g butter in cubes, 2 tsp lemon juice, 1 pinch salt, 60 g mixed roe (trout, vendace, lumpfish), 3 tbsp chives
- **Vegetables**: 1 yellow pepper, 1 yellow courgette, 1 small leek, 1 bunch radishes, 2 tbsp chives, 200 g boiled new potatoes, 2 tbsp butter, 1 tsp lemon juice, 1 pinch salt

**Method**
1. Set the oven to **100 °C, or 90 °C fan**. *(verbatim)*
2. Trim and salt the char fillets and leave them in the fridge about 30 minutes. *(verbatim)*
3. Put the fish on a tray lined with baking paper and cook in the middle of the oven for about **15 minutes, to an internal temperature of 45–50 °C**. *(verbatim)*
4. Pull the skin off the fish and grate lemon zest over it with picked dill. *(verbatim)*
5. Make the sauce by reducing wine, vinegar, garlic and shallot to a quarter, adding the cream, then whisking in butter with a stick blender until thick and glossy; finish with lemon juice and salt, and fold in roe and chives at serving. *(paraphrase)*
6. Separately roast the pepper at 225 °C for about 20 minutes, slice the courgette and leek, shave the radishes, and warm the vegetables and potatoes in butter with lemon and chives. *(paraphrase)*

**Recorded temperature**: **45–50 °C** at a **100 °C** oven. The source's own reason is stated in its introduction: the low oven temperature is what makes the fish "läckert mjäll" (delicately flaky). Note the two-stage oven use — 100 °C for the fish, 225 °C for the pepper — recorded here so the air temperature is not misread.

### 2. Ugnsbakad röding à la Catarina König — Johan Lidby Vinhandel (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.johanlidbyvinhandel.se/privat/recept/ugnsbakad-roding-catarina-konig/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Recipe by Catarina König (*Sveriges mästerkock*, decade champion 2020), commissioned by the wine merchant Johan Lidby. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 10–12 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 2–3 arctic char fillets, 1 tbsp olive oil, salt
- ½–1 cucumber, ½ lemon, 1 tsp chopped dill
- 1 shallot, 6–7 whole white peppercorns, 1 tbsp white wine vinegar, 1 dl white wine, 150 g butter, ½ dl whipping cream, 1 tsp finely chopped dill

**Method**
1. Trim the char fillets and remove the pin bones (*byxa*). *(verbatim)*
2. Oil the bottom of an oven dish, lay the fillets in it **skin side down**, and salt them. Start the oven at **150 °C fan**. *(verbatim)*
3. Dice the cucumber, discarding the watery middle, and mix with chopped dill and lemon juice; keep it cool. *(paraphrase)*
4. Reduce the shallot, white wine, vinegar and peppercorns by half and strain; whisk in butter, then cream, and season. *(paraphrase)*
5. Put the char in the oven **last of all** and bake about **10–12 minutes, or until the core reaches 42 °C**. *(verbatim)*
6. Pull the skin off, plate, and top with the warm sauce, the cucumber, roe and chervil; serve immediately. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **42 °C** at **150 °C fan** — the lowest target anywhere in this cut, and lower than the cut's `medium_rare` minimum of 46 °C. A named competition-winning chef publishing 42 °C for char is a strong signal that the low end of the register is deliberate in Swedish restaurant cooking, not a typo. Carryover after 10 minutes in a 150 °C oven is small, so the served fish is unlikely to exceed the mid-40s.

### 3. Bakad röding med pressad savojkål — Allt om Mat / Tom Sjöstedt (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://alltommat.expressen.se/recept/bakad-roding-med-pressad-savojkal/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Allt om Mat, recipe by chef Tom Sjöstedt, photography Ulrika Pousette, 4.3/5 from 9 votes. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 10 (as a cold starter) · **Prep**: 30 min + 8 h chilling · **Cook**: ~40 min · **Rest**: chilled overnight before serving

**Ingredients**
- 4 arctic char fillets (about 600 g), previously frozen at least 3 days
- 1 tbsp salt, 1 tsp caster sugar, dill
- **Pressed savoy**: 1 small savoy cabbage (600 g), 1 onion, 3 l water, ½ dl salt, 1 tbsp dill seed
- **Garnish**: watercress, 16 dill tops, 1 tbsp olive oil, grated lemon zest, ground black pepper

**Method**
1. Cut the cabbage into wedges, slice the onion, and boil with dill seed in salted water about 10 minutes; drain. *(verbatim)*
2. Pack the cabbage into a cling-film-lined mould, press out the liquid, weight it, and chill about 8 hours. *(paraphrase)*
3. Remove the skin from the char, mix salt and sugar, and coat the fish; leave at room temperature about 30 minutes. Set the oven to **75 °C**. *(verbatim)*
4. Pat the fish dry, spread dill over it, sandwich the fillets together, roll in aluminium foil, and cook in the middle of the oven **about 40 minutes, to 52 °C internal temperature**. *(verbatim)*
5. Let it cool, then serve cold with the pressed cabbage, watercress, dill, oil, lemon zest and pepper. *(paraphrase)*

**Recorded temperature**: **52 °C** at **75 °C** — the longest, lowest bake in the leaf, and the only one written for a *cold* dish, which is why it carries the highest target of the three Swedish fillet records: at 42–48 °C a chilled fish eats raw, so the chef takes it to 52 °C and stops. The page states the alternative explicitly: if the oven cannot hold 75 °C, bake at 100 °C and shorten the time, because "the important thing is the internal temperature of 52°".

### 4. Klassisk röding med fluffigt mos, lingon och brynt smör — Matfiskodlarna (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://matfiskodlarna.se/svensk-fiskuppfoedning/recept-paa-roeding-och-regnbaage/klassisk-roeding-med-fluffigt-mos-lingon-och-brynt-smoer/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Matfiskodlarna, the Swedish fish farmers' association; the page names the species (*Salvelinus alpinus*) and the Swedish supply chain. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 12–15 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 arctic char fillets, 150 g each, skin on
- 1 tbsp butter or neutral oil for the dish, salt, ground black pepper
- ½ lemon, zest and juice
- **Mash**: 1 kg floury potatoes, 50 g butter, 2–2½ dl warm milk, salt, pepper, a pinch of sugar
- **Lingonberries**: 2 dl lingonberries, about ½ dl caster sugar
- **Brown butter**: 100 g butter
- **Pickled kohlrabi**: 1 kohlrabi, ½ dl 12 % vinegar, 1 dl caster sugar, 1½ dl water

**Method**
1. Pickle the kohlrabi by boiling the vinegar, sugar and water and pouring the hot liquor over thinly sliced kohlrabi; rest at least 30 minutes. *(paraphrase)*
2. Stir the lingonberries with the sugar until it dissolves. *(verbatim)*
3. Boil and press the potatoes, then beat in butter and warm milk and season. *(paraphrase)*
4. **Ugnsbakad röding**: set the oven to **175 °C**. Lay the fillets skin side down in a buttered ovenproof dish; season with salt, pepper, a little grated zest and lemon juice. Bake **12–15 minutes, or until the internal temperature is 48 °C**. The fish should be just cooked through but still juicy. *(verbatim)*
5. Melt the butter and let it foam until golden and nutty, then pour it off the heat. *(paraphrase)*
6. Serve the fish with the mash, lingonberries, kohlrabi and brown butter. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **48 °C** at **175 °C** — the highest oven setting and the middle of the recorded range, which makes it the ordinary everyday case in this leaf. The source's own wording ("precis genomstekt men fortfarande saftig", *just* cooked through but still juicy) is the clearest statement in the corpus of what 48 °C is supposed to mean for char.

### 5. Baked Arctic Char with Orange Gremolata — Skipper Otto (Canada)
**Inspired by**: https://skipperotto.com/baked-arctic-char-with-orange-gremolata/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Skipper Otto's Community Supported Fishery (British Columbia), a named fishermen's cooperative publishing recipes for its own catch. **Culinary Group B**.
**Serves**: 3–4 · **Prep**: ~5 min · **Cook**: ~15 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 portioned arctic char fillet, about 450 g, thawed
- 1 tbsp olive oil, 1 tsp garlic powder, ½ tsp salt, generous black pepper
- **Gremolata**: 1 orange (zest and juice), 1 garlic clove, a handful of flat-leaf parsley, ¼ cup extra virgin olive oil, salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **400 °F (204 °C)**. *(verbatim)*
2. Lay the char skin side down on a lined baking sheet and pat it dry with paper towel. *(verbatim)*
3. Drizzle with oil, add garlic powder, salt and pepper, and rub it into the flesh until evenly distributed. *(verbatim)*
4. Bake for approximately **10–12 minutes or until the flesh easily flakes with a fork**; cook time can be tailored to the desired level of doneness. *(verbatim)*
5. Combine parsley, olive oil, orange zest and a generous squeeze of orange juice with garlic and seasoning, and spoon it over the finished fish. *(paraphrase)*

**Recorded temperature**: **none — visual cue only ("flakes easily with a fork")** at 204 °C, the hottest oven in the leaf and roughly twice the next-hottest. This is the one non-Nordic record and it is also the one that does not measure, which is worth stating plainly rather than filling in with a plausible number.

## Temperature summary

Baked arctic char fillet, as published:

| Oven air temp | Pull / target | Source | Form |
|---------------|---------------|--------|------|
| 75 °C | **52 °C** | Allt om Mat / Sjöstedt (SE) | foil-wrapped fillets, served cold |
| 100 °C | **45–50 °C** | Arla (SE) | tray-baked fillets |
| 150 °C fan | **42 °C** | Catarina König (SE) | fillets in oiled dish |
| 175 °C | **48 °C** | Matfiskodlarna (SE) | fillets, skin side down |
| 204 °C | *no temperature* | Skipper Otto (CA) | fillet, flake test |

**What this supports.** The recorded targets cluster at **42–52 °C**, i.e. below this cut's `medium` target of 54 °C everywhere except the cold-dish record. Four independent Swedish sources — a dairy test kitchen, a competition-winning chef, a newspaper food desk and the fish farmers' association — land inside a 10-degree window while using oven settings from 75 °C to 175 °C. That is the strongest consensus in the whole cut.

**What it does not support.** Nothing here evidences `well_done` for oven baking, and the single non-Nordic source publishes no number at all, so the method's evidence base is four Swedish records. The correlation between oven temperature and pull temperature is *not* simple either: the 75 °C oven takes the fish to 52 °C and the 175 °C oven to 48 °C, which is explained by purpose (cold dish vs hot dinner) rather than by physics.

## What makes this method special

Oven baking is the only method in this cut where the cook can be careless about timing and still hit the number, and the records use that freedom differently: Sjöstedt uses it to reach a cold-service 52 °C without a gradient, König to stop at 42 °C before a warm sauce goes on, Matfiskodlarna to hit 48 °C for a plain dinner. Because the fish is never in contact with a hot surface, there is no crust to trade against the centre — the target is the only decision, which is why every one of these recipes states it and none of them states a sear.

## Summary

Five records, four with a number: **42 °C, 45–50 °C, 48 °C and 52 °C**, from ovens at 150 °C fan, 100 °C, 175 °C and 75 °C respectively, plus one Canadian 204 °C bake that judges by flake. The agreement among four unrelated Swedish institutions on a 42–52 °C window for a baked char fillet is the most consistent temperature evidence anywhere in this cut; the absence of any non-Nordic number is the method's clearest gap.
