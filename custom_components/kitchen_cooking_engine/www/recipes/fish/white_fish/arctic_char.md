# Arctic Char — Cut Overview

## Cut profile

Arctic char (*Salvelinus alpinus*, Swedish: röding, German: Saibling / Alpensaibling, French: omble chevalier, Norwegian: røye, Finnish: nieriä) is a cold-water salmonid fish of the family Salmonidae, native to alpine lakes and Arctic/subarctic waters. It is closely related to both salmon and trout and shares their characteristic firm, flaky flesh with pink-to-orange colour depending on diet and habitat. Fat content is moderate (5–10 g per 100 g), placing it between lean trout and fatty salmon. Arctic char is considered a premium fish across Scandinavia, Canada, Iceland, and the Alpine regions of Austria and Switzerland (where farmed *Saibling* is widely sold). The flesh is delicate with a clean, slightly sweet flavour milder than salmon. It is suited to the same cooking approaches as trout and salmon: pan-searing skin-side down in butter, grilling, oven-baking, cold or hot smoking, and sous vide. Hot smoking in stovetop box smokers (*abu-röken* / *röklåda*) is a traditional Scandinavian preparation. Icelandic and Canadian preparations often feature it simply pan-fried or poached. Farmed Austrian Saibling is frequently cold-smoked. Probe placement: insert into the thickest part of the fillet, avoiding pin bones.

### What the twenty-two fetched source records say about temperature

The cut now has a leaf for every method it declares, and the 22 records behind them divide in a way
that is worth stating before any band: **19 name an internal temperature for the fish, 3 do not** — two of
the three give only an oven or smoke setting, one gives no temperature at all — and the figures cluster in
two distinct registers rather than one continuous spread.

- **Fillet methods pull low.** Oven baking: 42 °C (König), 45–50 °C (Arla), 48 °C (Matfiskodlarna), 52 °C
  (Sjöstedt). Pan searing: 54–61 °C across four Alpine and Nordic records. Grilled fillet: 45 °C
  (Tasteline). Four unrelated Swedish institutions land inside a ten-degree window for a baked fillet,
  which is the most consistent evidence in the cut.
- **Whole-fish methods run hotter**, because the cavity and backbone have to heat too: 50–54 °C for a
  stuffed whole char on a grill, 55 °C "glassy" / 60 °C "done" in the German record, 61 °C for a whole
  floured char in the Austrian pan record.
- **The three records with no internal target are the ones that cook by hand**: a Japanese charcoal
  salt-grill (MAFF, Miyagi) with no temperature of any kind, a Canadian 204 °C oven bake judged by flake,
  and the Swedish box-smoker article, which gives an ambient 60–80 °C and a ten-minute cue.
- **One genuine conflict is recorded rather than smoothed**: the Canadian smoke recipe finishes char at
  an internal 54–60 °C for a vacuum-packed keeping product, while the Alaska Cooperative Extension
  requires ≥71 °C internal for hot-smoked fish as a food-safety matter. Both are in the smoker leaf; the
  cut's band does not adopt either, because a smoke target and a serving target are different objects.

The `medium` recommendation (54 °C target, 52–57 °C range) remains the best-supported single serving
choice: it sits at the top of the whole-fish cluster and above every fillet record, and no source in the
corpus publishes a char served at 60–63 °C except as a smoke product.

## Styckesprofil

Röding (*Salvelinus alpinus*; tyska: Saibling; franska: omble chevalier) är en kallvattensalmonid nära besläktad med lax och öring, med fast, fjälligt rosa-orange kött och måttlig fetthalt (5–10 g/100 g). Röding är en premiumfisk i Skandinavien, Island, Kanada och Alperna (Österrike, Schweiz) och anses ha en renare, något sötare smak än lax. Den lämpar sig väl för pannstekning (skinnsida ned i smör), grillning, ugnsbakning, varmrökning i röklåda (abu-röken) och sous vide. Sond placeras i tjockaste delen av filén, fri från pinbenen.

De 22 källorna bakom bladen fördelar sig så här: **19 anger en innertemperatur för fisken, 3 gör det
inte** — två av dem anger bara ugns- eller rök temperatur, en anger ingen temperatur alls — och siffrorna
klustrar i två register snarare än i en enda glidande skala. Filémetoderna drar lågt (ugn 42–52 °C,
panna 54–61 °C, grillad filé 45 °C) medan helfiskmetoderna går högre (50–54 °C på grill, 55/60 °C i den
tyska källan, 61 °C i den österrikiska pannan). De tre källorna utan innertemperatur är de som lagas på känn: japansk
kolgrillad saltfisk, kanadensisk ugnsbakning bedömd på flakighet, och den svenska röklådsartikeln som anger
60–80 °C i röken och tio minuter men ingen innertemperatur.
