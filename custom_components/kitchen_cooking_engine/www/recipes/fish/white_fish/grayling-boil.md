# Grayling × Boil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

European grayling (*Thymallus thymallus*; Swedish *harr*) is a lean freshwater salmonid with delicate flesh and small whole-fish portions. Pelles Fiske's recipe cuts cleaned, scaled fish into thick pieces and boils them in seasoned water, a direct preparation suited to lakeside cooking when equipment and ingredients are limited. The source gives a qualitative endpoint—flesh releasing from the bones—but no water temperature, cooking duration, thermometer target, or resting guidance. As small pieces are removed from boiling liquid, carryover is likely limited, but the source does not measure it; no numerical value is assigned.

## Source recipes

### 1. Pelles Fiske — Kokt harr i bit
**Inspired by**: http://www.pelles-fiske.se/recept/kokt.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish fishing and outdoor-fishing recipe site; the page gives no individual author for this recipe.
**Serves**: 2–3 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: until flesh releases from bones; duration not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 grayling, about 700 g each
- Water, enough to cook the fish
- Salt, amount not stated
- A little white pepper, amount not stated
- Rice and sauce of choice, for serving

**Method**
1. Clean and scale the grayling, then cut each fish into pieces about the thickness of a thumb.
2. Bring a suitable amount of water to a boil; season with salt and a little white pepper.
3. Add the fish pieces and boil until the flesh releases from the bones. The source does not specify a time or internal temperature; do not infer a pull temperature.
4. Serve with rice and a sauce of choice. Final serving temperature is not stated by the source.

### 2. Mats Sjöstrand / Flugfiskemagasinet Rackelhanen — harr kokt i court-bouillon
**Inspired by**: http://rackelhane.se/swe/1403.htm (fetched 2026-10-23). “Recept på harr,” credited © Mats Sjöstrand 2010; a Swedish angling-magazine recipe page.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: broth simmer at least 20 min · **Cook**: gently simmer until done; duration not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Grayling, amount not stated
- Water, enough to cover the fish
- 1 tbsp vinegar or wine
- 1 white onion, amount/size not stated
- 1 piece of carrot, amount not stated
- 1 bunch of parsley, size not stated
- A pinch of thyme
- A few teaspoons of salt
- A few peppercorns, quantity not stated

**Method**
1. Combine water, vinegar or wine, onion, carrot, parsley, thyme and salt. Add the peppercorns near the end of cooking the broth.
2. Boil the broth for at least 20 minutes, then strain it.
3. Bring the strained broth to a boil and add the grayling.
4. Let the liquid nearly return to a boil, skim the surface, then simmer gently until the fish is cooked. The source states no duration, internal temperature, or explicit pull point.
5. If cooking a larger fish to serve cold, cool it in the cooking liquid as the source describes. No final serving temperature is stated.

## Temperature consensus

The two directly fetched Swedish sources describe endpoints rather than thermometer targets: Pelles Fiske boils pieces until flesh releases from the bones; Sjöstrand/Rackelhanen brings the court-bouillon nearly back to a boil and then gently simmers the fish. Neither states a fish internal temperature or a measured final serving temperature, so no numerical range, doneness band, or recommended doneness can be derived. The sources are both from the same national/regional tradition, and therefore do not meet the required four-to-six-source, four-distinct-tradition collection threshold.

## What makes this method special

Boiling transfers heat through seasoned water, cooking small pieces evenly without browning their delicate surfaces. This can be practical for freshly caught harr at a riverbank, but it sacrifices the browned skin and concentrated flavours produced by pan-frying or grilling. The recipe endpoints are qualitative; temperature and carryover should not be inferred.
