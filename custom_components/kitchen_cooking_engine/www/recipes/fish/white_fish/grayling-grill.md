# Grayling × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

European grayling (*Thymallus thymallus*, Swedish *harr*, German *Äsche*) is a lean, fine-textured river salmonid whose delicate flesh suits fast, dry radiant heat. Grilling is documented both as whole-fish ember cookery in the Nordic tradition and as filleted fish finished skin-side on a hot plancha or barbecue in professional Anglophone kitchens. The small typical size (roughly 300–400 g whole) means the fish cooks through in minutes, so the retrieved recipes use short times and visual endpoints rather than probe targets. None of the source pages reviewed for this leaf states an internal pull temperature, so none is inferred here; carryover for a small whole fish or thin fillet is expected to be modest but the sources do not quantify it.

## Source recipes

### 1. Chef Phil Yeomans / Andrej Cooks Fish — European Grayling Part 2 (plancha)
**Inspired by**: https://andrejcooksfish.co.uk/videos/european-grayling-2/ (fetched 2026-10-23). UK catch-and-cook episode filmed at Season Cookery School, Lainston House, Hampshire; chef and pit master Phil Yeomans.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: filleting and tying, not timed · **Cook**: 7–8 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole grayling
- Romesco dressing: 1 tomato (diced), 1 shallot (diced), 3 tbsp roasted pine nuts, a splash of sherry vinegar, a drizzle of olive oil, 1 garlic clove (minced), 1 tbsp smoked paprika
- Guacamole: 1 avocado, 1 tbsp crème fraîche, a splash of lemon juice

**Method**
1. Fillet the fish and prepare it as demonstrated in the video; the fillets are tied with kitchen twine.
2. Place the fish skin-side up on the hot plancha or barbecue and grill for 7–8 minutes. Remove the kitchen twine and scorch the skin.
3. Romesco sauce: combine the tomato, shallot, pine nuts, sherry vinegar, olive oil, garlic and smoked paprika in a bowl and season to taste.
4. Guacamole: smash the avocado and combine with the crème fraîche and lemon juice.
5. Plate the fish and garnishes on a platter and serve. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 2. Pelles Fiske — Harr på macka
**Inspired by**: http://www.pelles-fiske.se/recept/harrmacka.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish fishing and outdoor-cooking recipe site; the page gives no individual author.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: until grilled through; duration not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Fresh grayling, 3–4 per fish (300–400 g each)
- Salt, moderate amount
- Crispbread (*knäckebröd*), for serving
- Butter, optional
- Caviar, optional (the source's serving tip)

**Method**
1. Gut and scale the very fresh grayling. Salt them moderately inside and out.
2. Grill the grayling over glowing embers (*glöd*) until they are cooked through. The source gives no time, internal temperature, or explicit pull point; do not infer a pull temperature.
3. Flake pieces of the grilled grayling onto a slice of crispbread, with or without butter (the source suggests caviar instead of butter as a variation).
4. Serve immediately. The source states no final serving temperature.

## What makes this method special

Grilling exposes grayling to intense radiant heat, crisping the skin and adding smoke-adjacent flavour while the interior stays moist for only a narrow time window — the delicate, lean flesh dries quickly once overcooked. The plancha variant deliberately finishes skin-side up after the skin is set and scorched, protecting the flesh from direct contact; the Swedish ember variant grills the whole fish and flakes it immediately onto crispbread. Neither source quantifies internal temperature, so stated times remain source-specific and must not be converted into a common probe target.

## Summary

Grilling grayling gives crisp, scorched skin and a short, high-heat cook that flatters the delicate flesh if it is watched minute by minute. One British professional-kitchen source and one Swedish ember tradition confirm the method's culinary relevance, but neither states a probe target, so this leaf supports method coverage rather than a temperature recommendation.
