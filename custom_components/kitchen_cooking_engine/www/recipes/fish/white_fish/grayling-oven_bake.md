# Grayling × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

European grayling (*Thymallus thymallus*, Swedish *harr*, German *Äsche*) is a lean, delicate river salmonid that dries out quickly under sustained heat, so oven preparations for it rely on protective coverings — wrapped ham, an aromatic salt crust, or a covered baking dish — rather than exposed roasting. Oven baking suits grayling because it is hands-off for serving several people at once and because a crust or parcel buffers the lean flesh from the dry oven air. The fetched recipes cover a Swedish baked-fillet tradition (frozen harr fillet layered with air-dried ham) and a British salt-crust whole-fish tradition. The sources state oven settings and times, not internal pull temperatures; no internal target is inferred where the source is silent.

## Source recipes

### 1. Pelles Fiske — Pelles skinkharr
**Inspired by**: http://www.pelles-fiske.se/recept/skinkharr.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish fishing and outdoor-cooking recipe site; the page gives no individual author. The recipe is explicitly aimed at frozen harr fillet.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 15 min at 225 °C · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- About 800 g harr fillet (*harrfilé*), preferably thawed frozen fish
- 8 slices air-dried ham (*lufttorkad skinka*)
- Just under 1 kg Brussels sprouts
- About ½ litre cream
- Västerbotten cheese (*Västerbottenost*), generous amount for the sauce, plus grated for garnish
- Salt and white pepper, to taste
- Chopped parsley, for garnish

**Method**
1. Lightly season the fillets with salt and white pepper. Butter a baking dish and lay four slices of ham side by side. Place the fish fillets on top of the ham, then cover with the remaining four ham slices.
2. Bake in a 225 °C oven for about 15 minutes, until the ham is crisp and the fish is done. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.
3. Sauce: bring the cream to a boil and add plenty of Västerbotten cheese until the sauce has a clear cheese flavour; season with salt and pepper.
4. Vegetables: boil the Brussels sprouts in lightly salted water, optionally halved, then fold them into the sauce.
5. Serve a generous piece of the baked fish with the sauce, garnished with grated Västerbotten cheese and parsley. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 2. Pernilla Persson / Pelles Fiske — Pernillas vitlöksharr
**Inspired by**: http://www.pelles-fiske.se/recept/garlic.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish recipe site contribution credited to Pernilla Persson.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: until cooked through; duration and oven setting not stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 scaled grayling, about 400 g each
- 16 garlic cloves
- Dill and parsley, quantities not stated
- Salt and white pepper, to taste
- Rice and dill sauce, for serving

**Method**
1. Remove the heads and fins from the grayling; season inside and out with salt and white pepper.
2. Put dill, parsley and at least four garlic cloves inside each fish.
3. Bake in the oven (*stek i ugnen*) until the fish is cooked through. The source gives no oven temperature, time, internal temperature, or explicit pull point; do not infer any of them.
4. Serve with rice and dill sauce. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 3. The Big Fat Mushroom Hunter — Salt Baked Grayling
**Inspired by**: https://thebigfatmushroomhunter.wordpress.com/2013/04/20/salt-baked-grayling/ (fetched 2026-10-23). English angler/forager/cook blog; whole grayling in a fennel salt crust.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: salt-crust mixing and stuffing · **Cook**: 35–40 min at 220 °C · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 grayling, about 2 lb (about 900 g)
- 1 kg fine table salt
- 1 kg coarse table salt
- Fennel seeds, crushed, quantity not stated
- 2 clementines (or similar citrus)
- Tenderstem broccoli, for serving
- A sprinkling of water

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 220 °C (425 °F / Gas 7).
2. Gut the grayling and stuff it with clementines and crushed fennel seeds.
3. Make the fennel salt crust: mix fine and coarse salt 50:50 with crushed fennel seeds and a sprinkling of water until the salt is claggy.
4. Lay the fish on a layer of the salt mixture and encase it with the rest, keeping as little salt as possible in the cavity. Pack tightly and bake for 35–40 minutes.
5. Remove from the oven and crack open the salt crust. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; the crust itself is the endpoint. Serve with tenderstem broccoli and a drizzle of clementine juice. The source states no final serving temperature.

### 4. Russian (Дикое Качество, Yekaterinburg) — Хариус в фолие
**Inspired by**: https://dikoekachestvo.ru/retsepty/gotovim_rybu/kharius-v-folge/ (fetched 2026-11-01). A fish supplier's recipe page: whole grayling in a double layer of foil with lemon, bay and olive oil, baked at 170 °C and then flashed under the grill for a crust.
**Serves**: not stated (2 fish) · **Prep**: 30 min marinade · **Cook**: about 20 min at 170 °C + 5–6 min grill · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 grayling, gutted, gills removed
- 2 tsp fresh lemon juice
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Salt and ground pepper
- To finish the sauce: a little white wine and a knob of butter

**Method**
1. Gut, remove the gills and rinse the fish; dry thoroughly, checking that no blood remains inside.
2. Rub with pepper and coarse salt, sprinkle with lemon juice and olive oil, lay a bay leaf on each fish and leave 30 minutes under film.
3. Wrap each fish in a double layer of foil, sealing tightly, and bake at **170 °C for about 20 minutes**.
4. Open the foil, switch on the grill if available and **grill 5–6 minutes** until a crust forms; without a grill, finish in the ordinary oven mode.
5. Reduce the juices from the foil with white wine and butter into a sauce; serve with boiled potatoes and fresh vegetables.

Notes: the page gives an oven temperature and a separate grill stage but no internal temperature at either stage. Its foil-plus-grill structure is the same two-stage shape as the Swedish ham-wrapped recipe.

## What makes this method special

Oven baking surrounds grayling with moderate dry heat, which suits a lean fish only when something buffers the surface: the ham-wrapped fillet bastes in rendered fat, the garlic-stuffed whole fish steams inside its own skin, and the salt crust seals the fish against a wall of superheated salt that effectively double-steams it. The crust and parcel variants therefore behave more like a moist-heat cook than an exposed roast, which is why a 220–225 °C oven can run for 35–40 minutes without drying the flesh. None of the sources quantifies internal temperature or carryover, so times remain source-specific.

## Summary

Oven baking suits grayling when the fish is shielded — ham, garlic-and-herb stuffing, or a fennel salt crust keep the lean flesh moist while the oven does the work for a crowd.
