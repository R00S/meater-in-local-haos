# Grayling × Pan Fry — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

European grayling (*Thymallus thymallus*, Swedish *harr*, German *Äsche*) is a lean, delicate freshwater salmonid; recipes usually cook it whole or as a skin-on fillet. Pan-frying is documented in Alpine and northern Swedish preparations, often with butter, flour, bacon or a light sauce. Because the fish is small and its flesh is delicate, the retrieved recipes use short cooking times and visual doneness cues rather than a probe. The source pages reviewed for this leaf do not state an internal pull temperature, so none is inferred here. Carryover is expected to be small for a small whole fish, but the cited recipes do not quantify it.

## Styckesprofil

Europeisk harr (*Thymallus thymallus*, tyska *Äsche*) är en mager och ömtålig laxfisk från sötvatten. Recepten tillagar den hel eller som filé med skinn. Pannstekning är dokumenterad i alpina och norrländska svenska tillagningar, ofta med smör, mjöl, bacon eller en lätt sås. Eftersom fisken är liten och köttet ömtåligt använder de hämtade recepten korta tider och visuella tecken på färdighet, inte termometer. De granskade källorna anger ingen innertemperatur; därför anges ingen påhittad dragtemperatur. Källorna kvantifierar inte eftervärmen.

## Source recipes

### 1. Gebratene Äsche mit Zitronen-Kräuterbutter — Angeln.at
**Inspired by**: https://www.angeln.at/fischrezepte/rezept-aesche.html (fetched 2026-10-23). Recipe page; no named author is shown.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 8–10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 gutted grayling, about 300 g each
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- 1 lemon, sliced and juiced
- 3 tbsp butter
- Fresh parsley and chives, quantity not stated

**Method**
1. Clean the fish inside and out, pat dry, then season with salt and pepper.
2. Fill the cavities with lemon slices and chopped herbs.
3. Heat butter in a frying pan over medium heat.
4. Fry each fish for 4–5 minutes on each side.
5. Remove from the pan and finish with lemon juice. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 2. Gebratene Äsche — Bobby Bräuer / 7hauben
**Inspired by**: https://www.7hauben.com/rezept/gebratene-aesche/ (fetched 2026-10-23). Austrian chef Bobby Bräuer, recipe in a video-cooking course.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 7–8 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 800 g cleaned, scaled grayling
- Neutral frying oil, quantity not stated
- Salt, quantity not stated
- Flour for dusting, quantity not stated
- Butter for basting, quantity not stated
- Lemon and parsley, to finish; quantities not stated

**Method**
1. Score both sides of the cleaned grayling.
2. Heat oil in a non-stick pan over medium heat.
3. Season the fish and dust both sides with flour.
4. Fry for about 4 minutes, turn, add butter, and baste continuously for another 3–4 minutes.
5. Finish with lemon juice and parsley. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 3. Smörstekt harr på Jämtlandsvis — Birgitta Höglund
**Inspired by**: https://birgittahoglundsmat.wordpress.com/2009/09/13/smorstekt-harr-pa-jamtlandsvis/ (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish home-cooking recipe explicitly presented as a Jämtland dish.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: a few minutes plus sauce simmer · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 grayling; weight not stated
- Butter, quantity not stated
- Sea salt and freshly ground white pepper, to taste
- ½ dl white wine
- 3 dl cream
- 2 tbsp chopped chives
- 2 tbsp goat whey cheese (*getmese*)
- Green beans, cauliflower and cocktail tomatoes; quantities not stated

**Method**
1. Bring salted water to a boil and cook the green beans and cauliflower.
2. Brown butter in a pan, add the fish and fry for about 1 minute per side; season lightly with salt and pepper.
3. Add white wine, cream and goat whey cheese. Gently simmer the fish in the sauce for a few minutes.
4. Remove the fish; stir chives into the sauce and reduce until thickened. Return the fish briefly to warm through.
5. The source provides no internal temperature or explicit pull point.

### 4. Harr med bacon — Mats Sjöstrand / Rackelhane
**Inspired by**: http://rackelhane.se/swe/1403.htm (fetched 2026-10-23). Swedish fishing magazine page credited © Mats Sjöstrand 2010.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 8 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 grayling, about 250 g each, cleaned, heads and tails retained, gills removed
- 1 tsp salt
- Black pepper, quantity not stated
- 8 slices bacon
- 4 tbsp flour
- 1 lemon, sliced

**Method**
1. Pat the cleaned fish dry and season the cavities with salt and pepper.
2. Slowly fry the bacon until browned and crisp. Remove it, reserving the rendered fat.
3. Dredge the grayling in flour and shake off the excess.
4. Fry the fish in the bacon fat for 4 minutes per side.
5. Serve immediately with lemon and crumbled bacon. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point.

## What makes this method special

Pan-frying puts the small fish in direct contact with hot fat, browning the skin quickly while the interior cooks over a short interval. The Jämtland recipe adds a brief simmer in cream and goat whey cheese, whereas the Austrian and Swedish bacon preparations keep the pan-fried crust more exposed. The recipes therefore document several regional expressions of pan-cooked grayling, but they do not quantify internal temperatures. Their stated times are source-specific and should not be converted into a common probe target.

## Summary

Pan-frying gives grayling a quickly browned exterior while keeping the delicate fish preparation simple. The retrieved recipes document Austrian and Swedish approaches, but none states an internal pull temperature, so they support the method’s culinary relevance rather than a temperature setting for the app.
