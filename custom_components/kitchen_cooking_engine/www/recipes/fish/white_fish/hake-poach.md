## Cut profile

Poaching is the gentlest heat there is for hake. The lean, soft fillet is submerged in liquid
held below a rolling boil, so the surface never browns and the flesh sets evenly from the
outside in; because hake flakes along wide myosepta, the poaching liquid also carries away the
loose surface flakes that a hot pan would tear. The fetched sources split into two families:
**court-bouillon poaching** in a flavoured stock (French/Irish/UK) and **sauce poaching**, where
the fish is laid on a finished sauce, covered, and left to cook in its steam and liquid
(Iberian/Azorean/South African). The second family overlaps with `simmer`, which is recorded
separately at [hake-simmer.md](./hake-simmer.md); the split used here is whether the fish sits
in a deep liquid bath (poach) or on a shallow sauce base (simmer).

None of the six fetched sources states an internal temperature. Every one of them gives time
and a visual cue only, so this leaf records no pull temperature and draws no temperature
consensus.

## Source recipes

### 1. Poached Hake with Honey and Soy Vinaigrette and Quick-Pickled Mushrooms — Michael Caines
**Inspired by**: Great British Chefs; https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/poached-hake-recipe-soy-vinaigrette (fetched 2026-10-24). UK publication, Chinese five-spice/soy influence.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 2 minutes off the heat plus component work · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 720 g hake fillet, cut into 4 portions
- 500 ml vegetable stock; 1 fresh bay leaf; 1 large sprig lemon thyme; salt; pepper
- 200 g baby chestnut mushrooms, quartered; 1 tbsp chopped shallots; 1 large sprig thyme; ½ lemon; 100 ml olive oil
- Vinaigrette: 25 g clear honey; pinch Chinese five-spice; 15 g Dijon mustard; 12 g balsamic vinegar; 25 ml white wine vinegar; 5 g soy sauce; 100 g light olive oil
- 4 radishes, finely sliced; 2 spring onions, finely sliced

**Method**
1. Sweat shallots in olive oil with salt until soft; add thyme, mushrooms, seasoning and lemon juice; cook 3 minutes and cool.
2. Whisk honey, five-spice and mustard, then both vinegars, soy sauce and finally the oil to emulsify.
3. Bring stock, bay leaf and lemon thyme to the boil and season. Add the fish, return to the boil, remove from the heat and leave the fish 2 minutes. Drain on parchment.
4. Remove the skin; plate over vinaigrette with mushrooms, spring onion and radish.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature; the source's only instruction is "leave the fish for 2 minutes" off the heat after the stock returns to the boil.

### 2. Poached Hake — Reesha Chibba
**Inspired by**: On The Board; https://www.ontheboard.co.za/blog/poached-hake (fetched 2026-10-24). South African site; coconut-milk poach.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 10 minutes · **Rest**: 2 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 4 hake medallions
- Oil for frying
- ½ tsp red chilli flakes; 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped; 1 tsp grated fresh ginger; sprigs of fresh thyme
- 1 cup coconut milk; 1 tsp quinoa flour; 16 cherry tomatoes; salt and black pepper

**Method**
1. Heat oil on medium-high. Fry tomatoes, chilli, garlic, ginger and thyme until the tomatoes sear, 1–2 minutes.
2. Add coconut milk and salt.
3. Add the frozen hake, lower the heat to a simmer, cover and poach 10 minutes.
4. Uncover, turn off the heat and season with black pepper. Stir flour into the sauce at the side of the pot to thicken.
5. Rest 2 minutes before serving.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature stated.

### 3. Poached Hake with a Simple Tomato Sauce — Atlantis of Kilmore Quay
**Inspired by**: Atlantis of Kilmore Quay; https://www.atlantisofkilmorequay.ie/recipes/poached-hake-with-a-simple-tomato-sauce/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Irish seafood recipe.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 17 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 900 g skinless, boneless hake fillets
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley leaves; salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp rapeseed or olive oil; 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- 400 g tin chopped tomatoes; pinch of sugar; grated zest of ½ lemon
- ½ tsp turmeric; ¼–½ tsp chilli flakes; 100 ml water
- Mashed potatoes and green salad, to serve

**Method**
1. Heat a large frying pan over high heat and add the sauce ingredients; stir and bring to a boil.
2. Reduce the heat and simmer a couple of minutes.
3. Season the hake and lay the fillets on the gently simmering sauce.
4. Cover and cook 15 minutes, until the fish is cooked through. Sprinkle with parsley and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature; the source's cue is "until the fish is cooked through" after a covered 15 minutes.

### 4. Fresh Hake Poached in Onion, Garlic and Tomato Sauce — Maria Lawton
**Inspired by**: Azorean Greenbean; https://azoreangreenbean.com/fresh-hake-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Portuguese/Azorean family cooking; the page lists "cod or hake".

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 40 minutes total · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 lb fresh cod or hake, cut into portions
- 2 lb ripe tomatoes, peeled, seeded and chopped (or 2 large cans)
- 2 large sweet onions, sliced; 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 6 tbsp olive oil; 1–2 tbsp chopped hot pepper sauce
- 2 cups white wine; salt and pepper; 1 tbsp paprika; 1 small bunch parsley

**Method**
1. Heat the oil in a large deep skillet over medium heat and cook the onions until golden, about 15 minutes.
2. Add the garlic, then the tomatoes, wine, seasoning and pepper sauce. Lower the heat and simmer about 30 minutes until the sauce thickens.
3. Lay the fish on the sauce, season with salt, pepper and paprika, add parsley, cover and cook 5–10 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "It should flake easily and be just opaque through the center."

### 5. Coconut Poached Hake — Food Lover's Market
**Inspired by**: Food Lover's Market; https://foodloversmarket.co.za/recipe/coconut-poached-hake/ (fetched 2026-10-24). South African publication, tagged Asian/Mediterranean.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 15 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 hake portions
- 1 onion, finely diced; 2 garlic cloves, minced; 1 tbsp grated ginger; 1 chopped chilli
- 1 can coconut milk; 1 stalk lemongrass, halved; 1 tbsp fish sauce; 2 tsp sesame oil; 1 tbsp lime juice; 1 tbsp oil
- Salad: 100 g baby spinach; ½ yellow pepper; 80 g cherry tomatoes; 2 tbsp toasted coconut flakes; 1 tbsp olive oil; 1 tbsp lime juice

**Method**
1. Pat the hake dry.
2. Over medium heat, sauté the onion in the oil until soft; add garlic, ginger and chilli and sauté until fragrant.
3. Add coconut milk, lemongrass, fish sauce, sesame oil and lime juice; simmer on low 5 minutes.
4. Keep the heat low, add the hake and cover. Cook 8–10 minutes, until firm but flaky.
5. Dress the salad and serve with the hake and extra sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until the hake is firm but flaky."

### 6. Colin poché, sauce crémeuse aux herbes — WW France
**Inspired by**: WW France; https://www.weightwatchers.com/fr/recette/colin-poche-sauce-cremeuse-aux-herbes/5625ad5db7878c0b34e74a66 (fetched 2026-10-24).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 25 minutes · **Rest**: 30 minutes (court-bouillon)

**Ingredients**
- 500 g colin; 2,000 ml water; 1 onion; 1 clove; 1 bouquet garni; 1 small carrot; 100 ml white wine; coarse salt
- 1 tbsp potato starch; 120 g light semi-thick cream; 20 ml lemon juice
- 1 tsp chopped fresh parsley; 3 tsp chopped fresh tarragon; salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Bring water, salt, onion studded with the clove, bouquet garni, sliced carrot and wine to the boil; boil 10 minutes, cool 30 minutes and strain.
2. Put the fish in a fish kettle, cover with cold court-bouillon, bring to the boil, lower the heat and poach 10 minutes on low; keep warm in the broth.
3. Thicken the reserved broth with cream, lemon juice and starch; finish with parsley and tarragon.
4. Lift out the fish, remove skin and central bone, and coat with the herb sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature stated. **Species caveat**: French *colin* can denote either pollock (*lieu noir*) or hake (*colin lieu*) and the page does not say which, so this record is counted as French court-bouillon practice rather than species-specific hake evidence.

## Evidence limits

Five of the six fetched recipes name hake explicitly (Great British Chefs, On The Board, Atlantis of Kilmore Quay, Azorean Greenbean, Food Lover's Market); the sixth is the ambiguous French *colin*. **Not one of the six publishes an internal temperature**, so no pull temperature, final serving temperature or cross-source consensus is stated here. Two South African recipes use a near-identical coconut-milk poach, which is recorded as a same-tradition pair rather than two independent traditions.

## What makes this method special

Poaching is the only hake method in this collection where the fish never meets hot fat. That matters for a fillet this lean and this soft: there is no crust to set the surface, so the cook has to rely on the liquid temperature and a short clock instead — and every source compensates with a strongly flavoured broth or sauce (soy and honey, coconut and lemongrass, tomato and paprika, herb cream). Court-bouillon poaching also gives a second product, the cooking liquor, which the French source turns into the sauce itself. The cost is that poached hake has no browning at all, so it depends on the sauce for contrast.

## Summary

Poached hake divides into court-bouillon poaching in a deep flavoured bath (2–15 minutes depending on the source and the vessel) and sauce poaching on a shallow base (5–15 minutes covered). Six fetched sources, five of them hake-specific, meet the collection count; none supplies an internal temperature, so the leaf records method and timing evidence only.
