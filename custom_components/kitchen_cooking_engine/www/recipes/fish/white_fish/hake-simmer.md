## Cut profile

Simmering is the stew-adjacent end of hake cookery: the fillet or portion is laid on — or into —
a shallow sauce that is already at a bare simmer, covered, and finished in 5–10 minutes. It is
the dominant hake treatment along the Iberian Atlantic (Basque *merluza en salsa verde*, Ecuadorian
*sudado*), in southern African potjie stews and in West African fish stews, because the sauce both
flavours the lean flesh and protects it from direct heat. The engineering difference from
`poach` (see [hake-poach.md](./hake-poach.md)) is depth of liquid and the presence of a sauce
built in the same pan; the difference from `braise` is that nothing is seared dry and no long
covered cook is involved.

No fetched source in this leaf publishes an internal temperature. Times range from 5 to 10
minutes once the fish goes in, with visual cues ("flaky", "tender", "cooked") doing the work of a
thermometer.

## Source recipes

### 1. Hake in Green Sauce (Merluza en salsa verde) — Alba Luna Meyer
**Inspired by**: Recipes From Europe; https://www.recipesfromeurope.com/hake-in-green-sauce/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Basque/Spanish.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: about 20 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 large hake fillets
- 1 medium yellow onion; 2 garlic cloves; 1 large bunch fresh parsley
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 1 tbsp all-purpose flour; 1 cup fish stock; 1 cup white wine
- 1 cup baby clams (optional); ½ tsp salt

**Method**
1. Soak the clams in salted cold water in the refrigerator if using.
2. Dice the onion, mince the garlic and chop the parsley.
3. Heat the olive oil over medium heat and cook the onion and garlic 5–7 minutes, until golden and fragrant.
4. Add the flour and cook a couple of minutes, stirring, until slightly golden.
5. Pour in the stock and wine, mix, add the parsley and season with salt.
6. Add the clams and the hake, cover, and cook 10 minutes over low heat, until the fish is cooked and the clams have opened.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until the fish is cooked and the clams have opened."

### 2. Pescado en Salsa Verde (Fish in Green Sauce) — Laylita
**Inspired by**: Laylita's Recipes; https://www.laylita.com/recipes/pescado-en-salsa-verde-fish-in-green-sauce/ (fetched 2026-10-24). The page identifies the dish as traditional Spanish and names hake/*merluza* as the fish.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: about 15 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- ¼ cup olive oil; 4 crushed garlic cloves
- 1 tbsp all-purpose flour (or cornstarch); 4 tbsp (40 g) chopped fresh parsley
- ½ cup white wine; 1 cup fish stock
- 4 hake/*merluza* fillets, about 600 g; 1 cup clams (250 g); salt and pepper
- Lemon wedges, bread and optional boiled eggs, to serve

**Method**
1. Heat the olive oil over medium heat; sauté the garlic with a pinch of salt 30 seconds.
2. Add the flour and cook 1 minute; add half the parsley.
3. Add the wine and stock; bring to the boil and simmer 2–3 minutes. Season.
4. Add the fish, keeping it as covered by liquid as possible, and arrange the clams around it.
5. Cover and cook over low-to-medium heat about 10 minutes, until the fish is tender and the clams have opened.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature stated.

### 3. Ecuadorian Sudado de Pescado (Fish Stew with Plantains) — Laylita
**Inspired by**: Laylita's Recipes; https://www.laylita.com/recipes/ecuadorian-sudado-de-pescado-fish-stew-plantains/ (fetched 2026-10-24). The fish list explicitly includes hake.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 minutes · **Cook**: about 30 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 large or 2 small green plantains
- 2 large or 4 small fish fillets (mahi mahi, corvina or hake, red snapper, halibut or sea bass); ½ lime
- 2–3 tbsp achiote oil; ½ large red onion, finely chopped; 2 crushed garlic cloves
- 1 small green bell pepper, finely chopped; 2 medium tomatoes, grated or crushed
- ½–1 cup water; 1 large bunch cilantro; salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Peel and slice the plantains; boil in slightly salted water 15–20 minutes until soft.
2. Season the fish with salt, pepper and lime juice.
3. Heat the achiote oil in a deep sauté pan; cook the onion, garlic and pepper 3–5 minutes.
4. Add the tomatoes, cilantro stems, salt and pepper; cook about 5 minutes.
5. Add ½–1 cup water and the cooked plantains.
6. Lay the fish on top, spoon over some refrito, cover and cook over medium-low heat 7–10 minutes, depending on fillet thickness.
7. Adjust salt, add the remaining cilantro leaves and lime juice, and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "Once the fish is flaky, you are done!"

### 4. Hake and Potato Potjie (Stew) — Georgia East
**Inspired by**: Marine Stewardship Council, *The SA Ocean Cookbook*; https://www.msc.org/what-you-can-do/sustainable-fish-seafood-recipes/recipe/hake-and-potato-potjie-stew (fetched 2026-10-24). South African.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 30 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g MSC-certified hake fillets, cut into portions; olive oil
- Coarse sea salt and black pepper; 1 tsp paprika
- 1–2 large onions, finely chopped; 3–4 garlic cloves, finely chopped; 1 sprig rosemary
- 150 ml white wine; 8–10 baby potatoes; 10–15 cherry tomatoes
- 250 ml cream; 400 ml fish or vegetable stock; juice of 1 lemon; 3–4 sprigs parsley

**Method**
1. Drizzle the hake with oil and season with salt, pepper and paprika.
2. Heat oil in a heavy casserole and sear the hake skin-side down; remove the fish.
3. Fry the onions until soft, add garlic and rosemary and fry 1 minute.
4. Deglaze with wine, add potatoes and tomatoes and sauté 1–2 minutes.
5. Add cream and stock; simmer 15–20 minutes until the potatoes and tomatoes are soft.
6. Return the hake with the lemon juice and simmer a further 5 minutes.
7. Stir through the parsley and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature stated. The sear stage is also recorded in [hake-pan_sear.md](./hake-pan_sear.md).

### 5. Fresh Hake Curry — Dan Toombs
**Inspired by**: The Curry Guy / Great Curry Recipes; https://greatcurryrecipes.net/2012/04/17/hake-curry/ (fetched 2026-10-24). UK-published Indian-restaurant-style recipe; the page describes the method as a mix of authentic Indian and restaurant-style curry.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 30 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g skinless, boneless hake fillets
- 500 g chopped onions; 3 tbsp corn oil; 5 tbsp plain flour
- 1 tbsp garlic purée; 1 tbsp ginger purée; pinch ground cloves; pinch ground cinnamon; pinch hot red chilli powder
- 150 ml plain Greek yogurt; 100 ml double cream; 3 chopped red chillies; pinch turmeric; chopped coriander

**Method**
1. Cut the hake into 2-inch chunks and dust with flour.
2. Brown the fish in batches over medium-high heat, cooking only about 50%; remove.
3. Brown the onions slowly until translucent and light brown.
4. Add the ginger, garlic, clove, cinnamon, yogurt and cream.
5. Blend the sauce smooth (about 2 minutes) and return it to the pan.
6. Fold in the hake and cook about 2 minutes, until heated through and finished cooking.
7. Season and garnish with coriander and chopped chilli.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature stated. This is a fry-then-finish-in-sauce hybrid; the fry stage is not counted as pan-fry evidence.

### 6. Nigerian Fish Stew — My Active Kitchen
**Inspired by**: My Active Kitchen; https://www.myactivekitchen.com/nigerian-fish-stew-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). The page lists hake among the fish that work.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 30 minutes marinade · **Cook**: about 35 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg fish steaks (red headless, or hake, mackerel or croaker)
- 2 large red bell peppers; 1 can chopped tomatoes; 1 Scotch bonnet; 2 medium onions
- Salt; 1 cup vegetable oil; 1½ tbsp chicken bouillon powder
- Marinade: 1 tsp garlic granules; 1 tsp ginger powder; 1 tsp dried parsley; 1 tsp onion powder

**Method**
1. Cut the fish into steaks, rinse with water and lemon juice and drain.
2. Marinate with the spices and 1 tsp bouillon for at least 30 minutes.
3. Grill the fish 20 minutes total, 10 minutes per side.
4. Blend the peppers and boil about 10 minutes; fry onions in oil, add the pepper base and boil about 10 minutes; season and cook another 10 minutes.
5. Add the grilled fish, cover and cook on low heat 5 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature stated. The fish is grilled first and stewed only at the end, which is recorded as a hybrid rather than a raw-in-sauce simmer.

## Evidence limits

Six fetched recipes; four put raw or briefly seared hake into a sauce (Basque ×2, Ecuadorian, South African potjie), two are hybrids that grill or brown the fish first (UK-Indian curry, Nigerian stew). Two of the six are Basque green-sauce recipes from different sites — a same-tradition pair, not independent traditions. **No source states an internal temperature**; cooking times once the fish enters the pan run 2–10 minutes.

## What makes this method special

Simmering suits hake because the sauce does the work the fish cannot do for itself: a lean fillet gives up moisture fast in dry heat, but in a covered pan the sauce keeps the surface wet and the gentle heat sets the flesh before it can dry. It is also the only method here that produces a complete dish from one pan — sauce, fish and often the starch (potatoes, plantains) all in the same vessel. The risk is mechanical: hake separates along its seams, so a vigorous simmer breaks the portions up, which is why every source lowers the heat and covers the pan.
