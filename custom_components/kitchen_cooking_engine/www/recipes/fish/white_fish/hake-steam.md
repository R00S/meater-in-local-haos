# Hake × Steam — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Hake's lean, tender flakes cook quickly in moist heat; steaming avoids a browned crust and limits direct handling of the delicate flesh. The sealed steam environment cooks the surface and centre gently, but the thin fillets can become dry or tough if left too long. The sources give time and visual endpoints, not an internal pull temperature or carryover measurement. Probe placement is best attempted from the side into the thickest portion, avoiding the tapered tail; these sources do not prescribe a probe.

## Source recipes

### 1. Charo Val — Steamed Hake and Vegetables
**Inspired by**: Marine Stewardship Council, *The SA Ocean Cookbook 2024*, recipe by Charo Val (fetched 2026-10-24): https://www.msc.org/what-you-can-do/sustainable-fish-seafood-recipes/recipe/sa-steamed-hake-and-vegetables
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 60 min · **Cook**: 8–10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 200 g hake pieces
- 50 g artichokes; 100 g green asparagus; 3 pea pods
- ½ glass each squeezed orange juice and lemon juice
- 6 bay leaves; 6 lemon slices; olive oil; salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Marinate hake in the orange and lemon juice for 1 hour.
2. Sauté artichokes, peas and asparagus in a little olive oil.
3. Put bay leaves and lemon slices in a steamer, place hake above, cover and steam 8–10 minutes. The source warns that over-steaming makes hake tough.
4. Serve with vegetables and season to taste. The recipe gives no internal temperature or rest time; a probe may be inserted laterally in the thickest piece, away from the tapered edge, as a separate monitoring aid.

### 2. Jakub Baster — Steamed Hake with English Peas
**Inspired by**: StarChefs, chef Jakub Baster of Yingtao, New York (fetched 2026-10-24): https://www.starchefs.com/recipes/steamed-hake
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 10 min steaming plus component preparation · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 400 g hake fillet; kosher salt and ground white pepper
- English peas: 20 g scallions, 50 g blanched peas, 10 g pickled ramps, 10 g pickled ginger, canola oil
- Infused cream: 100 g heavy cream, 10 g ginger, 2 bay leaves, 2 garlic cloves
- Pea purée: 100 g cooked peas, 15 g spinach, infused cream
- Gastrique: 100 g soy sauce, 35 g rock sugar
- Broth: 100 g clarified fumet, 10 g white soy sauce, 5 g fish sauce, sugar and salt; 3 g mint oil and 3 g ginger-garlic oil

**Method**
1. Sauté scallions in oil for 2 minutes; add peas, ramps and ginger, season, cook until tender and chill.
2. Gently cook cream, ginger, bay and garlic for 10 minutes; strain. Blend 100 g cooked peas and spinach with infused cream to make the purée.
3. Reduce soy sauce and rock sugar to make the gastrique. Warm fumet with white soy and fish sauces; season and stir in the oils.
4. Season hake and set it in a bamboo steamer over boiling water. Cover and steam 10 minutes, or until cooked through. The recipe states no internal temperature or rest; a probe can be inserted from the side into the thickest section as a separate monitoring aid.
5. Plate over pea purée, add broth and pea mixture, then finish with gastrique and pea tendrils.

### 3. Dominic Franks — Steamed Sticky Hake with Ginger, Soy and Garlic
**Inspired by**: Dom in the Kitchen (fetched 2026-10-24): https://www.dominthekitchen.com/2021/05/steamed-sticky-hake-with-ginger-soy-and-garlic.html
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 hake fillets, cut into large chunks
- 1 tbsp honey; 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 thumb-sized piece fresh ginger, peeled and finely grated
- 1 large garlic clove, finely grated
- Juice and zest of 1 medium orange; 5 spring onions, chopped

**Method**
1. Heat the fan oven to 180°C. Lay parchment on foil in a baking tray.
2. Place hake on the parchment and scatter spring onions over it. Mix honey, soy, ginger, garlic and orange juice and zest; pour over the fish.
3. Fold the foil and parchment into an airtight tent. Bake 10 minutes, open carefully, then bake 5 minutes more to thicken the sauce. This is a parcel-steaming method; no internal temperature or probe step is stated by the source. A probe can be placed laterally in the thickest chunk if temperature monitoring is desired.
4. Serve with lettuce and radish or noodles.
