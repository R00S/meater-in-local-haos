## Cut profile

Grilling is the method that rewards halibut's geometry. The fillet is thick and dense enough to
survive a grate without a basket, and its tight flake holds together when turned once at the
halfway point — the opposite of a thin, fragile white fish. What the fire adds is a charred, smoky
surface on a fish that otherwise browns reluctantly; what it costs is control, because the window
between "just opaque" and dry is narrow on a thick, lean fillet. Every fetched source solves that
by leaving the fillet whole and turning it once, and none of them measures the interior: doneness
is judged by opacity, by flake, or by time per side.

The five sources in this leaf span Moroccan-style chermoula, Thai, Korean, Cambodian and Jamaican
jerk treatments, so this is the cut's strongest culinary-group coverage of any method.

## Source recipes

### 1. Grilled Halibut with Chermoula over Moroccan-spiced Couscous — Erin Berdan
**Inspired by**: White Plate Blank Slate; https://whiteplateblankslate.com/grilled-halibut/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Moroccan-style.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 8–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1¼ lb halibut fillet, preferably a thicker cut; olive oil; salt and pepper
- Chermoula: 1 small garlic clove; handful cilantro; leaves from 4 sprigs parsley; ¼ tsp ground cumin; 2 tsp lemon juice; 3 tbsp olive oil
- Couscous: 1 cup dry pearled couscous; 1 tbsp olive oil; 1 garlic clove; ½ small onion; 1 yellow squash; ½ red bell pepper; 1½ tsp ras el hanout; 2 tsp lemon juice

**Method**
1. Cut the fish into 4 pieces, season and coat with a little oil. Heat a grill to high.
2. Pulse the chermoula ingredients to a coarse sauce and season.
3. Cook the couscous; sauté onion, garlic, squash and pepper, stir in ras el hanout and the couscous, finish with lemon juice.
4. Grill the halibut 4–5 minutes per side, until the centre is just opaque. Thinner cuts cook faster.
5. Serve over the couscous with the chermoula.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until the center is just opaque."

### 2. Grilled Halibut with Tatsoi and Spicy Thai Chiles — Bon Appétit
**Inspired by**: Bon Appétit; https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/grilled-halibut-with-tatsoi-and-spicy-thai-chiles (fetched 2026-10-24). Thai-style.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 minutes · **Cook**: 8 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 5 tbsp sugar; 5 tbsp fish sauce; ¼ cup water; 3 tbsp fresh lime juice
- 2 tbsp minced fresh ginger; 2 garlic cloves, minced; 2 Thai bird chillies (or ½ large jalapeño), minced
- 1 small carrot, cut into matchsticks; 4 6–7 oz halibut fillets; 3 tbsp vegetable oil, divided
- 1 shallot, thinly sliced; ¾ lb tatsoi or baby spinach

**Method**
1. Mix the sugar, fish sauce, water, lime juice, ginger, garlic and chilli into a sauce; season.
2. Prepare a barbecue at medium-high heat. Soak the carrot in iced water 15 minutes; drain.
3. Brush the fish with 2 tbsp oil, season, and grill until just opaque in the centre, about 4 minutes per side.
4. Wilt the shallot and tatsoi in the remaining oil and divide among plates.
5. Top with the fish, scatter the carrot and spoon over the sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until just opaque in center", about 4 minutes per side.

### 3. Grilled Halibut, Eggplant and Baby Bok Choy with Korean Barbecue Sauce — Bon Appétit
**Inspired by**: Bon Appétit; https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/grilled-halibut-eggplant-and-baby-bok-choy-with-korean-barbecue-sauce (fetched 2026-10-24). Korean-barbecue style.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 minutes · **Cook**: about 7 minutes for the fish · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 tbsp oil, divided; 2 garlic cloves, minced; 1½ tsp minced serrano chilli
- ⅓ cup soy sauce; ¼ cup packed dark brown sugar; 3 tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar; 3 tbsp water; 1 tbsp Asian sesame oil
- 8 baby bok choy, halved; 4 Japanese eggplants, halved lengthwise
- 4 6–7 oz halibut fillets, about 1 inch thick; 2 green onions, sliced

**Method**
1. Sauté the garlic and chilli in 1 tbsp oil until fragrant, add the soy, sugar, vinegar and water, boil and reduce to ¾ cup; whisk in the sesame oil.
2. Prepare a barbecue at medium heat and toss the vegetables with 2 tbsp oil.
3. Brush the fish with the remaining oil and season.
4. Grill the vegetables and fish, turning and brushing with sauce — about 10 minutes for the vegetables and **7 minutes total for the fish**, until the fish is just opaque in the centre.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "fish is just opaque in center"; total grill time 7 minutes rather than minutes per side.

### 4. Nhoam Trey Mhasamout (Grilled Halibut Salad) — Chanthy Yen
**Inspired by**: Bon Appétit; https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/nhoam-trey-mhasamout-grilled-halibut-salad (fetched 2026-10-24). Cambodian.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 minutes · **Cook**: about 5 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 12 oz halibut fillet (or haddock); 1 tsp vegetable oil; kosher salt
- Dressing: 1 tbsp each finely chopped lemongrass, ginger and galangal; 2 limes; 2 tbsp fish sauce; 2 tbsp vegetable oil; 1 tbsp shrimp paste; salt and pepper
- Salad: 1 pomelo (or 3 small grapefruit); 3 medium tomatoes; 1 bunch scallions; cilantro and mint; 3 cups chopped cabbage; 2 shallots; 1 Thai chilli; ¼ cup crispy fried onions; ¼ cup puffed rice

**Method**
1. Prepare a grill for medium-high heat. Rub the halibut with oil, season with salt, and grill turning halfway, until just cooked through, about 5 minutes total.
2. Pound the aromatics to a paste, add lime zest and juice, fish sauce, oil, shrimp paste and seasoning.
3. Toss the salad ingredients, flake in the grilled halibut with its juices, and dress.
4. Top with shallots, chilli, fried onions and puffed rice.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until just cooked through, about 5 minutes total."

### 5. Jamaican Jerk Halibut with Pico de Gallo — Traeger Kitchen
**Inspired by**: Traeger; https://www.traeger.com/recipes/jamaican-jerk-halibut-pico-de-gallo (fetched 2026-10-24). Jamaican-jerk-inspired.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2 hours marinade · **Cook**: 12–14 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 (6 oz) halibut steaks, 1 inch thick
- Marinade: ½ cup orange juice; ¼ cup pineapple juice; ¼ cup rum; 1 tbsp Jamaican jerk seasoning; 1 tbsp brown sugar; ¼ cup vegetable oil
- Pico de gallo: 1 large tomato; 1 sweet onion; ½–1 jalapeño; 1 garlic clove; ¼ cup cilantro; ½ lime; salt

**Method**
1. Rinse the steaks and lay them in a shallow dish.
2. Whisk the marinade, pour over the fish, cover and refrigerate no more than 2 hours.
3. Combine the pico de gallo ingredients and season.
4. Set the grill to 500 °F and preheat, lid closed, 15 minutes; oil the grate.
5. Pat the fish dry, dust with more jerk seasoning, and grill 6–8 minutes. Turn with a spatula and continue until the fish flakes easily with a fork, about 6 minutes more.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until fish flakes easily with a fork."

## Evidence limits

Five fetched sources across Moroccan-style, Thai, Korean, Cambodian and Jamaican-jerk treatments — the best group coverage of any halibut method in this collection. **None of the five states an internal temperature.** Two of the five are Bon Appétit recipes published within the same editorial tradition, which is recorded as a publisher overlap rather than five fully independent traditions.

## What makes this method special

Halibut grills well for a structural reason: the fillet is thick and its myotomes run long, so a single turn at the halfway point does not tear it, and the dense flesh holds juices through the charring stage. The five sources all build flavour *outside* the fish — a chermoula, a Thai chilli-lime sauce, a Korean barbecue glaze, a Cambodian lemongrass dressing, a jerk marinade — because halibut itself is bland and lean. The uniform risk is overshoot: 4–7 minutes a side is the whole cook, and no source uses a thermometer to police it.

## Summary

Grilled halibut is a short, high-heat, single-turn cook — 4–5 minutes per side, or about 5–7 minutes total for a thinner fillet — finished on visual cues ("just opaque", "flakes easily"). Five fetched sources across Groups A, B and C; none publishes an internal temperature.
