## Cut profile

Roasting a halibut fillet is the low-attention counterpart to pan searing: the thick, dense fillet
goes into a hot oven (375–450 °F) either bare on a tray, under a sauce, or sealed in parchment,
and comes out in 8–22 minutes depending on thickness and whether the fish is covered. The cut
tolerates this because its tight myotomes hold moisture far longer than a thin flatfish fillet's
would — but the same density means heat penetrates slowly, and every source in this leaf stacks
the odds in its favour by covering the fish (foil, parchment, sauce, or a lid of vegetables).
Carryover is the reason the sources pull early in spirit even where they state no temperature:
a 1½-inch fillet keeps climbing after it leaves the oven.

Six fetched recipes, across Asian-style, French-style, Indian, Italian-style, US and
Moroccan-style treatments. **None of the six states an internal temperature**: each gives an oven
setting, a time and a visual cue instead (see evidence limits).

## Source recipes

### 1. Asian Baked Halibut — A Pinch of Salt Lake
**Inspired by**: A Pinch of Salt Lake; https://apinchofsaltlake.com/asian-baked-halibut/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Asian-style.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 12 minutes · **Rest**: 5 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 2 halibut fillets, about 6 oz each
- Sauce: 1 tsp sesame oil; 3 tbsp honey; 1 tbsp dark soy; 1 tbsp fish sauce; 1 tbsp hoisin; 2 tsp soy sauce; 2 tsp brown sugar; 1 tsp lime juice; 1 tsp grated ginger; 1 garlic clove, minced; 2 green onions, sliced
- 1 tsp lime zest; cilantro, to garnish

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 400 °F and line a pan with parchment or foil.
2. Pat the fillets dry and arrange them skin-side down with 2 inches between pieces.
3. Mix the sauce and spoon it over the fish; it will pool around it.
4. Bake 10 minutes, baste with the pooled sauce, and bake 2 minutes more.
5. Rest 5 minutes, then spoon the thickened sauce over and finish with zest, onion and cilantro.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. The source's only timing is 10 + 2 minutes at 400 °F plus a 5-minute rest.

### 2. Halibut en Papillote — Geoffrey Zakarian
**Inspired by**: Food Network; https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/geoffrey-zakarian/halibut-en-papillote-9986026 (fetched 2026-10-24). French-style parchment cooking.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 minutes · **Cook**: 8–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- ½ cup Kalamata olives; 2 tbsp capers, rinsed; 2 garlic cloves; 1 anchovy fillet; ½ cup olive oil
- 1 head fennel, sliced paper-thin; 2 leeks, white parts only, thinly sliced; 1 lemon, thinly sliced
- 4 (4–6 oz) halibut fillets; 4 tbsp white wine; 1 Calabrian chilli, minced; basil

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 400 °F.
2. Process the olives, capers, garlic, anchovy and oil into a tapenade.
3. On each of 4 parchment circles, layer fennel, leek, tapenade and lemon; set the fish on top and season.
4. Fold and crimp the parchment, adding 1 tbsp wine to each packet halfway through closing.
5. Bake 8–10 minutes; open the packets at the table and garnish with chilli and basil.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. The sealed packet is the doneness control: 8–10 minutes for a 4–6 oz fillet.

### 3. Garam Masala Roasted Halibut in a Tomato Curry Sauce — Kevin Lynch
**Inspired by**: Closet Cooking; https://www.closetcooking.com/garam-masala-roasted-halibut-in-tomato/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Indian-inspired.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 minutes marinade · **Cook**: 10–15 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 (6 oz) halibut pieces
- 1 tbsp oil; 1 tbsp garam masala; ¼ tsp turmeric
- Sauce: 1 tbsp oil; 1 small onion; 1 tbsp garlic; 1 tbsp ginger; 2 green chillies; 1 (28 oz) can crushed tomatoes; ½ cup coconut milk; 1 tsp cumin seeds; 1 tsp coriander seeds; ½ tsp turmeric; 1 tbsp garam masala; cilantro

**Method**
1. Mix the oil, garam masala and turmeric and brush over the fish; rest 20 minutes in a baking dish.
2. Roast in a preheated 450 °F / 230 °C oven until cooked, about 10–15 minutes.
3. Meanwhile sauté the onion, then garlic, ginger and chilli; add tomatoes, coconut milk and toasted ground spices and simmer about 15 minutes.
4. Finish the sauce with garam masala and cilantro; serve the fish on the sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "White juices will appear on the fish when it is cooked."

### 4. Baked Halibut with Wine and Herbs — Food Network Kitchen
**Inspired by**: Food Network; https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/baked-halibut-with-wine-and-herbs-recipe2-1951660 (fetched 2026-10-24).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: 20–22 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 sprigs thyme; 8 sprigs parsley; 2 bay leaves; 8 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 (1 lb) halibut steaks, 1¼ inches thick, from the tail end
- Kosher salt and pepper; ¾ cup dry vermouth; 4 tbsp olive oil; 2 tsp lemon juice
- Cherry tomatoes, to garnish; optional olive tapenade

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 450 °F.
2. Make a bed of thyme, parsley and bay in a gratin dish; scatter the garlic over.
3. Season the fish, lay it on the herbs, add the vermouth and 2 tbsp oil, and cover loosely with foil.
4. Bake until the fish is opaque, about 20–22 minutes.
5. Reduce the pan juices by half, whisk in the remaining oil and the lemon juice, and pour over the fish.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "Bake until the fish is opaque, about 20 to 22 minutes" for a 1¼-inch steak.

### 5. Baked Halibut with Arugula Salsa Verde — Giada De Laurentiis
**Inspired by**: Food Network; https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/giada-de-laurentiis/baked-halibut-with-arugula-salsa-verde-recipe-2124814 (fetched 2026-10-24). Italian-style salsa verde.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 minutes · **Cook**: 9–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 (6 oz) halibut fillets, each about 1½ inches thick
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 1 tsp kosher salt; ¾ tsp black pepper
- Topping: 2 tbsp chopped chives; 2 tbsp chopped parsley; 1 tsp lemon zest
- Salsa verde: 1 cup chopped arugula; ⅔ cup chopped parsley; ¼ cup olive oil; ¼ cup capers; juice and zest of 1 lemon; ¾ tsp salt; ½ tsp crushed red pepper

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 375 °F and position a rack in the centre.
2. Mix the chive, parsley and lemon-zest topping.
3. Coat the fish with oil, set on a rimmed baking sheet, season and press on the topping.
4. Bake just until opaque in the centre, 9–10 minutes.
5. Stir the salsa verde together and spoon alongside.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "Bake the fish just until opaque in the center, 9 to 10 minutes."

### 6. Halibut Tagine — Margot Kravette (via Food.com)
**Inspired by**: Food.com; https://www.food.com/recipe/halibut-tagine-192057 (fetched 2026-10-24). Tagine/Moroccan-style; the page does not establish authenticity.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 30 minutes · **Cook**: about 30 minutes in the oven after the sauce · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 lbs halibut; 1 lemon, halved; ½ cup olive oil
- 1 lb onions; 8 garlic cloves; 3 tbsp grated lemon peel; 2 tsp paprika; 1½ tsp cumin; 1½ tsp ginger; ¼ tsp cayenne
- 8 tbsp parsley; dill; 8 tbsp mint; 4 cups stock; 6 artichoke hearts; 3 fennel bulbs; 5 carrots

**Method**
1. Sauté the onions in oil until translucent, add the spices and herbs and cook 3 minutes.
2. Add the stock and vegetables, bring to the boil, cover and simmer 15 minutes, then uncover and cook 15 minutes more.
3. Preheat the oven to 400 °F. Put the fish in an oiled baking dish and cover with the tagine.
4. Cover with foil and cook about 30 minutes, until the fish is tender and not overdone.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "Check by sliding a fork under it; if it starts to fall apart, then it's done."

## Evidence limits

Six fetched recipes across Asian-style, French-style, Indian, Italian-style, US and Moroccan-style
treatments. **No source states an internal temperature**: cooking times are 8–22 minutes depending
on whether the fish is bare, sauce-covered or sealed. Three of the six come from one publisher
(Food Network) so publisher diversity is lower than the raw count suggests.

## What makes this method special

The oven gives halibut a controlled ambient temperature and frees the cook from the pan, which
suits a thick fillet that would otherwise need basting. But every one of the six sources covers
the fish — foil, parchment, a sauce, or a lid of vegetables — because a bare, lean fillet in a
450 °F oven dries out at the edges before the centre sets. That shared habit is the real finding
of this leaf: for halibut, "oven roast" in practice means "oven roast under cover", and the cover
is what makes the 8–22 minute range workable.

## Summary

Oven-roasted halibut is a covered bake at 375–450 °F for 8–22 minutes depending on thickness and
covering, judged by opacity or flake. Six fetched sources; none publishes an internal temperature.
