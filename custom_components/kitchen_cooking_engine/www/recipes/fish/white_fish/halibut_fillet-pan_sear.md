## Cut profile (method-specific)

Pan searing is the test of a halibut fillet's density. The fillet is thick, lean and structurally
tight, so it can take a dry, smoking-hot pan in a way that thinner flatfish cannot: the surface sets
into a crust before the interior climbs, and the fillet survives being pressed flat with a spatula
to keep full contact. What it cannot do is wait — with almost no fat in the flesh there is nothing
to buffer overshoot, so the difference between a moist centre and a chalky one is a couple of
minutes for a 3 cm fillet.

The six fetched recipes fall into two shapes. Bare, dry-seared fillets (US house recipes, BBC) are
cooked entirely in the pan, 3–5 minutes a side. Basted or floured fillets (Nut Free Wok,
Chef Merito, BBC) add butter, oil aromatics or a starch dust that changes the surface behaviour.
Note what the sources do *not* do: **none of the six puts halibut skin-side down**, so this leaf
does not claim a skin-sear sequence for this cut the way the hake leaf does. Two sources state a
target reading — 145 °F (Momsdish) and 130–135 °F for medium (Fountain Avenue Kitchen, Lively
Table) — which bracket a wide divergence, from well-done to just-set; both are recorded as
published, neither is reconciled.

## Source recipes

### 1. Pan-Seared Halibut (Only 4 Ingredients) — Momsdish
**Inspired by**: Momsdish; https://momsdish.com/pan-seared-halibut (fetched 2026-10-24).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 5–7 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 lb halibut fillet; 1 tbsp garlic parsley salt
- ¼ cup unsalted butter; 1 lemon, sliced
- 2 tbsp chopped herbs (parsley, green onion, cilantro or dill)

**Method**
1. Preheat a skillet with the butter over medium-high heat.
2. Season the fillets generously on all sides and add them gently to the skillet; arrange lemon slices around them.
3. Cook 3–4 minutes until a crust forms. Flip, reduce the heat to medium and cook 2–3 minutes more.
4. Squeeze over lemon juice, scatter herbs and serve immediately.

**Probe and endpoint**: the page states "Cooked halibut should read at 145°F" and instructs checking the internal temperature with a meat thermometer. It gives no separate pull temperature and no rest.

### 2. Quick Pan-Fried Halibut — Anjum Anand
**Inspired by**: BBC Food, from *Indian Food Made Easy*; https://www.bbc.co.uk/food/recipes/quickpanfriedhalibut_86557 (fetched 2026-10-24). Indian.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 7–8 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 halibut steaks
- ½ tsp ground turmeric; ¼ tsp red chilli powder; 1 tsp ground coriander; pinch carom seeds; ¼ tsp salt
- ½ tsp grated ginger; ½ tsp crushed garlic; 2 tbsp vegetable oil; 2 tsp flour
- Lemon juice, to serve

**Method**
1. Sprinkle the spices and salt over the fish and rub in the ginger, garlic and 1 tsp of the oil.
2. Heat the remaining oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Dust the fish with flour on both sides.
3. Put the fish in the pan, reduce the heat slightly and fry 4 minutes. Turn and fry another 3–4 minutes, until cooked through and crisp.
4. Serve with lemon juice.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until cooked through and crisp."

### 3. Pan-Seared Greenland Halibut Steaks with Ginger and Scallions — Sharon Wong
**Inspired by**: Nut Free Wok; https://nutfreewok.com/greenland-halibut-steaks/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Recipe card labels the cuisine "Asian inspired, Chinese-American".

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: about 5 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 (5 oz) Greenland halibut steaks, thawed
- 4 tbsp cornstarch
- 1 tbsp rice wine; 1 tbsp soy sauce; ½ tsp sugar; ¼ tsp white pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 stalks scallions, cut into 1-inch diagonal pieces; 1½-inch piece ginger, julienned

**Method**
1. Rinse and dry the steaks and sprinkle both sides with cornstarch.
2. Mix the rice wine, soy, sugar and white pepper until the sugar dissolves.
3. Preheat a large non-stick pan on medium for 5 minutes; add the oil, fry the ginger 1 minute and the scallions 30 seconds; remove and set aside.
4. Add the steaks in one layer and sear until slightly browned on one side, about 3 minutes (up to 4 for thicker pieces). Flip.
5. Top with the aromatics, pour over the sauce, let it sizzle 30 seconds, flip to coat the other side for 30 seconds and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "slightly browned" on the first side, then a 30-second sauce finish per side.

### 4. Fletán a la Sartén (Pan-Seared Halibut) — Chef Merito
**Inspired by**: Chef Merito; https://chefmerito.com/es/blogs/recipes/pan-seared-halibut (fetched 2026-10-24). Spanish-language page on a US seasoning brand's site; the recipe does not name a national cuisine.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: 6 minutes plus sauce · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter; 3 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp shallots, finely chopped; 1 cup portobello mushrooms; 2 oz white wine; 1 tbsp lemon juice
- Salt and pepper; 1 tbsp Italian parsley
- 4 halibut fillets, 6–7 oz each; 4 tbsp fish seasoning; ¼ cup all-purpose flour; 2 tbsp cooking oil

**Method**
1. Melt the butter with the olive oil; cook the shallots 2 minutes, add the mushrooms and cook about 4 minutes.
2. Add the wine, boil 1 minute, add the lemon juice and parsley, cook 1 minute and season.
3. Season the fillets, dust lightly with flour.
4. Heat a large pan over medium-high with the cooking oil.
5. Cook the fish on one side until golden, about 3 minutes; turn and cook about 3 minutes more, "hasta que la carne pase de translúcida a opaca" (until the flesh turns from translucent to opaque).
6. Warm the sauce, pour over the fish and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: flesh turning from translucent to opaque.

### 5. Pan Seared Halibut — Ann Fulton
**Inspired by**: Fountain Avenue Kitchen; https://fountainavenuekitchen.com/pan-seared-halibut/ (fetched 2026-10-24). US home cooking.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 5–8 minutes · **Rest**: carryover described, time not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb halibut fillets
- ½ tsp garlic powder; ¼ tsp each smoked paprika, black pepper and dried oregano; ¾ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- Lemon wedges or a sauce, to serve

**Method**
1. Pat the fillets dry.
2. Combine the seasonings and sprinkle evenly over the fish, rubbing gently.
3. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high and sear 3–4 minutes on the first side, until lightly golden. Flip and cook 2–4 minutes more, until just cooked through.
4. Serve with lemon or a sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: the page states that medium halibut "registers as 130°F to 135°F on a quick-read thermometer" and describes the ideal visual as "mostly opaque with the tiniest hint of translucence in the very center, which will finish cooking in the carryover heat." No pull temperature is separated from the serving temperature, and no rest time is given.

### 6. Easy Pan Seared Halibut — Kaleigh McMordie
**Inspired by**: Lively Table; https://livelytable.com/pan-seared-halibut/ (fetched 2026-10-24). US; recipe card cuisine "seafood".

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 30 minutes marinade · **Cook**: 6–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb Alaska halibut
- 3 garlic cloves, minced; juice of 1 large lemon
- ½ cup dry white wine; ¼ cup extra virgin olive oil plus 1 tbsp for the pan
- Sea salt and black pepper; 1 tbsp butter; lemon slices

**Method**
1. Combine the garlic, lemon juice, wine and oil; marinate the portioned halibut in it about 30 minutes in the refrigerator.
2. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat; season the fish.
3. Cook, pressing gently with a fish spatula, until a golden crust forms on the bottom, 3–5 minutes; do not move the fish before the crust forms.
4. Flip and cook 3–5 minutes more, until the internal temperature reaches 130–135 °F.
5. Add butter, spoon the foaming butter over the fish and serve with lemon.

**Probe and endpoint**: states an internal temperature of 130–135 °F as the finish point without giving a separate pull temperature or rest.

## Temperature evidence

| Source | Stated reading | What it describes |
|---|---|---|
| Momsdish | 145 °F | "Cooked halibut should read" — well done |
| Fountain Avenue Kitchen | 130–135 °F | "medium halibut registers" — just set, still translucent at the centre |
| Lively Table | 130–135 °F | finish point, "center is opaque but no longer glossy" |

Two of the three readings sit 15–20 °F apart. The split is a doneness preference, not a disagreement about safety, and both are recorded as published. The other three sources state no temperature at all.

## What makes this method special

Pan searing is the only halibut method that produces a crust, and the crust is what lets a lean fish
carry butter, spice pastes or a pan sauce. It is also the least forgiving method in this collection:
no cover, no steam, no sauce to hide behind, and three of six sources explicitly warn not to move
the fish until the first side has formed a crust. The starch dust in the BBC and Nut Free Wok
recipes is a workaround for the same problem the halibut has in every pan — a wet, low-fat surface
that will not brown on its own.

## Summary

Pan-seared halibut is a 3–5 minute-per-side cook in a hot, lightly oiled or buttered pan, usually
with a single turn and sometimes a starch dust or a basting finish. Six fetched sources; three state
no temperature, three bracket 130–145 °F.
