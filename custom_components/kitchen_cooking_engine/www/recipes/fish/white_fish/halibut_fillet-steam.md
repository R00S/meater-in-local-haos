## Cut profile

Steaming is the East Asian answer to halibut's density. The fillet sits on a plate over boiling
water, covered, for 8–10 minutes; the moist heat sets the surface before it can dry, and because
the fish is not turned or pressed, the flake structure stays intact. Halibut suits this
particularly well when cut no thicker than about an inch — the Cantonese source says so
explicitly — because steam transfers heat more slowly than a pan, and a thicker fillet would set
at the edges before the centre. The classic finish is the Cantonese one: hot oil poured over
ginger and scallion, and a soy/rice-wine dressing.

Four fetched sources (Cantonese, Chinese-Malaysian, Thai, US-Asian-inspired). None publishes an
internal temperature; every source uses time plus a physical test.

## Source recipes

### 1. Steamed Halibut with Ginger and Scallions — Sally Ling
**Inspired by**: Family Focus Blog, recipe from *Dumpling Daughter* by Sally Ling; https://familyfocusblog.com/recipe-for-steamed-halibut-with-ginger-and-scallions/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Chinese/Cantonese.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 8–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1½ lb halibut, skin on, no more than an inch thick at its thickest
- 4 tbsp light soy sauce; 2 tbsp Shaoxing rice wine
- 4 scallions, julienned; 3-inch piece fresh ginger, peeled and julienned
- 6 tbsp vegetable oil, plus more for brushing; white pepper; 12 sprigs cilantro

**Method**
1. Clean the fish and pat dry.
2. Combine the soy and rice wine; add the scallions (submerged) and half the ginger.
3. Oil a heat-proof plate, lay the fish skin-side down, sprinkle with white pepper and the remaining ginger.
4. Bring 2 inches of water to the boil in a steamer pot; put the plate in, cover and steam 8–10 minutes depending on thickness.
5. Test by pushing a metal skewer or chopstick into the thickest part: "if the fish is punctured without resistance, it's done." Drain the liquid.
6. Spread the scallion-ginger mixture over the fish, scatter cilantro, and pour over oil heated until very hot.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published physical cue: a skewer meets no resistance at the thickest point.

### 2. Steamed Halibut Fillet with Chinese Rice Wine — Otto Goh
**Inspired by**: Marine Stewardship Council, *The Ocean Cookbook 2024*; https://www.msc.org/what-you-can-do/sustainable-fish-seafood-recipes/recipe/steamed-halibut-fillet-with-chinese-rice-wine (fetched 2026-10-24). Chinese-style, Malaysia-based chef.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 15 minutes · **Cook**: 8 minutes plus 2–3 minutes resting in steam · **Rest**: 2–3 minutes in the steamer

**Ingredients**
- 400 g MSC-certified halibut fillet
- 500 ml water salted with 10 g salt
- 3 mushrooms, sliced; goji berries, soaked; 2 spring onions, chopped; 50 g shredded ginger
- 80 ml Chinese rice wine; 30 ml water; 100 g steamed fish soy sauce; 15 g sugar
- Shredded spring onion; spring onion oil; coriander

**Method**
1. Slice the fillet into 45-degree pieces and soak in the salted water 10–15 minutes; pat dry.
2. Top the fish with mushrooms, goji berries, ginger and spring onion.
3. Mix the rice wine, soy sauce and sugar into the 30 ml water and pour over.
4. Steam at high temperature for 8 minutes, then turn off the heat and leave covered in the steam 2–3 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. The source's control is 8 minutes at high steam plus a 2–3 minute carryover rest in the closed steamer.

### 3. Steamed Halibut Flashed with Thai Bird-Kaffir Lime Oil — Ming Tsai
**Inspired by**: Food Network; https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/steamed-halibut-thai-bird-kaffir-lime-oil-turmeric-fried-rice-long-bean-salad-recipe-1924616 (fetched 2026-10-24). Thai.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 minutes brine · **Cook**: 8–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lime, juiced; 2 tbsp salt; 2 tbsp sugar; 4 cups water
- 4 (6 oz) thick, square halibut fillets; red leaf or banana leaf for steaming
- 4 scallions, cut into ribbons; ½ cup peanut oil
- 6 kaffir lime leaves, ribboned; 3 Thai bird chillies, sliced; 1 tbsp ginger ribbons; 1 tbsp garlic slices
- Turmeric fried rice and long-bean salad, to serve

**Method**
1. Mix the lime juice, salt, sugar and water and brine the fish 20 minutes.
2. Line a steamer that holds all four fillets with red leaf or banana leaf; add the fillets, top with scallions and steam 8–10 minutes until cooked through.
3. Heat the peanut oil until almost smoking with the lime leaves, chilli, ginger and garlic.
4. Plate the halibut and flash it with the hot aromatics just before serving.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "steam for 8 to 10 minutes until cooked though."

### 4. Steamed Halibut with Mushroom Broth — Coastal Seafoods
**Inspired by**: Coastal Seafoods (Minneapolis); http://www.coastalseafoods.com/blog/steamed-halibut-with-mushroom-broth (fetched 2026-10-24). Asian-inspired; no country stated.

**Serves**: 2–4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: 5–10 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2–4 (6–8 oz) halibut fillets, skin off
- 16 oz mushrooms, sliced; 6 scallions; 2 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp grated ginger; 1 Fresno chilli
- 2 cups mushroom ramen base or stock; 1 tbsp chilli or sesame oil; 1 tbsp rice wine vinegar
- 2 tbsp shoyu or soy sauce; juice of ½ lime; 2 tbsp vegetable oil

**Method**
1. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium-high and sauté the scallion whites, garlic and ginger 1–2 minutes.
2. Add the mushrooms and cook until they soften; add the chilli oil and vinegar and reduce.
3. Add the chilli, shoyu and stock; bring to a simmer.
4. Reduce the heat to medium, add the halibut, cover and cook until just steamed through, about 5–10 minutes depending on fillet size.
5. Finish the broth with scallion greens and lime juice and serve over the fish.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until the fish is just steamed through, approximately 5-10 minutes depending on fillet size."

## What makes this method special

Steam is the only halibut method in this collection with no fat and no browning, which makes it the
one that exposes the fish completely: there is nothing to hide an overshoot behind. In exchange it
is the most forgiving on structure — no turn, no press, no crust to break — and the Cantonese and
Malaysian sources both use the closed-steamer carryover (2–3 minutes off the heat) as their
equivalent of a rest. It is also the fastest route to a dressed dish: aromatics and soy go on the
plate, not in the pan.
