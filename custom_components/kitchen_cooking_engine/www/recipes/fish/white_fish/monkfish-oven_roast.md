# Monkfish × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Monkfish (*Lophius piscatorius*, Swedish: marulk, Danish: havtaske, French: lotte, Spanish: rape) is not a
flat fish but a thick one: the tail is a solid cylinder of dense, boneless muscle with almost no fat and a
lot of water, and it behaves more like a fillet of veal than like cod. That structure is why oven roasting
suits it — a 300–400 g tail piece holds its shape on a tray and can be brought up slowly without falling
apart — and why the four records here all use **low ovens and low targets**: 50 °C, 100 °C, 150 °C and a
47–58 °C reference band, for core temperatures of 46–52 °C.

The common step is a crust. Monkfish has no skin worth crisping, so three of the four records give it a
surface before the oven — flour, sesame seeds, a salt bed — because the meat itself will not brown. The
other half of the method is the rest: monkfish at 50 °C is springy and sliceable, and it firms noticeably
as it cools, which is why the Swedish records treat 46–52 °C as the whole usable window and warn that a
55 °C tail eats like chicken breast.

## Styckesprofil

Marulk (*Lophius piscatorius*, danska: havtaske, franska: lotte, spanska: rape) är inte platt utan tjock:
stjärten är en solid cylinder av tät, benfri muskel med nästan inget fett och mycket vatten, och den beter
sig mer som en kalvfilé än som torsk. Det är därför ugnsrostning passar — en bit på 300–400 g håller formen
på en plåt och kan värmas långsamt utan att falla sönder — och därför använder alla fyra källorna här **låg
ugnsvärme och låga mål**: 50 °C, 100 °C, 150 °C och ett referensspann på 47–58 °C, för kärntemperaturer på
46–52 °C.

Det gemensamma steget är en yta. Marulk har inget skinn värt att krispa, så tre av fyra källor ger den
panering — mjöl, sesamfrön, saltbädd — eftersom köttet inte bryner sig själv.

## Source recipes

### 1. Ugnsbakad marulksfilé med potatispuré — Köket.se / Martin Persson (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.koket.se/martin_persson/huvudratter/fisk_och_skaldjur/ugnsbakad_marulksfile_med_potatispure (fetched 2026-10-26). Köket.se, recipe by Martin Persson from the TV programme *Halv åtta hos mig*; Köket.se states the recipe was composed by an amateur cook and does not vouch for it. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 800 g monkfish fillet, cut into 4 portions
- 10 floury potatoes, 2 dl cream, 200 g butter
- 1 tsp truffle oil, a piece of fennel, 50 g vendace roe, 3 dl broad beans, dill

**Method**
1. Turn the fish in wheat flour and brown it briefly in butter on all sides. *(verbatim)*
2. **Finish it in a 50 °C oven until the core temperature is 50 °C.** *(verbatim)*
3. Boil and press the potatoes, bring butter and cream to the boil and whisk into the potato with truffle oil; season. *(paraphrase)*
4. Clarify butter with fennel, spoon it over the fish, top with a teaspoon of roe and a dill sprig. *(paraphrase)*

**Recorded temperature**: **50 °C**, in a 50 °C oven. This is cooking at the target temperature — the oven cannot overshoot because it is not hotter than the fish should get.

### 2. Ugnsbakad marulk med citronsmör — Mårtenssons Kök (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.martenssonskok.se/2014/09/ugnsbakad-marulk-med-citronsmor-och.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Mårtenssons Kök, a Swedish home-cooking blog written by two named authors, cooking and photographing their own food. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 20 min brine + searing · **Cook**: 20–30 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 300 g monkfish
- **Brine**: 100 g salt, 1 tsp sugar, 1 litre water, 20 minutes
- sesame seeds for the crust
- rapeseed oil with lemon and butter, for frying
- lemon-dill butter: 30 g butter, 1 lemon (zest and juice), ½ dl chopped dill, salt

**Method**
1. Stir the salt and sugar into the water until dissolved, put the fish in and leave it 20 minutes; lift out and drain. *(verbatim)*
2. Coat the fish in sesame seeds all round. *(verbatim)*
3. Brown it in butter and oil, **3 minutes on each side**, then move it to an ovenproof dish and insert a roasting thermometer into the middle. *(verbatim)*
4. Pour the frying fat over it and put the dish in the middle of a **150 °C oven for about 20–30 minutes; when the thermometer reads 52 °C the fish is done**. *(verbatim)*
5. Serve with lemon-dill butter and a root-vegetable sauté. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **52 °C**, 150 °C oven, after a 20-minute brine and 3 minutes a side in the pan. The brine is worth noting: 100 g salt per litre for 20 minutes is roughly a 10 % brine, and the authors say they brine more and more of their fish because it improves the raw material.

### 3. Sandefjordsås och marulk — Lagat & Klart / Andreas Domeij (Sweden/Norway)
**Inspired by**: https://lagatochklart.nu/recept/sandefjordsas-och-marulk (fetched 2026-10-26). Lagat & Klart, recipe by Andreas Domeij, published 24 August 2020, tagged as Norwegian. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 5 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: ~50 min total · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg monkfish, 1½ tbsp salt
- **Sandefjord sauce**: 2 dl cream, 75 g red caviar mix or lumpfish roe, 2 dl crème fraîche, 200 g butter, 2½ tbsp chopped chives, 1 tbsp dill, lemon juice and zest

**Method**
1. Set the oven to about **100 °C** and spread a little coarse salt in a large dish or roasting pan. *(verbatim)*
2. Lay the fish on it, scatter a little coarse salt over, and insert a digital thermometer diagonally into the fish. *(verbatim)*
3. **The fish is done when the thermometer shows 46 °C. It should have an internal temperature of 45–50 °C**; cut into portions and serve. *(verbatim)*
4. For the sauce, mount the butter into cream and crème fraîche with a stick blender until foamy, then stir in chives, dill, lemon and the roe just before serving; season carefully because the roe is salty. *(paraphrase)*

**Recorded temperature**: **46 °C at the probe, 45–50 °C stated as the target range** — the lowest pull in the leaf, and the only record that gives both a single number and a range for the same fish. The salt bed is the interesting part: it is a dry-heat poaching medium rather than a crust.

---

### 4. Kärntemperatur för olika fiskarter — godfisk.nu, citing Svenskfisk (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.godfisk.nu/page/tillagning-temperatur (fetched 2026-10-26). godfisk.nu (Swedish seafood quality site), temperature guidance with an explicit source citation to svenskfisk.se. **Culinary Group D**.
**Applies to**: fish generally, with a monkfish row

**Facts as published**
1. Measure the core, not the surface; different fish have different targets. *(paraphrase)*
2. Slow cooking at 100–125 °C in the oven gives a gentler cook and an even, juicier result; when you stop the cook, the core rises only a couple of degrees more. *(verbatim)*
3. Cooking at an extremely low 50–52 °C oven means the oven temperature equals the desired core temperature; the flesh changes appearance very little and the finished fish can look more or less raw. *(verbatim)*
4. Fast cooking at 175–225 °C gives a less even result and suits a browned surface; at high temperatures the heat keeps travelling into the fish after it leaves the oven, so stop earlier. *(verbatim)*
5. **Core temperatures for fillet: white fish such as cod, pollock and saithe about 52 °C; halibut 48–50 °C; flatfish 50–52 °C; monkfish 47–58 °C; catfish about 52 °C; salmon about 48 °C; eel about 70 °C; tuna 31 °C.** Add about 4 °C for a whole fish. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **47–58 °C for monkfish fillet**, the widest single band in the leaf, plus the general rule that slow ovens behave differently from fast ones. The page's own source line is svenskfisk.se, which is why it is counted as a publication record rather than a blog.

## Temperature summary

| Oven | Pull / target | Source | Method detail |
|------|---------------|--------|---------------|
| 50 °C | **50 °C** | Köket.se / Persson (SE) | floured, seared, cooked at target temperature |
| 100 °C | **46 °C** (45–50 °C) | Lagat & Klart / Domeij (SE–NO) | salt bed, probe inserted diagonally |
| 150 °C | **52 °C** | Mårtenssons Kök (SE) | 20 min brine, sesame crust, 3 min/side sear |
| *any* (reference) | **47–58 °C** | godfisk.nu / Svenskfisk (SE) | fillet target, +4 °C for whole fish |

**What this supports.** Four records, four numbers, all inside 46–52 °C on the pull and inside a 47–58 °C
band overall — the tightest fruit in this cut so far. The oven temperature varies by a factor of three (50,
100 and 150 °C) while the target barely moves, which is the useful finding: **for monkfish the oven setting
is a speed control, not a doneness control.** The 47–58 °C reference band is wide precisely because a tail
that thick goes from springy to firm across a few degrees, and the flatfish and white-fish rows on the same
page (50–52 °C) show that Svenskfisk puts monkfish deliberately higher than its neighbours.

**What it does not support.** All four records are Swedish or Swedish-Norwegian, so this leaf is one
tradition — `duplicate_tradition` is open. No record here probes a whole monkfish, only tail pieces and
fillets, so the +4 °C rule on the reference page is applied by nobody in the corpus. And no source in this
leaf discusses the 60 °C sous vide result recorded elsewhere in the cut, so the two do not contradict each
other so much as sit in different methods.

## What makes this method special

Monkfish is the one white fish in this collection where a low oven is not a compromise. Because the tail is
a solid cylinder with no bones and no flaky seams, it can be held at 50 °C for as long as it takes the core
to arrive, and it will not dry out or fall apart on the way. That is also why the method rewards a crust
before the oven: the meat gives the cook nothing to look at, so the surface has to say when it is done.

## Summary

Four records, four numbers: **50 °C in a 50 °C oven (Köket.se), 46 °C with a stated 45–50 °C range (Lagat
& Klart), 52 °C after a brine and sesame crust (Mårtenssons Kök), and a 47–58 °C reference band from
Svenskfisk via godfisk.nu.** Ovens from 50 to 150 °C all land in the same narrow window, which makes the
oven a speed control rather than a doneness control for this fish. All four records are Nordic, and none of
them probes a whole fish.
