# Plaice × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Plaice (*Pleuronectes platessa*, Swedish: rödspätta, Danish: rødspætte, German: Scholle) is a flatfish,
and flatness is the whole reason oven baking suits it: a whole fish is only 3–5 cm thick through the
thickest part of the back, so there is no gradient to manage and no reason to sear it for colour before it
goes in. The four records here bake whole fish at 210–225 °C for 15–20 minutes, or hold a pan-seared fish
at 80 °C to finish — and every one of them does the same small physical thing first, **slitting the dark
skin along the backbone** so the fish does not curl up in the heat. That detail appears in the Danish and
Swedish records independently, and it is the method's real technique: a flatfish that curls lifts its
thickest part out of contact with the tray.

The skin is also the reason oven baking is contested for this fish. Plaice skin is thin and does not crisp
in an oven the way it does in butter in a pan, so the Swedish records split: two bake it plain and pull the
skin off at the table, and one sears it first and finishes in a very low oven purely to serve four people
from one pan.

## Styckesprofil

Rödspätta (*Pleuronectes platessa*, danska: rødspætte, tyska: Scholle) är en plattfisk, och plattformen är
hela skälet till att ugnsbakning passar: en hel fisk är bara 3–5 cm tjock över ryggen, så det finns ingen
temperaturgradient att hantera och ingen anledning att bryna den för färgens skull. De fyra källorna här
ugnsbakar hel fisk vid 210–225 °C i 15–20 minuter, eller håller en pannbrynt fisk i 80 °C ugn för att bli
klar. Alla gör dessutom samma lilla fysiska sak först: **de snittar längs ryggbenet** så att fisken inte
krullar sig i värmen — en krullad plattfisk lyfter sin tjockaste del från plåten.

## Source recipes

### 1. Ugnsstekt rödspätta med skogschampinjoner och kapris — Tina Nordström (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://tina.se/ugnsstekt-rodspatta-med-skogschampinjoner-och-kapris/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Tina Nordström, Swedish chef and television cook; recipe on her own site. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: ~15 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 large plaice, whole
- 150 g soft butter, 4 sprigs rosemary
- 300 g wild mushrooms
- 1 tbsp olive oil + 1 tbsp butter, for frying
- 1 bunch chopped flat-leaf parsley (about 30 g)
- 1 red onion, 3 tbsp capers
- salt and white pepper from the mill

**Method**
1. Set the oven to **225 °C**. *(verbatim)*
2. Lay the plaice on a baking tray lined with baking paper. **Slit the fish along the backbone** and make a small pocket for the rosemary sprigs between fillet and backbone. *(verbatim)*
3. Spread butter generously over the fish, season with salt and pepper, and put the trays in the oven. *(verbatim)*
4. Bake about **15 minutes, until the fish feels firm and springy — about 48 °C at the bone if you like a thermometer.** *(verbatim)*
5. Fry the mushrooms dry for a couple of minutes, then add oil and butter, and finally fold in the red onion, parsley and capers; season with white pepper only, because the capers salt it. *(paraphrase)*
6. Serve the plaice straight off the tray with the mushrooms spooned over. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **48 °C at the bone**, with a 225 °C oven. Note the probe site: plaice is probed *at the bone*, not in the thickest flesh, because the backbone is the last part to heat on a fish this thin.

### 2. Rödspätta — Tasteline ingredient guide (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.tasteline.com/ingrediens/rodspatta/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Tasteline (Swedish recipe site), editorial ingredient page with photography and styling by Lisa Löwenborg. **Culinary Group D**.
**Applies to**: 85 published plaice recipes indexed on the page

**Facts as published**
1. Plaice is a saltwater fish and Sweden's most popular flatfish, and "går utmärkt att grilla, steka eller ugnsbaka" — it can perfectly well be grilled, fried or oven-baked. *(verbatim)*
2. Use a roasting thermometer to make the cooking easier. *(verbatim)*
3. **The plaice is done at an internal temperature of 50–52 °C.** *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **50–52 °C**, stated as the editorial house position rather than as a single recipe. This is the only record in the leaf that generalises across methods, and it sits two degrees above the Swedish chef's 48 °C at the bone.

### 3. Helstekt rödspätta med pepparrotssmör och rödbetor — Allt om Mat / Mitt kök, Annika Broman (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://alltommat.expressen.se/recept/helstekt-rodspatta-med-pepparrotssmor-rodbetor/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Allt om Mat (Expressen), recipe from *Mitt kök* by Annika Broman, 3.6/5 from 69 votes. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 15–20 min in the oven after searing · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 whole plaice, heads and fins removed
- 500 g butter
- 3 dl horseradish, coarsely grated
- 2 apples, in wedges
- 2 dl pickled beetroot, in pieces
- 2 dl caper berries (or ordinary capers)
- 2 lemons, salt, pepper

**Method**
1. Set the oven to **80 °C** and put four serving plates and an extra tray in it. *(verbatim)*
2. Sear the fish one at a time in butter, gently, for about **2 minutes per side**; season. Do not use high heat or the butter burns. *(verbatim)*
3. Put the seared fish on the warm plates in the oven and let them finish there, about **15–20 minutes**. If you are cooking only one fish, put the frying pan itself in the oven. *(verbatim)*
4. Meanwhile brown the butter until it smells nutty, and turn the horseradish, beetroot, capers and apple through it to warm them. *(paraphrase)*
5. Take the fish out and pour a generous spoonful of the butter over it; serve with new potatoes and lemon. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **none published** — an 80 °C holding oven and a 15–20 minute finish, judged by the fact that the fish was already seared. The method here is a service technique (four fish from one pan) rather than a doneness method, and it is the only record in the leaf that finishes at a temperature below any plausible target.

### 4. Hel rødspætte i ovn — Holger Rørby Madsen, Madens Verden (Denmark)
**Inspired by**: https://madensverden.dk/hel-roedspaette-i-ovn-2/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Madens Verden, Danish recipe site, 4.5/5 from 13 votes; recipe and video by Holger Rørby Madsen. **Culinary Group D**.
**Serves**: 4 (one fish per person) · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 20 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 large plaice per person (recipe written for 4 large fish)
- 1 tbsp olive oil (or browned butter)
- salt and freshly ground pepper
- lemon juice

**Method**
1. Wash and rinse the plaice thoroughly and pat it dry with kitchen paper. *(verbatim)*
2. Lay it on a baking tray lined with baking paper. *(verbatim)*
3. **With a sharp knife, cut down along the backbone so the plaice does not curl up during baking.** *(verbatim)*
4. Lay the fish dark side up and drizzle with a little olive oil — browned butter works too. *(verbatim)*
5. Season with salt and freshly ground pepper. *(verbatim)*
6. Bake at **210 °C fan for 20 minutes**. *(verbatim)*
7. Take the plaice out and drizzle lemon juice over it straight away. *(verbatim)*

**Recorded temperature**: **none published** — 210 °C fan for a fixed 20 minutes. The page's value is the backbone slit stated as a rule with its reason, and the confirmation that the same approach scales to 5–7 fish at once as long as they do not overlap.

## Temperature summary

| Oven | Recorded temperature | Source | Form |
|------|----------------------|--------|------|
| 225 °C | **48 °C at the bone** | Tina Nordström (SE) | whole fish, butter, ~15 min |
| *any* (editorial) | **50–52 °C** | Tasteline (SE) | house rule for grill, pan or oven |
| 80 °C | *none published* | Allt om Mat / Broman (SE) | seared first, held to finish |
| 210 °C fan | *none published* | Madens Verden (DK) | whole fish, 20 min fixed |

**What this supports.** Two independent numbers, **48 °C at the bone and 50–52 °C in the flesh**, from a chef and a recipe publication — consistent once the probe site is accounted for, since the bone runs a degree or two behind the flesh. Both sit below the cut's `well_done` target of 63 °C, which the hub currently recommends; nothing in this leaf evidences 63 °C for baked plaice.

**What it does not support, and why this leaf is thin.** Only two of four records publish any number, and **all four are Nordic** (three Swedish, one Danish) — a `duplicate_tradition` gap. The two silent records are not sloppy: both are explicit that plaice is done by feel ("fast och stunsig") or by a fixed time, which is what a 3–5 cm fish invites. A German *Scholle*, Dutch *schol* or British plaice record with a temperature would give this leaf a second tradition and a fourth number.

## What makes this method special

Oven baking is the least interventionist way to cook a flatfish, and the records treat it that way: butter, salt, a slit along the bone, and a short blast of heat. What it cannot do is crisp the skin, which is why one Swedish record sears first and then uses the oven as a warm holding box — a hybrid that exists for serving logistics rather than for the fish. The method's real technical content is the slit: without it the fish cups upward, the thickest part leaves the tray, and the top of the back cooks while the bone stays cold.

## Summary

Four records, two with a number: **48 °C at the bone from a Swedish chef's 225 °C bake, and 50–52 °C as Tasteline's house rule** for plaice however it is cooked. Two Danish and Swedish records bake whole plaice at 210–225 °C or hold it at 80 °C and publish no internal temperature at all, judging by feel and time. All four are Nordic, which is the leaf's clearest gap, and all four slit the backbone first so the fish does not curl.
