# Sea Bass × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven roasting is how the whole fish is cooked in most of Europe, and the four fetched
sources disagree about almost every variable except the one that matters: a whole sea bass
is done in **20 to 35 minutes** at some temperature between **180 °C and 205 °C**. What they
differ on is the geometry. The US/Greek-style source roasts at 400 °F for 5 minutes on one
side, turns the fish, finishes 5–7 minutes, checks **145 °F internally**, and then broils for
3–4 minutes to char the skin — a two-stage cook that puts colour on last. The Italian source
beds the fish on par-boiled potatoes at 180 °C fan for 20–35 minutes depending on weight. The
Russian source sears the whole fish 2 minutes per side in a pan first, then roasts it on a
vegetable bed at 180 °C for 20 minutes — a hybrid that starts the Maillard reaction on the
stove. The Swedish source brushes a spiced oil over a 500 g fish and roasts at 200 °C for
about 30 minutes. Because the fish is whole, the probe goes into the shoulder behind the
head: the belly will always read hotter and the tail earlier still.

## Source recipes

### 1. The Mediterranean Dish — Greek-Style Roasted Branzino (Suzy Karadsheh, US)
**Inspired by**: https://www.themediterraneandish.com/roasted-branzino-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Suzy Karadsheh, The Mediterranean Dish (US publication, Greek tradition).
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: ~5 min · **Cook**: 13–16 min + 3–4 min broil · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 whole branzino (European sea bass), about 1–1½ lb, cleaned, head and tail on
- Extra virgin olive oil, a generous amount
- About ½ lemon, sliced into thin rounds (plus more for the sauce)
- About ½ red onion, sliced
- Chopped fresh dill
- Cherry tomatoes, halved, to top the fish
- **Ladolemono**: lemon juice, garlic, dried oregano, salt, pepper, extra virgin olive oil

**Method**
1. Pat the fish dry, make **two slits on both sides**, and season generously with kosher salt and black pepper on both sides, through the slits and in the cavity.
2. Insert half a sliced lemon and half a sliced red onion into the cavity. Set the fish on a lightly oiled sheet pan.
3. Roast in the centre of a **400 °F (about 205 °C)** oven for **5 minutes on one side**, then turn carefully with a large spatula and cook **another 5–7 minutes**, until the fish flakes easily.
4. The source's own numbers, verbatim: *"Cooked fish will have an internal temperature of 145 degrees F when checked with a meat thermometer."* Turn the broiler on and broil **3–4 minutes about 6 inches from the heat** until the skin chars.
5. **Pull at 145 °F (63 °C)** — the source's stated internal temperature. Final serving temperature: **145 °F / 63 °C** (the source gives one value for both internal doneness and serving).
6. Whisk the ladolemono and pour it over the fish **immediately** out of the oven, into the cavity as well; spoon the salted cherry tomatoes over and finish with dill.

### 2. Fatto in Casa da Benedetta — Branzino al forno con patate (Anna, Italy)
**Inspired by**: https://blog.giallozafferano.it/pelledipollo/branzino-al-forno-con-patate/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Anna ("pelledipollo"), GialloZafferano blog (Italian home cooking).
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 45 min stated (fish 20–35 min) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 branzino (spigola / European sea bass)
- 600 g potatoes
- 1 lemon
- 2 fresh chillies (optional)
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 bunch parsley
- Extra virgin olive oil; salt; dried aromatic herbs (oregano, thyme, rosemary, sage, marjoram)

**Method**
1. Peel the potatoes and **par-boil or steam them 15–20 minutes**, then cut into pieces and dress with oil, salt and dried herbs.
2. Clean the fish (the source has the fishmonger do it), rinse and pat dry. Put a couple of lemon slices, parsley, a garlic clove and a pinch of salt **inside the belly**.
3. Line a baking tray with parchment, oil it lightly, set the fish in the centre, salt the surface, add a thread of oil and two or three more lemon slices on top.
4. Arrange the dressed potatoes around the fish without overlapping them; add the sliced chillies.
5. Bake in a **fan oven at 180 °C** for **about 20 to 35 minutes depending on weight** — *"Quello che vedete in foto pesava 650 g, io l'ho cotto per 30 minuti."*
6. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; doneness is the potatoes well browned and the fish lifting cleanly off the bone. House guidance (not the source's): probe through the shoulder and **pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**, carrying to the 63 °C (145 °F) USDA line. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Povar.ru — Сибас в духовке (Ju Lia, Russia)
**Inspired by**: https://m.povar.ru/recipes/sibas_v_duhovke-42081.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Ju Lia, Povar.ru (Russian recipe publication).
**Serves**: not stated (recipe uses 3 fish) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 20 min + 4 min sear · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 whole sea bass (*сибас*), gutted, fins removed, scaled, heads left on
- 1 tsp cumin
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1 small courgette
- 1 red onion
- 3 tomatoes
- Salt, pepper to taste

**Method**
1. Clean the fish, rub salt and pepper inside and out, scatter the cumin over and leave it while you prepare the vegetables.
2. Slice the tomatoes about 2 cm thick, the onion into slightly thinner rings, and the young courgette into rings; salt the courgette lightly.
3. Oil a baking tray and build a **bed of vegetables** — courgette first, then onion, then tomato.
4. **Sear the fish in a hot oiled pan 2 minutes per side** to form a crust, then lay the fish on the vegetable bed.
5. Bake **about 20 minutes at 180 °C**. Optionally scatter grated parmesan over the vegetables **5 minutes before the end**.
6. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; doneness is the crust and the 20-minute time at 180 °C. House guidance (not the source's): probe through the shoulder and **pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. Köket.se — Hel havsabborre (Maryam Madaresi, Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://www.koket.se/hel-havsaborre (fetched 2026-10-26). Maryam Madaresi, Köket.se (Swedish recipe publication; the fish is served with Persian *sabzi polo*, so the flavour base is Iranian while the publication and technique are Swedish).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 30 min (45 min total stated) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g whole sea bass (*havsabborre*)
- 1 tsp salt; 1 tsp black pepper; 1 tsp ground turmeric; 1 tsp garlic powder; 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tbsp fresh lime juice
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- **Filling**: 3 garlic cloves; 1 tbsp ground coriander; 4 lime slices

**Method**
1. Mix the spices with the lime juice and olive oil and **brush the paste over the whole fish, inside and out**.
2. Put the garlic, coriander and lime slices **in the belly**.
3. Heat the oven to **200 °C**.
4. Cook the fish for **about 30 minutes in the middle of the oven**.
5. Serve with sabzi polo.
6. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; doneness is the 30 minutes at 200 °C for a 500 g fish. House guidance (not the source's): probe through the shoulder and **pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Published pull / doneness signal | Tradition, oven setting and time |
|--------|--------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| The Mediterranean Dish (US, Greek-style) | **145 °F / 63 °C internal** | 400 °F (~205 °C), 5 min + 5–7 min, then broil 3–4 min |
| Fatto in Casa da Benedetta (Italy) | No temperature; 20–35 min by weight (650 g → 30 min) | 180 °C fan, on a potato bed |
| Povar.ru (Russia) | No temperature; 20 min | 180 °C, after a 2 min/side pan sear, on a vegetable bed |
| Köket.se (Sweden) | No temperature; about 30 min | 200 °C, 500 g fish, spiced oil paste |

Only one of four sources publishes a number, and it is the US/Greek-style one: **145 °F
(63 °C)**, which is exactly the cut's USDA floor. The other three are time-and-weight cooks
at **180–200 °C for 20–35 minutes**, and their spread is explained by weight and by what sits
under the fish: the Italian 650 g fish gets 30 minutes at 180 °C fan on a potato bed, the
Russian three-fish tray gets 20 minutes at 180 °C after a pan sear (a seared surface is
already partway cooked), and the Swedish 500 g fish takes 30 minutes at 200 °C. Mapping to
the engine: the observed **63 °C** endpoint matches `FISH_WELL_DONE` and the cut's
`usda_safe_c` exactly, so this is the one leaf in the cut where a source and the registry
agree without interpretation; the house-guidance 60 °C pull in the three numeric-silent
rows is what carries the fish up to 63 °C during the rest before serving.
`recommended_doneness` for this method: **`well_done`** — the only measured source states
145 °F, and none of the four describes pulling a whole roasted sea bass rare.

## What makes this method special

An oven heats a whole fish by air, and air is a poor conductor compared with a pan — which
is why roasting is forgiving where searing is not. The fish sits enclosed in a moving hot
airstream, the cavity traps steam, and the temperature difference between the shoulder and
the belly narrows as the cook proceeds, so a whole sea bass tolerates a twenty-minute window
where a fillet would be ruined in three. The skin behaves differently from the pan: with no
contact surface it dehydrates and tightens rather than crisping, and the US source's decision
to broil for the final 3–4 minutes is really an admission that a roasting oven cannot crisp
skin on its own. The Russian pan-sear-then-roast hybrid is the other way to solve the same
problem, front-loading the browning and letting the oven finish the centre gently. The
Italian potato bed is doing thermal work as well as flavour work: it lifts the fish off the
tray, moderates the bottom heat, and keeps the belly from overcooking.

## Summary

Roasting is the most forgiving way to cook a whole sea bass: 20 to 35 minutes at 180–205 °C,
depending mostly on the fish's weight and on whether anything sits under it. The oven's dry
air will not crisp the skin, so either broil for the last few minutes or sear the fish in a
pan first, as the Russian version does. A vegetable or potato bed is worth the trouble — it
lifts the fish off the tray and keeps the thin belly from overcooking before the shoulder is
done. Pull it as the centre reaches 145 °F / 63 °C, and probe behind the head rather than in
the belly.
