# Swordfish × Braise — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Braising cooks swordfish in a shallow bath of sauce at gentle heat — the opposite bet to the
sear: instead of protecting the outside with crust, the liquid protects the inside from
overshooting. The fetched sources span four traditions of the same idea — Sicilian *alla
ghiotta* (sear briefly, then finish covered in tomato-olive-caper sauce), Madeiran/Portuguese
*estufado* (fish stewed 20 minutes on a vegetable-tomato-wine base), Chinese red braising
(sweet-savoury soy-sugar glaze, here executed sous vide at a constant bath temperature) and
Moroccan *chermoula* stew (fish baked over a spiced vegetable bed). Swordfish's firm, steak-like
flesh is what makes the method viable at all: 20–35 minutes of moist heat would turn a flaking
white fish to mush but leaves swordfish in thick, moist flakes. The gentle window means the
cut's usual overcook risk is inverted — undercooking and timing are the issues, not chalkiness.
Only one of four sources states a temperature (60 °C / 140 °F).

## Styckesprofil

Bräsering tillagar svärdisk i en grund sås på mild värme — motsatsen till stekning: i
stället för att skydda uteytan med skorpa skyddar vätskan insidan från att överskjuda.
Källorna spänner fyra traditioner av samma idé: sicilianska *alla ghiotta* (korta steka,
sedan täckt i tomat-oliv-kaparsås), madeiransk/portugisisk *estufado* (fisk stuvad 20
minuter på tomat-vin-bas), kinesisk röd brasering (sötstark soss, här med konstant
sousvide-bad) och marockansk *chermoula*-gryta (fisk bakad över kryddad grönsakssäng).
Det täta köttet gör metoden möjlig: 20–35 minuter fukt vore trasigt för flaggfisk, men
svärdisk blir kvar i tjocka, saftiga flagor. Enda mätta källan anger 60 °C (140 °F).

## Source recipes

### 1. Caroline's Cooking — Sicilian Swordfish with Olives and Capers (Pesce Spada alla Ghiotta)
**Inspired by**: https://www.carolinescooking.com/swordfish-with-olives-and-capers-pesce-spada-alla-ghiotta/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Caroline's Cooking; classic Sicilian (also Calabrian) dish, recipe tagged Cuisine: Italian.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 25 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb swordfish in steaks, around 1 in / 2.5 cm thick
- 1 small red onion (Tropea if possible), or 2 small shallots; 1 stem celery; 1 clove garlic
- 2 medium tomatoes (about 7 oz / 200 g)
- 2½ oz pitted green olives (Castelvetrano); 1 oz capers, drained
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil; salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Peel and halve the onion, slice thinly; halve the celery lengthwise and slice; finely dice the garlic. Cut the swordfish into 2–3 in (5–7 cm) pieces; sprinkle lightly with salt and pepper.
2. Soften the onion and celery gently in the olive oil until translucent and sweet — the source insists on enough oil and no browning (about 10 minutes).
3. Add the garlic briefly, then the chopped tomatoes, olives and capers; keep the heat low.
4. Sear the fish first in a hot pan **1–2 minutes per side only** (the source's explicit rule: do not cook too long at this stage).
5. Return the fish to the sauce; keep the heat **low**, cover, and let it finish in the sauce — the source notes this second cook is "very low and gently moist thanks to being in the sauce and covered". Total cook 25 minutes.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; endpoint is time plus the sauce's gentle heat. House guidance (not the source's): probe a centre piece — **pull at 60–63 °C (140–145 °F)**. Serve with crusty bread. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Portuguese Cooking (Ana) — Peixe Espada Estufado de Madeira (Black Scabbard Stew of Madeira)
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: ~15 min · **Cook**: ~25 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- ¼ cup olive oil; 1 large onion, chopped medium; 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped; 1 bay leaf; generous pinch dried chili pepper (or 1 small whole, stem removed)
- 2 cups peeled, seeded, coarsely chopped tomatoes; ½ cup white wine
- 2 pounds swordfish, cut in chunky pieces
- Coarse salt; fresh ground white pepper; ½ cup finely chopped parsley; 2 tbsp butter

**Method**
1. Heat the oil in a medium stew pot; sweat the onion until soft and translucent, about 3 minutes.
2. Add the garlic, bay leaf and chili; when aromatic, stir in the tomatoes and bring to a simmer over medium-low heat.
3. Pour in the wine; simmer 1 more minute.
4. Season the fish with salt, pepper and parsley; add it to the pot.
5. Cover tightly and bring to a boil over medium heat; reduce to medium-low and stew **about 20 minutes**.
6. Remove from heat; stir the butter slowly into the stew. Serve with plenty of bread.
7. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is the 20-minute covered stew. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest chunk — **pull at 60–63 °C (140–145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Anova Culinary (Chef Nicole Poirier, San Francisco) — Sous Vide Red Braised Swordfish Steak
**Inspired by**: https://recipes.anovaculinary.com/recipe/sous-vide-red-braised-swordfish-steak (fetched 2026-10-25). Anova Culinary recipe platform; the page frames it explicitly as "Red braising is a traditional Chinese cooking method used for all proteins, but deep water fish steaks or filets take especially well to it."
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb swordfish steaks
- 2 tsp tamari/soy sauce (or coconut aminos); 2 tsp rice wine vinegar; 1 tsp brown sugar (or coconut palm sugar/erythritol); ½ tsp sesame oil
- 1 spring/green onion, chopped (white and green parts separated); 1 tbsp grated or finely minced ginger
- Optional: 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds; 1 tsp chili sauce (Sriracha); cilantro sprigs; lime wedges

**Method**
1. Set the Anova precision cooker to **140 °F (60 °C)** — the source's stated bath temperature.
2. Combine the fish, tamari, rice wine vinegar, sugar, sesame oil, white parts of the onion and minced ginger in a zip-locking bag.
3. Use the water-displacement method to expel the air and seal.
4. Place the bag in the water bath; set the timer for **30 minutes**.
5. **Pull at 60 °C (140 °F)** — bath temperature is the pull temperature in sous vide; the bath holds the fish at exactly that doneness.
6. Remove from the bath and tip onto the serving plate with the bag juices; garnish with sesame seeds, green onion, chili sauce, cilantro and a spritz of lime. Final serving temperature: **60 °C / 140 °F** (bath temperature, stated).

### 4. The Delicious Crescent (Roxana Begum PhD, RD) — Moroccan Fish Stew With Chermoula
**Inspired by**: https://www.thedeliciouscrescent.com/moroccan-chermoula-fish-stew/ (fetched 2026-10-25). The Delicious Crescent; author states she learned the tagine method from her Moroccan neighbour. **Method lead flagged**: the source names firm white fish (cod, haddock, halibut, "or similar") and does not name swordfish; retained as a flagged *chermoula*-stew method lead for firm fish, not as a cut recipe.
**Serves**: 5 · **Prep**: 20 min (+ optional 1 h marinating) · **Cook**: 35 min · **Rest**: brief

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb firm white fish, 2-inch pieces (source: cod, haddock, halibut or similar)
- **Chermoula**: 2 tsp paprika; 2 tsp ground ginger; 2 tsp ground cumin; 1½ tsp crushed red pepper flakes (or 1 tsp cayenne); 1¼ tsp turmeric; ¾ tsp coarse salt; 4 cloves garlic; ⅓ cup extra-virgin olive oil; juice of ½ lemon + lemon slices; 2 tbsp tomato paste; 3 tbsp parsley; 3 tbsp cilantro; 1 medium onion, chopped; ½ cup water
- **Stew**: ½ lb ripe tomatoes, sliced; ¾ lb yellow potatoes, 1-in pieces; ¾ lb zucchini, 1-in pieces; 1 red bell pepper, diced; ½ cup black olives; ½ cup sun-dried tomatoes; cilantro, to garnish

**Method**
1. Blend the chermoula ingredients until smooth; toss the fish with one-quarter of it and set aside (optionally marinate 1 h–overnight; bring to room temperature 30 minutes).
2. Thin the remaining chermoula with the water into a pourable paste.
3. Layer potatoes, tomatoes, zucchini and bell pepper in a wide oven-safe pot; drizzle with the chermoula paste. Cover and simmer over low-medium heat 10–15 minutes until the vegetables just soften.
4. Preheat the oven to **425 °F (220 °C)**. Uncover; arrange the fish over the vegetables; top with lemon slices, olives and sun-dried tomatoes.
5. Bake **uncovered 10–15 minutes**, until the fish is opaque and flakes easily with a fork and the juices bubble at the edges; broil 1–2 minutes if the tops need colour.
6. Rest briefly; garnish with cilantro. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is opacity plus flaking. House guidance (not the source's): probe a centre piece — **pull at 60–63 °C (140–145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caroline's Cooking (Sicilian/Italian) | Italian *alla ghiotta* | 1–2 min sear + covered low-heat finish; total 25 min | Not stated |
| Portuguese Cooking (Madeira) | Portuguese *estufado* | 20-minute covered stew | Not stated |
| Anova (Chinese red braise, US platform) | Chinese red-braise tradition | Constant bath, 30 min | **60 °C (140 °F)** stated |
| The Delicious Crescent (Moroccan) | Moroccan *chermoula* stew | Bake 10–15 min at 220 °C until opaque and flaky | Not stated |

Only the red-braise source publishes a number: a constant **60 °C (140 °F)** — the single
observed value for this leaf, sitting at the bottom edge of `FISH_WELL_DONE` (60–66 °C). The
other three sources stop at time plus texture ("covered and low", "about 20 minutes",
"opaque and flaky"), all of which describe cooked-through fish. Mapped to the cut file,
the whole observed picture is `well_done` at ~60 °C — notably gentler than a 63 °C seared
well-done, because a moist-heat medium carries heat evenly with no crust driving the centre
higher. The variation across sources is about *method of finishing* (stovetop covered, oven
baked, water bath), not about doneness disagreement — everyone cooks the fish through.
`recommended_doneness` for the method: **`well_done`**, the only measured band and the plain
meaning of every source's endpoint.

## What makes this method special

Braising removes the failure mode that defines every dry-heat method for this cut: the
liquid medium (tomato-olive sauce, stew liquor, chermoula juices, a 60 °C water bath) holds
the flesh at a ceiling it cannot exceed, so the centre reaches doneness without any part of
the steak seeing the 200 °C-plus surfaces that make crust. Swordfish can absorb the long,
gentle cook because its muscle segments are thick and bound firmly — where a flaking white
fish would disintegrate at 20–35 minutes of simmering, swordfish stays in moist, steak-like
flakes, and its moderate fat keeps the lean interior from drying even as the surface sits in
sauce. The chemistry is different from searing too: there is essentially no Maillard browning
on the submerged flesh, so flavour comes from the sauce diffusing in rather than crust
diffusing out — which is why every tradition here pairs the fish with a strongly seasoned
braise base. The trade is texture identity: braise gives up the firm, chewy steak bite that
pan and grill methods are chosen for.

## Summary

Braising cooks swordfish slowly in sauce — 20–35 minutes of gentle moist heat — and the dense,
meaty flesh is what lets it hold together through a cook that would destroy a flaking white
fish. It's a good match when you want the fish infused with a strongly flavoured base
(Sicilian tomato-olive, Portuguese wine-tomato, Moroccan chermoula) rather than a crust of
its own; the trade is giving up the seared surface and the firm steak bite. All four sources
cook the fish through rather than to a blush, and the one that measures does so at a gentle
60 °C. Pick this method for flavour and forgiveness, not for char.
