# Swordfish × Charcoal Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

The charcoal-specific variant of grilling: swordfish over a settled coal bed, where radiant
heat from glowing embers combines with airborne smoke compounds that no gas burner produces.
Two forms dominate the fetched sources — Turkish *izgara* kebabs on skewers held 10–13 cm
(4–5 in) above the coals for ~8 minutes, and whole steaks seared 4–7 minutes per side over a
two-zone coal bed (US kettle setups) or a screaming-hot grate (German *Glut* technique).
Swordfish's dense, non-flaking flesh is what makes the skewer form possible: the cubes stay
on the metal and take the frequent turning without shedding. The charcoal sources publish
more temperature data than the general-grill set: 130–135 °F (54–57 °C) from the US kettle
recipe, 55–58 °C from the German pro cook, 145 °F (63 °C) from the Canadian retailer — the
same medium/well-done split seen across the cut.

## Source recipes

### 1. Yummy Istanbul — Izgara Kılıç Kebabı (Turkish Char-Grilled Swordfish Shish Kebab)
**Inspired by**: https://yummyistanbul.com/turkish-swordfish-shish-kebab-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Yummy Istanbul food-tours recipe library (Istanbul).
**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: ~15 min · **Cook**: ~8 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 900 g swordfish, cut into 1-inch cubes
- **Marinade**: 1 small onion, grated; 3 garlic cloves, minced; ⅓ cup lemon juice; 2 tsp paprika; ½ cup olive oil; 4 fresh or dried bay leaves, crumbled; salt and black pepper
- 2 tbsp coarsely chopped fresh parsley

**Method**
1. Whisk the marinade ingredients; season with salt and pepper. Place the fish in a nonreactive dish, pour over the marinade and turn to coat. Cover and refrigerate **about 2 hours**.
2. Prepare a charcoal grill with the rack set **4–5 inches (10–13 cm) above the coals**.
3. Thread the cubes onto 4 skewers. Grill **about 8 minutes**, turning frequently and gently brushing with marinade from the dish.
4. Pull the cubes from the skewers, sprinkle with parsley, serve at once.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is time plus the brush-and-turn routine over charcoal. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre of the largest cube — **pull at 54–57 °C (130–135 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**. The source's own warning: do not over-marinate (lemon starts breaking the fish down; ~2 hours, never overnight).

### 2. SnS Grills — Best Swordfish Recipe: Grilled Swordfish with Lemon Caper Butter Sauce
**Inspired by**: https://snsgrills.com/pages/swordfish (fetched 2026-10-25). SnS Grills (US charcoal-grill manufacturer) recipe site; Slow 'N Sear charcoal-kettle method.
**Serves**: not stated (steaks by count) · **Prep**: dry brine 1 h–1 day · **Cook**: ~8–10 min · **Rest**: a few minutes

**Ingredients**
- Swordfish steaks, at least 1 in. thick
- ½ tsp kosher salt per pound (dry brine); freshly cracked black pepper; olive oil, lightly brushed
- **Lemon caper sauce**: 1 shallot finely diced; ½ cup white wine; ½ cup cream; 2 tbsp Dijon mustard; 2 tbsp capers; juice of ½ lemon; 4 tbsp cold unsalted butter

**Method**
1. Dry-brine the steaks (kosher salt, ½ tsp per pound) **one hour to one day ahead**; pat very dry, brush with olive oil, crack pepper over them.
2. Light **half a chimney of coals (~40 briquettes or lump)** until about 70% ashed over; add to the Slow 'N Sear, add the drip pan and grate, close the lid 5–10 minutes until very hot.
3. Place the swordfish **over direct heat above the coals; cook 4 minutes**, until the fish releases from the grate. Flip and cook **4 minutes more**.
4. Move to the indirect side and take an internal temperature: **Pull at 130–135 °F (54–57 °C)** — the source's stated window ("anywhere from 130–135 degrees before letting them rest"). If not there, finish indirect with the lid cracked until it is.
5. Rest a few minutes. Meanwhile reduce the sauce ingredients on the grill and mount with butter. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (rest carries the fish a few degrees further).

### 3. North Coast Seafoods — Simple Grilled Swordfish Recipe
**Inspired by**: https://www.northcoastseafoods.com/blogs/in-the-kitchen/simple-grilled-swordfish-recipe (fetched 2026-10-25). North Coast Seafoods (Canadian seafood retailer) cooking guide.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: ~15 min (incl. 20 min grill preheat) · **Cook**: ~8–9 min · **Rest**: carryover noted

**Ingredients**
- Swordfish steaks, 1–1.5 in. thick
- Neutral high-smoke-point oil (canola, avocado or grapeseed) — brushed on the fish, not the grill
- Salt and seasoning of choice (marinade optional: soy sauce, olive oil, lemon juice, garlic, herbs)

**Method**
1. Thaw steaks to room temperature for even cooking; pat dry.
2. Preheat the grill **20 minutes to medium-high**; for a charcoal grill, use a half chimney of lit coals.
3. Brush oil directly onto the fish (never the grates — prevents sticking and flare-ups).
4. Grill **~2 minutes**, rotate a quarter turn for crosshatch marks, **2 minutes more**, then flip and cook **another 4–5 minutes** depending on thickness.
5. Probe the centre: the source states the thermometer should read **about 145 °F (63 °C)**; it also notes carryover will raise the temperature a further 10–15 °F off the heat. **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)** per source. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (carryover raises it after removal).

### 4. Thomas Sixt — Schwertfischfilet grillen: einfach und saftig
**Inspired by**: https://thomassixt.de/rezept/schwertfischfilet-grillen/ (fetched 2026-10-25). German cooking-school site (chef Thomas Sixt, "Die Kochschule ohne Rezepte").
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: optional hold at 100 °C oven

**Ingredients**
- 2 swordfish fillets (Schwertfischfilets)
- 4 tbsp corn-germ oil (Maiskeimöl) — high smoke point
- 4 pinches salt; 4 pinches pepper; 2 tsp olive oil
- Optional sides: braised vegetables, almond pesto

**Method**
1. Pat the fillets completely dry — wet fish steams instead of browning.
2. Heat the grill pan (or the garden grill grate over the embers — *die Glut*) until extremely hot; add the corn-germ oil so it runs over the ridges. The source insists on a very hot, even smoking surface.
3. Lay the dry fillets on; press lightly onto the surface. Grill **1–3 minutes per side** depending on thickness (the pan instructions: 2–3 min, turn, 2 min more).
4. Probe the centre: **Kerntemperatur 55–58 °C** — the source's stated window for a juicy result, checked with a thermometer.
5. Rest on a warm plate, optionally 5–10 minutes in a 100 °C oven to finish evenly ("garziehen lassen"). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

Three of the four charcoal sources publish numbers: **54 °C to 63 °C** full observed pull
range, clustering at 54–58 °C (the two professional/technique sources, SnS and Sixt) with
the Canadian retailer at 63 °C. Mapped to the cut file, the cluster sits in

two of three temperature-stating sources — while the North American 63 °C well-done

## What makes this method special

Charcoal changes grilling in two ways the physics make real: the coal bed emits broad-spectrum
radiant heat that is harder for the steak's surface to reflect than a gas burner's convective
flow, and the dripping fat pyrolyses into phenolic smoke compounds that deposit on the moist
surface of the fish in the first minutes. Swordfish tolerates both because its flesh is dense
and moderately fatty — the surface lacquers rather than scorches, and the interior stays cool
through the short direct phase. The two-zone arrangement the US sources use (sear direct,
finish indirect) exists precisely because this cut has no rendered fat to buffer a hot zone:
once the crust is set, moving off the coals buys the few minutes the centre needs to reach
54–58 °C without pushing past 60 °C into dryness. Skewered cubes (the Turkish form) multiply
the exposed surface area and shorten every exposure to seconds per turn, which is why that
tradition needs no thermometer at all.

## Summary

Cooking swordfish over live charcoal adds what a gas grill can't: real radiant heat from the
coals and smoke that lands on the fish while the crust forms. The cut is well suited — its
firm, meaty flesh survives skewers, frequent turning and a hard sear, which is exactly why
the Turkish kebab tradition and the American two-zone kettle method both work on it. The
measured sources agree the centre should come off at 54–58 °C for juice, with one Canadian
voice quoting the safer 63 °C line. It rewards attention: the direct phase is short, and the
difference between a great and a ruined swordfish here is about ten degrees and two minutes.
