# Swordfish × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling is swordfish's most international method: the steak's dense, non-flaking flesh
lifts across grates and skewers without breaking, so it takes direct radiant heat and
heavy char marks that would destroy a laminar white fish. Retail steaks (2–3 cm / 1–1½ in)
grill in 3–7 minutes per side over high heat; the whole risk window is the transition from
translucent centre to chalky dryness, which happens fast once the core passes ~60 °C. The
fetched sources split between two endpoints: the thermometer-guided American approach pulls
at 130 °F (54 °C) and rests to ~140 °F, while the Trinidadian and Saveur kebab sources cook
by char and opacity ("cooked through", 3–5 min per side) with no probe at all. Kebab-style
grilling on chunks (India, Caribbean, Turkey) shortens the exposure per piece; whole steaks
(Germany, US) need the grate two-zone or move-to-indirect finish.

## Source recipes

### 1. Saveur — Grilled Swordfish Kebabs (machli kebab)
**Inspired by**: https://www.saveur.com/grilled-swordfish-kebabs-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Saveur; recipe by chef Meherwan Irani (Chai Pani Restaurant Group), featured in "6 Magnificent Indian Grilling Recipes You Can Pull Off Indoors or Outdoors."
**Serves**: not stated (2 lb fish, ten 8-inch skewers) · **Prep**: ~10 min + 30 min skewer soak · **Cook**: 4–6 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 lb swordfish steaks (or another firm white fish), skinned and cut into 1½-in. chunks
- **Marinade**: ¼ cup fresh lime juice; 1 tsp sugar; ½ tsp kosher salt; pinch roasted ground cumin; 20 fresh mint leaves; 2 medium garlic cloves; 1 cup grapeseed or vegetable oil
- Garnish: ¼ cup coarsely chopped dill; ground sumac; extra-virgin olive oil

**Method**
1. Blend the lime juice, sugar, salt, cumin, mint and garlic; with the machine on low, drizzle in the oil. Transfer ⅓ cup of the marinade to a small bowl, cover and refrigerate; reserve the rest.
2. Season the fish chunks lightly with salt, toss with the remaining marinade, cover loosely and refrigerate **at least 10 minutes — no longer than 30**, or the lime juice begins to "cook" the fish.
3. Soak ten 8-inch bamboo skewers in cold water for 30 minutes.
4. Heat a charcoal or gas grill to high (**about 450 °F / 230 °C**). Thread 3–4 chunks per skewer, leaving a slight gap between pieces.
5. Grill with the lid open until lightly charred on one side, **2–3 minutes**; flip and cook **2–3 minutes more** until lightly charred and cooked through.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is char plus opacity. House guidance (not the source's): insert an instant-read or MEATER probe into the centre of the thickest chunk. **Pull at 54–57 °C (130–135 °F)** for a moist kebab.
7. Drizzle the reserved marinade over the skewers; garnish with dill, sumac and olive oil. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Girl With The Iron Cast — Marinated Grilled Swordfish with Olive Chimichurri
**Inspired by**: https://www.girlwiththeironcast.com/marinated-grilled-swordfish-with-olive-chimichurri/ (fetched 2026-10-25). American food blog "Girl With The Iron Cast" (Stephanie); author learned the method at a Long Island fish market.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 15 min marinating · **Cook**: ~10 min · **Rest**: 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 swordfish steaks, 1–1½ in. thick
- 2 tsp Dijon mustard; 1 tbsp lemon juice; 2 tsp olive oil; 2 garlic cloves (minced); 1 tsp chopped parsley; ¼ tsp kosher salt; ⅛ tsp black pepper; 1 whole lemon, halved
- **Olive chimichurri**: 4 pitted green olives (crushed, roughly chopped); 1 garlic clove (minced); 1 Fresno pepper (deseeded, minced); 2 tbsp parsley; 2 tbsp cilantro; 2 tsp red wine vinegar; ½ tsp lemon zest; ¼ cup olive oil; ¼ tsp salt; ⅛ tsp pepper

**Method**
1. Combine the mustard, lemon juice, olive oil, garlic, parsley, salt and pepper; marinate the steaks for **15 minutes only** — longer and the acid degrades the texture.
2. Heat the grill to **400 °F (205 °C)**. Place the steaks and the halved lemons on the grates.
3. Cook about **10 minutes, flipping halfway**; the fish should be firm yet flaky and no longer translucent. Check with an instant-read thermometer.
4. **Pull at 130 °F (54 °C)** — the source's stated aim for medium; its published bands are 130 °F (54 °C) medium, 135 °F (57 °C) medium-well, 140–145 °F (60–63 °C) well-done.
5. Rest ~10 minutes; carryover raises it to about **140 °F (60 °C)** for serving. Final serving temperature: **60 °C / 140 °F** (stated).

### 3. Steven Raichlen, Barbecue Bible — Trinidadian Grilled Swordfish
**Inspired by**: https://barbecuebible.com/recipe/trinidadian-grilled-swordfish/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Steven Raichlen's Barbecue Bible (named cookbook/author); Caribbean (Trinidad) tradition, "Method: Direct grilling".
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2–4 h marinating (+ bakes dough rising) · **Cook**: 6–10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 swordfish steaks, each 6–8 oz, about ¾ in. thick
- **Green seasoning**: ½ bunch chives/scallions; 2 shallots (or ½ small onion); 2 garlic cloves; 1 rib celery; ¼ cup cilantro; ¼ cup flat-leaf parsley; 2 tbsp mint; 2 tsp thyme (or 1 tsp dried); ½ scotch bonnet chile (seeded); ¾ cup water; ¼ cup lime juice; 2 tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper
- **Bakes (fried dough)**: 1 tbsp active dry yeast; 1 tbsp sugar; 1¼ cups warm water; 2½ cups flour; 2 tsp baking powder; 1¼ tsp salt; 2–3 tbsp vegetable oil
- Mango salsa and Caribbean hot sauce, to serve

**Method**
1. Rinse and blot the steaks dry; place in a nonreactive dish.
2. Puree the green-seasoning ingredients; pour over the steaks, turn to coat, cover and marinate in the refrigerator **2–4 hours**.
3. Make the bakes dough, let rise 1–2 hours, roll into four 6–7 in. circles.
4. Preheat the grill to medium-high; oil the grate. Grill the bakes 2–4 minutes per side; keep warm.
5. Blot the steaks dry, brush lightly with oil, season with salt and pepper; arrange on the hot grate.
6. Grill **3–5 minutes per side** until cooked through in the centre when pierced with a knife; rotate 90° after 2 minutes per side for crosshatch marks.
7. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is time plus knife-pierce. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre of a steak — **pull at 57–63 °C (135–145 °F)** for "cooked through". Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. Weber Grill Original (DE) — Schwertfisch-Offensive mit Tomaten-Paprika-Sauce und Oliven
**Inspired by**: https://www.weber.com/DE/de/rezepte/fisch-schalentiere/schwertfisch-offensive-mit-tomaten-paprika-sauce-und-oliven/weber-58766.html (fetched 2026-10-25). Weber Grill Original German recipe site.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 30 min · **Cook**: 15–19 min grill · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 swordfish steaks (180–220 g each, 2.5 cm thick)
- 125 ml dry white wine; 1 can (400 g) peeled Italian plum tomatoes; 125 g (125 ml) Kalamata olives, pitted and halved; 4 tbsp chopped parsley; 1½ tbsp red-wine vinegar; 1 tsp dried oregano; ¼ tsp chili flakes; 1 red bell pepper; 1 large garlic clove; olive oil; coarse sea salt; black pepper

**Method**
1. Prepare the grill for **direct high heat (230–290 °C)**.
2. Char the bell pepper over direct high heat with the lid closed, 10–12 minutes, until blackened and blistered; steam in a covered bowl 10 minutes, peel, seed and slice.
3. In a pan, soften the garlic and chili flakes in 1 tbsp oil ~1 minute; add tomatoes, wine and oregano, boil, then simmer 5 minutes. Stir in the pepper strips, olives, vinegar and seasonings; reduce uncovered 10–12 minutes into a sauce.
4. Brush the steaks generously with oil; season evenly with ¾ tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper.
5. Grill over direct high heat with the lid closed **5–7 minutes, turning once**, until at the core they are **no longer glassy but still juicy** ("im Kern nicht mehr glasig, aber noch saftig").
6. The source publishes no core temperature; its doneness test is the translucent-to-opaque transition at the centre. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **pull at 54–57 °C (130–135 °F)** matches "no longer glassy, still juicy". Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Saveur (India) | Indian kebab | Char per side 2–3 min + 2–3 min, "cooked through" | Not stated |
| Girl With The Iron Cast (US) | American thermometer-guided | Firm yet flaky, no longer translucent; instant-read checked | **130 °F (54 °C)** pull, bands to 140–145 °F (60–63 °C); serves ~140 °F (60 °C) |
| Raichlen (Trinidad/Caribbean) | Caribbean | 3–5 min per side, knife-pierce "cooked through" | Not stated |
| Weber DE (Germany) | German | 5–7 min, "no longer glassy but still juicy" at the core | Not stated |

Only one of the four sources (the American) publishes numbers: a **54–63 °C (130–145 °F)**
pull window with serving at ~60 °C after rest. The other three stop at time plus a visual or
tactile endpoint, so the full observed numerical range across this leaf is **54–63 °C** —
spanning `FISH_MEDIUM` (54 °C target) to `FISH_WELL_DONE` (63 °C), with the practice-based
"cooked through" endpoints of the Caribbean and Indian sources mapping to `well_done` by
convention rather than measurement. The variation is driven by endpoint convention, not by
disagreement about the fish: probe-guided sources pull early and let carryover finish the
job; time-guided sources cook until opacity and never see a number. `recommended_doneness`
for the method stays **`well_done`** — three of four sources cook to "cooked through", and
the US source's 54–57 °C medium band is recorded as a strong tradition sitting outside it.

## What makes this method special

Direct radiant heat from below browns the steak's flat cut faces fast (the Maillard crust
that gives grilled swordfish its identity) while the interior is still cool, something a
liquid medium can never do. Swordfish can take it because its muscle fibres are bound in
coarse, meaty segments rather than the fragile sheets of flaking fish — the steak survives
being lifted, turned and skewered, and the moderate intramuscular fat bastes the exposed
surface during the short cook. The physics of the window is unforgiving: 2–3 cm of flesh
gives only a few minutes between "translucent centre" and "chalky", which is why every
fetched source either works in a tight 3–7 minutes-per-side band or leans on a thermometer.
Charcoal adds volatile smoke phenols on top of the crust; gas gives cleaner heat and more
even gradients — the difference the sources taste but rarely measure.

## Summary

Grilling turns a swordfish steak into something closer to a barbecued chop: high direct
heat lays down a charred crust in a few minutes per side while the dense, non-flaking flesh
holds together on the grate or the skewer. It suits this cut extremely well — the fish's
firmness is exactly what makes it one of the few fish that grills like meat — but the margin
is thin, because the same few minutes that give you char can push the centre from juicy to
chalky. The sources split between probe-guided pulling at 54 °C and cooking by sight until
"cooked through". Choose this method when you want crust and smoke, and keep a thermometer
handy if you want it moist.
