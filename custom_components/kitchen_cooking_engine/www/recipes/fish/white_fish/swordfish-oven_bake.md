# Swordfish × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven baking surrounds the steak with air instead of a pan surface or a flame: the fetched
sources use three distinct configurations — an all-in-one sheet-pan bake with a breadcrumb
crust (Italian), a two-stage sear-then-roast (Russian), a topping-glazed fast bake under a
toaster oven's top heat (Japanese-style), and a saucy covered bake where the fish poaches in
its own tomato-onion base (Greek *plaki*). All land in the same 15–25 minute window at
204–220 °C (400–425 °F), except the Russian two-stage which roasts only 10 minutes after a
2-minute-per-side sear. The method's virtue for swordfish is evenness: no side is closer to
the heat than the centre by much, so the dense flesh cooks uniformly — its risk is the
opposite of the grill's, a slow approach to opacity with no crust to distract the cook from
overcooking. Only one of four sources publishes an internal temperature.

## Source recipes

### 1. Lidia — Baked Swordfish (Lidia Bastianich)
**Inspired by**: https://lidiasitaly.com/recipes/baked-swordfish-2/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Lidia Bastianich, *Lidia's Commonsense Italian Cooking* (named chef/cookbook).
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: ~15 min · **Cook**: ~25 min (10 min tomatoes + 15 min fish) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Six ¾-inch-thick boneless, skinless swordfish fillets, about 5 oz each
- ½ cup dried breadcrumbs; ¼ cup grated Grana Padano; 6 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil; 2 tbsp chopped fresh basil; ½ tsp kosher salt
- 6 medium tomatoes, halved; ½ cup white wine; 2 tbsp white-wine vinegar; ¼ tsp crushed red-pepper flakes; ½ cup drained tiny capers in brine; 2 tbsp chopped parsley

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to **400 °F (204 °C)**. Toss the breadcrumbs with the cheese, 2 tbsp olive oil and the basil. Season the fish with salt and rub with 2 tbsp oil.
2. Arrange the tomatoes cut-side up on a rimmed sheet pan; drizzle with 2 tbsp oil. Pour the wine, vinegar and red-pepper flakes around them. Press three-quarters of the crumb mix onto the tomatoes.
3. Bake the tomatoes on the middle rack **10 minutes**, until they soften.
4. Toss the fish with the remaining crumb mixture; scatter the capers and parsley into the pan juices and fit the swordfish into the gaps.
5. Bake **about 15 minutes**, until the fish is cooked through and the crumbs on fish and tomatoes are browned and crisp.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; its doneness language is "cooked through" plus browned crust, with the explicit warning not to overcook (time varies with thickness). House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre of the thickest fillet — **pull at 60–63 °C (140–145 °F)** for opaque-but-moist. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Tokyo Recipes by Nadia (Emi Matsuyama) — Запечённая рыба-меч с мисо, майонезом и сыром (Baked Swordfish with Miso, Mayonnaise and Cheese)
**Inspired by**: https://tokyo-recipes.com/ru/user/113/recipe/160 (fetched 2026-10-25). Tokyo Recipes by Nadia; author Emi Matsuyama describes Korean roots and Japanese-family home cooking; Japanese-style *yakumi* topping bake.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 8–10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 swordfish steaks
- Salt, sake and black pepper, to taste
- **Topping (A)**: 3 tbsp mayonnaise; 1 tbsp miso; 2 tsp sugar; ½ cup grated pizza cheese (or mozzarella); coarse black pepper, optional

**Method**
1. Pat the steaks dry; rub lightly with salt, pepper and sake.
2. Mix the mayonnaise, miso and sugar to a paste.
3. Line the toaster-oven tray with foil. Lay out the steaks and cover each with a quarter of the miso-mayonnaise; distribute the grated cheese over the top.
4. Bake in the toaster oven at **425 °F (220 °C) for 8–10 minutes**, until golden.
5. Serve hot with rice. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the golden topping plus time. House guidance (not the source's): probe under the topping — **pull at 57–60 °C (135–140 °F)** (the source notes the same treatment suits delicate white fish such as cod or halibut). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Povar.ru (Marina Nemets) — Меч-рыба в духовке (Swordfish in the Oven)
**Inspired by**: https://m.povar.ru/recipes/mech-ryba_v_duhovke-56577.html (fetched 2026-10-25). Russian recipe platform Повар.ру; unattributed house recipe by Marina Nemets.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: ~15 min · **Cook**: ~14 min (2 × 2 min sear + 10 min oven) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 800–900 g swordfish steaks
- **Rub**: 1 tsp garlic powder; 1 tbsp dried basil; 1 tsp fennel seeds; pinch red pepper flakes; salt and black pepper
- **Sauce**: 45 g pitted olives; 200 g tomatoes; 10 g fresh basil; 3 garlic cloves; pinch chili; 2 tbsp olive oil; juice of ½ lemon

**Method**
1. Mix the rub ingredients and coat the fish.
2. Blend the sauce ingredients (olives, tomatoes, basil, garlic, chili, oil, lemon); season.
3. Sear the marinated steaks in a pan **2 minutes per side**.
4. Transfer to a preheated oven for **10 minutes** — the source's stated two-stage method ("сначала обжаривают на сковороде, а потом запекают в духовке").
5. Serve with the sauce. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is the timed roast. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **pull at 60–63 °C (140–145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. The Mediterranean Dish (Suzy Karadsheh) — Greek Fish with Tomatoes and Onions (Psari Plaki)
**Inspired by**: https://www.themediterraneandish.com/baked-greek-fish-psari-plaki/ (fetched 2026-10-25). The Mediterranean Dish (US-published Greek-food specialist). **Method lead flagged**: the source names halibut or cod as its fish and does not name swordfish; the *plaki* genre is documented here for firm fish generally and applied to this cut as a flagged lead, not as a swordfish recipe.
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: ~15 min · **Cook**: 25 min total (10 min sauce + 10–15 min bake) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Firm white fish fillets (source: halibut or cod; swordfish-equivalent thickness works for the genre)
- 1 large onion, sliced; 28 oz can whole tomatoes; 4 garlic cloves; 3 tbsp Greek extra-virgin olive oil, plus more
- 1 tsp dried oregano; 1 tsp sweet paprika; ½ tsp ground cumin; kosher salt and black pepper; 1 lemon (juice + wedges); parsley to finish

**Method**
1. Cook the onions in olive oil in an oven-safe pan over medium heat ~5 minutes until collapsed and coloured; season with salt.
2. Add garlic and tomatoes; season with oregano, paprika, cumin, salt and pepper. Break up the tomatoes; simmer ~10 minutes until thickened.
3. Toss the fish with salt, pepper and the juice of 1 lemon; nestle the fillets into the sauce and spoon sauce over them.
4. Bake at **400 °F (204 °C) for 10–15 minutes**, until the flesh is white and flakes easily.
5. **Pull at 140–145 °F (60–63 °C)** — the source states "the internal temperature of cooked fish should be around 140 °F to 145 °F". Finish with parsley and more lemon. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## What makes this method special

Baking trades the grill's steep surface-to-centre gradient for a gentle, enveloping one: the
air heats every face of the steak nearly equally, so the flesh cooks uniformly from the edge
inward with almost no crust. For swordfish's dense, low-connective-tissue flesh this means
even doneness edge-to-centre — but it also means there is no browning to slow the cook or
signal progress, so the endpoint must come from time, opacity or a probe. The two-stage
Russian version compensates by searing first (Maillard crust) and letting the oven finish the
centre gently; the Greek *plaki* version turns the oven's evenness into a sauce braise, the
fish poaching in tomato-onion liquid while the top bakes. The mayonnaise-miso glaze of the
Japanese-style bake serves the same physics from the other side: an insulating, fat-rich
layer that browns on top while keeping the centre from drying during the short hot bake.

## Summary

Baking cooks a swordfish steak evenly in surround heat — 15–25 minutes at around 200–220 °C
in most kitchens — and suits the cut's dense flesh, which stays uniform edge-to-centre and
never falls apart. What you gain is consistency and hands-off timing; what you give up is
crust and the quick caramelised surface that pan or charcoal methods deliver, which is why
every source here relies on time or opacity rather than a sear to judge doneness. The one
measured source puts the finish at 60–63 °C, comfortably at the well-done end of this cut's
range. Choose the oven when you want even, forgiving results or a sauce-baked dish, and a
probe if you want it moister than "cooked through".
