# Bison Steak × Charcoal Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Charcoal is the traditional fire for bison steak in the cut's home continent, and the fetched
sources treat the lean meat as a fire-management problem rather than a timing problem. The
shared mechanics are all about heat control: Sayersbrook builds a two-zone kettle (chimney-
lit natural charcoal plus a hardwood log or chips for smoke, sear uncovered, then lid on to
smoke and tame flare-ups), Wild Idea piles coals high until white-ashed, spreads them and
drops the grate low and close, and the German community recipe simply says the steaks go on
the *Holzkohlegrill* 3–5 minutes per side with 2–3 turns and a tong test. Only one of the
four sources publishes a number for the meat, and it is a breeder's tip quoted by a Swedish
food writer: **52 °C internal** — the lowest measured endpoint anywhere in this cut's corpus.
The German source's doneness words are "rare to at most medium-rare", and it carries the
corpus's recurring warning in its own text: never cook bison too long at too low a
temperature, or it takes on a liver-like taste (*leberartiger Geschmack*). The lean profile
that makes bison punish overcooking also helps on live fire: as Sayersbrook notes, less
dripping fat means fewer flare-ups than a beef rib steak.

## Source recipes

### 1. DasKochrezept.de — Gegrilltes Bison-Filet-Steak in süß-scharfer Bier-Chili-Marinade (user msuchy, Germany)
**Inspired by**: https://www.daskochrezept.de/rezepte/gegrilltes-bison-filet-steak-suess-scharfer-bier-chili-marinade (fetched 2026-10-26). DasKochrezept.de (German community recipe database), by user msuchy, published 2022-10-01, rated 4.8/5 by 163. German community-cooking tradition. **Integrity note**: this page was recorded twice in the pre-ToR leaves under both `grill` and `pan_sear` with two different ingredient lists and two different invented pull temperatures (57–60 °C vs 52–54 °C); this record replaces both — the fetched page publishes **no internal temperature at all**, and the two retired numbers are retracted in the Temperature consensus below.
**Serves**: 4 (1 portion column shown by the page; the method cuts 4 steaks) · **Prep**: 55 min incl. marinating · **Cook**: 6–10 min · **Rest**: 5 min (overnight marination)

**Ingredients**
- 4 bison fillet steaks (*Filetsteaks*, 250 g each)
- 350 ml dark beer (*Bier dunkel, alt*)
- 100 g brown sugar; 2 limes; 6 garlic cloves; ½ red onion
- 3 tsp Worcestershire sauce; 3 tsp coarse mustard; 2 tsp rapeseed oil; 2 tsp ginger
- 2 tsp Tabasco; 1 heaped tsp chili powder

**Method**
1. Trim the bison fillet if necessary (better done by the butcher); wash and pat dry. Cut 4 slices of 250 g each.
2. Finely chop the garlic and onion, grate the ginger, juice the limes. In a large bowl mix the beer, sugar, lime juice, onions, garlic, Worcestershire sauce, mustard, oil, ginger and the pepper sauce until the marinade is creamy. Season; add chili powder to taste.
3. Marinate the steaks in the liquid **overnight** in the fridge (the source's tip: freezer bag, air pressed out, kneaded so the marinade reaches all sides).
4. The next day, take the bison out **at least 3 hours** before grilling so it reaches room temperature — the source states this is what makes it properly tender.
5. Best done on the **charcoal grill (Holzkohlegrill): 3–5 minutes per side** depending on thickness, turning 2–3 times in between, testing doneness with tongs. The source's own doneness rule: bison should always be eaten **"rare" to at most "medium-rare"**, and never cooked too long at too low a temperature, "as it otherwise takes on a liver-like taste". (For pan or oven preparation the source likewise forbids low-temperature cooking: sear hot, rest, then give it a final hot sear.)
6. Rest about **5 minutes** wrapped in foil. Serve with herb butter or sweet-spicy sauce; grilled vegetables or baked potatoes alongside.
7. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; its doneness evidence is the tong test and the words "rare" to "medium-rare". House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest steak — **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)** for the source's own rare-to-medium-rare window. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Sayersbrook Bison Ranch — Grilling Bison Steak to Perfection (United States)
**Inspired by**: https://www.sayersbrook.com/blog/grilling-bison-steak-to-perfection/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Sayersbrook Bison Ranch (US bison ranch, Missouri) recipe blog. US live-fire tradition.
**Serves**: not stated (rib steaks) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 6–8 min · **Rest**: 5–10 min

**Ingredients**
- Bison rib steaks (the source gives no piece count or weight)
- Garlic salt; black pepper; a few drops of olive oil per steak

**Method**
1. Light the charcoal with a chimney — the source explicitly says **no lighter fluid**, to keep chemical taste off the natural bison — and add a small hardwood log or wood chips for wood-smoke flavour (the wood goes beside the coals, never under the meat).
2. Dust the rib steaks with garlic salt and black pepper ("a lot of the seasoning will be lost on the grill") and add a few drops of olive oil per steak so they don't stick.
3. Sear the first side **2 minutes with the lid off**, then put the lid on for **another 2 minutes** while the steaks bathe in wood smoke. Turn at **4 minutes**; repeat for the second side (the lid has cooled the fire a little, making the turn easier).
4. Take the steaks off after **6–8 minutes total**. The source notes the lean bison drips less fat than a beef rib steak, so flare-ups are less of a problem.
5. Rest **up to 10 minutes, minimum 5** — "you will lose much more juice if you cut the bison meat before 5 minutes than after."
6. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; its endpoint is the 6–8 minute total and the lid sequence. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest steak — **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Birgitta Höglunds mat — Grillad, marinerad bisonfilé med zucchinisallad (Sweden)
**Inspired by**: https://birgittahoglundsmat.wordpress.com/2014/09/06/grillad-marinerad-bisonfile-med-zucchinisallad/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Birgitta Höglunds mat (Swedish food blog, cookbook author), published 2014-09-06. Swedish tradition — **Culinary Group D** source. The bison is Swedish-farmed (Jemtbison, Månsåsen in Jämtland; about 30 animals on grass pasture). **Cut-form flag**: the recipe grills a whole *bisonfilé* (tenderloin) or its slices — the fillet form of this cut — not a rib or strip steak.
**Serves**: 5–6 (1 kg filé) · **Prep**: 15 min + 1–2 days marinating · **Cook**: 4–8 min for slices, longer for whole filé · **Rest**: a few minutes under foil

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg bison filé or other meat (the source's noted alternative: beef filé, steak, pork filé, neck, turkey or chicken)
- **Herb marinade**: 1 dl olive oil; 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce; 1 tbsp liquid honey; 1 tbsp dried thyme; 1 tbsp dried rosemary; 2 tsp coarsely ground black pepper

**Method**
1. Mix the marinade well. Put the meat (whole or in slices) in a freezer bag, pour in the marinade, massage it round so it reaches everywhere. Refrigerate until the next day — **preferably two days**.
2. Take the meat up and **wipe off the spices** — they otherwise burn on the grill.
3. For sliced pieces: grill on **high heat a couple of minutes per side**, salt lightly on both sides; then lay them **beside the embers (glöden)** a couple of minutes more so the heat distributes evenly. The source's own rule: bison filé is lean meat and is at its best *lättstekt* (lightly cooked).
4. For a whole filé: colour it a few minutes per side, then finish over **indirect embers**, grilled slowly to done.
5. **Internal temperature should be 52 °C for bison — a tip the author got from the breeder** (*Innertemperaturen ska vara 52 grader för bison fick jag som tips från uppfödaren, då är den som bäst*). Rest a few minutes under foil before slicing so the juices stay in the meat.
6. **Pull at 52 °C (126 °F)** (stated — attributed by the source to the breeder). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (52 °C at removal, rested under foil).

### 4. Wild Idea Buffalo — Basic Cooking: Bison Steaks (Jill, United States)
**Inspired by**: https://wildideabuffalo.com/blogs/recipes/bison-steak-basic-cooking-instructions (fetched 2026-10-26). Wild Idea Buffalo (US grass-fed bison purveyor, South Dakota), "A Note from Jill", published 2024-07-24. US purveyor-cooking tradition. **Multi-method flag**: the page gives instructions for gas grilling, char grilling and pan searing; this record covers its **char-grilling** section (its gas and pan sections are recorded in the grill and pan_sear leaves).
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 2 h tempering · **Cook**: by thickness · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- Wild Idea bison steaks (tenderloin, ribeye, New York, top sirloin, bone-in ribeye, T-bone, porterhouse etc.)
- Olive oil; salt; pepper or seasonings of choice

**Method**
1. Rinse the steaks under cold water and pat dry; rub with olive oil, salt and pepper. Loosely cover and rest at room temperature **two hours** before cooking. (The source's freezer trick: after tempering, 5–10 minutes in the freezer evens the temperature gradient.)
2. **Char grilling**: ensure the grate is clean; pile the coals high in the centre, light and let them become **white** for best heat; spread the coals evenly with tongs; oil the grate and place it **low and close to the coals**. Grill with the lid closed.
3. Cooking times by thickness, turning every 1½ minutes: ½" steaks 1½ min per side + 30 s per side for medium-rare; 1" 1½ min per side then 1 min per side; 1½" 1½ min per side repeated, then 30 s per side or on the edges; thicker cuts +1 min per ½" per side.
4. Remove, plate, cover with foil and rest **5 minutes** before carving.
5. **Internal temperatures of the meat before resting: Rare 125 °F / Medium Rare 135 °F / Medium 145 °F** (stated). The source's standing rule: "cook them no more than medium."
6. **Pull at 135 °F (57 °C)** for medium-rare, **145 °F (63 °C)** for medium (stated chart values, before resting). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (the chart is explicitly before rest).

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| DasKochrezept.de (Germany) | German community charcoal grill | 3–5 min/side, 2–3 turns, tong test; "rare to at most medium-rare"; no low-temp cooking | Not stated (the two retired pre-ToR numbers — 57–60 °C and 52–54 °C — were the old records' own, not the page's) |
| Sayersbrook Bison Ranch (US) | US ranch kettle grill, hardwood smoke | 2+2 min/side (lid off/on), turn at 4 min, 6–8 min total; 5–10 min rest | Not stated |
| Birgitta Höglunds mat (Sweden) | Swedish farm bison, embers + indirect | slices 2 min/side + beside embers; whole filé coloured then indirect | **52 °C (126 °F)** — breeder's tip quoted by the source |
| Wild Idea Buffalo (US) | US purveyor, coals white-ashed, grate low | times by thickness, turn every 1.5 min | **125/135/145 °F (52/57/63 °C)** chart before rest |

One of four sources publishes a number for the meat — the Swedish breeder's **52 °C** — and a
second publishes a three-step chart (**125/135/145 °F** before rest, i.e. 52/57/63 °C). The
two silent sources agree with them in words rather than numbers: the German source's ceiling
is literally "at most medium-rare", and the ranch times (6–8 min total) land in the same
window. The observed spread for the medium-rare endpoint is therefore **52–57 °C (126–135 °F)**
— the Nordic breeder's tip at the bottom, the US purveyor chart at the top — with the German
verbal rule consistent with either. **Tie-break**: the two measured rows differ by one doneness
step (52 vs 57 °C for medium-rare); the split is cultural — the Swedish source's "lättstekt"
(lightly cooked) filé tradition versus the US purveyor's chart — and both sit below medium.
**Applied target**: 52–57 °C (126–135 °F) for medium-rare over charcoal, house pull **54 °C
(130 °F)** between the two published values. **Temperature used in the cut file: 54 °C
house pull within the existing `medium_rare` band (52–57 °C)**. `recommended_doneness` for
this method: **`medium_rare`** — both measured rows and both verbal rows land rare-to-medium-
rare; nothing in the corpus supports medium or beyond on live fire.

## What makes this method special

Charcoal is where bison's leanness cuts both ways. The fat cap is thin, so — as the ranch
source points out — the flare-up problem that makes beef rib steaks hard over coals mostly
disappears, and the fire can be kept aggressive; but the same leanness means there is no fat
reserve to forgive a slow minute, which is why all four sources converge on short, hot cooks
and three of them explicitly forbid the low-temperature approach that works for collagen-rich
cuts. The German source's warning is the corpus's most specific piece of food chemistry: too
long at too low a heat and the lean, iron-rich muscle develops a **liver-like taste** — the
same off-note the hub profile records from German and American literature. The two North
American sources solve the fire differently but identically in spirit: the ranch uses the lid
as its main instrument (two minutes naked for crust, two minutes covered for smoke, so the
wood smoke and the fire's own heat alternate), while Wild Idea manipulates the fuel itself
(white-ashed coals spread low under the grate) and turns every ninety seconds. The Swedish
source adds the one number and the one Nordic idea: a breeder's **52 °C** and *lättstekt* —
lightly cooked filé, finished beside the embers rather than over them so the heat evens out
without a crust burning on the lean surface.

## Summary

Charcoal-grilling bison steak is a short, hot cook with the fire managed for a lean meat that
has no fat to spare: white-ashed coals or a two-zone kettle, one or two turns, 6–10 minutes
total, and a real rest. The one measured endpoint in the corpus is a Swedish breeder's 52 °C
and the US purveyor chart lands one step higher at 135 °F for medium-rare, so the working
window is 52–57 °C — rare to medium-rare and never beyond, exactly as the German source's
"at most medium-rare" rule states. Hardwood smoke and a covered second phase are the
charcoal-specific moves, and the low-temperature technique that ruins lean bison (the
liver-like note) is forbidden by the source that names it. Salt late or expect to lose the
seasoning to the fire.
