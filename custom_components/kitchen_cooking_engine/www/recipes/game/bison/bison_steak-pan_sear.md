# Bison Steak × Pan Sear — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Pan searing is bison steak's indoor method and the corpus here splits cleanly into two layers.
The freshly fetched US layer is a cast-iron lesson in the cut's leanness: salt far ahead (one
source up to three days), temper at least an hour, sear hot in oil and finish with butter,
garlic and herbs basted over the crust, and rest 8–10 minutes — and even then the two US blogs
disagree about the target by a full doneness level on the very same words: "medium-rare" is
**120 °F** at one and **135 °F** at the other. The older German layer — three Chefkoch.de
community recipes built around the "80-Grad-Methode" (hard sear, then a 25–40 minute hold in
an 80 °C oven) — cannot be re-verified: the pages now return 403, and the two of them that
were recorded twice contradict themselves (the two preserved records disagree on both the pull
temperature, 54 versus 60 °C, and the hold time). Those numbers are therefore **retracted as
evidence** and preserved only as a disputed record; what survives of the German layer is its
technique — sear hard, hold gently at 80 °C, forbid *Niedrigtemperatur* cooking — which the
one re-fetchable German source (the Daskochrezept charcoal recipe, recorded in the
charcoal_grill leaf) states in its own words: bison cooked long at low heat takes on a
liver-like taste. All layers agree on the ceiling: never past medium, ever.

## Styckesprofil

Pannstekning är bisonstekens inomhusmetod, och materialet här delar sig rent i två lager. Det
nyhämtade amerikanska lagret är en gjutjärnslektion i snittets magerhet: salta långt i förväg
(en källa upp till tre dagar), temperera minst en timme, bryn hett i olja och avsluta med
smör, vitlök och örter som öses över skorpan, och vila 8–10 minuter — och ändå är de två
amerikanska bloggarna oeniga om målet med ett helt doneness-steg på exakt samma ord:
"medium-rare" är **120 °F** hos den ena och **135 °F** hos den andra. Det äldre tyska lagret —
tre Chefkoch.de-communityrecept byggda kring "80-Grad-Methode" (hård bryning, sedan 25–40
minuters hållning i 80 °C ugn) — går inte längre att verifiera: sidorna ger nu 403, och de två
som registrerats dubbelt motsäger sig själva (de två bevarade posterna är oeniga både om
pulltemperaturen, 54 mot 60 °C, och om hålltiden). De talen är därför **återtagna som bevarad
bevisning** och bevaras bara som en omdisputerad post; det som överlever av det tyska lagret är
tekniken — bryna hårt, hålla varsamt vid 80 °C, förbjuda *Niedrigtemperatur*-garning — som den
enda återhämtbara tyska källan (Daskochrezept-kolreceptet i charcoal_grill-bladet) uttrycker i
egna ord: bison kokt länge på låg värme får en leberartad smak. Alla lager är eniga om taket:
aldrig förbi medium.

## Source recipes

### 1. Chefkoch.de — Bisonsteak mit Büffelmozzarella auf Wildpilzgemüse (Germany) — preserved record
**Inspired by**: https://www.chefkoch.de/rezepte/2619231411385428/Bisonsteak-mit-Bueffelmozzarella-auf-Wildpilzgemuese.html (record preserved from a fetch of 2026-04-24; the page returns 403 to fetches as of 2026-10-26 and could not be re-verified). Chefkoch.de community recipe. German community-cooking tradition. **Preserved-record flag**: kept as a bibliographic record of a page that is no longer fetchable; its numbers are the old record's and are not re-verified. (Chefkoch search listings still show the recipe with "50 Min. Gesamtzeit; 20 Min. Arbeitszeit", consistent with the record's 30-minute cook.)
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 30 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 bison steaks; 2 buffalo mozzarella, sliced
- 200 ml game stock (*Wildfond*); 500 g wild mushrooms (chanterelles, porcini, oyster or button)
- 2 tbsp clarified butter; 1 tbsp oil; 2 tbsp dried parsley; 4 shallots, finely chopped

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven with an ovenproof dish to **80 °C**.
2. Heat the oil in a pan until very hot; sear the steaks hard on each side (about 2 min per side); transfer to the ovenproof dish and top with the mozzarella slices.
3. **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)** for medium (the preserved record's value, marked house-preserved, not re-verified; the low oven means very gentle carry-over).
4. While the steaks rest, sauté mushrooms and shallots in the clarified butter until golden; deglaze with game stock and reduce; season with salt, pepper, parsley. Serve the steaks on the mushroom bed (the record suggests rösti).
5. Final resting temperature per the preserved record: **57–59 °C (135–138 °F)**.

### 2. Chefkoch.de — Bisonsteak in Rosmarin ("80 Grad Methode", user Klaumix, Germany) — DISPUTED preserved record
**Inspired by**: https://www.chefkoch.de/rezepte/2841471436190764/Bisonsteak-in-Rosmarin.html (records preserved from fetches of 2026-04-24 and 2026-06-08; the page returns 403 as of 2026-10-26 and could not be re-verified). Chefkoch.de community recipe by user Klaumix ("Bisonsteak in Rosmarin – 80 Grad Methode", confirmed still listed in Chefkoch search results). German community-cooking tradition. **Conflict flag**: the two preserved records of this page disagree — record A says "roast at 80 °C for 30–40 min until the probe reads **54 °C**", record B says "25 min at 80 °C for medium, pull at **60 °C**". Because at least one record is wrong and the page is unverifiable, **neither temperature is taken as the source's value in this leaf**; the disputed readings are preserved here verbatim and excluded from the consensus.
**Serves**: 1–2 · **Prep**: 10–15 min · **Cook**: 25–40 min at 80 °C (disputed) after the sear

**Ingredients** (both preserved records agree on the core)
- 250 g bison entrecôte; 2 sprigs fresh rosemary; olive oil (or clarified butter); salt; black pepper

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 80 °C (top/bottom heat). Chop the rosemary; warm 4 tbsp olive oil with it in a flat ovenproof dish in the oven.
2. Sear the seasoned entrecôte hard on both sides in a heavy cast-iron pan until a deep crust forms.
3. Place the steak in the warm rosemary oil; hold in the 80 °C oven (the disputed 25 or 30–40 minutes) as a gentle finish — the sear does the real cooking in both records.
4. **Pull temperature: DISPUTED — 54 °C (record A) vs 60 °C (record B); the page publishes no verifiable value.** Neither is used as evidence; house guidance for this leaf: probe and pull at **54 °C (130 °F)**, the low end of the two preserved readings. Final serving temperature: **not verifiable**.

### 3. Chefkoch.de — Bisonsteak auf Paprika ("80 Grad Methode", user Klaumix, Germany) — DISPUTED preserved record
**Inspired by**: https://www.chefkoch.de/rezepte/2841491436191027/Bisonsteak-auf-Paprika.html (records preserved from fetches of 2026-04-24 and 2026-06-08; the page returns 403 as of 2026-10-26 and could not be re-verified). Chefkoch.de community recipe by user Klaumix (confirmed still listed: "55 Min. Gesamtzeit; 25 Min. Arbeitszeit; 30 Min. Koch-/Backzeit", consistent with a 30-minute oven hold rather than either preserved cook time). German community-cooking tradition. **Conflict flag**: the two preserved records disagree — record A says "80 °C for 30–40 min until **54 °C**", record B says "25 min for medium, pull at **60 °C**". **Neither temperature is taken as the source's value.**
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10–25 min · **Cook**: ~30 min at 80 °C (per the page's own listing) after the sear

**Ingredients** (the two preserved records also disagree on seasoning; the shared core is listed and the divergences noted)
- 2 bison entrecôte, about 250 g each; 1–2 bell peppers (record A: 1 finely diced red; record B: 2 sliced mixed colours); 1 onion; garlic and/or fresh ginger and chilli (record A: red chilli + 3 cm ginger + honey + Maggi; record B: 2 garlic cloves + paprika spice) — **seasoning disputed between records**; oil; salt; pepper

**Method**
1. Sweat the pepper-onion base (with the disputed aromatics) in oil until soft; season; spread in a baking dish and warm in the 80 °C oven.
2. Sear the bison steaks hard, about 2 min per side; lay them on the pepper bed.
3. Hold in the 80 °C oven (the disputed 25 or 30–40 minutes) as a gentle finish.
4. **Pull temperature: DISPUTED — 54 °C vs 60 °C; no verifiable value.** House guidance for this leaf: **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not verifiable**.

### 4. Good Food Baddie — How To Cook Bison Steak (Pan-Seared) (United States)
**Inspired by**: https://goodfoodbaddie.com/how-to-cook-bison-steak-pan-seared/ (fetched 2026-10-26; page states "Last updated June 11, 2026"). Good Food Baddie (US food blog). US home-cooking tradition. **Method note**: the source's own temperature school is the corpus's lowest — its chart is explicitly "before resting".
**Serves**: not stated (sirloin in the recipe) · **Prep**: salt 1 h to 3 days ahead · **Cook**: 5 min · **Rest**: 8–10 min

**Ingredients**
- Bison steak (grass-fed and finished; sirloin or ribeye named as the usual grocery cuts)
- Salt and pepper; butter and avocado oil; fresh garlic cloves; fresh rosemary or thyme
- (The source serves it with red chimichurri: roasted red pepper, garlic, herbs, vinegar — its own recipe)

**Method**
1. Season generously with salt and pepper at least **1 hour** before cooking (the source salts up to three days ahead, refrigerated in an airtight container). Bring to room temperature **at least 60 minutes** — "DO NOT SKIP THIS STEP".
2. Heat the oil in a cast-iron skillet over high heat. Sear the steak **2–4 minutes** first side (the source's rule for a 1" steak: about **3 minutes first side, 2 minutes second side** for medium-rare; thicker cuts +1 min per ½" per side); halfway through, add the butter, garlic and herbs and baste.
3. Flip with tongs and sear the second side, basting with the garlic butter.
4. **"Cook to 120 °F (49 °C) for medium rare, or 125 °F (52 °C) for medium"** — the source's stated targets. Its chart, "internal temperature before resting": Rare 115 °F (46 °C); Medium-Rare 120 °F (49 °C); Medium 125 °F (52 °C); Medium Well 135–145 °F (57–63 °C).
5. Rest **8–10 minutes** before slicing. The source's standing rules: "DO NOT cook past medium-well"; bison is lean so it needs about **one-third less cooking time than beef**; use an instant thermometer ("if you are going to buy this pricey cut of meat, make sure to have all the tools").
6. **Pull at 120 °F (49 °C)** for medium-rare (stated). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (chart is explicitly before rest).

### 5. Wild Idea Buffalo — Basic Cooking: Bison Steaks (Jill, United States) — pan-searing section
**Inspired by**: https://wildideabuffalo.com/blogs/recipes/bison-steak-basic-cooking-instructions (fetched 2026-10-26). Wild Idea Buffalo (US grass-fed bison purveyor, South Dakota), published 2024-07-24. US purveyor-cooking tradition. **Multi-method flag**: this record is the page's **pan-searing** section (its gas and char sections are in the grill and charcoal_grill leaves; one source, three method rows, never counted twice in one leaf).
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 2 h tempering · **Cook**: by thickness · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- Wild Idea bison steaks (tenderloin, ribeye, New York, top sirloin etc.); olive oil; salt; pepper

**Method**
1. Rinse and pat dry; rub with olive oil, salt and pepper; temper loosely covered **2 hours** (freezer trick 5–10 min after tempering).
2. Heat a seasoned (oiled) cast-iron skillet over **medium-high** until very hot; lay the steaks in, snugging each side to the pan.
3. Times by thickness, turning every 1½ minutes (same table as the grill sections): ½" 1½ min per side + 30 s per side; 1" 1½ min per side then 1 min per side; 1½" and thicker per the table's extension rule. (The page notes its 1" row as "1.5 seconds each side" — an evident typo for minutes in the source's own table; recorded as printed.)
4. Remove, plate, cover with foil, rest **5 minutes**.
5. **Internal temperatures before resting: Rare 125 °F / Medium Rare 135 °F / Medium 145 °F** (stated); "cook them no more than medium".
6. **Pull at 135 °F (57 °C)** for medium-rare (stated chart value before rest). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

The freshly verifiable half of this corpus publishes numbers, and the two US sources disagree
by a full doneness step on identical words: Good Food Baddie's "medium-rare" is **120 °F
(49 °C)** before rest, Wild Idea's is **135 °F (57 °C)** before rest — the same pull-temperature
versus finished-temperature split the grill leaf records. The German half contributes its
technique but **no usable temperature**: two of its three pages are unverifiable and
self-contradictory (54 vs 60 °C), so the disputed numbers are preserved and excluded, and the
one preserved non-conflicting reading (54 °C) is kept flagged as unverifiable. The shared
rules across all layers: season ahead, temper (except Vindulge's dissent in the grill leaf),
hot fat + butter baste or warm infused oil, short sear, real rest, and the hard ceiling at
medium / "DO NOT cook past medium-well". **Tie-break**: of the two verifiable measured rows the
spread is 49 vs 57 °C for the same word; the applied target takes the finished-temperature
school's **57 °C (135 °F)** (same as the grill leaf's middle row, and the reading that does
not depend on measuring carryover) with the 49 °C low-pull school recorded as the corpus's
persistent dissent. **Temperature used in the cut file: 57 °C within the existing
`medium_rare` band**. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`medium_rare`** — both
measured rows and the preserved 54 °C reading land rare-to-medium-rare; nothing supports
medium or beyond.

## What makes this method special

The skillet is where bison's leanness is most exposed: a grill spreads heat over the whole
surface and a bath removes the gradient entirely, but a pan drives a hard crust through a
1–2 cm lean sheet with no fat layer to slow it, and the corpus's technique list reads like a
series of compensations — salt days ahead to restructure the surface, temper for an hour so
the centre isn't fighting the crust, baste butter over the sear to add the fat the animal
didn't, rest 8–10 minutes while the (measurable) carryover finishes the walk to doneness. The
German "80-Grad-Methode" is the most elaborate compensation: the sear does all the Maillard
work in two minutes, and the 80 °C oven holds the meat at its target without cooking it
further — the culinary equivalent of a resting drawer, which the source that names the danger
(*Niedrigtemperatur* → liver-like taste) explicitly permits precisely because the total
temperature stays low and short. The temperature divergence here is the cut's signature
problem in miniature: two US blogs, one recipe genre, two conventions, a full doneness step
apart — and two German community pages whose contradictory preserved records show exactly why
this project's source rule exists. The pan is also the method where the "never past medium"
rule is stated most bluntly ("DO NOT cook past medium-well", "do not overcook"), because a
skillet gives the lean meat the fastest path from perfect to dry of any method in this cut's
corpus.

## Summary

Pan-seared bison steak is a five-minute cast-iron cook with a long rest, and its two verifiable
sources disagree about the target by a full doneness step: 120 °F for medium-rare at one blog,
135 °F at the other — the cut's pull-versus-finished temperature argument in its sharpest form.
The German 80-Grad-Methode contributes the cleverest technique (sear hard, hold at 80 °C) but
its preserved records contradict themselves and the pages are now unverifiable, so their
numbers are retracted and only the technique stands. Season ahead, temper, baste with butter,
rest 8–10 minutes, and stop at medium — every source on this page that states a ceiling states
it as an absolute. The applied middle is 57 °C (135 °F), with the 49 °C school preserved as
real dissent for the cut's doneness pass.
