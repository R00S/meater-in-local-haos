# Buffalo Burger × Air Fryer — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

An air fryer is a small, fast convection oven, and for a ground bison patty that changes the
method's physics rather than its timing: 190–205 °C air moves across both faces at once, so the
patty browns without being turned, and the perforated basket lets the small amount of rendered
fat drain away instead of frying the crust in it. The cut is the same lean coarse-ground bison
— 90/10 in two of these four recipes, unstated in the other two — formed into patties of 115 g
to 170 g and roughly 1.3 to 2 cm thick. Lean bison is unusually well suited to this box
because the thing that usually goes wrong in an air fryer, a greasy crust steaming in its own
fat, cannot happen; the thing that goes wrong instead is the opposite, over-drying, because
moving air strips moisture from a surface with very little fat to lose. Two of the four sources
solve that with a binder (egg and breadcrumbs, or egg and Italian seasoning) and the other two
cook the bare patty and rely on a shorter time. Carryover is minimal — the patty is thin, the
cook is short, and the basket is already cooling as it comes out — so the reading at removal is
close to the reading at the table.

## Source recipes

### 1. The Top Meal (US) — Juicy Air Fryer Bison Burgers
**Inspired by**: https://thetopmeal.com/air-fryer-bison-burgers/ (fetched 2026-10-26). The Top Meal (US recipe blog). US home air-fryer tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: 2 min

**Ingredients**
- 450 g (1 lb) ground bison
- ½ onion, finely chopped
- 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- ½ tsp liquid smoke
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp dried parsley

**Method**
1. Combine the ground bison, chopped onion, Worcestershire sauce, liquid smoke, garlic powder, salt and dried parsley in a bowl. Mix gently but thoroughly — the source's warning is not to overwork the meat.
2. Divide into **4 equal portions**, roll each into a ball and flatten gently into an even-thickness patty. Press a shallow thumb indent in the centre so the patties do not dome and crack.
3. Preheat the air fryer to **380 °F (193 °C)** and lightly grease the basket.
4. Lay the patties in the basket in one layer, not touching or overlapping.
5. Cook at **380 °F for 6–8 minutes** (the recipe card says 8 minutes); add 1–2 minutes only if you want them more done, remembering that "bison is lean and can dry out if overcooked".
6. Probe horizontally in from the edge of a patty, tip at the centre.
7. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — **the source's own figure**: "use a meat thermometer to ensure the burgers have reached an internal temperature of at least 160 °F which is recommended for ground meats".
8. Rest on a plate **a couple of minutes** so the juices redistribute. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.
9. Add cheese in the last few seconds of air frying if making cheeseburgers.

### 2. Bucket List Tummy (Sarah Schlichter, MPH RDN, US) — Air Fryer Bison Burgers
**Inspired by**: https://www.bucketlisttummy.com/air-fryer-bison-burgers/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Bucket List Tummy (US registered-dietitian food blog). US home air-fryer tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 5 (recipe card) / 4–5 patties · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 450 g (16 oz) ground bison
- 1 large egg
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp onion powder
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- 2 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 3 tbsp breadcrumbs
- 4 cheese slices; 4 burger buns

**Method**
1. Thaw the ground bison overnight if frozen.
2. Set the air fryer to **400 °F (204 °C)**.
3. Season the meat and mix in the egg, spices, Worcestershire sauce and breadcrumbs by hand. The egg and breadcrumbs are the source's binding answer to lean bison holding together in a moving-air basket.
4. Form into **4–5 medium patties** and place them in the basket without stacking or overlapping.
5. Set the timer to **10 minutes**. The source notes it did not need to flip the burgers, but that other air fryers may require it.
6. Check with a thermometer that the burgers have reached **145 °F (medium rare) to 160 °F (medium)** and that the juices run clear, not red.
7. **Pull at 63–71 °C (145–160 °F)** — the source's own window, stated as a range rather than a point, with the choice left to the cook.
8. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**. Optional: put the assembled burgers on buns back in the warm (switched-off) air fryer for 1–2 minutes to melt the cheese and toast the buns; frozen pre-made patties need **11–13 minutes**.

### 3. Summer Yule (Summer Yule, MS RDN, US) — Air Fryer Bison Burgers
**Inspired by**: https://summeryule.com/bison-burgers-air-fryer/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Summer Yule (US registered-dietitian food site). US home air-fryer tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Form flag**: this record cooks a **ready-made 6-ounce, ½-inch bison patty** with no seasoning and no binder, so it is the corpus's bare-patty control.
**Serves**: 1 · **Prep**: none · **Cook**: 8–13 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 bison burger patty, **6 oz (170 g)**, about ½ inch thick
- Hamburger bun and toppings, as desired

**Method**
1. Place the burger in a single layer in the air-fryer basket or on the air-fryer oven rack (the source's small basket holds one; its air-fryer oven holds two or three on the top rack).
2. Air fry at **375 °F (190 °C) for 8 minutes**.
3. Flip the burger and air fry **3–5 minutes more**, until done — timings are for 6-ounce patties, and 4-ounce patties cook faster.
4. Probe in from the side, tip at the centre — a ½-inch patty leaves little margin, so the probe must not pass through to the far side.
5. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — **the source's own figure**: "Whether burgers are fresh or frozen, they're ready when they reach 160 °F (71 °C) internally."
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**. For a cheeseburger, lay the cheese on the cooked patty and leave it in the **switched-off** hot air fryer for 1 minute to melt; for toasted buns, preheat to 400 °F (200 °C) and toast cut-side up for 2 minutes.

### 4. Basil and Bubbly (Marianne, US) — Bison Burgers (air-fryer method)
**Inspired by**: https://basilandbubbly.com/bison-burgers/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Basil and Bubbly (US home-cooking blog). US home air-fryer tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Multi-method flag**: the page also gives skillet and grill instructions (skillet 2 min/side, grill 475 °F for 2–3 + 2 min, with the advice to "use a charcoal grill instead of a gas grill" for best flavour); this record covers its **air-fryer** section, and those two variants are noted in the summary rather than counted as separate records.
**Serves**: 3 · **Prep**: 6 min · **Cook**: 7 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 340 g (12 oz) lean ground bison, **90/10**
- 2 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- ½ tsp onion powder
- ¼ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp grapeseed oil (or another high-heat mild oil)
- ½ tsp kosher salt (optional)

**Method**
1. Sprinkle the seasonings evenly over the ground bison and mix gently — over-mixing makes the burgers dense.
2. Form **3 patties, 4 inches across and about ¾ inch (2 cm) deep**, pressing only enough that they just hold together.
3. Preheat the air fryer to **370 °F (188 °C)**. (For the skillet: add the oil to a heavy cast-iron pan over medium-high and cook 2 minutes, flip, 2 minutes more. For the grill: heat to **475 °F**, 2–3 minutes, flip, 2 minutes more.)
4. Place the patties in a single layer on the air-fryer grate and cook **5 minutes**, then flip and cook **another 2 minutes**.
5. Probe horizontally from the edge into the centre of a patty.
6. **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)** — **the source's own preferred figure**: "I like my burgers a solid medium, which is around 145°F. The USDA recommends cooking ground bison meat to 160°F, which is well-done and a bit dry for my tastes." This is the lowest pull in the leaf. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.
7. Serve with onion (red or caramelised), tomato and a hearty mustard, or gorgonzola/American cheese.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Published doneness evidence | Internal temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Top Meal (US) | US bare-ish patty, 380 °F, 6–8 min | thermometer check, "at least" | **160 °F (71 °C)** |
| Bucket List Tummy (US) | US egg-and-breadcrumb patty, 400 °F, 10 min | thermometer, range offered | **145–160 °F (63–71 °C)** |
| Summer Yule (US) | US ready-made bare patty, 375 °F, 8 + 3–5 min | thermometer, "no guessing needed" | **160 °F (71 °C)** |
| Basil and Bubbly (US) | US lean 90/10 patty, 370 °F, 5 + 2 min | cook's preference vs USDA | **145 °F (63 °C)** preferred; USDA 160 °F acknowledged |

Here the corpus is fully numeric and still splits — **63 °C or 71 °C**, nothing in between except
the one source that publishes the whole interval. The split is not about the appliance: all
four use 370–400 °F air and 7–13 minutes, and all four tell the cook to use a thermometer. It
is about food-safety philosophy. Two sources state the USDA figure as the requirement; one
states it as a range with the choice left open; the fourth names 145 °F as what it actually
prefers and says plainly that 160 °F is "well-done and a bit dry for my tastes". Mapping to the
engine: `well_done` (68–77 °C, target 71) contains two of the four rows and is what
`usda_safe_c` requires, while the 63 °C rows land in medium. `recommended_doneness` for this
method: **`well_done`**, because a ground patty of mixed-provenance mince is the one place this
project's USDA floor should not be traded away for preference — but the 63 °C rows are recorded
verbatim rather than smoothed over, since they are what a substantial share of the corpus
actually cooks to.

## What makes this method special

Three things separate an air fryer from the cut's other methods, and all three are consequences
of moving air. First, **no turn is required**: the 400 °F air reaches both faces, and the one
source that mentions flipping calls it optional and appliance-dependent — a grill or pan
cannot do that, and it matters for a patty that would otherwise dry on the exposed side.
Second, **the basket drains**: rendered fat falls away, so the crust is built by hot air and
Maillard browning rather than by frying, which is why the bare-patty record here needs no oil
at all and still browns. Third, **the air is drying**, and that is where the lean bison problem
returns in a new form — the two sources that add a binder (egg with breadcrumbs; egg with
Italian seasoning) are not adding flavour so much as insurance, and the two that do not are
either cooking a thicker manufactured patty or cooking it for less time. The setting spread is
narrow (370–400 °F) and the time spread is not (7–13 minutes for very similar patties), which
tells you the appliance's real variable is the basket's airflow and load rather than the
dial — one source notes its small basket fits a single burger while its air-fryer oven takes
three, and the times are quoted for one machine.

## Summary

Air-frying a bison burger works because moving 370–400 °F air browns both faces without a turn
and the basket drains away what little fat renders out. Times run 7–13 minutes for patties of
115–170 g, and the sources split on the only number that matters: two call for 160 °F, one
gives a 145–160 °F range, and one prefers 145 °F and calls 160 °F dry. Probe from the side
into the centre, treat the times as machine-specific, and add a binder if the patty is bare
lean bison. The cheese trick — one minute in the switched-off basket — is worth keeping.
