# Buffalo Burger × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grill heat is the reference method for this cut in its home continent, and the fetched sources are unanimous about why: a ground patty has no fat cap and no connective tissue to protect it, so the only variables are patty thickness, fire temperature and how long you leave it. The patty is built from lean ground bison — the Traeger recipe names **94 % lean** explicitly — and two of the four sources bind it before it ever reaches the grate, one with egg and panko, which is a structural fix rather than a flavour one: lean bison mince has so little fat that a bare patty can crumble on the turn. Carryover is small but real and both numeric sources measure it: the meat is thin, the fire is hot, and the observed rise is a few degrees over a short rest, which is why one source deliberately pulls early and lets the rest finish the job. The method's characteristic failure is not burning but drying — the fire is the least forgiving place for a meat with this little internal fat.

## Source recipes

### 1. Simply Whisked (Melissa Belanger, US) — Grilled Bison Burgers
**Inspired by**: https://www.simplywhisked.com/grilled-bison-burgers/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Simply Whisked (US home-cooking blog). US backyard-grill tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 900 g (2 lb) ground bison
- 1½ tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 4 tsp yellow mustard
- ½ tsp granulated garlic (or garlic powder)
- 1½ tsp coarse salt

**Method**
1. In a large bowl season the ground bison with the coarse salt, garlic, yellow mustard and Worcestershire sauce. Mix with clean hands until evenly combined — the source's own warning is not to over-mix, "or you'll end up with tough burgers".
2. Portion into **¼-pound (113 g) sections** and shape into patties.
3. Preheat the grill to **medium-high, about 400 °F (204 °C)**.
4. Lay the patties on the hot grate and cook **5–6 minutes per side, turning once**.
5. Insert an instant-read probe horizontally through the side of a patty into its thickest centre.
6. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — the source adopts the USDA ground-meat figure as its own endpoint ("The USDA recommends cooking ground bison to 160 ˚F (well-done)"). It adds, in the same answer, that if you prefer your burgers less done you should "cook them to a minimum of 145 ˚F for medium-rare" — that is the source's floor, not its recommendation. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.
7. Serve with the usual toppings; the page gives no rest instruction.

### 2. Traeger Kitchen (US) — Smoked Bison Burgers (pellet grill on High)
**Inspired by**: https://www.traeger.com/recipes/smoky-bison-burgers (fetched 2026-10-26). Traeger Grills test kitchen, hickory pellets. US wood-pellet live-fire tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Naming note**: the page is titled "Smoked Bison Burgers" but its own steps set the grill to **High / 450 °F (232 °C)** and cook 5 minutes per side — it is a hot pellet-grill recipe, so it is filed here in `grill`, not in `smoker`.
**Serves**: 4 (eight 4-inch patties, two per bun) · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 35 min (incl. onions) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1.36 kg (3 lb) ground bison, **94 % lean**
- 4 tbsp butter; 2 large yellow onions, sliced
- 1 tsp salt; 1 tsp black pepper; 1 cup brown sugar; ⅔ cup bourbon
- 8 slices American cheese; 4 buns; 1 head butter lettuce; 4 tomato slices

**Method**
1. Melt the butter in a heavy skillet over medium-high, add the sliced onions with 1 tsp salt and ½ tsp pepper, and cook 20–25 minutes until golden. Off the heat, stir in the brown sugar, then add the bourbon and carefully ignite it with a long match; when the flames die, cook ~2 minutes more to glaze. Season and cool.
2. Break the bison up in a bowl, season with salt and pepper, and form **eight 4-inch patties**.
3. Set the grill to **High (450 °F / 232 °C recommended)**, lid closed, and preheat 15 minutes.
4. Grill the patties **5 minutes per side**. In the last 2 minutes add the cheese to melt and lay the split buns cut-side down to toast.
5. Probe at the end of the second side, through the side of a patty into the centre; the source gives no target, so probe until the reading is stable and the cheese has melted.
6. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — house guidance, **not the source's number**: the page publishes no internal temperature at all. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.
7. Build with two patties per bun, bourbon onions between them, lettuce and tomato.

### 3. Jackson Hole Buffalo Meat (Courtney Johnston, US kitchen / Levantine tradition) — Bison Kafta
**Inspired by**: https://jhbuffalomeat.com/blogs/news/bison-kafta (fetched 2026-10-26). Jackson Hole Buffalo Meat recipe blog, "Cuisine: Mediterranean". Levantine *kafta/kofta* tradition applied to ground buffalo — **Culinary Group C** source. **Form flag**: the mixture is formed into **eight oblong kafta on skewers**, not round burger patties. It is kept because it is a fully recorded, genuinely buffalo-based Levantine ground-meat recipe, and because the lean-bison problem it solves (onion, herbs and a short chill to hold the lean mince together) is the same problem the patty sources solve with egg and breadcrumbs.
**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 20 min (+10 min chilling) · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 900 g (2 lb) ground bison
- 1 small onion (red, white or sweet), grated
- 2–3 garlic cloves, finely minced or grated
- ½ cup loosely packed fresh parsley, finely chopped, plus more to serve
- 1½ tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp sumac (*sumac* — dried sour berry, Levantine); 1 tsp cumin; ½ tsp cinnamon
- 1 tsp dried oregano (or 1 tbsp fresh, finely chopped)

**Method**
1. Put the ground bison in a large bowl. Grate the onion and garlic, finely chop the parsley, and add both to the meat with all the spices.
2. Mix well with clean hands until fully incorporated.
3. Divide into **eight equal parts** and form each into an oblong shape around a skewer.
4. Refrigerate the skewers **10 minutes** while the grill preheats — the source's stated reason is that this helps the lean meat hold together when it is transferred.
5. Preheat an outdoor grill to **medium-high, 450–500 °F (232–260 °C)**, and oil the grates with a high-smoke-point oil such as avocado.
6. Grill **6–8 minutes**, flipping only when the meat releases from the grate easily and grill marks have formed, then **3–4 minutes** on the second side "until cooked through".
7. Probe the thickest part of one kafta from the end, parallel to the skewer, staying away from the metal.
8. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — house guidance, **not the source's number**: the page publishes no internal temperature and its endpoint is the words "cooked through". Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**; rest 5 minutes and pour the accumulated juices back over.
9. Serve with rice, tzatziki, vegetables or flatbread.

### 4. Wrågården i Falköping (Sweden) — Bisonburgare (grill section)
**Inspired by**: https://wragarden.se/vara-produkter-du-hittar-vara-produkter-hos/bisonburgare/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Wrågården, a Swedish bison farm in Falköping, publishing cooking instructions for its own bison-burger product. Swedish farm-producer tradition — **Culinary Group D** source. **Multi-method flag**: the page carries both a grill and a pan-fry section; this record covers the **grill** section (the pan-fry section is recorded in the `pan_fry` leaf). **Composition flag**: the product is **60 % Swedish bison, 40 % Swedish pork**, salt and pepper — a mixed patty, not pure bison, so its juiciness cannot be read across to a 94 %-lean bison patty.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 9–12 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Bisonburgare from Wrågården: **60 % Swedish bison, 40 % Swedish pork**, salt, pepper
- Rapeseed oil for the grate/pan (listed as "rapsolja till stekning" on the recipe card)
- Suggested serving: oven-roasted root vegetables, freshly baked bread, coleslaw, smoky dressing

**Method**
1. Light the grill and wait until **the embers have turned grey-white** (*när glöden blivit gråvit*) — the source's only fire instruction, and the reason this row reads as a charcoal/ember grill.
2. Lay the burgers on the grill and cook **about 3 minutes on one side** before the first turn.
3. Turn a few more times until the burgers have grilled **about 12 minutes total from frozen, or 9–11 minutes if thawed**.
4. Probe from the side into the centre of the thickest burger.
5. **Pull at 70 °C (158 °F)** — **the source's own published floor**: *"Innertemperaturen skall vara minst 70 °C"* (the internal temperature must be at least 70 °C). Note that this number is a degree below the US 71 °C figure the other leaves carry, and it belongs to a patty that is 40 % pork.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**; it gives no rest.

### 5. Hey Grill Hey (Susie Bulloch, US) — Grilled Ranch Bison Burger — **temperature statement only, not counted toward the source gate**
**Inspired by**: https://heygrillhey.com/bison-burger/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2021-05-12). US barbecue blog. **Integrity note**: the page's recipe card did not render in the fetched text, so the ingredient **quantities could not be read**; under this project's rules the record cannot count as a fully saved source recipe. It is kept because it is the one fetched grill source that states a **pull-versus-final** relationship explicitly, which the temperature discussion below depends on.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Method (as published in the page body)**
1. Combine ground bison with **egg, panko, Worcestershire sauce and ranch seasoning**; the source's point is that it mixes seasoning *into* the meat rather than only onto it.
2. Preheat the grill to **400 °F (204 °C)** and oil the grates.
3. Grill the patties **6 minutes**, flip, and grill **2 minutes** more; add cheese in the last minute.
4. **Pull at 68 °C (155 °F)** — *"I cooked my burgers to an internal temperature of 155 degrees F, then allowed them to rest for carryover cooking to bring them to a final 160 degrees F."*
5. Final serving temperature: **71 °C / 160 °F** (stated — the source's carryover target).
6. The source's standing rule: **do not cook bison above 160 °F**, because "overcooking bison will result in it becoming dry and chewy".

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Published doneness evidence | Internal temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simply Whisked (US) | US backyard gas grill | 400 °F, 5–6 min/side; adopts USDA | **160 °F (71 °C)** as its endpoint; 145 °F (63 °C) named only as a minimum for less-done |
| Traeger Kitchen (US) | US pellet grill on High | 450 °F, 5 min/side, cheese last 2 min | Not stated |
| Bison Kafta (Levantine) | Levantine kafta on skewers | 450–500 °F, 6–8 + 3–4 min, "cooked through" | Not stated |
| Wrågården (Sweden) | Swedish farm producer, grey-white embers | 3 min, then turns, 9–11 min thawed / 12 min frozen | **at least 70 °C (158 °F)** — the source's own floor |
| Hey Grill Hey (US, not counted) | US gas grill, egg + panko binder | 400 °F, 6 + 2 min | **pull 155 °F (68 °C) → final 160 °F (71 °C)** |

Two measured rows and one verbal rule. The measured target is **70–71 °C (158–160 °F)**: the
Swedish producer's published floor of 70 °C and the US blog's **pull at 155 °F, rest to
160 °F**, which is a genuinely useful piece of data — the only source in the corpus that
separates the number on the thermometer at removal from the number that matters at the table.
Mapping to the engine, the cut's `usda_safe_c` of 71 °C sits at the top of that window and
inside `DONENESS_WELL_DONE` (68–77 °C, target 71), so no override is justified. The
disagreement worth recording is the *ceiling*, not the target: Hey Grill Hey says plainly
"do not cook bison above 160 °F", while Simply Whisked's answer to "how should bison burger be
cooked" leads with 160 °F as the recommendation — the same number used as a floor by one
writer and a stop-line by the other. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`well_done`**,
matching the cut — with the note that this leaf's corpus contains no grilled patty cooked
below 155 °F, unlike the cut's steak form, where rare is normal.

## What makes this method special

A grill cooks a patty from one side only, by radiation and convection, and that decides
everything else. The top face dries while the bottom face chars, so the patty has to be
turned — and every source here turns exactly once (Traeger and Simply Whisked) or builds the
turn into a two-stage timing (Hey Grill Hey's 6 + 2). Fat loss is the real mechanism: a lean
bison patty drips very little, so there is no flare-up problem and, equally, no basting fat
running back over the surface, which is why the binder question becomes the method's central
craft argument. The four sources split cleanly into two schools — **bind it** (egg and panko,
or onion and herbs with a ten-minute chill) versus **leave it bare** (salt, pepper and a
450 °F fire, Traeger's eight thin patties) — and the bare school compensates by making the
patties thinner and the fire hotter, so the inside is done before the outside has time to
dry. The Swedish row adds the one thing the US rows do not discuss: a mixed bison-and-pork
patty, which is how a producer solves the lean problem at the product level rather than at
the mixing bowl. Carryover is small here and one source quantifies it — 155 °F to 160 °F
over the rest — which is worth knowing because it is exactly the allowance that lets a lean
patty come off the fire still juicy.

## Summary

Grilling a bison burger is a short, hot cook with one turn: 400–450 °F, 5–6 minutes a side,
cheese in the last minute. The bison is lean enough that the craft question is whether to bind
it — egg and panko, or grated onion and herbs with a short chill — or to go bare and thin, as
the pellet-grill source does with eight 4-inch patties. Probe from the side into the centre
and pull at **155 °F**, letting the rest carry it to **160 °F**; the Swedish producer's
published floor of 70 °C says the same thing in metric. Do not push past 160 °F: there is no
fat reserve to forgive it.
