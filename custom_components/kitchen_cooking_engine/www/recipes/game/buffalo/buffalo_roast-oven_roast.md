# Buffalo Roast × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

An oven roast is the whole-muscle form of this cut: a compact, boneless or bone-in piece from
the chuck/shoulder, sirloin tip, round or rib, weighing 1–2 kg and, in bison, carrying almost no
intramuscular fat. The structure that matters is the **grain** — long, dense bundles of lean
fibre with a thin sheath of connective tissue and, on some sub-cuts, a bone — and the reason oven
roasting is difficult here is that there is no fat to baste the interior while it heats. Dry
oven air at 200–230 °C will pull moisture from the surface faster than heat can travel inward,
so every source in this leaf lowers the oven instead: 225 °F (107 °C) or 275 °F (135 °C), with a
single high-heat burst at the start or the end for crust. Carryover is the largest of any method
in this cut's corpus, and the sources say so in numbers — one measures a **10 °F rise** (125 →
135 °F) over a 20-minute rest, another runs a two-stage oven (roast at 225 °F to 110 °F, then
450 °F to 130 °F) that does its final climb in the oven rather than on the bench.

## Source recipes

### 1. Bison Basics (US) — Roast Bison Sirloin Tip
**Inspired by**: https://bisonbasics.com/sirlointip.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Bison Basics (US bison cooking site, © 2017). US low-oven roast tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. This is the only record in the corpus that documents **both** rare and medium-rare as full, separately measured cooks.
**Serves**: not stated (a 3–4 lb / 1.4–1.8 kg roast) · **Prep**: 90 min tempering + 6–8 min browning · **Cook**: ~1 h 20–35 min · **Rest**: 20 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 sirloin tip roast, about 1.4–1.8 kg (3–4 lb)
- 2 tbsp cooking oil (grapeseed or peanut preferred)
- 1–2 tbsp olive oil, **or** 2 strips bacon cut in half
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper

**Method**
1. Take the roast out of the refrigerator and let it stand at room temperature **at least 90 minutes**. (If it needs thawing, thaw in the refrigerator over 2–3 days.)
2. Set the oven to **225 °F (107 °C)**.
3. Pat the roast dry. Heat the cooking oil in a large heavy-bottomed pan over moderately high heat until nearly smoking, then brown the roast on all sides, turning every couple of minutes — **6–8 minutes** total.
4. Coat the browned roast lightly with olive oil (or lay the bacon strips over it), salt and pepper.
5. Put it on an elevated roasting pan, insert a digital probe into the **centre** of the roast, and run the lead out to a monitor outside the closed oven door.
6. **For rare: roast until the monitor reads 115 °F (46 °C)** — about 1 h 20 min. Then **turn the oven off, keep the door closed**, and let the roast coast to **120 °F (49 °C)** before removing it.
7. **For medium-rare: roast until the monitor reads 125 °F (52 °C)** — about 1 h 35 min. Turn the oven off and let it coast to **130 °F (54 °C)** before removing.
8. **Pull at 52 °C (125 °F)** for medium-rare (or **46 °C / 115 °F** for rare) — the source's own staged figures, deliberately below the serving temperature.
9. Rest **20 minutes**, then slice thinly. Final serving temperature: **about 54 °C / 135 °F** for medium-rare (stated — the source calls this the "finish temperature after 20 minutes resting"), or **52 °C / 125 °F** for rare.

### 2. Jackson Hole Buffalo Meat (Courtney Johnston, US) — Reverse Seared Bison Prime Rib Roast
**Inspired by**: https://jhbuffalomeat.com/blogs/news/reverse-seared-bison-prime-rib-roast (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2023-11-28). Jackson Hole Buffalo Meat recipe blog, "Cuisine: American". US reverse-sear tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Cut-form flag**: this roasts a **boneless prime rib** — the rib sub-cut of the roast form, not the chuck.
**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 5 min (+2 h tempering) · **Cook**: 2–3 h · **Rest**: ~10 min per pound (≈22 min/kg)

**Ingredients**
- 1 bone-out bison prime rib roast, 1.4–1.8 kg (3–4 lb)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tbsp granulated garlic
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper

**Method**
1. A couple of hours before cooking, take the roast out and bring it to room temperature.
2. Preheat the oven to **225 °F (107 °C)**.
3. Pat the roast dry, rub with the olive oil and then the garlic, salt and pepper until evenly coated.
4. Put the roast on a rack in a pan. (The source's substitute: thick onion rings, or celery ribs and carrot sticks, to prop it up.)
5. Insert the probe into the **thickest part** of the meat and set the alarm to **110 °F (43 °C)**.
6. When it reaches 110 °F, turn the oven to **450 °F (232 °C)** and reset the probe for **130 °F (54 °C)**; rotate the roast so it sears on all sides.
7. **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)** — the source's own medium-rare target, reached in the hot phase of the reverse sear.
8. Cover loosely with foil and rest **about 10 minutes per pound of roast** so the juices redistribute. Final serving temperature: **not stated as a number by the source** (the 130 °F is the removal point).
9. The page's own argument for the technique, quoted: a Serious Eats study it cites found meat seared first then roasted **lost 1.68 % more juices** than meat roasted first and seared last — "it's not a massive difference, but it's a game-changer in understanding how searing doesn't conclusively lock in juices".

### 3. Back in Time Bison Ranch (US) — The Perfect Bison Roast (sirloin tip and round sections)
**Inspired by**: https://www.backintimebison.com/post/the-perfect-bison-roast (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2025-12-01). Back in Time Bison Ranch (US bison ranchers), describing their own cooks; the sirloin-tip method is credited to the **Wisconsin Bison Producers Association**. US rancher tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Quantity flag**: the ranch writes from experience rather than as a tested recipe card, so its quantities are loose ("about 1 cup of broth", seasoning to taste) and no serving count is given. It is counted because it is the only record in the corpus that gives **different targets for two different roasts** from the same kitchen.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 15–30 min · **Cook**: 45 min–3 h · **Rest**: 15–30 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 sirloin tip roast, or 1 round roast
- Olive oil, for coating
- Garlic salt, onion salt and pepper (the source's blend), to taste
- About 1 cup (240 ml) broth — homemade bone broth or beef broth
- **Round roast only**: bison or beef broth to cover the bottom of the pan

**Method**
1. **Sirloin tip**: coat the roast with olive oil and season with garlic salt, onion salt and pepper. Add about 1 cup of broth to the pan.
2. Preheat the oven to **450 °F (232 °C)** and roast **15 minutes**, then turn the heat down to **300 °F (149 °C)** and continue at **10 minutes per pound** (≈22 min/kg).
3. Probe the centre; the source's stated internal target is **140 °F (60 °C)**.
4. Cover and rest **15–30 minutes**. Slice thin across the grain. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** beyond the 140 °F internal figure.
5. **Round roast** (a cut with "no fat at all"): season and let it sit about 30 minutes, brown in a hot pan about **2 minutes per side**, put broth in the bottom of the pan, then oven at **450 °F for 10 minutes** followed by about **3 hours at 175 °F (79 °C)**.
6. Check the round at 2½ hours and **remove at 145 °F (63 °C)**; wrap completely in foil for 15–30 minutes and slice thin against the grain.
7. **Pull at 60 °C (140 °F)** for the sirloin tip and **63 °C (145 °F)** for the round — the source's own figures, and the highest in this leaf.
8. The source's standing rule, quoted: "low and slow is the way to go with all bison meat and a meat thermometer is a must. Overcooked Bison will be tough."

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Oven and time | Pull | Final after rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bison Basics (US) | US low oven, oven-off carryover | **225 °F**, ~1 h 20 min (rare) / ~1 h 35 min (MR) | **115 °F rare / 125 °F MR** (46 / 52 °C) | **125 °F / 135 °F** (52 / 57 °C) |
| JH Buffalo (US) | US reverse sear, 225 → 450 °F | 225 °F to 110 °F, then 450 °F | **130 °F (54 °C)** | not stated |
| Back in Time (US) | US rancher, 450 → 300 °F or 450 → 175 °F | 10 min/lb at 300 °F; round 3 h at 175 °F | **140 °F (60 °C)** tip / **145 °F (63 °C)** round | not stated |

Three measured rows spanning **52–63 °C at removal**, and the spread is explained entirely by
**which roast is in the oven**, not by disagreement about bison. The two sirloin-tip and rib
records pull at 52–54 °C (125–130 °F) and let carryover finish the job — one of them measuring
the rise (125 → 135 °F, i.e. +5 °C, over 20 minutes), the other doing the climb inside the oven
in a 450 °F sear phase. The rancher's round roast pulls at 63 °C because a round — the driest
sub-cut of the cut — is being sliced thin for sandwiches and benefits from the extra 10 °C.
Mapping to the engine: the registry's `medium_rare` band (52–57 °C) contains the two premium-cut
pulls exactly, and the round's 63 °C sits on the boundary of `medium`. `recommended_doneness` for
this method: **`medium_rare`**, with the note that a round roast is the one sub-cut the corpus
takes to medium. The staged oven-off technique is worth carrying into the engine's probe logic:
the roast is pulled *twice*, once at the alarm and once when the coasting temperature is reached.

## What makes this method special

An oven roast of bison is a race between heat penetration and surface evaporation, and every
record here manipulates the **air** rather than the meat. The lowest oven (225 °F) is paired with
a **timed high-heat burst** — either before (Back in Time's 450 °F for 15 minutes) or after (JH's
450 °F sear at the end) — because a lean roast cannot be both browned and gently heated at the
same temperature. The second distinguishing mechanic is that two of the three sources treat the
oven as a **two-stage device**: Bison Basics turns the oven *off* and lets the roast coast inside
it to a specific second target, and JH raises the heat and re-sets the alarm, so the "rest" is
partly done with residual oven heat rather than on the bench. That is the opposite of the
steak-form advice, where the rest happens under foil on a board. The third is sub-cut
dependence, which this leaf documents better than any other in the corpus: the same rancher gives
a sirloin tip 10 minutes per pound at 300 °F and a round three hours at 175 °F, because a round
has "no fat at all" and needs both a lower temperature and much more time. Carryover dominates
both premium-cut records — one measured at +5 °C over 20 minutes — which is why every one of them
pulls well below its serving target and none of them slices immediately.

## Summary

Oven-roasting bison means a low oven (225–300 °F), a short high-heat burst before or after, and a
probe in the centre from the start. Pull a sirloin tip or rib at 125–130 °F and let a 20-minute
rest carry it to 135 °F; the sources measure that climb rather than guess it, and one turns the
oven off so the roast coasts inside it. A round roast is the exception — no fat at all, so three
hours at 175 °F and out at 145 °F for thin slicing. Never slice without resting, and never trust
the clock over the probe.
