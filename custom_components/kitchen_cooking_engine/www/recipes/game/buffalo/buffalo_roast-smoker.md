# Buffalo Roast × Smoker — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Smoking is the only method in this cut's corpus that treats the roast as a **barbecue problem**
rather than a roast problem: the goal is not a pink centre held at a precise temperature but the
collapse of the chuck's collagen at 90 °C and above, long after the meat has passed through every
doneness band the engine knows. The cut is a **chuck roast of 1–2 kg**, bone-in or boneless, and
its lean structure matters differently here than in an oven: with almost no fat cap, there is
nothing to protect the surface through six or seven hours of moving smoke, so all three sources
intervene with **wrapping** — butcher paper, foil, or a covered pan with broth — at a defined
internal temperature, usually 165 °F. Carryover is negligible at these temperatures: the roast
comes off at 95 °C or above and gets cubed and rested, so the last degrees are irrelevant and no
source relies on them.

## Source recipes

### 1. Benjamin Lee Bison (US) — Smoked Pulled BBQ Bison
**Inspired by**: https://benjaminleebison.com/blogs/bison-roasts-and-bbq-recipes/smoked-pulled-bison-roast (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2021-05-26). Benjamin Lee Bison (US bison producer). US backyard-smoker tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Quantity flag**: the source calls itself "a shell of a recipe" and leaves the rub to the cook, so the spice quantities below are not the page's.
**Serves**: not stated (a 1–1.8 kg roast) · **Prep**: 1 h salting · **Cook**: ~7–8 h · **Rest**: not stated as a time

**Ingredients**
- 0.9–1.8 kg (2–4 lb) bison chuck roast
- Kosher salt — **½ tsp per pound of meat** (the one measured ratio the source gives)
- 1–2 cups beef or bison broth, for spritzing and for the wrap
- A favourite BBQ rub, applied "completely coating it" (the page names Black Hills BBQ Seasoning from Savory & Spice and lists its components)
- Hickory and mesquite hardwood

**Method**
1. Heat the smoker to **225 °F (107 °C)** on hardwood.
2. Take the roast out of the fridge **at least an hour** before smoking and rub ½ tsp of salt per pound over it; let it sit 1 hour.
3. Rub the BBQ rub over the whole roast.
4. Place the bison in the centre of the smoker, directly on the grate, and **insert the thermometer** — the source's own advice is to leave an accurate digital probe in the meat, in the centre away from bones, for the entire cook.
5. Adjust the vent to keep the smoke circulating and the smoker at **225 °F**, spritzing with broth **every 1–2 hours**.
6. **Smoke until the internal temperature reaches 165 °F (74 °C)**.
7. Remove, put on foil with the sides folded up, add some broth, and **wrap tightly**; return to the smoker.
8. **Cook until the internal temperature reaches 205 °F (96 °C)**, then remove, open the foil to vent and rest.
9. **Pull at 96 °C (205 °F)** — the source's own second-stage target, after the 74 °C wrap point.
10. Rest, then shred. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**; serve with BBQ sauce and sides.

### 2. The Roaming Bison Ranch (Laura Wheeler, US) — RBR Smoked Chuck Roast
**Inspired by**: https://roamingbisonranch.com/recipes/rbr-smoked-chuck-roast (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2021-04-12). The Roaming Bison Ranch (US bison ranch recipe blog), "recipe designed for pellet smoker such as Pit Boss or Traeger". US pellet-smoker tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 1 roast · **Prep**: 10 min (+24–36 h optional dry-brine) · **Cook**: 7 h · **Rest**: 20 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 grass-finished bison (or beef) chuck roast
- ½ cup apple cider vinegar, for spritzing
- 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tbsp sea salt; 1 tbsp ground pepper
- 2 tsp chili powder; 1 tsp paprika; 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- Hot beef broth, for the pan (quantity not stated)

**Method**
1. Thaw the roast. Mix the dry rub — salt, pepper, chili powder, paprika and Italian seasoning — in a small bowl.
2. Coat the roast liberally with Worcestershire sauce, then coat freely with the dry rub. The source's optional step: bag it in the refrigerator for **24–36 hours** for better flavour.
3. Bring the smoker to **200–225 °F (93–107 °C)** and let the roast come to room temperature — the source calls this "very important".
4. Smoke until the internal temperature reaches **145 °F (63 °C)**, expected to take **around 4–5 hours**, spritzing with apple cider vinegar **every hour**.
5. Warm the broth and pour it into a BBQ-safe pan — the source's reason is that hot broth "prevents it from cooling the roast". Put the roast in the pan, cover with aluminium foil and return it to the smoker.
6. Turn the smoker up to **275 °F (135 °C)**, monitoring the internal temperature: **205–210 °F (96–99 °C) for a "pulled" texture**, or remove at **195 °F (91 °C)** for slicing or chopping. Expect about **2 hours** for this stage.
7. **Pull at 96–99 °C (205–210 °F)** for pulled, or **91 °C (195 °F)** for slicing — the source's own figures, given as a choice of texture.
8. Rest **20 minutes** (the page says to rest it in an oven), then chop, slice or shred. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.
9. The cooking broth can be kept as stock or used as a dipping sauce.

### 3. The Taste of Montana (Mountain Man Radd, US) — Bison Burnt Ends
**Inspired by**: https://thetasteofmontana.com/bison-burnt-ends/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2023-04-13). The Taste of Montana (US regional food site), "Cuisine: Smoked". US Kansas City burnt-ends tradition applied to bison — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 6 h · **Rest**: 20 min

**Ingredients**
- 1.36 kg (3 lb) bison chuck roast
- ½ cup Headwaters BBQ Baldy Mountain Beef Rub (or another beef rub)
- 2 tbsp brown sugar, plus ¼ cup for the pan
- ½ cup Hot Dr. Pepper BBQ sauce — made from 12 oz Dr. Pepper, 2 cups ketchup, ½ yellow onion finely diced, ½ tbsp unsalted butter, 1 cup brown sugar, 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, ½ tsp smoky heat rub, 1 tsp mango habanero sauce
- Hickory wood chunks

**Method**
1. Heat the smoker to **275 °F (135 °C)** and add hickory chunks.
2. Rub the chuck roast liberally on all sides with the beef rub and put it on the smoker, lid closed.
3. Smoke until the internal temperature reaches **165 °F (74 °C)** — roughly **3–4 hours**; a heavy, dark bark will have formed.
4. Wrap in butcher paper or foil and return to the smoker until the internal temperature hits **195 °F (91 °C)**.
5. Remove and rest **20 minutes**.
6. Cut into **1-inch cubes**, put them in a foil baking pan, sprinkle with ¼ cup brown sugar and drizzle with the BBQ sauce; toss gently to coat.
7. **Pull/cube at 91 °C (195 °F)** — the source's own wrap-stage target, reached before the cubes go back on; they then cook a further **1–1½ hours** in the sauce, which is where the burnt-end texture is built.
8. Return the pan to the still-hot smoker for **1–1½ hours**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**; serve immediately.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Smoker setting | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Pull |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benjamin Lee Bison (US) | US offset smoker, hickory + mesquite | **225 °F** | **165 °F (74 °C)** then foil + broth | to **205 °F (96 °C)** | **96 °C (205 °F)** |
| Roaming Bison Ranch (US) | US pellet smoker | **200–225 °F** → **275 °F** | **145 °F (63 °C)** then pan + hot broth + foil | to **195 °F slice / 205–210 °F pulled** | **91 °C (195 °F)** or **96–99 °C** |
| Taste of Montana (US) | US KC burnt ends, hickory | **275 °F** | **165 °F (74 °C)** then wrap | to **195 °F (91 °C)** | **91 °C (195 °F)**, then 1–1½ h sauced |

Three measured protocols that agree on the destination and disagree on the route. The
**wrap point** is where the corpus splits: two sources wrap at **165 °F** and one at **145 °F**,
and the earlier wrap is paired with a *lower* first-stage smoker setting (200–225 °F, 4–5 hours),
so it is really a stall-management choice — wrap early and spend longer in the pan, or wrap at
the classic 165 °F and finish quickly. The **hit-the-wall temperature** is much tighter:
**195 °F for slicing, 205–210 °F for pulling**, in all three. Note what these numbers mean for
the engine: every one of them is far above the cut's `medium_rare` band, and the roast passes
through 52–63 °C within the first two hours. `recommended_doneness` for this method is therefore
recorded as the cut default only for registry consistency; the probe targets that matter are the
**74 °C wrap** and the **91–99 °C finish**, and the text below says so rather than pretending the
strip-loin bands apply.

## What makes this method special

Three mechanics define smoking a bison roast. First, **evaporative cooling dominates the middle
of the cook**: the surface of a lean roast with no fat cap starts shedding water as soon as it
heats, and the internal temperature stalls somewhere around 65–75 °C while that happens — which is
exactly why every source wraps at or just past that point, whether it calls it 145 °F or 165 °F.
Second, **the wrap is the braise**: foil with broth, a covered pan, or butcher paper all trap
moisture so the meat finishes in a saturated atmosphere, which is the same physics as the braise
leaf but reached from the smoke side, and the roasting juices that collect become the sauce. Third,
**smoke is a surface treatment, not a cooking method**: the bark forms in the first three or four
hours, before the wrap, and after that the roast is effectively being braised in a smoky
environment. The burnt-ends record shows the endpoint of that logic — the meat is deliberately
cooked past fork-tender, cubed, sauced and returned to the smoker so the exterior caramelises a
second time. Salt timing is the one point the sources differ on in a way that is not about
temperature: Benjamin Lee salts an hour ahead at a measured rate, Roaming Bison dry-brines for
24–36 hours, and Taste of Montana simply rubs and goes.

## Summary

Smoking a bison chuck roast is a 6–8 hour cook at 200–275 °F that ends not at a doneness band but
at 195 °F for slicing or 205–210 °F for pulling. Wrap at 165 °F with broth in the foil (or earlier
at 145 °F if you want a longer pan finish), keep the surface wet with a spritz, and rest twenty
minutes before you shred. The wrap is the whole trick: a lean roast with no fat cap stalls
without it, and the juices it traps become the sauce. Nothing in this method is served pink.
