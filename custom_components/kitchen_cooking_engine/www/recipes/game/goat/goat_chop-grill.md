# Goat Chop × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

A goat loin or rib chop is the same anatomical piece as a lamb chop — loin from between the last
rib and the hip, rib from the rack — but smaller and markedly leaner, typically 2–3 cm thick and
around 100–150 g each. Grilling suits it because the cut is naturally tender and needs very
little time: the muscles that make goat a braising animal are in the shoulder and leg, not here.
The problem grilling creates is that there is almost no fat to insulate the meat, so the window
between pink and dry is short and the fire has to be managed rather than simply hot. Two
compensations recur in the fetched records: a **marinade or dry brine** that flavours and lightly
tenderises the surface, and a **two-zone fire** — sear over the coals, finish over the thin side —
which lets a small chop build a crust without its centre overshooting. Carryover is real but
modest on a thin chop; the pulling temperatures in this leaf are deliberately below the target
because the rest adds a few degrees.

## Source recipes

### 1. The Splendid Table (Lynne Rossetto Kasper, Italy/Puglia) — Grilled Goat Chops with Garlic, Oregano and Lemon
**Inspired by**: https://www.splendidtable.org/story/2013/06/07/grilled-goat-chops-with-garlic-oregano-and-lemon (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2013-06-07). The Splendid Table (US public-radio food programme), recipe from the author's travels in **Puglia, Italy**. Italian wood-fire tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 15 min (+1½–2 h marinating) · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 6 large garlic cloves
- 1 medium onion, coarsely chopped
- ½ cup plus 2 tbsp good extra-virgin olive oil
- Shredded zest of 2 large lemons
- Juice of 1 large lemon (about 6 tbsp)
- ½ cup dry white wine
- 4 tsp dried oregano
- ¼ tsp each salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 8–9 small rib or loin goat chops, **1 inch (2.5 cm) thick**

**Method**
1. In a food processor combine the garlic, onion, ½ cup of the oil, the lemon zest, lemon juice, wine, oregano, salt and pepper. Process to **just short of a purée** — the source wants visible bits of rind, onion and garlic.
2. Pour the marinade into a heavy plastic bag or bowl, toss the chops through it, and refrigerate **1½–2 hours**.
3. **On the grill**: burn hardwood charcoal until **grey ash** forms, then build a **two-zone fire** — a heap of coals on one side for searing, a thin layer on the other for slow cooking.
4. Drain the chops but **do not wipe off the marinade**. Sear over the high heat, then move them to the low side to finish, cooking slowly.
5. The source's doneness test is manual: **"when you press them and they seem to be barely firm, they are done"** — and they should be "blushed with pink inside".
6. **Pull at 57 °C (135 °F)** — house guidance, **not the source's number**: the page publishes no temperature, only the finger test. 57 °C is the house pull for the source's "blushed with pink" description on a 2.5 cm chop. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.
7. Serve hot. (The same marinade works in a skillet: brown quickly on both sides in the remaining 2 tbsp oil over medium-high, then 2 minutes a side over medium-low.)

### 2. Butter Over Bae (Wajiha, Pakistan) — Mutton Chaap Fry (goat chops, steamed then grilled)
**Inspired by**: https://butteroverbae.com/mutton-chaap-fry/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2021-11-12). Butter Over Bae (Pakistani food blog), 4.89/5 from 17 votes. Pakistani/South Asian *chaap* tradition — **Culinary Group A** source. **Technique flag**: the chops are **steamed in their marinade first**, then charred — the only record in this leaf where the grill is a finishing step rather than the cooking method.
**Serves**: 10 pieces · **Prep**: 5 min (+30 min resting) · **Cook**: 45 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g goat chops, about 9–10 pieces (the source uses **goat ribs**; lamb or beef chops also work)
- ½ cup yogurt
- ½ tbsp black pepper powder
- ½ tbsp garam masala powder (or allspice/baharat)
- 1 tsp red chilli powder
- 1 tsp table salt, or to taste
- 2 tbsp white vinegar
- 1 tbsp garlic-ginger paste
- 2 tbsp oil, plus extra for grilling

**Method**
1. Whisk all the marinade ingredients to a smooth consistency, toss the chops through, and leave on the counter **30 minutes** so the meat and yogurt come to room temperature.
2. Transfer the chops to a pot with **1 cup water**, cover, and simmer over **low heat 30–40 minutes**.
3. After 30 minutes check with a fork: if tender, cook with the lid off to evaporate the liquid. If still firm at 40 minutes, add more water and simmer 10 minutes more.
4. Lightly grease a grill pan (or *tawa* or frying pan) and grill the chops **on both sides over high heat until charred golden brown**.
5. There is **no temperature in this record**; the doneness signals are the fork test during the simmer and the char during the grill.
6. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — house guidance, **not the source's number**: the page publishes none, and by the time these chops hit the pan they are already fully cooked from the simmer, so the grill step only chars the surface.
7. Serve warm with lemon slices and a yogurt dip.

### 3. Forager Chef (Alan Bergo, US) — Rosemary Crusted Goat Chops (grill section)
**Inspired by**: https://foragerchef.com/goat-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2023-12-16). Forager Chef (US chef and author; the goat in the recipe is from Shepherd Song Farm). US dry-brine tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Multi-method flag**: the page documents a pan sear as its main method and a grill alternative; this record covers the **grill** section, and the pan sear is recorded in the `pan_sear` leaf.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 min (+2–24 h dry brine) · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: 4–5 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 goat chops, about 340 g (12 oz) total
- Kosher salt and black pepper
- 1 tsp finely chopped rosemary
- 2 tsp light olive oil or cooking oil
- Serving: lemon wedges; optional 2 tbsp unsalted butter, a rosemary sprig, flaky salt, 2 tsp sliced chives

**Method**
1. Score the fat of the chops in a cross-hatch, season all over with salt and pepper, and **dry-brine uncovered in the refrigerator 2–24 hours** — ideally overnight.
2. Bring the chops out **30 minutes** before cooking.
3. **To grill**: scrape and heat the grill, rub the grate with an oiled paper towel, toss the chops with 1 tsp oil and grill **3–4 minutes on each side**. The source's own warning: you want slow cooking on the grill, and rendering the fat over live fire risks flare-ups.
4. Use a thermometer and **aim for 5 °F under your target**.
5. **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)** for medium-rare to medium, or **57 °C (135 °F)** for medium to medium-well — the source's own figures.
6. Rest in a warm place **4–5 minutes** before serving. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** beyond the pull figures.
7. Serve with a lemon wedge, and eat the meat off the bones by hand — "this is part of the fun of eating small chops".

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Fire | Published doneness evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Splendid Table (Italy) | Puglian wood/charcoal, two-zone | grey-ash hardwood, sear then slow side | **No number** — finger test, "barely firm", "blushed with pink" |
| Butter Over Bae (Pakistan) | Pakistani *chaap*, steamed then charred | grill pan, high heat | **No number** — fork test, then char |
| Forager Chef (US) | US dry-brine, hot grate | hot grill, 3–4 min/side | **130 °F (54 °C)** medium-rare–medium; **135 °F (57 °C)** medium–medium-well |

Only one of the three records publishes a pull temperature, and its labels sit **lower** than the
US convention: the same chef calls 130 °F "medium-rare to medium" and 135 °F "medium to
medium-well". The two silent records are just as informative in a different way — both judge
doneness by **touch or by a fork**, and both describe the finished chop as pink inside (Italy) or
fork-tender before charring (Pakistan), which places them in the same 54–60 °C territory rather
than the 68–74 °C range the cut's old profile claimed. Mapping to the engine: this corpus lands
in the `medium_rare` and `medium` bands (52–63 °C), **not** in `well_done` (68–77 °C), and that
disagreement with the cut's `recommended_doneness` is recorded in the hub rather than papered
over. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`medium_rare`** (52–57 °C) for thin rib chops,
moving to `medium` for thicker loin chops.

## What makes this method special

Grilling a goat chop is a race between crust and centre on a piece of meat with almost no fat
buffer. The three records solve it in three different ways, and the differences are the method's
craft. The Italian record sears over a heap of coals and then **retreats to a thin coal bed** to
finish — a two-zone fire built specifically because the chops are small enough to burn before they
are done, and it leaves the marinade on for the crust. The Pakistani record inverts the order
entirely: the chops are **simmered until fork-tender in their own marinade**, then charred for
seconds on a grill pan, which is a braise with a grill's finish and makes the fire purely a
surface tool. The US record contributes the third idea — a **dry brine of 2–24 hours**, which
seasons to the centre and dries the surface so it browns faster, the same argument a steak gets.
What all three share is that the grill is used at high heat for a very short time and that none of
them trusts the clock: the signals are finger pressure, a fork, or a thermometer pulled 5 °F under
target. The flare-up risk from rendering fat is the one hazard two of them name explicitly.

## Summary

Grill goat chops hot and fast — 2–4 minutes a side on a 2.5 cm chop — over a two-zone fire so the
crust forms before the lean centre overshoots. Marinate 1½–2 hours in lemon, garlic, oregano and
olive oil, or dry-brine overnight and grill bare; either way pull at 130–135 °F and rest five
minutes. The Pakistani route simmers the chops first and only chars them at the end. Every source
judges doneness by touch, fork or thermometer, and every one of them expects pink.
