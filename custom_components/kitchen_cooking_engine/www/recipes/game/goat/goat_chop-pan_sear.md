# Goat Chop × Pan Sear — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

A goat loin or rib chop is small, lean and naturally tender — the same anatomical piece as a lamb
chop, roughly 100–150 g and 2–3 cm thick, with a thin fat layer over the eye muscle. A heavy pan
is the method that suits that structure best, because the chop needs total surface contact rather
than air: there is so little subcutaneous fat that a grill's hot air dries the exposed face while
the middle is still pink, whereas cast iron can be held hotter, heats the fat side first so it
renders and crisps, and lets the cook finish a thick chop in a moderate oven. Both US records here
do exactly that — fat side down in a cold or medium pan, then a short hot sear, then either the
oven or a butter baste. Carryover is small but worth counting on a chop this size: one source
pulls 5 °F under target by rule, another rests 5–10 minutes, and the same chef notes the rise can
be "several degrees" after a hard sear.

## Source recipes

### 1. Forager Chef (Alan Bergo, US) — Rosemary Crusted Goat Chops
**Inspired by**: https://foragerchef.com/goat-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2023-12-16). Forager Chef (US chef and author of *The Forager Chef's Book of Flora*). US dry-brine and cast-iron tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Multi-method flag**: the page's grill alternative is recorded in the `grill` leaf; this record covers its **pan sear**.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 min (+2–24 h dry brine, +30 min tempering) · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: 4–5 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 goat chops, about 340 g (12 oz) total
- Kosher salt and black pepper
- 1 tsp finely chopped rosemary
- 2 tsp light olive oil or cooking oil
- Optional finish: 2 tbsp unsalted butter, 1 small rosemary sprig, flaky salt, 2 tsp sliced chives
- Serving: lemon wedges

**Method**
1. Score the fat in a cross-hatch pattern. Season all sides with salt and pepper and refrigerate **uncovered 2–24 hours** — ideally overnight. This is the source's core technique: a dry brine that seasons to the centre and dries the surface for browning.
2. Take the chops out **30 minutes** before cooking to come to room temperature.
3. Heat a **cold cast-iron skillet** with the oil and put the chops in **fat side down**. Cook **2–3 minutes** until browned over medium heat, then turn the heat to **high**.
4. Flip and cook **2 minutes on each side** over high heat. Optionally add the garlic, rosemary and butter at the end and baste, turning the heat off so the butter does not burn.
5. "If using a digital thermometer, **aim for 5 degrees under your desired temperature**" — the source's rule.
6. **Pull at 54 °C (130 °F)** for medium-rare to medium, or **57 °C (135 °F)** for medium to medium-well — the source's own figures.
7. Rest on a plate lined with paper towel in a warm place **4–5 minutes**, then serve with lemon wedges. Final serving temperature: **not stated beyond the pull figures**.
8. The source's equipment note: without a good hood, open a window or use the grill/broiler route instead — a hard sear on a lean chop smokes heavily.

### 2. Shepherd Song Farm (Chef Alan Bergo, US) — Pan-Fried Goat Chops with Herb Butter
**Inspired by**: https://shepherdsongfarm.com/lamb-and-goat-recipes/spring-goat-chops-with-ramp-butter/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2021-04-24). Shepherd Song Farm (US grass-fed lamb and goat farm), recipe by James Beard Award-winning chef Alan Bergo. US farm-to-table tradition — **Culinary Group B** source. **Cut-form flag**: this cooks a **rack of goat cut into two-bone double chops**, not single loin chops — a thicker piece, which is why it finishes in the oven.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min (+optional overnight dry brine) · **Cook**: 17 min · **Rest**: 5–10 min

**Ingredients**
- 450 g (16 oz) goat rack — "2 bones per person, or roughly half a rack"
- 225 g (8 oz) fresh wild spring greens (or a mix of spinach, chard and kale)
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- ¼ cup ramp butter (*ramp* — wild leek; substitute garlic butter finished with lemon)
- A few dashes fresh lemon juice

**Method**
1. Trim the fat on top of the rack to about ⅛–¼ inch, and remove the thin membrane from the underside of the rib bones (optional, "but typically done in restaurants").
2. Score the fat in a ½-inch cross-hatch to help it render and crisp. Cut the rack in half into two **2-bone chops**, season all over with salt and pepper, and optionally rest uncovered in the fridge overnight.
3. Preheat the oven to **350 °F (177 °C)**.
4. Put the chops **fat-side down in a 10-inch cast-iron skillet** over **medium-high** and heat until the fat starts to render; drain the fat as needed.
5. Continue on the stove until the fat side is **golden brown**, then "gently kiss the sides for a few seconds".
6. Transfer the skillet to the oven, **fat-side down, and cook 10 minutes — or until a thermometer inserted in the centre reads 135 °F (57 °C)**.
7. **Pull at 57 °C (135 °F)** — the source's own figure, and the highest in this leaf.
8. Rest on paper towel in a warm place **at least 5–10 minutes** before slicing. Final serving temperature: **not stated beyond the 135 °F**.
9. Meanwhile steam the greens 3–4 minutes, melt the ramp butter with a dash of lemon, and serve the chops halved, bones up, with the butter drizzled over both.

### 3. Butter Over Bae (Wajiha, Pakistan) — Mutton Chaap (steam then pan/grill finish)
**Inspired by**: https://butteroverbae.com/mutton-chaap-fry/ (fetched 2026-10-26, published 2021-11-12). Butter Over Bae (Pakistani food blog), 4.89/5 from 17 votes. Pakistani *chaap* tradition — **Culinary Group A** source. **Multi-method flag**: its final step is described as "grill or pan fry" and is recorded in both the `grill` and `pan_sear` leaves. **Technique flag**: the chops are simmered to tenderness before they ever meet the pan.
**Serves**: 10 pieces · **Prep**: 5 min (+30 min) · **Cook**: 45 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g goat chops, about 9–10 pieces (goat ribs in the source; lamb or beef work)
- ½ cup yogurt
- ½ tbsp black pepper powder
- ½ tbsp garam masala powder (or allspice/baharat)
- 1 tsp red chilli powder
- 1 tsp table salt, or to taste
- 2 tbsp white vinegar
- 1 tbsp garlic-ginger paste
- 2 tbsp oil, plus extra for the pan

**Method**
1. Whisk the marinade smooth, coat the chops, and rest **30 minutes** on the counter.
2. Transfer to a pot with **1 cup water**, cover and simmer over **low heat 30–40 minutes** until a fork slides in easily; then cook uncovered to evaporate the liquid.
3. Heat a grill pan, *tawa* or frying pan with a little oil and **pan-fry over high heat on both sides until charred golden brown**.
4. The source gives **no temperature**; its signals are the fork test and the char.
5. **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** — house guidance, **not the source's number**: by the time these chops reach the pan they are already cooked through by the simmer, so this step is purely surface browning.
6. Serve warm with lemon and a yogurt dip. The source notes the cooked chops can be frozen at the post-simmer stage and pan-fried from thawed.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Pan and finish | Pull |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forager Chef (US) | US dry-brine, cast iron, butter baste | cold pan fat-side down, 2–3 min medium + 2 min/side high | **130 °F (54 °C)** MR–M; **135 °F (57 °C)** M–MW |
| Shepherd Song Farm (US) | US farm kitchen, rack → double chops | cast iron 2–3 min, then **350 °F oven 10 min** | **135 °F (57 °C)** |
| Butter Over Bae (Pakistan) | Pakistani *chaap*, simmer then char | high-heat pan, surface only | **No number** — fork test then char |

Two US records converge tightly on **130–135 °F (54–57 °C)** at removal, and the same chef who
wrote both gives the useful rule behind it: aim **5 °F under** the target, because the rest adds
it back. The Pakistani record publishes no number but is not a contradiction — its chops are
already fork-tender from a 30–40 minute simmer before the pan, so its pan stage is browning, not
cooking. That distinction matters for the engine: a steam-then-sear chop and a raw-seared chop
look identical in a pan but are at completely different points in the cook. Mapping to the
engine: 54–57 °C is the cut's `medium_rare` band, well below the cut's current `well_done`
recommendation — a mismatch recorded in the hub. `recommended_doneness` for this method:
**`medium_rare`**, with `medium` for the thicker rack-cut double chops.

## What makes this method special

A pan sears a goat chop where a grill cannot: on **one flat face at a time, in fat that stays in
the pan**. That matters because the chop's only fat is a thin layer over the eye, and rendering it
into the pan gives the cook something to baste with — which is exactly what the US records do,
one by adding butter, rosemary and garlic at the end and the other by starting the chop **fat side
down in a cold pan** so the layer renders slowly instead of scorching. The second mechanic is that
the pan can be moved: Shepherd Song's rack-cut chops go from stove to a 350 °F oven for ten
minutes, which is the pan's answer to thickness — you cannot sear a two-bone chop to the centre
without burning the crust, so you finish it in air. The third is the **dry brine**, which both US
records use and neither treats as optional: salt on the surface for 2–24 hours seasons to the
centre and dries the exterior, and on a lean chop that is the difference between a crust and a
grey band. The Pakistani record adds the fourth idea from a different tradition entirely — cook
the chop first, brown it last — and it is worth knowing that this is a legitimate way to get a
tender, charred goat chop in a pan, not a shortcut.

## Summary

Pan-sear goat chops in cast iron: render the fat side first in a cold or medium pan, then two
minutes a side over high heat, and pull at 130–135 °F — five degrees under target, because the
rest finishes the job. Thick double chops go into a 350 °F oven for ten minutes instead of
finishing on the stove. Dry-brine overnight if you have the time; it seasons to the centre and
gives a better crust. The Pakistani route simmers first and chars last, which is a different but
legitimate way to the same tender result.
