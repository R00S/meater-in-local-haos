## Cut profile

Braising is the dominant world treatment for goat leg, and for a reason rooted in the cut:
the leg's connective tissue is abundant and the muscle is leaner than lamb's, so hours of
gentle moist heat convert collagen to gelatin while the assertive fat mellows into the sauce.
All three fetched sources cut the leg into bone-in pieces (or use it in chunks), brown it,
then simmer it for 2–2½ hours in a flavoured liquid — curry (Jamaican), chile paste and
chicha (Ecuadorian), ancho marinade and tomato broth (Mexican). Not one states an internal
temperature: in every tradition the endpoint is "very tender", which for this cut means
well above the poultry-style safe line, in the 80+ °C region where collagen has fully
melted. No numeric endpoint is invented here.

## Source recipes

### 1. Jamaican Curry Goat — Chris De La Rosa / CaribbeanPot.com
**Inspired by**: https://caribbeanpot.com/jamaican-curry-goat-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Chris De La Rosa, CaribbeanPot.com; Jamaican tradition. The ingredient line says "2 1/2 lbs goat"; the author explicitly recommends goat meat with bones, cut by the butcher.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated (marinate overnight or at least 2 h) · **Cook**: about 2 h (1½ h + ~30 min) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 1/2 lbs goat (bone-in recommended)
- 3/4 teaspoon salt
- 2 small onions (or 1 large)
- 1/2 scotch bonnet pepper
- 1 1/2–2 tablespoon curry powder
- 2 tablespoon vegetable oil
- 5 sprigs thyme
- 2 scallions (chopped)
- 1 bay leaf (optional)
- 8 allspice berries (pimento berries)
- 3 cups water
- 3 medium potatoes
- (The page also describes washing the meat with lime or lemon juice, not listed in the ingredients.)

**Method**
1. Wash the goat with water and lime or lemon juice; drain.
2. In a large bowl, mix the goat with the salt, onion, scotch bonnet and curry powder. Refrigerate overnight or at least 2 hours.
3. Heat the oil in a wide, heavy pan with a lid over medium heat. Add the marinated goat and **sear, uncovered, about 5 minutes**.
4. Add the remaining listed ingredients except the water and mix. Add water to the seasoning bowl, swish to collect the remaining marinade, then pour it into the pot.
5. Bring to a boil over high heat, reduce to a gentle simmer, **cover, and cook slowly 1½ hours**.
6. Add the potatoes, tuck them under the liquid, and cook about **30 minutes** more. Taste and adjust salt.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is time plus tenderness.

### 2. Birria de Chivo (Mexican Goat Stew) — Chef's Pencil
**Inspired by**: https://www.chefspencil.com/birria-de-chivo/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Chef's Pencil; Mexican tradition. Ingredient line: "4 ½ pounds goat leg" (whether the leg is cut up or bone-in is not stated by the page).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 35 min (+8 h marinating) · **Cook**: 4 h 20 min · **Rest**: not stated (covered to keep warm)

**Ingredients**
- 3 ancho chili peppers
- 1 cup white vinegar
- 15 whole black peppercorns
- 1-inch piece fresh ginger root
- 2 garlic cloves, peeled
- 3 whole cloves
- 1 pinch marjoram
- 1 pinch ground cumin
- 1 pinch thyme
- 4 1/2 pounds goat leg

*Meat sauce*
- 2 cups water
- 3 whole black peppercorns
- 2 pounds plum tomatoes
- 2 garlic cloves, peeled
- pinch dried marjoram
- pinch dried thyme
- pinch ground cumin
- salt to taste
- 2 whole cloves

*Hot sauce*
- 30 chiles de arbol
- 1/4 cup white vinegar
- 10 whole black peppercorns
- 1 garlic clove
- 2 white onions, minced

**Method**
1. Bring a pot of water to a boil. Add the ancho peppers and boil about 5 minutes; turn off the heat and soak them in the hot water another 10 minutes.
2. Blend the softened chilies with the 1 cup vinegar, ginger, 2 garlic cloves, 3 cloves, marjoram, cumin and thyme into a paste. Strain excess liquid and transfer the marinade to a bowl.
3. Coat the goat evenly in the marinade, cover, and refrigerate **8 hours (overnight)**.
4. Preheat the oven to **350 ℉ / 175 ℃**.
5. Put the meat in a baking tray or dish, **cover with foil or a lid, and bake about 3½ hours** or until cooked through.
6. Fifteen minutes before the meat is ready, uncover and cook until roasted (the page's wording here is self-contradictory — cover to keep warm, then "uncover it and cook it again for 15 minutes until the meat gets brown"; recorded as published).
7. Pour the meat juices into a container and save them.
8. Boil the tomatoes 5–10 minutes until softened; drain, cool and peel.
9. Blend the tomatoes with the saved meat juice, 2 cups water, garlic, peppercorns, cloves, thyme, marjoram and cumin until smooth.
10. Bring the meat sauce to a boil over medium-high heat, then reduce to medium and simmer, stirring often.
11. Boil the chiles de arbol 5 minutes; drain. Blend with the 1/4 cup vinegar, peppercorns, garlic and salt into a smooth hot sauce; strain.
12. Chop the meat into bite-sized pieces and serve with the meat sauce and hot sauce, onions on the side.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is "until cooked through" and, after chopping, the sauce's consistency.

### 3. Seco de Chivo (Ecuadorian Braised Goat Stew) — Laylita's Recipes
**Inspired by**: https://www.laylita.com/recipes/seco-de-chivo-or-braised-goat-stew/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Laylita; Ecuadorian tradition. Ingredient line: "2 lb goat meat with bones, cut in medium to large chunks." (This recipe is a stovetop braise; it was misfiled under `oven_roast` in the previous version of this cut's research and is reclassified here.)

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min (+≥1 h marinating) · **Cook**: 2 h 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 lb goat meat with bones, cut in medium to large chunks
- 6 garlic cloves, crushed
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp dry oregano
- 2 cups chicha (corn beer; pale beer may substitute)
- 2 tbsp oil
- 2 tsp ground achiote
- 1 red onion, minced
- 1 bell pepper, minced
- 3 tomatoes, cut into quarters
- 1 ají or spicy pepper (red jalapeño or serrano)
- 1/2 bunch cilantro
- 2 whole naranjillas, fresh or frozen, or 1/2 cup naranjilla juice (from frozen pulp)
- 2 tbsp grated panela or piloncillo
- 1 tsp allspice
- 2 tbsp cilantro, finely chopped
- Salt to taste

**Method**
1. Season the goat with the garlic, cumin, oregano, salt and 1 cup of the chicha or beer. Marinate at least an hour.
2. Remove the meat from the marinade and save the marinade liquid.
3. Heat the oil over **high heat** in a large sauté pan and fry the meat until browned on each side.
4. Lower the heat to **medium**. Add the minced onion, achiote and minced bell pepper; cook until the onion is soft, about 5 minutes.
5. Blend the tomatoes, cilantro and hot peppers with the remaining cup of chicha and the naranjillas or naranjilla juice. Strain the purée and add it to the meat.
6. Add the reserved marinade, panela and allspice. Bring to a boil, lower the heat, and **simmer until the meat is very tender and the sauce thickens, about 2–2½ hours**. (The page does not specify whether to cover.)
7. Taste and add salt if needed; sprinkle with chopped cilantro and serve warm.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is "meat very tender, sauce thickened".

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Jamaican (CaribbeanPot, curry goat) | Not stated — 2 h simmer, tenderness endpoint | Not stated |
| Mexican (Chef's Pencil, birria de chivo) | "cooked through" — 350 ℉/175 ℃ covered braise | Not stated |
| Ecuadorian (Laylita, seco de chivo) | "meat very tender" | Not stated |

No fetched braise source states an internal or final serving temperature, so no numeric range
can be observed and none is invented. The convergence is qualitative and strong: all three
traditions cook the leg pieces until collagen-meltingly tender over 2–2½ hours of moist heat,
which physically implies an internal temperature well past the cut file's 63 °C safe line and
into the 80 °C-plus zone where connective tissue dissolves — but that is physics, not source
data, and is not written into any pull step here. The variation between the sources is in
liquid and thickening (curry-and-potato, chile-and-chicha, ancho-and-tomato), not in doneness.

## What makes this method special

Braising is what goat leg is built for: the cut's collagen content turns hours of moist heat
from a compromise into the point, and the resulting gelatin body is what gives curry goat,
birria and seco their clinging, glossy sauces. The three fetched sources all sear first —
fond and fat rendering for the sauce base — then hold the meat just below a simmer in liquid
that absorbs the goat's assertive flavour instead of wasting it. Unlike lamb leg, goat leg is
rarely served pink in these traditions; the long braise both tenderises and tempers the gaminess
that acid marinades (chicha, lime wash, vinegar-chile paste) begin addressing.

## Summary

Braising is goat leg's home method: bone-in pieces seared and simmered for hours until the connective tissue gives way, in curry, chile-beer and ancho-tomato forms across three continents. It is the best structural match for the cut, and the world's goat-leg cooking agrees on tenderness as the endpoint. None of it is measured, so this method contributes tradition and technique, not temperatures.
