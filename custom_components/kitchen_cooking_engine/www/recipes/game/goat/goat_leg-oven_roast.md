## Cut profile

Whole roasted goat leg is festive cooking in Germany (Ziegenkeule), Greece (katsiki-style
roasts), South Asia (raan), the Caribbean and Russia. The cut is lean-ish and collagen-heavy,
so every fetched source protects it the same way — a long covered or foil-wrapped phase at
moderate heat (130–200 °C) for 2–3½ hours, then an uncovered finish for colour. Acid or
dairy marinades (buttermilk, yoghurt, lemon) precede nearly all of them to temper gaminess.
Two of the four fetched sources publish internal-temperature guidance (≈70 °C for goat, and
160–165 °F general fork-tender guidance); the others cook to time and bone separation.

## Source recipes

### 1. Ziegenkeule im Römertopf — Chefkoch.de community recipe
**Inspired by**: https://www.chefkoch.de/rezepte/45381015326785/Ziegenkeule-im-Roemertopf.html (recorded fetched 2026-04-24; the page returned 403 on re-check 2026-10-24 — the recipe text was corroborated on 2026-10-24 via a reproduced copy at q33ny.jimdofree.de/galerie/rezepte, matching the recorded ingredients and method). German tradition (Ziegenkeule, goat leg).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 3-day buttermilk marinade · **Cook**: 2–2.5 h at 200 °C

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 kg goat leg (Ziegenkeule)
- Buttermilk, enough to cover the leg
- Salt and pepper
- 2 tsp sweet paprika powder
- 1/2 tsp rosemary
- 1 tsp garlic granules (or fresh garlic)
- 2 tbsp hot mustard
- Soup vegetables (Suppengrün: carrot, celery, leek, parsley — no quantity stated)
- 1/4 litre meat broth
- 1 tbsp starch
- 1 small tub sweet cream

**Method**
1. Put the leg in a vessel and cover it with buttermilk. Leave it in the closed vessel **3 days**, turning occasionally.
2. Remove the meat, pat dry, and rub with salt, pepper, garlic, rosemary, paprika and mustard.
3. Put the leg in a soaked Römertopf (clay roaster), arrange the soup vegetables around it and add the meat broth.
4. Put the Römertopf in the oven and cook **2–2½ hours at 200 °C**.
5. Keep the roast warm. Strain the cooking liquid, thicken it with starch and enrich it with cream. Serve with vegetables and parsley potatoes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step in the source. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is time-based.

### 2. Greek roasted goat leg — Efthimis "Makos" Koukakis / The Hungry Bites
**Inspired by**: https://www.thehungrybites.com/greek-roasted-goat-or-lamb-leg/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Efthimis Koukakis, The Hungry Bites; Greek tradition. Ingredient line: "3 pounds (1.3 Kg) goat leg **with the bone** (you can also use lamb)" — explicit bone-in.

**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 2 h 30 min · **Rest**: 15 min

**Ingredients**
*Meat*
- 3 pounds (1.3 kg) goat leg with the bone (lamb may be used instead)
- 2–3 cloves garlic, cut into little sticks
- 3–4 sprigs rosemary
- Juice from 1 lemon
- 1/4 cup olive oil
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 1 tablespoon dried oregano
- 1 cup white wine
- 2 pounds (900 g) baby potatoes, or potatoes peeled and cut into wedges

*Glaze*
- 3 tablespoons (50 g) pomegranate syrup (or grape syrup, maple syrup or honey)
- 1/2 teaspoon ground coriander seeds, optional
- Pinch of salt and pepper
- 1 teaspoon olive oil
- Pomegranate arils for decoration, optional

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **356 °F (180 °C)**.
2. Rub the meat with salt and pepper. Make small incisions throughout the meat and push a stick of garlic and some rosemary into each.
3. Place the meat and potatoes in a pan; add the wine and lemon juice, drizzle with olive oil and sprinkle with oregano.
4. **Wrap well with foil and bake 2 hours.**
5. Mix the glaze. Remove the foil and brush the meat with half the glaze. Gently smash the potatoes if you want crunchy edges.
6. Return the pan to the oven and bake **uncovered at least 30 more minutes**, until the meat surface is deep brown and tissue is separating from the bone.
7. When nearly done, brush once more with the remaining glaze and bake **5 more minutes** (add pomegranate arils if using).
8. Rest **15 minutes** before serving.

**Probe and endpoint**: the method requires no thermometer, but the page gives internal-temperature guidance — **about 160 °F (70 °C) for goat** (about 145 °F / 62.8 °C for lamb).
Final serving temperature: **~70 °C / 160 °F** for goat, as stated by the page's guidance (long roasting to "fall-off-the-bone" is its descriptive endpoint).

### 3. Козья нога, запеченная в духовке (Goat leg baked in the oven) — Сибирячка / iamcook.ru
**Inspired by**: https://www.iamcook.ru/showrecipe/29306 (fetched 2026-10-24). Author "Сибирячка" (Sibiryachka), iamcook.ru; Russian tradition. Cut: "Нога козья задняя (окорок) - 1 шт. (около 2 кг)" — one whole rear goat leg, about 2 kg.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 1–3 days marinating + ~2 h tempering · **Cook**: 2.5–3 h · **Rest**: 15–20 min

**Ingredients**
- Rear goat leg (ham) — 1 piece (about 2 kg)
- Prunes — 200 g
- Water — as needed
- Mustard — 3 tbsp
- Soy sauce — 2 tbsp
- Garlic — 5–6 cloves
- Vegetable oil — 2 tbsp
- Pepper mixture — 1/2 tsp
- Sumac — 1/2 tsp
- Salt — 1 tsp
- Red hot pepper (the author used pepper paste) — to taste

**Method** *(translated faithfully)*
1. If frozen, thaw the leg wrapped in film at room temperature. Rinse under cold water and pat dry. Rinse and dry the prunes; if very dry, soak in warm water 15 minutes and drain.
2. Remove membranes with a sharp knife. The author optionally trims the joint end and cleans the exposed bone for appearance.
3. Make deep cuts in the meat and insert the prunes, distributing them across both sides of the leg.
4. Mix the mustard, pressed garlic, salt, pepper, sumac, soy sauce, oil and red pepper. Coat the leg all over.
5. Put the leg in the baking dish, cover and refrigerate to marinate **1–3 days**. Before roasting, take it out and warm at room temperature about **2 hours**.
6. Pour about 2 cups water into the dish, enough to cover the bottom by about 1 cm. Cover the dish with foil and put it in a **cold oven**.
7. Set the oven to **130–140 °C** and roast about **2.5–3 hours**. During the last half-hour raise the oven to **180 °C**. Check under the foil periodically and add boiling water if needed.
8. Test the thickest part with a knife; the juices should be clear, without blood.
9. Transfer to a platter and rest **15–20 minutes**.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; the doneness test is a knife at the thickest part with clear juices. **no internal temperature stated**.

### 4. Roast Leg of Goat (Raan) — Nosheen Babar / Untold Recipes By Nosheen
**Inspired by**: https://untoldrecipesbynosheen.com/roasted-leg-of-goat-raan/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Nosheen Babar, Untold Recipes By Nosheen; the page labels the cuisine "Indian, Pakistani" (South Asian celebratory raan). Ingredient line: "2.5 lb whole goat leg (see notes)" — the page discusses the bone (do not discard it if a butcher trims it to fit the pot) but the ingredient line does not say "bone-in".

**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 12 h (incl. overnight marination) · **Cook**: 4 h · **Rest**: 20 min

**Ingredients**
*Spice blend*
- 1 piece mace; 1 inch cinnamon stick; 1 piece star anise; 3 pieces cardamom; 1 teaspoon fennel; 2 bay leaves
- 2 teaspoon salt; 2 teaspoon red chili powder; 1 teaspoon coriander powder; 1 teaspoon cumin powder; 1/4 teaspoon turmeric powder; 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

*Marinade*
- 1/2 onion; 1 tablespoon ginger; 1 tablespoon garlic; 2 tablespoon lemon juice; 1 cup yogurt; 3/4 cup tomato paste; 2 serrano chillies; 1/2 cup cilantro; the spice blend

*Roast*
- 2.5 lb whole goat leg
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- the marinade
- 1/2 cup cooking oil
- 6 cups water
- 4 potatoes

**Method (oven branch)**
1. Dry-roast the whole spices in a preheated frying pan. Cool, grind to a fine powder, mix with the powdered spices and set aside.
2. Slice the onion and fry it to a deep brown; strain from the oil and cool.
3. Blend the spice blend, cooked onion and remaining marinade ingredients to a paste.
4. Wash and pat dry the goat leg. Make 10–12 deep cuts and rub 1/2 teaspoon salt over it. Massage in the marinade, including into the cuts, and refrigerate overnight.
5. Remove the leg from the refrigerator and leave it on the counter **1 hour** before cooking.
6. Preheat the cooking oil to about **350°** (unit not stated on the page) and sear the leg 5–10 minutes per side (scrape the marinade coating off first).
7. Preheat the oven — the page's extracted text displays the setting as "325?" with no legible unit; recorded unresolved rather than assumed.
8. Put the meat in a roasting pan with the remaining marinade and 2 cups water. **Cover tightly with foil and cook 2 hours.**
9. Uncover, check whether the meat is "90% cooked", and continue cooking **uncovered 30–40 minutes** to its "ideal cooking temperature".
10. Rest **20 minutes**. If there is too much liquid, boil it down to a thick concentrate to pour over the leg.

**Probe and endpoint**: the oven method names no numeric internal target ("ideal cooking temperature"); the page's general slow-cooking guidance gives **160–165 °F internal** for fork-tender meat (stated in a stovetop discussion, not as the oven method's target — recorded as published).
Final serving temperature: not stated for the oven method; the page's general guidance is **71–74 °C / 160–165 °F** for fork-tender goat.

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| German (Chefkoch, Römertopf) | Not stated — 2–2.5 h at 200 °C | Not stated |
| Greek (The Hungry Bites) | "fall-off-the-bone tender"; guidance ≈160 °F for goat | ~70 °C / 160 °F (page guidance) |
| Russian (iamcook / Сибирячка) | "juices run clear" knife test | Not stated |
| Indian-Pakistani (Untold Recipes By Nosheen, raan) | "ideal cooking temperature"; general guidance 160–165 °F fork-tender | 71–74 °C / 160–165 °F (page guidance, not oven-method-specific) |

The observed range across the fetched sources where temperatures are stated is
**70–74 °C (160–165 °F)** as internal *guidance*, with two of four sources silent and working
to time or bone separation. Both numeric figures sit in the `well_done` band (68–77 °C) of
the cut file's doneness options and above the 63 °C safe line — consistent with the long-roast
practice every source shares. The variation is in what is measured: the Greek source states a
goat-specific figure (and, notably, a lower lamb figure of 145 °F, showing it treats goat as
the better-done meat); the raan source's number is general fork-tender guidance rather than
the oven method's own endpoint. No source supports a pink roast-goat target for this method.

## What makes this method special

Oven roasting a whole goat leg is a two-phase operation every fetched source converges on:
hours of gentle covered heat to convert the leg's collagen and protect its lean muscle from
drying, then an uncovered blast to build the crust the covered phase cannot. The bone-in leg
(the Greek source is explicit about it) buffers the thick muscles near the joint, which is
why times run 2.5–3.5 hours even at moderate oven settings. Marinades do double duty here —
dairy or acid tenderising the surface and tempering the goat's assertive flavour compounds
before the long cook mellow them further.

## Summary

Oven roasting suits whole goat leg well: covered slow heat tenderises the collagen-heavy cut, and an uncovered finish supplies the crust and glaze. Four traditions — German, Greek, Russian, South Asian — converge on the same two-phase architecture and on well-done results.
