# Minced Moose × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

A moose burger is a test of binding, not of heat. Ground moose is so lean that a patty formed
from it alone tends to break on the grill, so the four fetched records solve the problem in
three different ways: **bacon ground into the meat** at roughly 80/20 (two sources), a
**breadcrumb-and-egg binder** (one source), or **partial freezing** so the patty stays rigid
through the first flip (one source). Two of them also make the same structural point — the
patties must be shaped to an even thickness and handled as little as possible, because lean
game mince toughens with working.

The temperature evidence on this leaf is the clearest split in the whole cut. The Newfoundland
chef record specifies a **digital thermometer reading 160 °F / 71 °C in the centre** — the
correct ground-meat target. The other three records cook to sight and time, and two of them
actively instruct the reader to *undercook*: one says "cook burgers from rare to medium rare (do
not overcook)", the other says "grill or pan-fry to medium doneness, about 4–5 minutes per
side". Those are whole-muscle habits applied to ground meat, and they are exactly the mistake
the USDA 160 °F rule exists to prevent. They are recorded here as divergence, not as guidance.

## Source recipes

### 1. Reader's Digest Canada — The Ultimate Moose Burger (chef Katie Hayes, Newfoundland)
**Inspired by**: https://www.readersdigest.ca/food/recipes/ultimate-moose-burger/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Reader's Digest Canada, recipe by Ivy Knight with chef Katie Hayes of the Bonavista Social Club, Newfoundland. Canadian/Newfoundland tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 1 hour (chilling) · **Cook**: 12 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb (450 g) ground moose
- ½ cup caramelized onion, cooled · 4 tsp melted bacon fat · ½ tsp fresh savoury
- ½ egg, beaten · 1 garlic clove · 4 slices aged cheddar

**Method**
1. Combine the onion, garlic, egg, bacon fat and savoury; add the moose and mix just until combined.
2. Form into 4 patties, **each 2 cm thick**; cover and refrigerate **1 hour**.
3. Heat the grill to **medium-high** and oil the grates. Sear the patties, turning once.
4. Cook **until a digital thermometer inserted into the centre reads 160 °F (71 °C)**.
5. Top with cheddar and broil or cover until melted.

*Internal temperature: **160 °F / 71 °C, explicitly probed** — the only grill record in the
corpus that gives the correct ground-meat target. The bacon fat and the caramelized onion are
both moisture measures, and the hour in the fridge sets the patty so it survives the flip.*

### 2. Venison for Dinner (Canada) — Moose Burgers
**Inspired by**: https://venisonfordinner.com/moose-burgers/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Venison for Dinner (Canadian wild-game cooking site by Kate Schat), 5/5 from 2 ratings. Canadian hunting-kitchen tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min + freezing · **Cook**: ~10 min

**Ingredients**
- About 1 lb (450 g) ground moose, formed as quarter-pound patties
- 1 tsp salt per pound · minced dried onion (the largest spice component) · garlic · thyme · smoked paprika
- Cheese and toppings of choice

**Method**
1. Season the moose and mix well, but stop before the meat tightens.
2. Form patties about 3 in across and ½ in thick; lay on parchment and **put in the freezer until the outside is firm** — the page's key trick for a lean patty that would otherwise fall apart on the grates.
3. Grill on one side until the patty is cooked almost to the top when checked from the side, then flip and add cheese.
4. The page's alternative is a flat-top griddle, which it prefers for easier flipping.
5. No fillers at all: the author explicitly rejects breadcrumbs and egg as turning burgers into "meat loaves".

*Internal temperature: not published; the cue is visual ("cooked almost to the top when checked
from the side"). Note the deliberate contrast with source 1 — this record gets its binding from
cold and technique rather than from bacon or panade.*

### 3. From Field to Table (US) — His and Hers Moose Burgers (Mushroom & Swiss)
**Inspired by**: https://www.fromfieldtotable.com/recipes/his-and-hers-moose-burgers-hers-mushroom-swiss (fetched 2026-10-26). From Field to Table (US hunting-gear publisher), recipe by Greg Ray, using a 2019 British Columbia moose. US wild-game tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 3–4 · **Cook**: ~10 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb ground moose, ground with bacon at an **80/20 ratio**
- ½ cup chopped red onion · 1 garlic clove · favourite spice blend · 3 tbsp Heinz 57 (or ketchup/A1) for moisture
- Swiss cheese, bacon, portabella mushrooms, onion, Kaiser rolls

**Method**
1. Cook the bacon and use the grease to soften sliced mushrooms and onion with garlic, onion powder, salt and Worcestershire.
2. Mix the moose with the onion, garlic, spice blend and sauce; shape 3–4 patties slightly wider than the rolls.
3. Oil the grates and heat the grill **on high**.
4. **"Cook burgers from rare to medium rare (do not over cook)."**
5. Add cheese for the last minute on the grill; toast the buttered rolls.

*Integrity note: the instruction to cook ground moose to rare/medium-rare is quoted as published
and is **not** safe practice for ground meat — grinding distributes surface bacteria through the
patty. The page's own 80/20 bacon grind is a sound moisture measure; its doneness advice is not.*

### 4. Lawrence Bay Lodge (Canada, Saskatchewan) — Three Moose Burger Recipes
**Inspired by**: https://www.lawrencebay.com/three-moose-burger-recipes-youll-forever-crave/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Lawrence Bay Lodge (Saskatchewan moose outfitter), three-recipe article. Canadian prairie/outfitter tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 4 per recipe · **Cook**: 8–10 min

**Ingredients (classic version)**
- 1 lb ground moose · 1 small egg · ¼ cup breadcrumbs · salt and pepper
- 1 large onion, sliced · butter or oil · buns and toppings

**Method**
1. Mix the moose with egg, breadcrumbs, salt and pepper.
2. Form patties and press **a small indentation in the centre** so they cook evenly.
3. Caramelize the onions.
4. **"Grill or pan-fry burgers to medium doneness, about 4–5 minutes per side. Moose is lean, so avoid overcooking."**
5. Serve on toasted buns with the onions, cheese and lettuce.

*The article's other two variants — a chipotle-in-adobo burger with pepper jack, and a
Mediterranean burger with oregano, lemon zest and tzatziki — are cooked "over medium heat until
they reach your desired doneness" and "until cooked through" respectively, so this leaf counts
the article once.*

*Integrity note: "medium doneness" for ground moose is quoted as published and is not a safe
target for ground meat; the article's own lean-meat warning is the useful part.*

## Temperature summary

| Source | Published target | Binding strategy | Tradition |
|--------|------------------|------------------|-----------|
| Reader's Digest Canada | **160 °F / 71 °C, probed** | caramelized onion + bacon fat, 1 h chill | Newfoundland |
| Venison for Dinner (CA) | **none** | no filler; partial freeze before grilling | Canadian |
| From Field to Table (US) | **"rare to medium rare"** — unsafe for ground meat | 80/20 bacon grind | US |
| Lawrence Bay Lodge (CA) | **"medium doneness", 4–5 min/side** — unsafe for ground meat | egg + breadcrumbs | Canadian prairie |

**Conclusion for this leaf:** one of four sources publishes a safe, probed target, and two
publish actively unsafe ones. The cut keeps 71 °C / 160 °F and treats the "rare to medium-rare"
advice as the corpus's main integrity failure on this method. The technique finding is solid and
consistent: a moose burger needs added fat, a binder, or cold shaping to hold together, and it
needs to be shaped evenly so a lean patty does not dry at the edges before the centre is safe.
