# Minced Moose × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

The oven is where minced moose is stretched into a loaf or a layered casserole, and that changes
the temperature problem completely. A patty in a pan is thin and fast; a meatloaf is a 1–1.5 kg
mass of lean meat, breadcrumbs, egg and milk, and it heats slowly enough that the outside dries
and browns long before the centre is safe. Every one of the four fetched records answers that
with the same two moves: a **wet panade** (breadcrumbs soaked in milk or cream, plus egg) to hold
water inside a meat that has almost none, and a **glaze or cheese crust applied late**, so the
top does not burn while the middle catches up.

The temperature evidence here is the most instructive of any leaf on this cut, because it
**contradicts itself**. One Canadian moose site instructs the reader to bake until the loaf
reaches **150 °F**, which is 10 °F *below* the USDA ground-meat minimum; a US cast-iron cooking
site using the same kind of loaf says plainly that ground moose **must** reach **160 °F**. Both
pages are describing lean wild mince in a loaf pan. The cut file follows the 160 °F / 71 °C rule
because ground meat has no surface-only safety margin, and records the 150 °F instruction as a
divergence rather than adopting it. The two Nordic records publish no internal temperature at
all.

## Source recipes

### 1. All-About-Moose.com (Canada) — Yummiest of Moose Meatloaf
**Inspired by**: https://www.all-about-moose.com/moose-meatloaf.html (fetched 2026-10-26). All-About-Moose.com (Canadian hunting/cooking site), reader-submitted and site-hosted. Canadian wild-game tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 6–8 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 50 min + 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 1½ lb (680 g) moose mince
- ½ cup dry breadcrumbs · 1 tsp salt · ½ tsp ground sage · 2 tbsp parsley
- 2 eggs · ¾ cup skim milk · ½ medium onion, finely chopped
- **Sauce**: 1 tsp dry mustard · 2 tbsp brown sugar · ¼ cup ketchup

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **350 °F (175 °C)**.
2. Combine crumbs, salt, sage, parsley, eggs, milk, onion and the moose mince.
3. Pack into a parchment-lined loaf pan.
4. Bake **50 minutes, or until an internal temperature of 150 °F (65.6 °C) is reached.**
5. Mix mustard, sugar and ketchup, spread over the top, and bake **another 10 minutes**.
6. Serve with wild rice.

*Integrity note: the 150 °F figure is quoted exactly as published. It is 5.6 °C below the USDA
ground-meat minimum and would not be a safe target for this cut; it is recorded, not endorsed.*

### 2. Cast Iron Skillet Cooking (US) — Cheesy Loaded Moose Meatloaf Casserole
**Inspired by**: https://www.castironskilletcooking.com/moose-meatloaf-casserole/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Cast Iron Skillet Cooking (US), 5/5 from 12 ratings. North American home-cooking tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 50 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb (450 g) ground moose
- Diced onion · minced garlic · Worcestershire sauce · breadcrumbs · 1 egg · salt, pepper
- Leftover mashed/baked potatoes · grated cheddar · cooked bacon

**Method**
1. Cook the chopped bacon in a cast-iron skillet; set aside.
2. Preheat the oven to **400 °F (205 °C)**.
3. Combine the ground moose, onion, garlic, Worcestershire, breadcrumbs, egg, salt and pepper; press into a 9 × 13 in cast-iron baking dish.
4. Spread the mashed potato over the meat, then scatter the bacon and cheddar.
5. Cover with foil and bake **30 minutes**; uncover and bake **a further 20 minutes**, until cooked and the cheese is bubbly.
6. The page's own doneness instruction: *"Always check the internal temperature with an instant thermometer. Ground moose must be cooked to a minimum of 160°F."*

*Internal temperature: **160 °F (71 °C) stated explicitly** — the only oven-bake record in the
corpus that names the correct ground-meat target. The same page warns that the amount of
breadcrumbs needed to hold this loaf together is also what makes overcooking dry it out fast.*

### 3. Kulinaari (Finland) — Hirvikiusaus (moose mince and potato bake)
**Inspired by**: https://kulinaari.com/2014/11/hirvikiusaus.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Kulinaari (Finnish food blog by a Helsinki restaurant cook), published 2014-11-23. Finnish arkiruoka tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Cook**: about 1 hour

**Ingredients**
- 300 g minced moose (*hirven jauheliha*) — or other game mince
- 500 g potato-and-onion mix (or ~400 g firm potato + 100 g yellow onion)
- 2 dl single cream · 2 sprigs fresh rosemary · black pepper · 1 tsp salt · a knob of butter

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to **200 °C**; butter an oven dish.
2. Brown the mince in butter in a hot pan — the page wants real colour, and says the meat may stay in slightly larger pieces.
3. Season the meat with salt and pepper.
4. Layer half the potato-onion mix in the dish, season with salt, pepper and rosemary; add the browned meat; top with the rest of the potatoes and season again.
5. Pour the cream over and bake in the middle of the oven **about one hour**.
6. Serve with pickled beetroot, pickles or lingonberry jam.

*Internal temperature: not published. The cue is the hour at 200 °C, with the cream and the
potato layer both buffering the lean meat from the dry oven air.*

### 4. Norrmejerier (Sweden) — Ostgratinerade älgfärsbiffar
**Inspired by**: https://www.norrmejerier.se/goda-recept/ostgratinerade-algfarsbiffar (fetched 2026-10-26). Norrmejerier (Swedish dairy cooperative recipe site), 3.5/5 from 59 votes. Norrland home-cooking tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Cook**: about 25 min

**Ingredients**
- 500 g minced moose or beef (*älg- eller nötfärs*)
- 2 shallots · 1 tbsp finely chopped thyme · 4 tbsp breadcrumbs · 1 egg · 1 tsp salt · 1 pinch pepper
- 200 g haricots verts · 12 cherry tomatoes, halved · 2 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp concentrated game stock · 3 dl single cream · 2 dl grated Västerbotten cheese

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to **200 °C**. Soften the chopped shallots in butter; mix with the mince, thyme, breadcrumbs and egg; season.
2. Boil the haricots verts; put them and the tomatoes in an ovenproof dish with knobs of butter, salt and pepper.
3. Fry the patties in butter until coloured, then lay them in the dish.
4. Deglaze the pan with the stock and cream and pour over the patties; scatter the cheese on top.
5. **Gratinate in the oven about 10 minutes**, and warm the vegetables alongside for about 10 minutes.

*Internal temperature: not published. This record is the hybrid case — the patties are pan-fried
first and only finished under the grill element, so the oven is doing gratinating rather than
cooking through.*

## Temperature summary

| Source | Published target | Method shape | Tradition |
|--------|------------------|--------------|-----------|
| All-About-Moose (CA) | **150 °F / 65.6 °C** — below the USDA ground minimum | loaf, 350 °F, 50 + 10 min | Canadian |
| Cast Iron Skillet Cooking (US) | **160 °F / 71 °C** minimum, stated | casserole, 400 °F, 30 + 20 min | US |
| Kulinaari (FI) | **none** | layered kiusaus, 200 °C, ~1 h | Finnish |
| Norrmejerier (SE) | **none** | pan-fry then gratinate 10 min at 200 °C | Swedish |

**Conclusion for this leaf:** two of four sources publish a number and they disagree by 10 °F.
The cut follows the 160 °F / 71 °C ground-meat minimum. The convergent technique finding is that
an oven-baked minced moose dish needs a wet panade and a late-applied crust, and that the oven
form (loaf, casserole, layered kiusaus) is a Northern European and North American family-food
form rather than a restaurant one.
