# Minced Moose × Simmer — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Simmering is what minced moose is for in the Nordic kitchen: the lean meat is browned for
flavour, then given a long, wet, covered cook that both guarantees it is cooked through and
supplies by absorption the moisture it cannot supply itself. The four fetched records split into
two time scales that do the same job — a **long sauce** (one to six hours, cream or tomato,
served over pasta) and a **short braise** (ten minutes, meatballs or patties simmered in a cream
or stock sauce). Both are structural responses to the same problem, and both are described in the
sources as fixing dry mince: the Swedish recipe page says outright that moose mince is "lite torr"
and needs the cream, and the Finnish one says the meatballs should *hautua* (sweat/braise), not
*keittää* (boil).

Method discovery note: the cut file originally declared `braise` for this method. The fetched
corpus uses open-pan or covered **simmering** — sauces and *hautua* — rather than a true braise of
a large cut in a sealed pot, so the method is registered as `simmer`. Braise remains structurally
possible for a moose-mince ragù and is kept as a queued lead, not as a declared method.

**Temperature evidence:** none of the four sources publishes an internal temperature, and none
probes. They judge by time and by the sauce. As in the pan-fry leaf, this corpus supplies
technique and ratios only; the 71 °C / 160 °F floor comes from the USDA ground-meat rule.

## Source recipes

### 1. Svenska Jägareförbundet — Viltmat (Sweden) — Spagetti med viltfärssås
**Inspired by**: https://viltmat.jagareforbundet.se/recept/spagetti-med-viltfarssas (fetched 2026-10-26). The Swedish Hunters' Association recipe site, recipe taken from *Viltkokboken*. 3.6 rating. Swedish hunting-kitchen tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Cook**: 1–2 hours

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg game mince (*viltfärs*), mixing species is encouraged
- Optional: a few slices of bacon · 3 onions, finely chopped · 3 celery stalks, diced · 4 garlic cloves
- 3 tbsp tomato purée · 1 tin chopped tomatoes · 1 tsp thyme · 1 tsp oregano
- 1 stock cube + 7 dl water · 1–2 tsp sugar · salt, pepper

**Method**
1. Brown the game mince **in batches** and put it in a large pot.
2. Brown onion, celery and garlic in oil and add to the pot; fry the bacon too if using.
3. Add the tomato purée, thyme and oregano and let it fry a minute.
4. Add water, stock cube, chopped tomatoes and sugar, and **let it simmer 1–2 hours, preferably longer**, topping up with water as needed.
5. Serve with pasta and parmesan; leftovers go into lasagne.

*Internal temperature: not published. The browning-in-batches instruction is the technique point —
a kilogram of cold mince in one pan steams instead of browning, and this cut has no fat to spare.*

### 2. Vin & Matguiden (Sweden) — Långkokt älgfärssås (Anders Gustavsson)
**Inspired by**: https://www.vinochmatguiden.se/recept/anders-gustavsson/langkokt-algfarssas (fetched 2026-10-26). Vin & Matguiden, recipe by Anders Gustavsson. Swedish long-cook tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Active**: 30 min · **Cook**: 6 h (slow cooker) or "a few hours" in a pot

**Ingredients**
- 500 g minced moose (*älgfärs*) · 150 g smoked pork belly · 1 onion · ½ leek · 1 garlic clove
- 1 tbsp thyme · 1 tbsp oregano · 400 g chopped tomatoes · 5 cocktail tomatoes
- 2 tbsp concentrated game stock · 1 umami stock cube · 1 dl water · 3 crushed juniper berries
- 1 dl cream · salt, freshly ground pepper

**Method**
1. Brown the mince, the onion and the leek.
2. Put everything except the cream into a slow cooker or a lidded pot. **Slow cooker: 6 hours on low. Pot: let it barely bubble for a few hours.**
3. **Add the cream when one hour remains.**
4. If the sauce is loose at the end, reduce it uncovered to the wanted consistency.
5. Season; serve with spaghetti and grated parmesan.

*Internal temperature: not published. The page's own framing — "en älgfärssås som puttrar i många
timmar" — is the long-cook claim on this leaf, and the smoked pork belly and juniper are the fat
and the juniper-forward flavour that moose mince lacks.*

### 3. Poppamies (Finland) — Hirvipullat
**Inspired by**: https://poppamies.fi/reseptit/hirvipullat/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Poppamies (Finnish seasoning and BBQ company recipe site). Gluten-free. Finnish hunting-kitchen tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 2–4 · **Cook**: 60 min

**Ingredients**
- 600 g minced moose (*hirven jauheliha*) — or roe deer mince
- 2 medium onions, finely chopped · 2 tbsp Garlic RUB seasoning · 1 egg, lightly beaten
- 1 dl Smoky Apple BBQ sauce · 2 dl cream · 1 tsp salt

**Method**
1. Fry the onions in butter, careful not to burn them, and cool.
2. Mix the onions with the seasoning and the egg; add the mince and **mix only until combined — do not over-work the forcemeat**.
3. Form balls about the size of a golf ball.
4. Brown them in butter in a hot pan.
5. **Lower the heat, add the BBQ sauce, cream and salt, and let them *hautua* (braise/sweat, not boil) for about ten minutes**, until cooked through.
6. Serve with root-vegetable mash.

*Internal temperature: not published. The *älä keitä* ("do not boil") instruction is the method
point on this record: boiling a lean game meatball makes it tight and grainy, where a bare simmer
keeps it soft.*

### 4. Receptfavoriter (Sweden) — Köttfärssås med älgfärs
**Inspired by**: https://receptfavoriter.se/recept/kottfarssas-med-algfars (fetched 2026-10-26). Receptfavoriter.se, recipe by Henrik Mattsson (chef, meal-science graduate), published 2021-03-01, 4/5 from 86 ratings. Swedish home-cooking tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 1 h 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 500 g minced moose (*älgfärs*)
- 1 onion · 1 garlic clove · ½ carrot · 1 celery stalk · 2 tbsp butter · 4 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp tomato purée · ½ dl white or red wine · basil · 400 g chopped tomatoes · 1 tsp oregano · 2 pinches rosemary · ½ tsp chilli flakes
- 1 dl water · **2½ dl cream** · ½ tsp salt · 2 pinches black pepper

**Method**
1. Sweat the onion, carrot, garlic and celery in butter and oil for a minute; add the tomato purée and fry a minute more.
2. Add the wine and reduce 2 minutes; add the tomatoes, herbs, chilli, **cream** and water and bring to a bare simmer.
3. Separately brown the moose mince in butter and oil, breaking it up; season; add it to the sauce.
4. **Cover and let it barely bubble for one hour**, stirring now and then and topping up with water if it dries.
5. Season; serve with pasta, parmesan and parsley.

*Internal temperature: not published. The page's stated reason for the long cook is the same one
every other record gives in its own words: moose mince is dry, and cream plus time is how it is
made to taste rich rather than lean.*

## Temperature summary

| Source | Published target | Time scale | Tradition |
|--------|------------------|-----------|-----------|
| Jägareförbundet (SE) | **none** | 1–2 h tomato sauce | Swedish |
| Vin & Matguiden (SE) | **none** | 6 h slow cooker / hours in pot | Swedish |
| Poppamies (FI) | **none** | ~10 min *hautua* in cream sauce | Finnish |
| Receptfavoriter (SE) | **none** | 1 h covered with cream | Swedish |

**Conclusion for this leaf:** four sources, zero published temperatures, one consistent technique
— brown first, then a wet covered cook, with fat (cream, smoked pork belly, butter) added on
purpose. The two time scales (1–6 h sauce, 10 min meatball braise) are both genuinely simmering,
and both are described by their own authors as the cure for dry moose mince. The group coverage
on this leaf is deliberately narrow: all four records are Swedish or Finnish, so it adds no new
culinary group to the cut — that gap is recorded in the cut file.
