# Moose Roast × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

An oven roast is the method the Nordic kitchen actually uses on a moose haunch, and it works against the
grain of the meat: a moose roast carries 1–3 % fat, so the oven air has nothing to buffer it and the surface
dries while the centre is still cold. Every source in this leaf solves that the same way — a low oven
(100–160 °C) and a covered or liquid-lined vessel — so "oven roast" here means a semi-dry roast in a pan full
of water, wine or cream, not a free-standing roast on a rack.

What the temperature data shows is a much wider spread than any braise: the sources pull between 58 and 70 °C,
and the spread is cultural rather than technical. Finnish and Swedish game kitchens serve moose *rose*
(58–62 °C), the Norwegian tradition calls 65 °C medium and 70 °C fully cooked, and the German cream-braised
version sits at 62–68 °C. A probe user needs to decide which tradition they are cooking before choosing a
target, because the same roast is "done" across a 12-degree band.

Carryover is larger than in a lidded braise: the roast leaves a 100–160 °C oven and rests uncovered, and 5–8 °C
of climb is normal over a 20-minute rest on a 1.2–2 kg piece. Probe from the thick end into the centre of the
largest muscle, clear of the bone and of the seam between topside and silverside.

## Styckesprofil

Ugnsrostning är den metod de nordiska köken faktiskt använder på en älgstek, och den går mot köttets natur:
1–3 % fett ger ingen buffert mot ugnsluften. Alla källor här löser det likadant — låg ugn (100–160 °C) och en
form med vatten, vin eller grädde. Spridningen i temperatur är stor: finska och svenska viltskök serverar
älgen rosa (58–62 °C), den norska traditionen kallar 65 °C medium och 70 °C genomstekt, och den tyska
gräddbräserade varianten ligger på 62–68 °C. Eftervärme 5–8 °C på en ostekt stek under 20 minuter.

## Source recipes

### 1. German — Elchbraten in Sahne (inspired by Chefkoch.de)
**Inspired by**: https://www.chefkoch.de/rezepte/1097651215764212/Elchbraten-Aelg-i-grytan.html
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 1 hr 30 min · **Rest**: 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg moose roast (*Elchbraten*)
- 750 ml single cream
- 1–2 tbsp beet syrup (*Rübensirup*)
- Salt and milled black pepper
- Butter, for browning
- A dash of soy sauce, optional

**Method**
1. Tie the roast into a neat shape. Season generously with pepper and rub with beet syrup — a thin film, not a
   coating.
2. Brown in butter in a heavy lidded pot (*Schmortopf*) over high heat until a deep mahogany crust forms,
   3–4 minutes per side.
3. Add half the cream, enough to come halfway up the roast. Cover and cook at about 160 °C for 1.5 hours,
   turning once at the halfway point.
4. Insert the probe into the centre of the roast. **Pull at 62 °C** (144 °F) for pink, or **68 °C** (154 °F)
   for more cooked.
5. Rest 10 minutes on a warm plate. Reduce the braising cream with the remaining cream, season with salt,
   pepper and soy.
6. Slice thinly and serve with salted boiled potatoes, vegetables and blackcurrant or rowanberry jelly.
   Final serving temperature: **65–68 °C / 149–154 °F** (pink to medium).

### 2. Swedish — Klassisk älgstek (inspired by Svensk Jakt)
**Inspired by**: https://svenskjakt.se/uncategorized/klassisk-algstek/
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 1 hr 30 min–2 hr 30 min · **Rest**: 15 min

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg moose from the roast (*älgstek*)
- 1 kg mixed autumn vegetables
- 300 ml red wine
- 100 ml game stock
- 100 g butter, plus 50 g for the sauce
- 1 tbsp raw sugar
- Salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Brown the roast lightly on all sides — the source is explicit that this is a *light* browning, not a crust.
2. Put it in the oven at **80 °C fan or 100 °C conventional**; the very low oven is the whole technique.
3. Insert a thermometer into the roast and cook to **60–62 °C**. The source's timing: 1.5–2.5 hours,
   depending on the roast's starting temperature.
4. **Pull at 60–62 °C** (140–144 °F) — the Swedish game-kitchen target, distinctly pink.
5. Rest 15 minutes while the vegetables are finished in butter and water under a lid, and the red-wine sauce
   is reduced with the stock, raw sugar and butter.
6. Slice thinly. Final serving temperature: **63–66 °C / 145–151 °F** (rare to medium-rare by any beef scale).

### 3. Norwegian — Elgstek (inspired by Godt.no / Opplysningskontoret for egg og kjøtt)
**Inspired by**: https://www.godt.no/oppskrifter/kjoett/vilt/3705/elgstek
**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 1 hr 30 min–2 hr · **Rest**: 15 min

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 kg moose roast (*elgstek*)
- 2 tsp salt
- 1 tsp pepper
- 2 tbsp margarine or butter
- 6 juniper berries (*einebær*), crushed
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1 onion, in wedges
- 1 slice celeriac (*sellerirot*)
- 1 carrot, in chunks
- 500 ml water
- For the sauce: 2 tbsp butter, 2 tbsp plain flour, 500 ml stock, 4 thin slices brown cheese
  (*brunost*), 50 ml cream, ½ tsp salt

**Method**
1. Dry the roast, rub it with salt, pepper, crushed juniper and oregano, and brown it well on all sides in a
   frying pan.
2. Put the onion, celeriac and carrot in an ovenproof dish, set the roast on top, and **insert a roasting
   thermometer into the thickest part** before it goes in. Pour the water into the dish.
3. Roast at **125 °C** until the thermometer reads **65 °C** — pink and medium — or **70 °C** if the table
   wants it fully cooked.
4. **Pull at 65 °C** (149 °F), or 70 °C (158 °F), and rest at least a quarter of an hour.
5. Strain the juices, discard the vegetables, and make the sauce by browning butter and flour, adding the
   juices, and finishing with cream, brown cheese and seasoning.
6. Final serving temperature: **68 °C / 154 °F** (medium, barely pink).

### 4. Finnish — Marinoitu hirvipaisti (inspired by Annika Elomaa, Meillä kotona)
**Inspired by**: https://www.meillakotona.fi/reseptit/marinoitu-hirvipaisti
**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 20 min (+4 hr marinade) · **Cook**: 2 hr · **Rest**: 20 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 kg moose roast (*hirvenpaisti*)
- Marinade: 1 tbsp cumin seed, 2 tsp whole black peppercorns, 3 garlic cloves, 2 tbsp Worcestershire
  sauce, 2 tbsp red-wine vinegar, 2 tbsp rapeseed oil
- For browning: 1 tbsp rapeseed oil, 25 g butter
- 1 tsp salt, for the finished roast
- Dill-garlic oil: 1 garlic clove, ½ bunch dill, 100 ml rapeseed or olive oil, ⅓ tsp salt, ⅓ tsp pepper

**Method**
1. Crush the cumin and peppercorns, chop the garlic, and combine with the Worcestershire sauce, vinegar and
   oil. Marinate the roast in the mixture in a bag for at least 4 hours, turning it over.
2. Bring the roast to room temperature about an hour before cooking and pat the surface dry.
3. Brown it all over in the oil-butter mixture in a very hot pan — the source notes a large roast cools the
   pan quickly, so the pan must start hot.
4. **Insert a meat thermometer into the centre of the roast.**
5. Roast at **125 °C** for about 2 hours, to **58–60 °C** for a pink centre, or about **64 °C** if you want it
   cooked through. Baste a few times with the juices in the pan.
6. **Pull at 58–60 °C** (136–140 °F) — the lowest target in this leaf — and rest 20 minutes under foil.
7. Sprinkle salt on the surface and carve across the grain into thin slices; serve with the dill-garlic oil.
   Final serving temperature: **61–63 °C / 142–145 °F** (pink).

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Source | Oven | Pull temperature | Final serving temp |
|-----------|--------|------|------------------|--------------------|
| German | Chefkoch.de, *Elchbraten* | 160 °C, lidded, cream | **62 °C** pink / 68 °C more cooked | 65–68 °C |
| Swedish | Svensk Jakt, *Klassisk älgstek* | 80 °C fan / 100 °C | **60–62 °C** | 63–66 °C |
| Norwegian | Godt.no / Opplysningskontoret for egg og kjøtt | 125 °C, open dish with water | **65 °C** medium / 70 °C fully cooked | 68 °C |
| Finnish | Annika Elomaa, Meillä kotona | 125 °C, open dish, basted | **58–60 °C** pink / 64 °C cooked through | 61–63 °C |

The spread is **58–70 °C**, and it is not a disagreement about physics — it is four traditions drawing the
line differently on the same lean meat. The Finnish and Swedish targets (58–62 °C) are the lowest and would be
called rare on any beef scale; the Norwegian 65 °C is what most of Europe calls medium; the German 62 °C sits
between them. Oven temperature varies by 60 degrees (100 °C to 160 °C) and has no visible effect on the
target — it only sets the time, from 1.5 hours to 2.5.

Two practical notes that only appear in the sources: the Swedish recipe uses a *fan* oven at 80 °C, which is
the lowest setting in this leaf and relies on the roast's own thermal mass to finish; and the Finnish recipe
tells you to bring the roast to room temperature an hour before it goes in, which is the single biggest lever
on the carryover you will see at the probe.

## What makes this method special

Oven roasting with a liquid-lined dish is a hybrid: the meat surface is in air, so it browns and concentrates,
but the pan sits in water, wine or cream, so the air in the oven is humid and the roast does not desiccate.
For a 1–3 % fat cut that humidity is the difference between a sliceable roast and a dry one. The other
structural point is heat transfer: at 100–125 °C the oven is barely above the target internal temperature, so
the roast approaches its pull point asymptotically — the last three degrees take as long as the first thirty,
which is why every source tells you to use a thermometer rather than a clock.

## Summary

Four oven-roast traditions pull a moose roast between 58 and 70 °C, and the 12-degree spread is cultural, not
technical: Finnish and Swedish game kitchens serve it pink at 58–62 °C, the Norwegian source calls 65 °C medium
and 70 °C fully cooked, and the German cream-braised version sits at 62–68 °C. Oven temperature varies from
100 °C to 160 °C with no effect on the target — only on the time. Every source agrees on the technique (low
oven, liquid in the pan, thermometer mandatory) and on nothing else.
