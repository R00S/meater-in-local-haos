## Cut profile

Grilling is how most of the world cooks a lamb cutlet, and the cut's thin, bone-in geometry is
why: a 2 cm Frenched chop goes from raw to medium-rare over direct heat in 6–10 minutes, so the
fire's intensity does the work a pan would do with butter, and the exposed bone and fat cap give
the cook something to char. The handle of bone also makes the cut easy to turn by hand, which is
why every fetched source moves the chops individually rather than with tongs or a spatula.

The five sources in this leaf cover Indian, Turkish, Moroccan, Greek and Korean treatments — the
cut's best culinary-group spread of any method — and they disagree sharply on the endpoint. Two
publish a reading: **125 °F (52 °C)** for medium-rare in the Moroccan source and **135–145 °F
(57–63 °C)** in the Indian one, with the Greek source adding that the reading climbs about 5 °F
while resting. All three sit at or below the cut's 63 °C safe line except the top of the Indian
range, so the divergence is recorded rather than resolved.

## Source recipes

### 1. Indian Lamb Chops — Jennifer Pallian
**Inspired by**: Foodess; https://foodess.com/indian-spiced-grilled-lamb-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Indian.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 1 hour marinade · **Cook**: 8–10 minutes · **Rest**: 10 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 3 tbsp cooking oil; juice of 1 lime
- 3 large garlic cloves, minced; 2 tsp finely grated fresh ginger
- 1 tsp coarse salt; ¾ tsp cumin; ½ tsp coriander; ½ tsp turmeric; ⅛ tsp black pepper
- 4 lamb chops, each about 1½ inches thick

**Method**
1. Whisk the oil, lime juice, garlic, ginger, salt and spices.
2. Coat the chops, cover and refrigerate at least 1 hour (up to 24).
3. Take the chops out of the refrigerator 30 minutes before grilling and oil the grates.
4. Grill over direct high heat, lid closed as much as possible, 4–5 minutes per side for medium-rare or to the desired doneness.
5. Rest, tented with foil, 10 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: the FAQ states "Aim for medium-rare to medium doneness, around 135-145°F (57-63°C) internal temperature" and recommends a thermometer. No separate pull temperature is given.

### 2. Grilled Lamb Chops with Ezme, Yoghurt and Cumin Salt — Ottolenghi
**Inspired by**: Ottolenghi Recipes; https://ottolenghi.co.uk/pages/recipes/grilled-lamb-chops-ezme-yoghurt-cumin-salt (fetched 2026-10-24). Turkish; the ezme is credited as inspired by Esra Muslu.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 3 hours marinade · **Cook**: 6–8 minutes · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg lamb chops
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 5 garlic cloves, crushed; 2 lemons (1½ tbsp juice, the other quartered); salt
- Cumin salt: 1½ tbsp toasted cumin seeds; ½ tsp flaked sea salt; 1 tsp sumac
- Ezme: 2 banana shallots; 6 garlic cloves; 5 red chillies; 6 plum tomatoes; 60 ml olive oil; 1 tbsp tomato paste; 2½ tbsp pomegranate molasses; 20 g walnuts; 25 g mint; 20 g parsley
- 200 g Greek-style yoghurt, to serve

**Method**
1. Mix the oil, garlic, lemon juice and salt, coat the lamb and refrigerate at least 3 hours.
2. Crush the cumin with the salt and sumac.
3. Grill the shallots, garlic, chillies and tomatoes until softened and charred; peel, chop and mix with the remaining ezme ingredients.
4. Return the griddle to a high heat and grill the chops 3–4 minutes on each side, until cooked through with char marks — "this will cook the chops to medium".
5. Serve with the yoghurt, ezme and cumin salt.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: char marks and "cooked through"; the source identifies its timing as medium.

### 3. Grilled Chermoula Lamb Chops — Peay Vineyards
**Inspired by**: Peay Vineyards; https://peayvineyards.com/recipes/grilled-chermoula-lamb-chops (fetched 2026-10-24). Moroccan-style chermoula.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 2 hours marinade · **Cook**: 6–8 minutes · **Rest**: loosely covered

**Ingredients**
- 1 tsp caraway seeds; 1 tbsp cumin seeds; 1 cinnamon stick; 1 tbsp coriander seeds; 3 whole cloves
- 2 tbsp sweet paprika; pinch cayenne
- 3 tbsp olive oil; 4 garlic cloves, minced; 2 tsp grated ginger; juice of ½ lemon; ¼ cup honey
- 2 frenched 8-rib racks of lamb, each cut into 4 double chops
- 1½ tbsp kosher salt; 1 tsp black pepper; cilantro

**Method**
1. Toast and grind the whole spices; stir in the paprika and cayenne.
2. Mix the oil, garlic, ginger, lemon and honey, coat the lamb, season and toss with the spice mix. Marinate refrigerated at least 2 hours.
3. Prepare a charcoal or gas grill for medium-hot coals.
4. Bring the lamb to room temperature, season again and grill "3 to 4 minutes per side for medium rare: 125°F with an instant-read thermometer."
5. Separate the double chops into single-bone chops, garnish with cilantro and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: explicit — 125 °F (52 °C) medium-rare, read with an instant-read thermometer. That figure sits 11 °C below the cut's 63 °C safe line and is recorded as published divergence, not guidance.

### 4. Greek Grilled Lamb Chops (Paidakia Arnisia Stin Skara) — Mia Kouppa
**Inspired by**: Mia Kouppa; https://miakouppa.com/recipe-grilled-lamb-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Greek.

**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 3–4 hours marinade · **Cook**: 8–10 minutes · **Rest**: 5–10 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 12 lamb chops, preferably rib chops
- Marinade: ⅓ cup olive oil; 2 garlic cloves, minced; juice of ½ lemon; ½ tsp pepper; 1 tbsp fresh thyme; 1 tsp yellow mustard; 1 tsp salt
- Ladolemono: ⅓ cup olive oil; juice of 1 lemon; 1 tsp dried oregano

**Method**
1. Trim the chops and combine the marinade, emulsifying with an immersion blender if wanted.
2. Coat the chops and refrigerate 3–4 hours, mixing occasionally; make the ladolemono.
3. Bring the chops to room temperature in the marinade 20–30 minutes before grilling.
4. Season with salt, then grill on medium 4–5 minutes per side.
5. Transfer to a deep platter, pour over the sauce and rest 5–10 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: the page grills to "approximately 140 degrees" and states the temperature rises about 5 degrees while resting to 145 °F; its FAQ separately describes 145 °F as medium-rare. Both readings are recorded as published; the page's own use of the terms is inconsistent.

### 5. Korean Lamb Chops with Grilled Scallions — Shared Appetite
**Inspired by**: Shared Appetite; https://sharedappetite.com/recipes/korean-lamb-chops-with-grilled-scallions/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Korean.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: total 10 minutes stated · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 8 American lamb loin chops
- 3 tbsp soy sauce; 2 tbsp mirin; 2 tbsp brown sugar; 2 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp onion powder; ½ tsp garlic powder; ½ tsp ginger powder; ¾ cup plus 1 tbsp water; 1 tbsp cornstarch
- 2 bunches scallions; 1 tbsp canola oil; sesame seeds; cilantro or parsley

**Method**
1. Preheat the grill to medium-high. Simmer the soy, mirin, sugar, sesame oil, powders and water 3–4 minutes for the glaze; thicken with the cornstarch slurry.
2. Salt the chops generously and grill "until a rosy medium-rare", brushing with glaze in the final minute.
3. Grill the oiled scallions until slightly charred and serve with the chops and extra glaze.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. No per-side time is given either — the page's 10-minute total cook time is not assigned to the chops specifically.

## Evidence limits

Five fetched sources across Indian, Turkish, Moroccan, Greek and Korean practice — the widest group
coverage in this cut's leaves (Groups A and C both present, plus B via Greece). Two publish a target
reading (125 °F; 135–145 °F) and one describes a rest climb to 145 °F; the remaining two give none.
The spread between the lowest and highest published reading is 20 °F, which no source acknowledges.

## What makes this method special

Grilling gives a cutlet what a pan cannot: exposure. A thin chop with a bone handle can be moved,
turned and checked by hand, and the fat cap renders directly into the fire instead of into butter.
Every source in this leaf builds flavour outside the meat — a yoghurt marinade, a chermoula spice
rub, a soy-mirin glaze, an oil-lemon-thyme marinade — because the cut is small and cooks too fast
to develop much of its own crust. The method's cost is the same as its benefit: with 3–5 minutes a
side and no pan to hold heat, the endpoint is decided by attention, and the published targets show
how differently cooks read "medium-rare".

## Summary

Grilled lamb cutlets are a 3–5 minute-per-side cook over direct heat, turned by hand and usually
marinated, with published endpoints ranging from 125 °F to 145 °F. Five fetched sources across
Groups A, B and C.
