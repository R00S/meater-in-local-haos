## Cut profile

Simmering ground lamb is the Indian keema method: the mince is browned in the aromatic base,
then covered and gently simmered with water, tomatoes and peas until tender and the sauce
reduces. Unlike a whole cut, ground lamb needs no collagen conversion — the simmer exists to
blend flavours and drive off moisture to the desired consistency, and to guarantee the
mince's centre is completely cooked. The fetched source cooks covered 30–60 minutes and
finishes uncovered to thicken; it states no internal temperature.

## Source recipes

### 1. Lamb Keema Matar (Minced Meat Curry with Peas) — Shari / My Fancy Pantry
**Inspired by**: https://myfancypantry.com/2013/03/27/lamb-keema-matar-minced-meat-curry-with-peas/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Shari, My Fancy Pantry; Indian tradition. Ingredient line: "1 pound ground meat (I used lamb)."

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated as a total (covered 30 min–1 h + uncovered reduction) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 tbsp oil
- 1/2 tsp cumin seed
- 3 green cardamom pods
- 2-inch piece of cinnamon
- 1 medium red onion, diced (about 1 to 1.5 cup)
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed and minced
- 1/2–3/4-inch piece fresh ginger, smashed and minced
- 3 dried red chilies (optional)
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 1/2 tsp ground coriander
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp turmeric
- 1/2 tsp garam masala
- 1 pound ground meat (the author used lamb)
- About 1/2 cup diced roma tomatoes (optional)
- 1 cup green peas (frozen works fine)
- 1–1.5 cup water
- 2 tbsp yogurt, whipped
- Handful fresh coriander (cilantro), chopped
- Salt and pepper, to taste

**Method**
1. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed skillet with a lid over **medium-high heat**.
2. Add the cumin seeds, cardamom, cinnamon, bay leaves and dried chilies. When the seeds crackle, add the diced onion.
3. Cook until the onion begins to brown. Add the garlic, ginger and dried spices; cook about 3 minutes, until fragrant and no longer raw-smelling.
4. **Add the ground lamb and brown it**, stirring occasionally.
5. Add the tomatoes and cook 10 minutes.
6. Add the peas, chopped coriander and water. **Cover and cook at least 30 minutes, up to an hour**, checking often.
7. Season with salt and pepper. Remove the lid, stir in the whipped yogurt, and continue cooking until the excess liquid evaporates and the curry reaches the desired consistency.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is time (30–60 min covered) and the sauce's consistency.

## What makes this method special

Simmering gives ground lamb the treatment it shares with legumes and vegetables: long gentle
heat in a spice-laden liquid that the mince both flavours and absorbs. The browning step
still happens first — it is non-negotiable for depth — but the finish is the opposite of the
pan-fry's dry crust: a loose, spoonable curry whose fat has emulsified into the sauce. For
the cut, the simmer trades crust for integration; the peas and yogurt in the fetched source
are classic counters to lamb's richness.

## Summary

Simmering ground lamb builds keema: browned mince gently stewed with spices and peas until the sauce clings. It trades the pan-fry's crust for a depth of flavour only slow liquid cooking gives, and it suits the cut completely. One source supports the method; no temperature evidence exists in it.
