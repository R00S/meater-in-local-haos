# Pork Loin Steak × Air Fryer — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

An air fryer is a small convection oven: a fan drives hot air around all faces of the steak
simultaneously, so the surface dries and browns faster than in a still oven while the centre
climbs steadily — the same enveloping evenness as `oven_roast` with a shorter clock. For a
lean, uniform 2–3 cm loin steak the method's virtue is that it needs no pre-sear and no
turning: four of five sources cook in one uninterrupted basket cycle (one flips once at the
halfway point), at 200–204 °C (400 °F) basket temperature, for 8–14 minutes depending on
thickness. The observed numeric endpoints are narrow — 60.4–63 °C (140–145 °F) — because the
appliance's fast air is unforgiving and its enthusiasts probe: the two sources that state a
temperature both pull slightly below the USDA line and let carryover finish the job. The risk
is the cut's standing risk amplified: the moving air that browns the surface also wicks
moisture from the lean fibres, so an extra cycle past the probe reading dries it visibly.

## Source recipes

### 1. RecipeTeacher — Best Damn Air Fryer Pork Chops
**Inspired by**: https://recipeteacher.com/best-damn-air-fryer-pork-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26). RecipeTeacher (US food publisher).
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 12 min (source allows 12–14) · **Rest**: a couple of minutes (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 2 center-cut, bone-in pork chops, 1½–2 inches thick
- 2 tbsp brown sugar; 1 tbsp paprika
- 1½ tsp salt; 1½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tsp ground mustard; ½ tsp onion powder; ¼ tsp garlic powder
- 1–2 tbsp olive oil

**Method**
1. Preheat the air fryer to **400 °F for 5 minutes**.
2. Rinse the chops with cool water and pat completely dry.
3. Mix the dry ingredients in a small bowl.
4. Coat the chops with olive oil; rub the seasoning mixture liberally over them.
5. Air-fry at **400 °F for 12–14 minutes**, flipping after **6 minutes**.
6. **Pull at 140 °F (60.4 °C)** — the source's stated doneness target; it says carryover cooking brings the temperature to **145 °F (63 °C)**. Rest a couple of minutes before serving. Final serving temperature: **145 °F (63 °C)** (source's stated carryover).

### 2. BBC Food — Pork chops in air fryer
**Inspired by**: https://www.bbc.co.uk/food/recipes/air_fryer_peppered_pork_68056 (fetched 2026-10-26). BBC Food (British public-service food media).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: under 30 min · **Cook**: 10–30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 pork chops
- 1–2 tbsp vegetable oil, or a few sprays of low-calorie cooking oil spray
- 1 tbsp paprika; 1 tsp American burger seasoning
- 1 tsp onion granules; 1 tsp garlic granules
- 1 tsp dried parsley; 1 tsp crushed peppercorns; salt

**Method**
1. Preheat the air fryer to **200 °C**.
2. Dry the chops with kitchen paper; put them in a bowl and coat with oil.
3. Add the seasonings and mix to coat.
4. Air-fry for **10–12 minutes**, or **14 minutes** if the chops are extra-large.
5. Serve immediately.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is time plus size. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Australian Pork — Air Fryer Pork Loin Chops
**Inspired by**: https://www.pork.com.au/how-to-air-fry-pork/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Australian Pork Limited (industry body food media); recipe explicitly names pork loin chops.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 8–10 min · **Rest**: 3 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 400–450 g pork loin chops (2 per pack)
- 40 g butter, chopped
- 2 small garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 2 tbsp thyme leaves **or** ½ tsp mixed dried Italian herbs
- Olive oil, salt and pepper (quantities not specified by the source)

**Method**
1. Preheat the air fryer to **200 °C**.
2. Pat the pork dry; rub both sides with a little olive oil and season with salt and pepper.
3. Put the pork in the air-fryer basket; cook **8–10 minutes**, depending on thickness, until golden and just cooked through — the source says there is no need to turn the pork.
4. Remove, cover and **rest 3 minutes**.
5. For the optional butter drizzle: melt the butter in a small frying pan over medium-high; let it bubble **1 minute**; add the garlic and cook stirring **30 seconds**; stir in the lemon juice and thyme (or dried herbs) and cook **1 minute**; season and spoon over the chops.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is "golden and just cooked through" by time. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. Prairie Fresh (credited to the National Pork Board) — Air-Fried Korean-Style Pork Chops
**Inspired by**: https://www.prairiefresh.com/recipe/air-fried-korean-style-pork-chops (fetched 2026-10-26). Prairie Fresh recipe credited to the National Pork Board (US); Korean-style marinade.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: 3 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 4 bone-in ribeye pork chops, 1 inch thick
- 1 cup gochujang, hot or mild
- 2 tbsp honey; 3 tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp minced ginger; 1 tbsp minced garlic
- 4 thinly sliced green onions; 1 tbsp sesame oil

**Method**
1. Mix the gochujang, honey, soy sauce, vinegar, ginger, garlic, green onions and sesame oil to make the marinade.
2. Put the chops and marinade in a resealable 1-gallon bag; refrigerate **1–8 hours**.
3. Preheat the air fryer to **400 °F**.
4. Remove the chops from the marinade and put them on a foil-lined tray; brush the tops liberally with leftover marinade; discard the rest.
5. Cook **15 minutes**, or until the internal temperature reaches **145 °F**.
6. Remove and **rest 3 minutes** before serving. Final serving temperature: **145 °F (63 °C)** (the source's stated internal temperature).

| BBC Food (British) | British peppered-chop style | 200 °C, 10–12 min (14 min extra-large) | Not stated |

The full observed numerical range is **60.4–63 °C (140–145 °F)** — only two of four sources publish a probe temperature, and both sit at or just below the USDA 63 °C line, so the measured evidence maps to `cooking_data.py`'s `medium` band (60–66 °C, `usda_safe: true`) rather than `well_done`. What drives the variation is measurement style, not doneness preference: one source pulls at 140 °F explicitly to let carryover land on 145 °F, the other cooks straight to 145 °F, and the two untimed sources (UK, Australia) judge by clock and colour at a gentler 200 °C basket temperature. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`medium`** — both measured sources land there and the appliance's fast air makes the lower pull the practical choice; the cut-level `well_done` remains available but is not what this leaf's evidence shows in practice.

## What makes this method special

An air fryer concentrates the oven's convective mechanism into a tiny chamber, so the same air that browns the surface is also in constant motion across it — Maillard development happens quickly and on all faces at once, which is why four of five sources skip pre-searing entirely and one basket cycle is enough. For this cut that means edge-to-edge evenness with no hands-on work, but the moving air is a desiccant: the lean, low-intramuscular-fat loin has little internal fat to lubricate the fibres, so the margin between golden and dried-out is smaller than in a pan, where fat and basting counteract moisture loss. The two probe-stating sources both solve this by pulling at or below the USDA line (140–145 °F) and trusting carryover rather than time, and the two time-only sources compensate with a lower 200 °C basket and shorter cycles. The appliance's fixed temperature grid (200 °C / 400 °F in every source) leaves thickness as the only real variable, which is exactly the variable a uniform 2–3 cm loin steak controls best.

## Summary

An air fryer bakes a pork loin steak on all sides at once — 8–14 minutes at around 200 °C, no searing, no turning — and the two sources that probe pull it off at 140–145 °F, juicier than the appliance's reputation suggests. It is a good match for this cut because the steak is uniform and lean, so the even fast air cooks it edge to edge with almost no babysitting. The trade-off is that the same fan that browns also dries, and this cut has no fat to fall back on, so timing by probe rather than by clock matters more here than in a pan. Rest it a few minutes and it behaves like a pan-seared steak with a fraction of the attention.
