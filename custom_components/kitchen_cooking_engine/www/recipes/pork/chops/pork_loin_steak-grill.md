# Pork Loin Steak × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling exposes the steak to radiant and convective heat from below with no pan wall and no
liquid: the sources sear over direct heat (flat-top BBQ, open grill, charcoal) and four of
five finish by moving the meat to indirect heat or lowering the flame, because a 2–3 cm lean
loin steak colours fast and dries fast if the fire stays high. The observed endpoints run
60–71 °C — the widest span of any leaf on this cut — which reflects the method's real-world
variability: lid open or closed, direct or indirect, thickness 2.5–4 cm, and traditions that
judge doneness by timer, by feel, or by probe. The cut's uniform cross-section means it sits
flat on the grate and cooks without the hotspots a bone-in chop introduces; the risk is
exposure, since nothing shields the surface once the fat cap renders.

## Styckesprofil

Grilling blottlägger steaken för strål- och konvektionsvärme underifrån, utan pannvägg och
utan vätska: källorna steks över direkt värme (flat-top, öppen grill, kol) och fyra av fem
avslutar genom att flytta köttet till indirekt värme eller sänka lågan, eftersom en 2,5–3,5 cm
mager loin-steke färgas snabbt och torkar snabbt om lågan står kvar. De observerade
slutpunkterna löper 60–71 °C — den bredaste spännvidden av alla blad för detta stycke.
Styckets enhetliga tvärsnitt ligger platt på risten utan de heta punkter ett ben ger; risken
är blottläggning, inget skyddar ytan när fettkanten har renderats.

## Source recipes

### 1. Andy Cooks — Crispy Skin Pork Chop
**Inspired by**: https://www.andy-cooks.com/blogs/recipes/crispy-bbq-pork-chop (fetched 2026-10-26). Andy Cooks (Andy Berrisford, Australian food publisher); page labels the cuisine Australian.
**Serves**: 3 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: 10 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 3 large bone-in pork loin chops, skin on (around 300–400 g each)
- 3 tbsp olive oil; sea salt
- 1 tsp ground fennel; 1 tsp paprika; 1 tsp ground cumin

**Method**
1. Score the skin vertically every 5 mm through skin and just into fat; separate skin from flesh about one-third down from each side, leaving the middle attached. Push two metal skewers through the fat cap to hold the skin flat.
2. Rub the chops with olive oil; season the skin generously with sea salt; mix the fennel, paprika and cumin and sprinkle on the flesh sides, keeping spices off the skin.
3. Preheat a flat-top BBQ or large flat pan on low. Stand the chops upright with skin against the surface, supported by presses or heavy pots; cook on low **20–25 minutes**, checking and rotating after 10 minutes.
4. Once the skin is blistered/crispy and the fat rendered, move the chops to a wire rack and scrape excess fat off the grill.
5. Turn the grill to high and preheat 2–3 minutes. Lay the chops flesh-side down with a press on top; sear **30–45 seconds**, flip and sear the other side; flip 2–3 times until the loin reaches the stated internal temperature.
6. Remove and rest on the rack **10 minutes**.
7. Remove the skin from the skewers, chop it bite-size, carve the loin from the bone and slice thickly.
8. **Pull when the internal temperature reads 70 °C (158 °F)** — the source's stated doneness. Final serving temperature: **70 °C (158 °F)**.

### 2. My Korean Kitchen — Chargrilled Korean Style BBQ Pork Chops
**Inspired by**: https://mykoreankitchen.com/chargrilled-korean-style-bbq-pork-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26). My Korean Kitchen, Sue (Korean-Australian publisher; page labels the recipe Korean fusion).
**Serves**: 5 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 5 bone-in pork loin chops, about 4 cm thick (combined weight 2.5 kg / 5.5 lb)
- 1 tbsp minced ginger; 4 tbsp minced garlic
- 160 g onion, 280 g apple, grated
- 1⅓ cup soy sauce; 10 tbsp dark brown sugar; 7 tbsp rice wine
- 1 tbsp gochujang; ¼ tsp ground black pepper

**Method**
1. Blend the marinade; combine with the meat in a sealable container and refrigerate overnight — the author marinated 26 hours and recommends at least 12.
2. Take the meat from the fridge **30 minutes** before cooking.
3. On a grill, sear both sides **2–3 minutes each** over direct heat, then move to indirect heat; close the lid and cook slowly until the preferred doneness, **about 15–20 minutes**.
4. Put the cooked meat on a metal plate, cover with foil and rest **5 minutes**.
5. Serve — the author suggests grilled pineapple rings and red bell peppers.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the author's "preferred doneness" plus time. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)** for medium. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Let's Cook Some Food — Grilled Jerk Pork Chops
**Inspired by**: https://www.letscooksomefood.com/grilled-jerk-pork-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Let's Cook Some Food, author Mink; the page labels the recipe Caribbean and describes the flavours as especially Jamaican.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 1 min (+ marinating) · **Cook**: 8 min · **Rest**: about 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 4 bone-in pork loin chops (about 2 lb total)
- 4 tbsp Golden Mountain Seasoning Sauce (or light soy sauce)
- 3 tbsp agave (or sugar)
- 1 tbsp Jamaican jerk seasoning

**Method**
1. Mix the seasoning sauce, jerk seasoning and agave (or sugar) in a large zip-lock bag; add the chops, seal and remove as much air as possible; marinate in the fridge **at least an hour, preferably overnight**.
2. Take the chops out of the fridge **about an hour** before grilling.
3. Heat the grill to **about 350 °F**; put the chops on the grill.
4. Cover and grill over medium heat until the internal temperature is **160 °F**, **about 4 minutes per side** — time depends on thickness.
5. Rest on a platter covered with foil **about 5 minutes**; serve with mango salsa and coconut rice.
6. Final serving temperature: **160 °F (71 °C)** (the source's stated internal temperature).

### 4. Mama Maggie's Kitchen — Grilled Pork Chops Adobo (Chuletas Adobadas)
**Inspired by**: https://inmamamaggieskitchen.com/grilled-pork-chops-adobo/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Mama Maggie's Kitchen, Maggie Unzueta (Mexican-American food publisher); page labels the recipe Mexican.
**Serves**: 12 · **Prep**: 5 hours (incl. marinating) · **Cook**: 1 hour · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 6 guajillo chiles, deveined and seeds removed; 2 ancho chiles, deveined and seeds removed
- 5 roasted garlic cloves; ¼ roasted onion; 1 chile chipotle in adobo sauce
- 5 bay leaves; 1 tsp pepper; ¼ tsp whole oregano; ½ tsp ground cumin; ¼ tsp ground cinnamon; 5 cloves
- ¼ cup apple cider vinegar; ½ cup water; 1 tbsp achiote paste; 1 tbsp salt; ¼ cup pineapple juice
- 12 boneless pork chops

**Method**
1. Bring the water to a boil in a saucepan; turn the heat off; add the guajillo and ancho chiles, cover and let sit **5 minutes** until soft.
2. Char the garlic and onion **1–2 minutes**, watching carefully.
3. Blend the garlic, onion, chipotle, bay leaves, pepper, oregano, cumin, cinnamon, cloves, vinegar, water, achiote, salt, pineapple juice and reconstituted chiles — do not add the chile soaking liquid.
4. Pour the adobo into a large container; coat each chop on both sides; cover and refrigerate **4 hours, preferably overnight**.
5. Heat the grill; cook each chop **4–5 minutes per side**, depending on thickness.
6. Optional: cook the marinade in a small saucepan and pour over the cooked chops.
7. The source publishes no internal temperature; the endpoint is time plus thickness. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 5. Tasting Table — Grilled Tandoori Pork Chop Recipe
**Inspired by**: https://www.tastingtable.com/1542068/grilled-tandoori-pork-chop-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Tasting Table (US food media); the recipe applies an Indian tandoori marinade and technique to pork chops.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: ~10 min (+ at least 4 hours marinating) · **Cook**: ~12 min · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 4 bone-in pork chops, 1 inch thick
- ¾ cup yogurt; 4 garlic cloves; 2 tbsp grated ginger
- 1 tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper
- 1½ tbsp garam masala; 1½ tbsp cumin; 2½ tbsp paprika; 1 tsp turmeric; 1 tsp coriander
- Juice of 2 limes

**Method**
1. Mix the yogurt, garlic, ginger, salt, pepper and all the spices; coat the chops; refrigerate **at least 4 hours**.
2. Heat the grill to **400 °F**.
3. Grill over direct heat **2–3 minutes per side**, then move to indirect heat and cook **7–9 minutes** until the internal temperature reaches **140 °F**.
4. Rest **5 minutes**; the source's prose says pull at 140 °F and serve with the remaining lime.
5. Final serving temperature: **140 °F (60 °C)** (the source's stated internal temperature).

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Andy Cooks (Australia) | Australian flat-top BBQ, two-stage low-then-high | 20–25 min skin render + high-heat sear flips; rest 10 min | **70 °C (158 °F)** stated |
| My Korean Kitchen (Korean fusion, AU-published) | Korean marinade, direct-then-indirect | 2–3 min/side direct + 15–20 min indirect lid down; rest 5 min | Not stated |
| Let's Cook Some Food (Caribbean/Jamaican-style) | Jamaican jerk, covered grill | ~350 °F grill, ~4 min/side; rest ~5 min | **160 °F (71 °C)** stated |
| Mama Maggie's Kitchen (Mexican) | Adobo-marinated boneless chops | 4–5 min/side after 4 h marinade | Not stated |
| Tasting Table (Indian tandoori, US-published) | Tandoori marinade, direct-then-indirect | 400 °F grill, 2–3 min/side direct + 7–9 min indirect; rest 5 min | **140 °F (60 °C)** stated |

The full observed numerical range is **60–71 °C** — three of five sources publish a probe temperature (60, 70, 71 °C) and two rely on time, lid and feel. The span maps across `cooking_data.py` doneness keys: 60 °C sits at the bottom edge of `medium` (60–66 °C), 70 °C sits just under `well_done` (71 °C), so this leaf's evidence straddles both bands rather than clustering in one. The driver is technique divergence, not disagreement about pork: the Indian-tandoori source pulls early at 140 °F and carries over, the Australian source deliberately renders skin first and pushes to 70 °C, and the Caribbean source works to a round 160 °F under a closed lid. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`well_done`** at cut level retained, with `medium`-band pulls (60–63 °C) documented as the early-pull tradition in two of the three measured sources — the divergence is the finding and no source is treated as wrong.

## What makes this method special

Grilling cooks the steak with radiant heat from below and, when the lid closes, a convective oven's worth of hot air around it — a steeper surface-to-centre gradient than any indoor method, so the exterior advances faster than the centre and the timing risk concentrates at the grate surface. For a lean, uniform loin steak that gradient is the whole game: the direct phase builds the charred, smoke-touched crust no pan can duplicate, and the indirect phase (used by four of five sources) lets the centre reach temperature without incinerating the surface. The fat cap and skin, where present, must be rendered deliberately before the lean meat is done — the Australian source solves this by giving the skin its own slow 20–25 minute render standing upright, then blasting the flesh at high heat for seconds. Marinades matter more here than in the pan because the open flame dehydrates the surface: sugar- and fruit-based Caribbean and Korean glazes, yogurt-based Indian tandoori, and Chilean-Mexican adobo each lay down a protective, browning layer that also buffers the thin surface against scorching.

## Summary

Grilling gives a pork loin steak direct-fire char and a smoky crust while the centre climbs to wherever the tradition says doneness lives — the three measuring sources here land anywhere from 60 to 71 °C. It suits the cut's flat, even shape, which sits flush on the grate and cooks without hotspots, and the best results split the cook into a hard direct phase for surface and a gentler indirect phase for the centre. What you gain over the pan is smoke and flame character; what you give up is control, since the open fire dries the lean surface quickly and thickness decides everything. Move to indirect heat early, probe rather than clock it, and rest the steak before slicing.
