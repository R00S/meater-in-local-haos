# Pork Loin Steak × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven roasting surrounds the steak with still or fan-driven hot air: no pan contact, no liquid,
no turning, and an even approach to temperature from every face at once. The fetched sources
run 200–228 °C (400–450 °F) for 15–20 minutes, two of them seasoning the raw steak straight
onto a sheet pan and two building a bed (potatoes, or a sheet-pan vegetable spread) beneath it.
For a lean, uniform 2–3 cm loin steak the method's virtue is passivity — the flat cut sits
level, cooks without hotspots, and needs no handling — while its risk is the familiar one:
dry air moves moisture off the surface faster than a pan's fat film does, so the window between
roasted and dried is the method's own. The one Spanish source finishes the chops under the
grill setting after the potatoes, an extra-browning step the American sources get from sheer
oven heat instead. Carryover of 2–4 °C applies as with every method for this cut.

## Source recipes

### 1. Tastes of Lizzy T — Easy Baked Pork Loin Chops
**Inspired by**: https://www.tastesoflizzyt.com/oven-baked-pork-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Tastes of Lizzy T (US food publisher). Recipe explicitly names boneless pork loin chops at 1 inch — an exact cut match.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 20 min (source notes ~17 min actual) · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 4 boneless pork loin chops, 1 inch thick
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tbsp smoked paprika; ½ tbsp onion powder; ½ tbsp dried oregano
- ½ tsp salt; ¼ tsp garlic powder; ¼ tsp black pepper; ¼ tsp ground thyme

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **400 °F**. Spray a 9×13-inch baking pan with nonstick cooking spray.
2. Rub each pork chop with olive oil.
3. Mix the seasonings in a small bowl; rub both sides of the chops with the seasoning mix and place them in the pan.
4. Roast for **15–20 minutes**, or until the thickest part reads **145 °F** on a meat thermometer — the author says her chops took about 17 minutes.
5. **Rest 5 minutes**, then serve. **Pull at 145 °F (63 °C)** — the source's stated thermometer target. Final serving temperature: **145 °F (63 °C)**.

### 2. Food Network Kitchen — Baked Pork Chops
**Inspired by**: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/baked-pork-chop-3631185 (fetched 2026-10-26). Food Network Kitchen (US food media).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min active · **Cook**: 40 min total · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1½ cups panko; 5 tbsp vegetable oil; 3 tbsp grated Parmesan; 2 tsp dried Italian seasoning
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- Four ¾-inch bone-in pork chops (about 2¼ lb)
- Lemon wedges, for serving, optional

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **450 °F**.
2. Combine the panko, oil, Parmesan, Italian seasoning and ¾ tsp each salt and pepper in a large resealable bag.
3. Put the chops in a large bowl and toss with 1 tbsp water; put them in the bag and shake to coat, pressing the breadcrumb mixture firmly onto the meat.
4. Put the chops on a wire rack set over a baking sheet; top with any remaining breadcrumbs.
5. Bake until the crumbs are dark golden and the internal temperature reads **145 °F**, avoiding the bone — the source gives **15–20 minutes** as a guideline.
6. Serve immediately with lemon wedges, if using. Final serving temperature: **145 °F (63 °C)** (the source's stated doneness reading).

### 3. Creme de la Crumb — Sheet Pan Pork Chops with Potatoes and Broccoli
**Inspired by**: https://www.lecremedelacrumb.com/sheet-pan-pork-chops-with-potatoes-and-broccoli/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Creme de la Crumb (US food publisher).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: 3–5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- **Pork chops**: 4 thick-cut boneless pork chops; salt and pepper; 1 tsp garlic powder; 1 tsp Italian herb seasoning blend or Herbes de Provence; ½ tsp smoked paprika; 2 tsp dry ranch seasoning
- **Broccoli**: 3 cups broccoli florets; 1 tbsp olive oil; salt and pepper
- **Potatoes**: 1 lb baby gold or red potatoes, cut in half; 2 tbsp olive oil; salt and pepper; 2 tsp garlic powder; ⅓ cup grated Parmesan cheese

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **425 °F** (the source writes "425 degrees" without a unit; °F is its convention) and grease a baking sheet.
2. Toss the potatoes with oil, salt, pepper, garlic powder and Parmesan; arrange on one-third of the pan and bake **5–10 minutes** while preparing the pork.
3. Season the chops with salt and pepper, then garlic powder, Italian herb blend, paprika and ranch seasoning; add the pork to the centre third of the pan; return to the oven **10 minutes**.
4. Toss the broccoli with oil, salt and pepper; arrange on the remaining third; return to the oven **10–15 minutes**, until the broccoli and potatoes are fork-tender — the pork centre should read **145 degrees** if using a thermometer.
5. **Rest the pork 3–5 minutes**, then serve. Final serving temperature: **145 °F (63 °C)** (unit not written on the page; °F is the source's convention).

### 4. Bon Viveur — Chuletas de cerdo al horno con patatas
**Inspired by**: https://bonviveur.com/es/recetas/chuletas-de-cerdo-al-horno-con-patatas (fetched 2026-10-26). Bon Viveur (Spanish food media).
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 40 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 400 g potatoes
- 1 splash extra-virgin olive oil; salt; ground black pepper; a few bay leaves
- 75 ml white wine; 100 ml meat stock
- 4 pork loin chops (bone included, about 450 g total) — the source notes they were "not very thick"
- 1 garlic clove; fresh parsley

**Method**
1. Peel, rinse and slice the potatoes about **½ cm** thick.
2. Spread them on a greased oven tray; season with salt and pepper, add a splash of oil and distribute the bay leaves; add the wine and stock.
3. Bake in an oven preheated to **200 °C for 30 minutes**.
4. Season the four pork loin chops with salt and pepper and set them on top of the potatoes without overlapping.
5. Crush the garlic with a pinch of salt in a mortar until almost paste-like; mix with chopped parsley and loosen with a couple of tablespoons of oil; brush the chops on both sides.
6. Return the tray to the oven on the **grill setting for 10–15 minutes**, depending on thickness; turn the chops halfway through and watch them closely.
7. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is thickness plus the grill-setting window. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## What makes this method special

Roasting surrounds the steak with dry moving air, which behaves nothing like a pan: heat arrives by convection from every direction at once, so the centre and the surface approach temperature nearly together and the exterior browns without ever contacting fat. For this cut that is a genuine trade — the flat, uniform loin cooks with perfect evenness and zero hands-on time, but the dry atmosphere wicks moisture from the exposed surface exactly where the cut has the least intramuscular fat to spare, which is why three of four sources either build a vegetable bed under the meat (releasing steam into the pan) or coat the chops in oil and a breadcrumb shell (a sacrificial moisture barrier). The Spanish source's grill-setting finish exists for the same reason: oven heat alone browns slowly in still air, so the last minutes borrow a broiler's direct radiant heat to colour the top that the potatoes' steam has kept pale. Breading, bed or bare — every configuration is a workaround for the same physics: even heat, thirsty air.

## Summary

Oven roasting cooks a pork loin steak hands-off — 15–20 minutes at 200–228 °C — and the three sources that probe all pull it at 145 °F, the USDA-aligned line, showing how little this method's traditions disagree about doneness. It suits the cut's flat, even shape, which roasts uniformly with no turning and no hotspots, and the vegetable bed or breadcrumb coating some sources use keeps the lean surface from drying in the open air. What you gain is simplicity and capacity for a full tray at once; what you give up is crust development and the flavour that fat contact brings. Probe rather than clock it, and rest the steak before slicing.
