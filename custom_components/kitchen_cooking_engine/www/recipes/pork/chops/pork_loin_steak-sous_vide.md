# Pork Loin Steak × Sous Vide — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Sous vide removes heat as a variable: the steak is sealed and held in a water bath at a fixed
temperature until the entire cross-section reaches that exact degree, then seared for seconds
in a ripping-hot pan to build crust the bath never could. For a lean, uniform loin steak this
is the most controllable method available — the bath windows in the fetched sources cluster at
57–64 °C (135–147 °F) for 45 minutes to 4 hours, and because there is no overshoot above the
bath temperature, the dryness risk that defines every fast method for this cut simply does not
exist until the sear. Carryover is negligible (the meat leaves the bath already at target), so
the sear must be brief: every source caps it at 30–60 seconds per side. The method's cost is
texture rather than safety — long holds past the enzymatic window can turn the lean muscle
mushy, which is why the sources pair short-to-moderate times with their lower baths.

## Styckesprofil

Sous vide tar bort värmen som variabel: steaken förseglas och hålls i ett vattenbad på fast
temperatur hela vägen till exakt målgrad, sedan bränns den i hett panna i sekunder för att
bygga en skorpa badet aldrig kan ge. För en mager, enhetlig loin-steke är detta den mest
styrbara metoden som finns — badfönstren i de hämtade källorna klusterar vid 57–64 °C i 45
minuter till 4 timmar, och eftersom inget kan gå över badtemperaturen finns den torkningsrisk
som definierar alla snabba metoder för detta stycke inte förrän stekningen. Eftervärmen är
försumbar, så stekningen måste vara kort: varje källa begränsar den till 30–60 sekunder per
sida.

## Source recipes

### 1. Anova Culinary — Sous Vide Pork Rib Chop
**Inspired by**: https://recipes.anovaculinary.com/recipe/sous-vide-pork-rib-chop (fetched 2026-10-26). Anova Culinary (US appliance maker's recipe platform).
**Serves**: 1 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 2 h · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 1 (8- to 12-ounce) bone-in pork rib chop, 1–2 inches thick
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil or vegetable oil

**Method**
1. Set the Anova cooker to **140 °F (60 °C)**.
2. Season the chop generously with salt and pepper; put it in a medium zipper-lock or vacuum-seal bag and seal using water immersion or a vacuum sealer on the dry setting.
3. Put the bag in the bath and set the timer for **2 hours**.
4. Remove the chop from the bag and pat very dry.
5. Heat the oil in a medium cast-iron skillet over high heat; when shimmering, sear until well-browned, **about 30 seconds per side**.
6. Transfer to a cutting board and **rest 5 minutes**; serve.
7. The page also notes an optional **135 °F (57 °C)** medium-rare bath with an extra **30 minutes**, and compares conventional cooking at 145 °F where the chop rose to around 150 °F after resting — that comparison is not this recipe's pull instruction. Final serving temperature: bath setting **140 °F (60 °C)** (the source's stated bath).

### 2. Two Kooks in the Kitchen — Sous Vide Pork Chops (Vietnamese-Style)
**Inspired by**: https://twokooksinthekitchen.com/vietnamese-pork-chops-sous-vide/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Two Kooks in the Kitchen (US food publisher); Vietnamese-style seasoning.
**Serves**: 4 (or 3 generous) · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 2 h 5 min · **Rest**: not stated separately

**Ingredients**
- 2.7–3 lb bone-in rib pork chops, about 4 chops, 1–1½ inches thick (loin chops or boneless allowed in the notes)
- Salt, black pepper, garlic powder
- **Marinade**: 3 tbsp neutral olive oil or vegetable oil; ¼ cup soy sauce; ¼ cup brown sugar (honey or maple syrup may substitute); 1 tbsp fish sauce; 2 tsp fresh grated ginger (or ⅛ tsp powdered); ½ tsp red chili flakes; 2 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro
- **Glaze**: 3 tbsp lime juice; 2 tsp minced or grated garlic; ½ tsp cornstarch

**Method**
1. Heat the water bath to **142 °F (61 °C)** for medium.
2. Combine the marinade ingredients except fresh garlic, lime juice and cornstarch (makes about ½ cup; use ¼ cup for the chops and reserve the rest).
3. Season the chops on both sides with salt, pepper and garlic powder; put them in a large zip bag (or two medium bags), add **¼ cup/59 ml** marinade and massage to distribute.
4. Seal by water displacement or vacuum sealer. Cook **1–4 hours** — the source gives **2–2.5 hours** for 1¼–1½-inch chops and **1–1.5 hours** for ½–1-inch chops.
5. While they cook, stir the lime juice, garlic and cornstarch into the reserved marinade; microwave **45–60 seconds** to thicken.
6. Heat the grill to high, **about 500–550 °F**. Remove the chops from the bag and pat dry; brush glaze on both sides; oil the grill; grill **40–60 seconds per side** for grill marks, covering the grill between turns.
7. Transfer to a platter, brush with the reserved glaze, sprinkle with cilantro and serve.
8. Final serving temperature: bath setting **142 °F (61 °C)** — the source's stated doneness ladder also gives medium-rare **139–140 °F/59.4–60 °C**, medium-well **150 °F/65.6 °C** and well done **160 °F/71 °C** (bath settings, not pull readings).

### 3. A Duck's Oven — Sous Vide Pork Chops (Boneless or Bone-In)
**Inspired by**: https://aducksoven.com/recipes/sous-vide-pork-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26). A Duck's Oven (US food blog).
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 1 h 30 min · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 2 large boneless or bone-in pork chops
- Seasoning of choice; kosher salt if using saltless seasoning
- Ghee or another high-smoke-point fat (quantity not specified)

**Method**
1. Preheat the water bath to **145 °F** for medium, **140 °F** for medium-rare, or **150 °F** for well done.
2. Season the chops with seasoning and salt; seal in a vacuum bag.
3. Cook in the bath for at least **1 hour 30 minutes for boneless**, **2 hours for bone-in**, and up to **4 hours**.
4. Heat the fat in a cast-iron skillet on high; remove the chops from the bag and pat dry.
5. Sear in the very hot skillet until browned, **no more than 60 seconds per side**.
6. **Rest 5 minutes**, then slice and serve. Final serving temperature: bath settings **140/145/150 °F (60.4/62.8/65.6 °C)**.

### 4. StreetSmart Kitchen — Foolproof Sous Vide Pork Chops
**Inspired by**: https://www.streetsmartkitchen.com/sous-vide-pork-chops/ (fetched 2026-10-26). StreetSmart Kitchen (US food publisher).
**Serves**: not stated in recipe card · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 1 h (up to 4 h in bath) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 bone-in pork chops about 1.5 inches thick (about 1 pound) — the article says boneless chops also work and recommends loin chops
- Kosher salt; freshly ground black pepper; olive oil
- Fresh rosemary sprigs, optional
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter; 4 cloves garlic
- **Sun-dried tomato caper sauce**: 1 tbsp unsalted butter; 2 tbsp chopped sun-dried tomatoes; 1 tbsp chopped capers; juice from the sous vide chops, strained; 1 tbsp chopped fresh parsley

**Method**
1. Set up the circulator and preheat the water to **140 °F (60 °C)** or another chosen temperature.
2. Season the chops with salt and pepper; put in a vacuum bag with a splash of olive oil and rosemary if using; vacuum seal or use a zip bag.
3. Once the water reaches temperature, submerge the chops and set a timer for **1 hour**; the page says they may remain in the bath up to **4 hours**.
4. Remove the chops from the bag, reserve the cooking juices and pat dry. Heat a cast-iron skillet over high heat until very hot; add butter and sear each side **about 45 seconds**, until browned, basting with garlic and thyme if desired.
5. For the optional sauce, melt 1 tbsp butter in the same skillet over medium heat; add tomatoes and capers; slowly add strained pork juice 1 tbsp at a time; cook **about 5 minutes** until thickened.
6. Slice the chops, spoon sauce on top and garnish with parsley. Final serving temperature: bath setting **140 °F (60 °C)** (the source's doneness chart also lists 130–134 °F through 155–160 °F bath ranges).

### 5. Great British Chefs (Luke Holder) — Barbecued sous vide pork chop with borlotti beans and candied tomato
**Inspired by**: https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/sous-vide-pork-chop-recipe-borlotti-beans (fetched 2026-10-26). Great British Chefs; Luke Holder, chef director at Hartnett Holder & Co at Lime Wood (named chef, Hampshire, UK).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 1 h 30 min total (45 min bath) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 pork chops
- 4 slices of lemon zest; 4 garlic cloves; salt; olive oil
- **Borlotti beans**: 1 kg borlotti beans (freshly podded); 100 g pancetta; 1 onion, quartered; 2 carrots, peeled; 3 celery sticks; ½ bulb garlic, cut in half; 1 bay leaf; 1 bunch thyme; 3 l water or chicken stock
- **Candied tomatoes**: 4 large plum tomatoes; 500 g honey; 100 g sherry vinegar; 1 star anise; 1 vanilla pod; 1 sprig rosemary; 1 tsp coriander seeds; 2 garlic cloves
- **To finish the beans**: parsley, basil, lemon juice, olive oil

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **90 °C** and the water bath to **64 °C**.
2. Blanch the tomatoes in boiling water, refresh in iced water, peel; dry in the oven 90 minutes.
3. Simmer all the bean ingredients covered for **40 minutes** until tender; cool in the liquor.
4. Place each pork chop in an individual vacuum bag with a dash of oil, a strip of lemon zest, a clove of garlic and a pinch of salt; seal, place in the water bath and cook **45 minutes**.
5. Boil the tomato liquor ingredients, add the tomatoes and reduce until glazed.
6. Remove the chops from the bags, pat dry and **barbecue or chargrill over high heat** for a golden exterior.
7. Bring the beans up to heat with the braising liquor; finish with herbs, lemon juice and chopped tomato. Serve the chops with the beans and tomatoes. Final serving temperature: bath setting **64 °C** (the source states no internal reading).

The full observed numerical range is **60–64 °C (140–147 °F)** across the five chosen sources' primary baths (the wider published ladders stretch 57–71 °C / 135–160 °F as optional doneness settings). Because sous vide numbers are **bath settings rather than pull readings**, they map to `cooking_data.py` doneness keys differently: the cluster at 60–62 °C sits in `medium` (63 °C target, 60–66 °C band), the single 64 °C UK bath sits just under `medium`'s ceiling, and only the optional settings reach `well_done`. The variation across sources tracks the published doneness ladder each platform offers rather than a disagreement about pork — the physics of a bath allows every setting to be "correct". `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`medium`** — four of five primary baths land at 60–61 °C and the cut's lean muscle is at its juiciest there; no source's primary bath exceeds 64 °C.

## What makes this method special

Sous vide flips the usual risk profile of this cut: instead of fighting an overshooting heat source, the cook fixes the endpoint in advance and the water guarantees it edge to edge, so the entire 2–3 cm cross-section finishes at one exact temperature with zero gradient. That uniformity is uniquely valuable for a lean, uniform steak — there is no overcooked ring, no undercooked centre, and the moisture the fibres would otherwise lose to a hot pan stays bound until the final sear. The sear then becomes the whole remaining craft: with the meat already at target and no carryover to hide behind, 30–60 seconds per side in a cast-iron pan (every source agrees on this) is all that builds the Maillard crust, and a second longer starts undoing the bath's work. The method also decouples seasoning and serving — salt and aromatics diffuse during the hold, and the bag juices become a sauce base in two of the five recipes — which is why the technique suits a cut that otherwise gives flavour only from its short time in contact with fat.

## Summary

Sous vide holds a pork loin steak at an exact temperature — the sources cluster at 60–62 °C for 45 minutes to a few hours — so the whole steak finishes uniformly juicy and only the final 30–60 second sear provides crust. It is the most forgiving method this lean, fast-drying cut can meet, because nothing can overshoot and the centre is never behind the surface. What you give up is the pan's or grill's built-in flavour development during cooking: all the browning happens at the end, and long holds past a couple of hours soften the texture without adding anything. Choose it when precision and juiciness matter most, and keep the sear brutally short.
