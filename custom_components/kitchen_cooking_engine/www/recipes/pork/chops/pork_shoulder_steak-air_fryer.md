# Pork Shoulder Steak × Air Fryer — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

The air fryer is a small convection oven, and for a neck steak that means the same
enveloping heat as `oven_roast` delivered at higher velocity and in a smaller chamber: fat
rendering is faster, surface drying is the risk, and the basket's airflow browns all faces
without turning. The fetched sources use one configuration with local variations: preheat
then a single 8–15 minute blast at 180–200 °C with one flip or none, seasoning kept simple
(dry rub, garlic-salt-paprika, or a Korean-style soybean-paste marinade). Times cluster
narrowly — 8 minutes for thin US steaks, 13–15 minutes for thick UK or German cuts — and
two of four sources publish a probe target while the other two judge by "cooked through".
The method's physics for this cut mirrors the pan's gradient in miniature: the basket cooks
the outside hard while the centre lags, so thickness drives the time more than any source's
clock does. Probe into the centre of the thickest steak at the earliest published check
time, not at the end.

## Source recipes

### 1. The Dinner Bite — Air Fryer Pork Steak (Ájọkẹ)
**Inspired by**: https://www.thedinnerbite.com/air-fryer-pork-steak/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Ájọkẹ, The Dinner Bite (recipe card publishes its cuisine metadata as *"American, British"*; the page's safety text follows the USDA ladder, and it is attributed to the American tradition here with its dual label recorded).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: 2–3 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 boneless pork steaks, **about 1 inch thick, ~120 g each**
- 1 tbsp vegetable oil
- 1½ tbsp homemade pork rub (dried sage, savory, cilantro, paprika, cayenne, onion, garlic granules)
- Salt and black pepper

**Method**
1. Pat the steaks dry; **preheat the air fryer at 200 °C / 400 °F for 5 minutes**.
2. Brush lightly with oil; season salt, pepper and rub on both sides.
3. Cook **8–10 minutes, flipping halfway**.
4. **Pull at 145 °F / 63 °C** — check at 8 minutes: the source's verbatim instruction: *"inserting a cooking thermometer in the thickest part of the steak. It should read at least 145F / 63C"*. The page also publishes its own preference and ladder: *"I like to cook it to an internal temperature of 160F/71C"*; medium-rare 145–150 °F, medium 150–155 °F, medium-well 155–160 °F, well done 160 °F/71 °C.
5. Rest 2–3 minutes. Final serving temperature: **145–160 °F (63–71 °C) as published by the source**.

### 2. Feast Glorious Feast — How to Air Fry Pork Shoulder Steaks (Chloe)
**Inspired by**: https://feastgloriousfeast.com/air-fry-pork-shoulder-steaks/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Chloe, Feast Glorious Feast (UK).
**Serves**: 2 steaks · **Prep**: 2 min · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 pork shoulder steaks, about **170 g / 6 oz each and 3 cm / 1 inch thick** (UK supermarket standard range)
- ½ tsp vegetable oil
- ½ tsp seasoning (the pictured version: 50/50 garlic salt and paprika; the rule stated: seasoning must include salt)

**Method**
1. Dry the steaks thoroughly; drizzle the oil and seasoning and toss to coat — *"oil the meat and not the air fryer"*.
2. Place in the basket with space between them for airflow.
3. Cook **15 minutes at 200 °C / 390 °F**; turning after 10 minutes is optional (*"I don't need to using the rack in my air fryer"*). The page notes the same timing applies from frozen.
4. The source publishes no internal temperature; its doneness check is *"Check the pork is cooked through"*. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre at 12 minutes — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. EINFACH KOCHEN — Schweinesteak im Airfryer (Mark)
**Inspired by**: https://www.einfachkochen.de/rezepte/schweinesteak-im-airfryer (fetched 2026-10-26). Mark (Mark Kipson), EINFACH KOCHEN (German). **Metadata flag**: the page's serving selector shows "1 Portion" while its own step text says the recipe is *"für das Grundrezept von 4 Portionen"* — the inconsistency is on the page and is recorded rather than resolved.
**Serves**: page metadata inconsistent (see flag) · **Prep**: 10 min (+ 2 h marinate) · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: brief, duration not stated

**Ingredients**
- 700 g Schweinenacken (pork neck), cut into **4 evenly thick slices**
- 1 garlic clove; 2 tbsp sunflower oil
- 1 tbsp sweet paprika; 1 tsp rose-hot paprika; 2 tsp double-concentrated tomato paste; 1 tbsp medium-hot mustard; 1 tsp salt; pepper

**Method**
1. Rinse the neck cold, pat dry, cut into 4 even slices; mix the marinade and coat; refrigerate **about 2 hours**, overnight best.
2. **Preheat the air fryer at 180 °C for 3–5 minutes.**
3. Cook the steaks — one after another or together depending on basket size — for **about 15 minutes** in the closed air fryer until golden.
4. Rest briefly, season with pepper. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the timed golden cook. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. Lee Kum Kee (Hong Kong) — 氣炸韓式麵豉醬豬頸肉 (Air-Fried Pork Neck with Korean-Style Soybean Paste Sauce)
**Inspired by**: https://hk.lkk.com/zh-hk/recipes/air-fried-pork-jowl-with-korean-style-soy-bean-sauce (fetched 2026-10-26). Lee Kum Kee recipe site, Hong Kong edition (Chinese-language publisher; the recipe styles itself Korean-style, 韓式). **Cut-form flag**: the ingredient line reads *"豬頸肉 2塊"* — two pieces of pork neck — without stating a steak thickness or weight; recorded as a pork-neck piece recipe with the form unstated, not passed off as a specified 1-inch steak.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 15 min (+ overnight marinate) · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 pieces pork neck (豬頸肉)
- **Marinade**: 100 g Asian pear, grated; 2 tsp garlic; 3 tbsp Korean-style soybean paste (李錦記韓式麵豉醬); 1 tbsp light soy sauce; 1 tbsp sesame oil; 1 tbsp rice wine
- **Dipping sauce**: 2 tsp Korean-style soybean paste; 2 tsp light soy sauce; 1 tsp sesame oil; 2 tsp white sesame seeds

**Method**
1. Grate the pear (flesh and juice); pierce the pork neck surface all over with a fork.
2. Mix the marinade ingredients and coat the meat; marinate **overnight**.
3. Air fry at **180 °C for 13–15 minutes**, then rest **5 minutes**.
4. Slice and serve with the mixed dipping sauce.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the timed cook. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre of the thicker piece — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## What makes this method special

The air fryer compresses the oven's enveloping gradient into a smaller space and pushes it
faster: the same surround heat arrives at the steak's surface with the basket's airflow
stripping the boundary layer, so browning happens in minutes and the fat render that would
take twenty minutes in a full-size oven is underway by eight. For a neck steak the
practical consequence is that thickness, not time, becomes the variable — every source
says so in its own way: the UK source fixes 15 minutes for 3 cm steaks and tells thinner
ones to take less, the US source checks at 8 minutes because thin steaks are already done
at 10, and the German source's "about 15 minutes" for four 175 g slices is doing the same
arithmetic. The method's risk for this cut is the opposite of the pan's: there is no fat
pooling to baste with, so an un-oiled surface dries fast, which is why two sources make
oiling the meat itself a rule rather than a step. What it buys is the pan's speed with
the oven's evenness and none of the smoke.

## Summary

The air fryer cooks a pork shoulder steak at 180–200 °C for 8–15 minutes, and the four
sources agree almost exactly on the formula — preheat, oil the meat, single blast, one flip
or none — differing only in how long their particular thickness needs. It suits this cut
because the collar's fat renders quickly under fast surround heat and the basket browns
every face at once, which is the pan's speed without its one-sided scorch. Only the US
source publishes probe numbers: 145 °F at the earliest check, 160 °F as its author's
preference; the other three stop at "cooked through" or "golden". Because thickness drives
time here more than anywhere else, probe at the earliest published check rather than
trusting the full clock.
