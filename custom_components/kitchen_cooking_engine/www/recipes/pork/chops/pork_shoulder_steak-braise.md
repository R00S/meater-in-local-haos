# Pork Shoulder Steak × Braise — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Braising is the method this cut was practically designed for: the collar's collagen needs
moist, sustained heat to convert into gelatin, and a neck steak has exactly enough of it to
reward two hours in liquid without dissolving the way a whole shoulder would. The fetched
sources use six configurations: a breaded steak buried in mushroom-cream sauce (German),
a seared steak set over potatoes and onions in a covered Dutch oven with stock (Italian-
American), a bay-and-allspice braise thickened with flour, soy and mustard (Polish), a
lemon-honey-oregano marinade braised under foil then caramelised uncovered (Greek),
a stout-and-chipotle braise finished dry for a crust (American), and a Danish brown-then-
covered roast in balsamic stock. All operate in the same 150–200 °C oven for 1½–2 hours,
covered; only one source publishes a temperature, and it is high on purpose — 195–200 °F —
because this method's endpoint is tenderness, not safety. Probe vertically into the thickest
part after the covered phase; the steak should meet no resistance at the target.

## Source recipes

### 1. LECKER (Bauer Media Group) — Geschmorte Steaks in Champignonsoße
**Inspired by**: https://www.lecker.de/geschmorte-steaks-champignonsosse-70195.html (fetched 2026-10-26). LECKER.de; recipe credited to *kochen & genießen* 1/2015 (German magazine).
**Serves**: not stated (ingredients list **12 pork neck steaks of about 200 g**) · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 150 min total · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 500 g carrots; 750 g small mushrooms; 500 g onions; 4 garlic cloves
- 12 pork neck steaks (à ~200 g); salt, pepper
- 3 eggs (M); 200 g breadcrumbs; 6 tbsp clarified butter; 2 tsp dried rosemary
- 250 ml whipping cream; 2 tsp cornstarch

**Method**
1. Peel and slice the carrots; clean the mushrooms; slice the onions, chop the garlic. Pat the meat dry, season with salt and pepper; beat the eggs. Turn the meat first in egg, then in breadcrumbs.
2. Heat 4 tbsp clarified butter in a large pan and brown the steaks **in batches, golden on each side**; transfer to a large roasting dish or the roasting tin.
3. Clean the pan; heat 2 tbsp clarified butter, fry the carrots and mushrooms **about 10 minutes**, add rosemary, onions and garlic briefly; deglaze with **¾ l water** and the cream, bring to a boil; bind with cornstarch slurry; season.
4. Pour vegetables and sauce over the steaks; braise in a hot oven — **175 °C conventional / 150 °C fan** — for **about 1¾ hours**.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the 1¾-hour braise ("butterzart"). House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest steak — **Pull at 88–93 °C (190–200 °F)** to match the Greek source's published braising target, or 77 °C (170 °F) for merely tender. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. cookingitaliancomfortfood (Peter Bocchieri) — Braised Pork Shoulder Steak
**Inspired by**: https://cookingitaliancomfortfood.com/2011/02/15/braised-pork-shoulder-steak/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Peter Bocchieri, *cookingitaliancomfortfood* (Italian-American home cooking, US-published) — Italian tradition.
**Serves**: not stated (one steak) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 1 h 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 pork shoulder steak, **1½ inches thick, about 2¼ lb**
- 4 garlic cloves; ½ tsp oregano; ½ tsp thyme; 1 tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp olive oil plus more for searing; 2 tbsp wine vinegar
- ¾ cup chicken stock; 1 bay leaf
- 1 onion, quartered; 1 russet potato, quartered; 1 sweet potato, quartered

**Method**
1. Mash garlic, oregano, thyme, salt and pepper into a paste; mix half with the olive oil and vinegar; rub onto both sides of the steak.
2. In a Dutch oven over **high**, sear the steak **about 3 minutes per side**; remove and turn off the heat; rub the remaining seasoning on.
3. Set onions, potatoes, bay leaf and stock in the same pot; place the seared steak on top and cover.
4. Braise at **350 °F (175 °C)** for **1½ hours**, checking after 1 hour and adding liquid if necessary; the meat should be **fork tender**.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is fork-tenderness plus time. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 88 °C (190 °F)** or until a thermometer slides in with no resistance. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Everyday Healthy Recipes — Braised Pork Shoulder Steaks (Polish Style) (Monika Dabrowski)
**Inspired by**: https://www.everydayhealthyrecipes.com/braised-pork-shoulder-steaks-polish-style/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Monika Dabrowski, Everyday Healthy Recipes (Polish-inspired; the author states this was a staple in her Polish family).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 1 h 40 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 pork shoulder steaks (the page states plainly: *"Pork chops are not a suitable alternative"*)
- Oil; 1 bay leaf; 6–7 allspice berries; salt, pepper, paprika, herbes de Provence
- Onions, 2–3 garlic cloves
- Stock (amount not captured in fetched text); 1 tbsp flour; 1 tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp mustard; 1 tsp honey (optional)

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **325 °F / 170 °C / 150 °C fan**.
2. Season the steaks with salt, pepper, paprika and dried herbs.
3. Heat the oil with the bay leaf and allspice; brown the steaks over medium heat **2–3 minutes per side**.
4. Add the onions and about half the stock; cover (foil) and braise in the centre of the oven **1 hour**.
5. Whisk the flour with soy sauce, mustard and honey and the remaining cold stock; pour over; cover and return to the oven **another 30–40 minutes**, until tender. Optional: uncover at **425 °F / 220 °C for 10 minutes** to brown — the source warns not to let the meat dry.
6. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is tenderness plus time, and the page's own troubleshooting says chewy pork means it was not cooked long enough. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 88 °C (190 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. The Greek Foodie — Greek Pork Steaks (Oven-Baked, Fall-Apart Tender)
**Inspired by**: https://thegreekfoodie.com/greek-pork-steaks/ (fetched 2026-10-26). The Greek Foodie (Greek recipe site). The page itself states: *"Roast covered, in liquid… This is a braise, even though we're calling it a roast."*
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: not split out (+ 1 h–overnight marinate) · **Cook**: ~2 h · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Boneless pork shoulder steaks, **about ¾ inch thick** (bone-in works, +~10 min covered)
- **Marinade**: Dijon mustard, honey, lemon juice and zest, dried Greek oregano, dried thyme, extra-virgin olive oil
- Chicken stock (or vegetable broth/water); 1 head garlic; salt, pepper, chili flakes

**Method**
1. Marinate the steaks in the roasting pan — 1 hour minimum, overnight best.
2. Add stock and garlic; cover tightly with foil; roast at **190 °C / 375 °F for about 1 hour** for ¾-inch steaks (the page gives ranges for other thicknesses; bone-in adds ~10 min).
3. Uncover and roast a further **30–60 minutes**, flipping halfway, until the edges are deep golden and the stock has reduced into a sauce.
4. **Pull at 195–200 °F (90–93 °C)** — the source's verbatim target: *"The number to aim for is 195-200°F in the thickest part"*, with the fork test (*"a fork should slide in with almost no resistance"*) and its explicit warning that pulling at **145 °F** — the safe temperature for a pork chop — leaves this cut tough: *"If your steaks are still tough, they aren't overcooked. They're undercooked."* Probe into the thickest part. Final serving temperature: **195–200 °F (90–93 °C) as published by the source**. Slow-cooker variation published: **6–7 hours on low** or 3½–4 hours on high with 1 cup stock.

### 5. Craft Beering — Braised Pork Steak with Onions and Stout (Milena Perrine)
**Inspired by**: https://www.craftbeering.com/braised-pork-steak-and-onions/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Milena Perrine, Craft Beering (US food publisher).
**Serves**: not captured in fetched text · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 1.5–2 h at 300 °F · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Pork blade steaks, just over an inch thick, ~6 oz each (the page recommends thicker cuts for braising)
- Onions (yellow, red or white); olive oil; salt, pepper
- Tomato paste; chipotles in adobo (smoked paprika offered as substitute)
- Stout (other dark ales, lagers or red wine as alternatives); brown sugar; cumin; thyme; pork or beef stock

**Method**
1. Season the steaks liberally with salt and pepper; brown on both sides over medium-high in the skillet or braiser, **in batches**; sauté the onions until translucent.
2. Work the tomato paste and chipotle into the onions; deglaze with the stout, scraping the brown bits; add the rest of the stout, brown sugar, cumin, thyme and stock.
3. Set the seared steaks on top, cover, and braise at **300 °F (150 °C)** — the page's timing rule: *"Generally at 300 F about 1.5 to 2 hours of cooking time is about as long as it takes to transform thicker pork steaks into fork tender perfection"*, testing with a fork after 1.5 hours.
4. Finish **uncovered 10–15 minutes** so dry heat forms a crust on top; skim rendered fat.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is fork-tenderness, and the page states that fat and collagen melting cannot be rushed. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 88 °C (190 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 6. Madens Verden — Langtidsstegt nakkesteg som "bøffer" (Holger Rørby Madsen)
**Inspired by**: https://madensverden.dk/langtidsstegt-nakkesteg-boeffer/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Holger Rørby Madsen, Madens Verden (Danish). Recorded here as the covered-with-stock slow cook it is: browned on all sides, then baked sealed under foil in stock — a braise in format; the page's own title calls it a slow roast. **Leaf-move note**: this source was previously cited in the pre-ToR `pan_fry` leaf with pan-only steps that the page does not contain; it is re-cited here with what the page actually says.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 1 h 45 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 600 g nakkefilet (pork neck fillet), cut into slices **about 6 cm thick**, each halved to yield 4 "bøffer"
- Butter for browning; thyme; salt, fresh-cracked pepper; a few strips balsamic
- 3 dl pork fond or chicken stock

**Method**
1. Cut the neck fillet into ~6 cm slices and halve them into four steak-sized pieces.
2. Brown thoroughly on **all sides** in butter in a **very hot pan** for crust.
3. Season with thyme, salt, pepper and a few strips of balsamic; place in an ovenproof dish and pour in the stock.
4. Cover the dish **tightly with foil** so it seals completely; bake in an oven preheated to **200 °C convection for 1 hour 45 minutes**.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the 1 h 45 covered braise ("super mørt" — super tender). House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 88 °C (190 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| LECKER (Germany) | German breaded oven braise | 1¾ h at 175 °C / 150 °C fan in cream-mushroom sauce | Not stated |

One of six sources publishes a number: **195–200 °F (90–93 °C)** as its target for the
thickest part — the only measured band in this leaf, and it is deliberately far above the
chop-safe 145 °F that the same source explicitly rejects for this cut (*"If your steaks are
still tough, they aren't overcooked. They're undercooked."*). The other five stop at
time-plus-fork-tenderness, so the full observed range for this leaf is **90–93 °C** from a
single source and no lower band is observed at all — notable because the cut's other leaves
live at 63–74 °C. The driver is the method itself: braising's endpoint is collagen
conversion, which completes only well past the safety floor, so time and fork test replace
the probe in five of six traditions. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`well_done`**
— the only measured evidence and the shared "fork tender" language all land there; the
house-guidance 88–93 °C probe lines inside the recipes are labelled as house guidance, not
as sourced bands.

## What makes this method special

Braising is the only method in this cut's set where heat enters through a liquid rather
than a surface, and that changes the physics completely: there is no crust-versus-core race
because the surrounding medium never exceeds 100 °C until the sauce reduces, so the whole
stake climbs together and the only thing that matters is time at temperature for collagen
hydrolysis. The six sources differ mostly in *what surrounds the meat* — cream and
mushrooms, stock and allspice, lemon and honey, stout and chipotle, balsamic and fond —
because the meat itself behaves identically in all of them: it browns first (every source
sears, building Maillard compounds that will later dissolve into the sauce), then surrenders
structure to the moist heat until it can be pulled apart. The Greek source's refusal of
145 °F is the method's thesis in one line: this cut braised to chop temperature is not
underdone safety-wise, it is texturally unfinished. What the method costs is the steak's
identity — by the end it is falling-apart meat in sauce, not a slice you can pick up.

## Summary

Braising cooks a pork shoulder steak in a covered oven surrounded by liquid for one and a
half to two hours, and the six sources agree on the shape of that cook — sear first, cover,
hold at 150–200 °C, finish with a fork test — while differing entirely in the sauce around
it, from German mushroom cream to Polish allspice and Danish balsamic stock. It is the
method that most suits this cut's collagen, and the one that ends the steak's life as a
steak: the goal is meat that pulls apart. Only the Greek source publishes a number,
195–200 °F (90–93 °C), and it argues explicitly against pulling this cut at chop
temperature. Choose braise when tenderness matters more than slicing, and keep the pan
liquid sufficient so the difference between braising and roasting dry is never in doubt.
