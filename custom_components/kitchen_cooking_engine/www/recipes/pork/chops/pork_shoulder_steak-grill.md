# Pork Shoulder Steak × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling is where the neck steak's marbling earns its reputation: the fat that renders out
over the fire bastes the muscle and drips, so flare-ups char the surface without drying the
centre — the opposite failure mode to a lean chop, which turns to leather at the first
flare. The fetched sources span four fire configurations: German direct-heat grilling at
200–220 °C with a single flip (10–16 min total), South African *caveman* braai where the
steaks go straight onto hardwood coals (4–5 min per side, managed by moving them every
minute), American St Louis-style low-and-slow charcoal with a barbecue-sauce finish
(20–25 min per side indirect, then 3–4 min direct), and Asian sources that grill the
steak whole for a short time before slicing it thin for the salad or serving. The German
and American sources both publish core targets; the South African and Asian sources judge
by time and feel. Probe from the side into the geometric centre — the wide, flat steak's
top is browned long before its middle arrives.

## Source recipes

### 1. Die Frau am Grill — Nackensteak grillen (Anja Würfl)
**Inspired by**: https://die-frau-am-grill.de/nackensteak-grillen-rezept/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Anja Würfl, *Die Frau am Grill* (German grilling publication).
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 10 min (+ ≥1 h marinate) · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg pork neck, cut into **6 steaks of about 200 g**
- 6 tbsp sunflower oil; 1 tbsp sweet paprika; 1 tsp hot paprika; 1 tsp garlic powder; 1 tsp onion powder; 1 tsp thyme; 1 tsp oregano; 2 tsp salt; ½ tsp pepper

**Method**
1. Cut the neck into steaks with a sharp knife; mix the oil and spices into a marinade and brush it all over; refrigerate **at least 1 hour** (overnight preferred).
2. Bring the grill to **direct heat at about 200 °C** (the article body gives **200–220 °C**) and lay the steaks on the grate.
3. Grill **5–8 minutes** on the first side, turning 90° once at half time for an even pattern; flip and grill a further **5–8 minutes** — **10–16 minutes** total depending on thickness.
4. **Pull at 80 °C** — recipe card verbatim: *"Optional ein Kerntemperaturthermometer verwenden und die Nackensteaks bei etwa 80 Grad Kerntemperatur (durchgegart) vom Grill nehmen."* The article body publishes the same endpoint as *"etwa 82 Grad"*; both figures appear on the page and are recorded as published, not reconciled. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (80–82 °C core is its stated through-cooked point).

### 2. Meat Ranger — Dirty Pork Neck Steaks & Creamy Mustard Sauce (South African braai)
**Inspired by**: https://meatranger.co.za/dirty-pork-neck-steaks-creamy-mustard-sauce/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Meat Ranger (signed "Kevin"), South African braai publication.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: ~50 min · **Rest**: "a few minutes" in the sauce, duration not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 pack pork neck steaks (product: 'The Flying Pig' pork neck steaks)
- Salt and pepper, dry rub
- **Creamy mustard sauce**: 240 ml chicken stock; 120 ml thick cream; 5 tbsp Dijon mustard; ½ tsp salt; ½ tsp ground pepper; ½ tsp onion powder; ½ tsp garlic powder; dash Worcestershire sauce; parsley
- 2 bags hardwood (Bosveld Hardekool) — the source insists on real hardwood, **not briquettes**

**Method**
1. Get the fire burning early; spread an **even bed of coals**. The source's rule: hardwood only, no briquettes.
2. Meanwhile heat a cast-iron pan over the coals with the chicken stock (3 min), add mustard, cream and Worcestershire, stir **4–5 minutes** until the sauce thickens.
3. Season the steaks only with salt and pepper; **blow the ash off the coals** and lay the steaks **directly onto the coals**, **4–5 minutes per side**, moving them about every minute to distribute heat and manage flare-ups (charred spots are wanted).
4. Move the finished steaks into the sauce pan and rest a few minutes.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is time plus the charred surface. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)** minimum, or 71 °C (160 °F) to match the German/American through-cooked endpoints. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Blackberry Babe — The Best Barbecued Pork Steaks (Michelle Goth)
**Inspired by**: https://blackberrybabe.com/2020/08/04/the-best-barbecued-pork-steaks/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Michelle Goth, Blackberry Babe (US, Missouri/St Louis pork-steak tradition).
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 1 h (+ marinate) · **Cook**: 45 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 3 lb pork shoulder steak, **5–6 steaks** (¾–1 inch thick)
- 24 oz light beer (marinade)
- **Rub**: 3 tbsp brown sugar; 2 tbsp smoked paprika; 1 tbsp onion powder; 1 tbsp garlic powder; 1 tsp chili powder; 1 tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper
- 8 oz sweet barbecue sauce; optional basting liquid 8 oz beer + 8 oz apple juice

**Method**
1. Marinate the steaks in light beer **at least 1 hour**, up to overnight; pat dry and press in the rub.
2. Prepare charcoal: let the coals **mellow 15–20 minutes** so the fire is not scorching.
3. **Preferred (low and slow)**: covered grill over low-medium heat or indirect heat on high, **20–25 minutes per side**, basting occasionally, lid closed so the smoke circles the pork; finish **3–4 minutes per side** over direct heat brushed with barbecue sauce. (Quick option: covered medium-high, 12 minutes per side.)
4. Rest **5 minutes**.
5. **Pull at 145 °F (63 °C)** — the source's verbatim safety line: *"For safety, you need to get the pork steaks to minimum internal temperature of 145 degrees Fahrenheit"*, and it adds: *"pork shoulder starts to pull apart and is extremely tender at 195 degrees Fahrenheit. I believe that somewhere between 145 and 195 degrees Fahrenheit is the right temperature for tender pork steaks, preferably on the higher side of that range."* Probe inserted into the centre. Final serving temperature: **145–195 °F (63–91 °C) as published by the source**, its stated preferred window for tender shoulder steaks.

### 4. Thai-Foodie — Moo Nam Tok (Grilled Thai Pork Salad)
**Inspired by**: https://thai-foodie.com/thaifood/nam-tok-recipe-thai-steak-salad/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Sherri, Thai-Foodie (Thai-cooking teacher; Isan *nam tok* tradition).
**Serves**: not stated (1 lb meat) · **Prep**: 4 h · **Cook**: 2–4 min · **Rest**: ~10 min before slicing

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb **pork scotch steaks, aka pork neck** (page's own cut name), or pork butt sliced into steak-shaped slabs
- Juice of 2–3 limes; 1 tbsp roasted rice powder; 2 tsp–1 tbsp Thai roasted chili powder; 1–1.5 tbsp fish sauce; 1 tsp sugar; ½–1 cup water or broth
- 1 cup green onion, 1 cup cilantro, 2–3 shallots, 1 cup fresh mint; salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Salt and pepper the meat.
2. **Grill the steak to your preferred doneness** (the page's cast-iron alternative: steaming-hot pan, **2–3 minutes per side for medium-well**).
3. Rest **about 10 minutes**, then slice thinly, keeping the pan juices.
4. Return the sliced meat to the pan off-high with water, lime juice, fish sauce, rice powder and chili; reduce to a sauce; finish with shallots, green onion and mint.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the griller's preference. House guidance (not the source's): probe the whole steak before slicing — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 5. Bon Appétit — Gochujang Pork Shoulder Steaks (recipe credits chef Rachel Yang)
**Inspired by**: https://www.bonappetit.com/recipe/gochujang-pork-shoulder-steaks (fetched 2026-10-26). Bon Appétit (US publication) crediting Seattle chef Rachel Yang; Korean barbecue preparation — attributed to its Korean tradition per this branch's convention.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated (+ ≥2 h marinate) · **Cook**: 8–10 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 8 garlic cloves, peeled and crushed; 1 (2-inch) piece ginger, sliced
- ½ cup dry sake; ½ cup gochujang (Korean hot pepper paste); ½ cup mirin
- ¼ cup vegetable oil, plus more for grilling
- 1½ lb skinless boneless pork shoulder (Boston butt), **sliced ¾ inch thick**

**Method**
1. Purée the garlic, ginger, sake, gochujang, mirin and ¼ cup oil; reserve ¼ cup of the marinade chilled; marinate the pork **at least 2 hours**, turning occasionally.
2. Prepare the grill for **medium-high heat**; oil the grate.
3. Grill the steaks, basting with the reserved marinade and turning occasionally (move to a cooler area if flare-ups occur), **8–10 minutes for medium-rare** — the source's published doneness language, quoted as written.
4. Rest **5 minutes**, then thinly slice against the grain.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is time plus its published "medium-rare" label. House guidance (not the source's): pork shoulder cooked to the source's 8–10 minute window typically reads **63 °C (145 °F)** at the centre — probe to confirm. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Die Frau am Grill (Germany) | German direct-heat grill | 5–8 min/side at 200–220 °C, 10–16 min total | **80 °C** (recipe card) / **82 °C** (article body), both "durchgegart" |
| Meat Ranger (South Africa) | South African hardwood *braai*, coals | 4–5 min/side directly on coals, moved every minute | Not stated |
| Blackberry Babe (US) | St Louis-style charcoal, low-slow + sauce finish | 20–25 min/side indirect + 3–4 min direct; rest 5 min | **145 °F (63 °C)** minimum; **195 °F (91 °C)** pull-apart; source prefers **between, "on the higher side"** |
| Thai-Foodie (Thailand) | Isan *nam tok* grill, then slice | Grill to preferred doneness; pan alternative 2–3 min/side | Not stated |
| Bon Appétit (Korean-style) | Korean BBQ gochujang grill | Medium-high, 8–10 min for "medium-rare" | Not stated |

Numerical evidence comes from two of five sources: the German 80–82 °C through-cooked
endpoint and the American 145 °F safety floor with its 195 °F pull-apart ceiling — a
published span of **63–91 °C (145–195 °F)** whose meanings differ (floor, preferred
window, through-cooked point), so the table records them as stated rather than averaging
them. The American source's preferred window explicitly sits "on the higher side" of
145 °F because it wants the connective tissue near 195 °F to soften; the German source
simply declares 80–82 °C "through-cooked". `recommended_doneness` for this method:
**`well_done`** — both measured sources describe fully cooked meat, and the one source
that offers a window brackets it well above medium. The Thai and Korean sources' doneness
language ("preferred doneness", "medium-rare" at 8–10 min) is recorded as published and
not converted into temperatures.

## What makes this method special

Fire gives this cut the two things a pan cannot: rendered fat that escapes instead of
pooling, and smoke that lands on a surface continuously basted from below. The gradient
is the mirror image of the pan's — the air and coals surround the steak, so the edge
leads only slightly and the flat faces cook almost together, which is why the German
source can be done in ten minutes and the American source can afford twenty-five per side
without the outside racing ahead. Each source's structure follows its fuel: direct high
heat for thin steaks, indirect low heat when the steak is thick or sauced (sugar burns
before collagen melts), and the South African *caveman* method removing the grate entirely
— its answer to flare-ups is motion, not distance. The cut's fat makes it the one steak
that forgives all three, which is exactly why every tradition here cooks neck steaks
harder than it would cook loin.

## Summary

Grilling a pork shoulder steak means direct heat for a thin German-style steak (10–16
minutes at 200–220 °C), a slow sauced finish American-style (20–25 minutes per side
indirect), or straight onto hardwood coals South African-style (4–5 minutes per side).
The collar's fat is what lets all three work — it renders into the fire instead of
drying the meat, so the cut takes the hard cooking a loin chop never could. Two sources
publish numbers: 80–82 °C through-cooked in Germany, and an American window from a
145 °F floor to a 195 °F pull-apart point with the author preferring the high side. Use
indirect heat the moment there is sugar in the rub or sauce, and probe sideways into the
centre before serving.
