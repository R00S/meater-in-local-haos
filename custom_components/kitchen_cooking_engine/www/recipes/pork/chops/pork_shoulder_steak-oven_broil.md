# Pork Shoulder Steak × Oven Broil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Broiling is the pan's gradient delivered from above: the element radiates intense heat onto
one face of the steak at a time, so the surface browns and blisters while the centre lags
— the same physics as a grill's direct zone, but with the steak flipped under a fixed
element rather than moved over moving coals. The fetched sources use one configuration:
rack positioned close to the element (6 inches below in one source, highest rack in the
other), steaks turned once at roughly the midpoint of a 4–6 minute-per-side schedule. For
a neck steak this is a workable match — the fat cap on top bastes downward as it renders
under the element — but both sources keep the window short (12–15 minutes total), which
means the centre of a thick collar steak will not be finished when the surface is. Only
one source publishes a probe target; the other judges by crust and thickness. Probe from
the side at the halfway point if the steak is over 2 cm.

Grillning i ugnen är pannans gradient levererad uppifrån: elementet strålar intensiv värme
på ena sidan av steaken åt gången, så ytan bränns och bilden medan mitten hänger efter —
samma fysik som grillns direktzonen, men med steaken vänd under ett fast element i stället
för förlagd över rörlig kol. De hämtade källorna använder en konfiguration: galler placerat
nära elementet (6 tum under i en källa, översta gallet i den andra), steaken vänd en gång
vid ungefär mitten av en 4–6 minuters-per-sida-schema. För en halssteak är det en fungerande
matchning — fettkanten på toppen bastar nedåt när den smälter under elementet — men båda
källorna håller fönstret kort (12–15 minuter totalt), vilket betyder att mitten av en tjock
halssteak inte blir färdig när ytan är det. Endast en källa publicerar sondmål.

## Source recipes

### 1. Just A Pinch — Broiled Pork Blade Steaks With Herb Rub (Mikekey)
**Inspired by**: https://www.justapinch.com/recipes/main-course/pork/broiled-pork-blade-steaks-with-herb-rub.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Mikekey, Just A Pinch (Seattle, WA, US; the page's own culture field reads *"American"*). The page states its own flexibility: *"This is written for the broiler, but could be done on the outdoor grill, or in a fry pan."* Cut note: *pork blade steak* is the shoulder cut, named as such on the page.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 2 h (marinate 1–8 h + 1 h temper) · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: not stated (temper 1 h before cooking is stated)

**Ingredients**
- 2 pork blade steaks (listed as 2 pounds total)
- 1 tsp dried thyme; 1 tsp dried sage; ½ tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper; ⅛ tsp ground allspice; 1 clove garlic, finely minced

**Method**
1. Mix the herbs and spices; rub half into one side of the steaks, turn, rub in the rest.
2. Cover and refrigerate **at least 1 hour, up to 8 hours**; remove **1 hour before cooking** to take the chill off.
3. Preheat the broiler with the **rack 6 inches below the element**; place the steaks on the broiler pan.
4. Cook **about 6 minutes on each side**, depending on thickness and desired doneness.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is time-plus-thickness plus its stated "desired doneness". House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre at first turn — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Chicago Steak Company / Steak University — Broiled Pork Steaks (Djacobson)
**Inspired by**: https://www.mychicagosteak.com/blogs/steak-university/broiled-pork-steaks (fetched 2026-10-26). Djacobson, Steak University (Chicago Steak Company, US). **Cut-form flag**: the page's recipe uses *"4 bone-in pork steaks"* and never names the shoulder or neck — its accompanying article discusses pork steaks generically — so it is recorded as a broiling source for pork steaks with the specific cut form unspecified, not passed off as a verified shoulder-steak recipe.
**Serves**: not stated (4 steaks) · **Prep**: not stated (+ optional 30 min–1 h marinate) · **Cook**: 8–12 min · **Rest**: "a few minutes", duration not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 bone-in pork steaks (cut not specified on the page)
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp dried thyme; 1 tsp dried rosemary; 1 tsp dried sage; ½ tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp salt; ½ tsp black pepper

**Method**
1. Preheat the broiler **5–10 minutes**; set the rack to the **highest position**; line the broiler pan with foil and grease lightly.
2. Pat the steaks dry; season generously on both sides.
3. Place directly under the broiler and cook **4–6 minutes per side** until golden-brown crust forms.
4. **Pull at 145 °F (63 °C)** — the source's verbatim target: *"Broil the pork steaks for about 4-6 minutes per side until they develop a golden-brown crust and reach an internal temperature of 145°F (63°C) for medium doneness"*, with its own warning that time must be adjusted for thickness.
5. Rest a few minutes before serving. Final serving temperature: **145 °F (63 °C) as published by the source**.

## What makes this method special

Broiling concentrates the whole method in one plane: heat arrives from a single direction
at a fixed distance, so the steak's top surface can reach Maillard temperature in minutes
while the underside is still cooking by conduction from above through the meat itself —
which is exactly why both sources flip at the midpoint rather than at the end. For a neck
steak the direction matters more than it would for a lean chop: the collar's fat cap sits
upward and renders under the element, basting the face beneath it, so the surface browns
in its own fat the way it would in a pan — but unlike a pan there is no metal under the
steak to counter-heat it, which is why both sources keep the total time in the 12–15 minute
range and one of them measures while the other does not. What the method buys is the
grill's crust with an oven's consistency; what it costs is the evenness of `oven_roast`,
since the half of the steak facing away from the element is always the last thing done.

## Summary

Broiling cooks a pork shoulder steak under a close element for about 12–15 minutes,
turning once so both faces take turns under the heat, and the collar's upward fat cap
bastes the top while it blisters. The two sources that exist for this cut and method agree
on position and timing; only the Chicago steak-retail page publishes a number, 145 °F,
which it calls medium. It is the shortest documented method in this cut's set, which makes
it the one most dependent on thickness — probe the centre at the first flip if the steak
is thick, because the surface will look finished long before the middle is.
