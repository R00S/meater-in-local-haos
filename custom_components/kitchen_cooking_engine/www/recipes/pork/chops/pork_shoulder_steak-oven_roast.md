# Pork Shoulder Steak × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven roasting surrounds the steak with air instead of a pan surface, and for a neck steak
that means the fat renders slowly into the flesh rather than being driven off against hot
metal — the collar's marbling works as an internal baste for the whole 20–60 minutes. The
fetched sources use four configurations: a plain sheet-pan bake of marinated ¾-inch steaks
(American), a tray roast of steaks buried between roasting vegetables and turned once
(German), a low-temperature bake in tomato and stock (Danish), and a fast tray roast of
1 cm marinated neck slices finished hotter for colour (Polish). Oven temperatures cluster
tightly at 175–200 °C (with one Danish outlier at 125 °C convection); times range from
20–25 minutes to a full hour depending on whether liquid is in the pan. Only one source
publishes numbers, and it publishes a range rather than a point — 145 °F minimum, up to
170 °F accepted. Probe from the side into the centre; the flat steak cooks evenly, so the
centre and the edge arrive close together.

## Source recipes

### 1. Craft Beering — Pork Steaks in Oven (Milena Perrine)
**Inspired by**: https://www.craftbeering.com/oven-baked-pork-steak-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Milena Perrine, Craft Beering (US food publisher); the page's companion articles are titled *"What are Pork Shoulder Steaks"*.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min (+ overnight marinate ideal) · **Cook**: 25 min · **Rest**: tented in foil, duration not stated

**Ingredients**
- Large pork steaks, **¾ inch thick** (boneless or bone-in shoulder/blade)
- **Marinade**: apple cider vinegar; olive or vegetable oil; Dijon mustard; dark brown sugar; shallot; thyme; salt and pepper
- Seasoning before baking: coarse salt and pepper plus cumin, caraway, paprika or garlic powder

**Method**
1. Marinate (overnight preferred, minimum a couple of hours); bring to room temperature; scrape the marinade off and pat dry; season.
2. Heat the oven to **350 °F (175 °C)**; arrange the steaks on a large baking sheet or dish.
3. Bake **about 20 minutes**, then flip and bake a little longer to brown the other side, **or** switch to the broiler for a few minutes, watching closely.
4. Tent with foil to rest. Total time **20–30 minutes for ¾–1 inch steaks**.
5. **Pull at 145 °F (63 °C)** — the source's verbatim line: *"Pork steaks are technically food safe at 145 F internal temperature measured with a meat thermometer in the center. However, because of the higher fat content of pork shoulder baking them a little bit longer… is very desirable. You can absolutely go to 170 F or even a bit higher without the meat drying out."* Probe into the centre. Final serving temperature: **145–170 °F (63–77 °C) as published by the source**.

### 2. eat.de — Nackensteak aus dem Ofen
**Inspired by**: https://eat.de/rezept/nackensteak-aus-dem-ofen/ (fetched 2026-10-26). eat.de (German recipe platform); developed and cooked by Christopher, written by Anna.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 18 min (+ 20 min marinate) · **Cook**: 35 min · **Rest**: 22 min (2 min stated after baking; 20 min covered marinate before)

**Ingredients**
- 2 pork neck steaks
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 1 garlic clove; zest of ½ organic lemon; 1 tsp raw cane sugar; salt, pepper
- 1 red onion; ½ fennel; 400 g potatoes (Drillinge); rosemary sprig; 2 thyme sprigs

**Method**
1. Lay the steaks in a roasting dish; half the oil, salt, pepper, pressed garlic, lemon zest and raw sugar; massage in and leave covered **about 20 minutes**.
2. Toss the cut onion, fennel and potatoes with the rest of the oil, salt and pepper; spread on a lined baking sheet and roast at **200 °C fan for 15 minutes**.
3. Pull the tray out, set the steaks among the vegetables, scatter rosemary and thyme, and roast a further **20 minutes**, turning the steaks once after **10 minutes**.
4. Rest **2 minutes** and serve with the vegetables.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the timed roast plus the 2-minute rest. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)** minimum, or 71–77 °C (160–170 °F) in the American source's generous range. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Madens Verden — Ovnstegte koteletter med tomater (Holger Rørby Madsen)
**Inspired by**: https://madensverden.dk/ovnstegte-koteletter/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Holger Rørby Madsen, Madens Verden (Danish). The page's own rationale names the cut: thick **nakkekoteletter** (neck chops) are used *because* their fat marbling keeps them from drying in the oven.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: ~1 h · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 thick nakkekoteletter (neck chops)
- Oil for browning
- 1 can chopped tomatoes; 1 dl fond or stock; 1 tsp dried oregano; salt, fresh-cracked pepper

**Method**
1. Pat the chops dry; oil a small roasting dish lightly.
2. Heat a pan **very hot** with a little oil and brown the chops quickly on both sides — **at most 30 seconds per side**, in batches if needed, just for crust.
3. Season with salt and pepper; arrange the chops in the dish so they overlap; pour the chopped tomatoes over and the stock alongside; sprinkle with oregano.
4. Bake in an oven preheated to **125 °C convection for about 1 hour**; serve with the tomatoes and pan juices.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is the one-hour low bake (the page explicitly credits the marbling for the result). House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 71 °C (160 °F)** at this long, low time. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. Smaki.pl — Karkówka pieczona w plastrach (Renata Materlińska)
**Inspired by**: https://smaki.pl/przepisy/obiady/10995054/karkowka-pieczona-w-plastrach-pomysl-na-szybki-obiad-z-piekarnika.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Renata Materlińska, Smaki.pl (Polish). *Karkówka* is the Polish pork neck/collar — the page's own instruction is to cut the neck into slices rather than roast it whole.
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 10 min (+ 2 h marinate, ~30 min tempering) · **Cook**: 25 min · **Rest**: ~30 min at room temperature before baking

**Ingredients**
- 6 slices of pork neck (*karkówka*), **about 1 cm thick**
- 2 tbsp oil; 1 tbsp mustard; 1 tbsp soy sauce; 4 garlic cloves; 1 tsp sweet paprika; ½ tsp hot paprika; 1 tsp marjoram; salt, pepper
- 1 onion; rosemary

**Method**
1. Cut the neck into **~1 cm slices** (the page notes they may be lightly flattened but need not be); mix the marinade and coat; refrigerate **about 2 hours**.
2. Remove, arrange in a **single layer** on a parchment-lined baking tray, top with garlic slices, onion and rosemary; leave ~30 minutes to take the chill off.
3. Bake at **180 °C for about 20–25 minutes**; the page suggests raising the oven to **200 °C near the end** so the meat colours.
4. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is time plus the final colour boost. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre of a slice — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Craft Beering (US) | American sheet-pan bake | 20 min + flip/broil at 350 °F; total 20–30 min for ¾–1 in | **145 °F (63 °C)** minimum; **up to 170 °F (77 °C)** stated fine |
| eat.de (Germany) | German tray roast with vegetables | 15 + 20 min at 200 °C fan, turn once; rest 2 min | Not stated |
| Madens Verden (Denmark) | Danish low bake in tomato and stock | 30 s/side brown, then ~1 h at 125 °C convection | Not stated |
| Smaki.pl (Poland) | Polish tray roast of neck slices | 20–25 min at 180 °C (200 °C to finish), single layer | Not stated |

The observed numerical range for this leaf is **63–77 °C (145–170 °F)** from a single
source (the American one); the German, Danish and Polish sources publish no temperature at
all, so three of four endpoints are time-plus-colour judgements. Notable: the one measured
source treats the range as *open upward* — higher is explicitly welcome because shoulder
fat protects against drying — which is the opposite of how the same publisher treats a lean
chop. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`well_done`** — the cut-level default is
supported by the only measured band's upper acceptance (170 °F/77 °C lands in
`PORK_WELL_DONE`) and by no source in this leaf arguing for a lower endpoint.

## What makes this method special

An oven removes the pan's one-sided gradient and replaces it with a slow, enveloping one:
every face of the steak receives the same air temperature, so the meat cooks edge-to-centre
at nearly the same rate and the fat renders evenly rather than being squeezed out against
hot metal. For this cut that is the whole argument — the collar's marbling, which would
spit and flare in a pan, melts quietly inside the meat for as long as the oven runs, which
is why the Danish source can hold 125 °C for an hour without drying and the Polish source
can blast 1 cm slices for 25 minutes without a probe. The difference among the four
sources is liquid, not heat: where tomatoes and stock are present (DK) the bake becomes a
gentle braise and time stretches to an hour; where the tray is dry (DE, PL, US) the window
closes to 20–35 minutes and the endpoint becomes colour. What the method gives up is the
crust that defines its siblings — the American source has to reach for the broiler to get
one back.

## Summary

Oven roasting cooks a pork shoulder steak in surround heat — 175–200 °C for 20–35 minutes
on a dry tray, or 125 °C for an hour when tomatoes and stock are in the dish — and it suits
this cut because the collar's fat bastes inward instead of flaring outward. Doneness is
judged by time and colour in three of the four sources; the American source publishes the
only numbers, a 145 °F floor with 170 °F explicitly welcome. The method's gain is evenness
and hands-off timing, its loss is crust, which is why one source finishes under the broiler.
Probe sideways into the centre if you want a specific doneness rather than a specific
dinner hour.
