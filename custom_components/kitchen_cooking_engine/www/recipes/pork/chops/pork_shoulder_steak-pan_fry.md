# Pork Shoulder Steak × Pan Fry — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Pan frying is the fastest way to render the collar's fat seams: a hot skillet (medium-high,
pan surface well above 180 °C) hits the irregular, marbled face of the neck steak, and the
first two to four minutes per side do what this cut wants most — crust on the outside while
the intramuscular fat below it melts into the flesh. The fetched sources use one configuration
with local variations: a fast single-flip sear finished at lower heat (German), a batched
cast-iron sear of marinated 1-inch collars (American), a high-heat glaze finish where the
honey-mustard is added off-peak heat (Norwegian), and a staged brown-then-reduce pan cook
with a Korean-style gochujang-pear marinade. All land in a 4+4 minute window per side. The
cut's fat content is what makes the method safe for it: unlike a loin chop, a neck steak
absorbs a missed minute without drying, so the pan cook tolerates the higher endpoints
(65 °C and up) that these sources actually publish. Probe from the side, horizontally into
the geometric centre — the steak is wide and its top surface is often browned long before
the middle is.

## Styckesprofil

Pannstekning smälter bogskivans fettssömmar snabbast: en varm stekpanna (medel-hög värme,
panyta klart över 180 °C) träffar halssteakens ojämna, marmorerade yta och de första två
till fyra minuterna per sida gör det stycket behöver mest — skorpa utanpå medan det
intramuskulära fettet smälter in i köttet. De hämtade källorna använder en konfiguration
med lokala variationer: en snabb enkelvändning avslutad på lägre värme (tysk), stegvis
stekning av marinerade 1-tumskålar i gjutjärn (amerikansk), högvärms-glasyr där honung-
senfspasset läggs på senare (norsk) och en koreansk variant med gochujang-päronmarinad.
Alla landar i ett 4+4 minutersfönster per sida. Sticketets fett gör metoden säker för
detta snitt: till skillnad från en loin-kotlett tål bogskivan en försvunnen minut utan
att torka, varför pannstekning tål de högre slutvärdena (65 °C och uppåt) som källorna
faktiskt publicerar. Sond insatt horisontellt från sidan in i steakens mitt. Efter-
tillagning 3–5 minuter täckt.

## Source recipes

### 1. eat.de — Nackensteak perfekt braten
**Inspired by**: https://eat.de/rezept/nackensteak-perfekt-braten/ (fetched 2026-10-26). eat.de (German recipe platform); developed and cooked by Michael, written by Nadine.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: temper 30 min from fridge (no cooked rest stated)

**Ingredients**
- 2 Schweinenackensteaks (pork neck steaks)
- 1 tsp sunflower oil
- Salt, black pepper

**Method**
1. Take the steaks out of the fridge and temper **about 30 minutes**.
2. Salt both sides lightly — the source notes this builds a better crust.
3. Heat the pan, add the oil and heat briefly; lay in the salted steaks and sear **about 2 minutes** until golden.
4. Turn, halve the heat, and cook the second side a further **4 minutes** toward the desired doneness.
5. **Pull at 65 °C** — the source's verbatim line: *"Bei einer Kerntemperatur von ca. 65 °C sind die Steaks durchgebracht, aber noch saftig."* (at about 65 °C core the steaks are cooked through but still juicy). Optional butter baste before serving. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (65 °C core is its stated doneness point).

### 2. Craft Beering — Marinated Pork Neck Chops (Pan Seared)
**Inspired by**: https://www.craftbeering.com/pork-neck-chops-pan-seared/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Milena Perrine, Craft Beering (US food publisher); the page explicitly equates pork neck chops with pork collar chops / pork neck steaks.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min (+ 4 h–overnight marinate) · **Cook**: 10 min · **Rest**: loosely tented in foil, duration not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 pork neck chops, about **1 inch thick**
- 2 tbsp cooking oil; salt and pepper
- **Marinade**: 3 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil; 1 tsp pepper; 3 garlic cloves halved and smashed; 2 tsp cumin; 1 sprig rosemary; 3–4 sprigs thyme; ¼ cup malty beer (dark German or Mexican lager; white wine or vinegar+sugar offered as substitutes)

**Method**
1. Mix the marinade; marinate the chops in a sealed container **4 hours to overnight**, then bring to room temperature.
2. Heat a heavy-bottomed pan or cast iron over **medium-high** for 2–3 minutes; scrape the marinade off, season with salt and pepper, add 1 tbsp oil.
3. Working in batches of two, sear **3–4 minutes per side**.
4. **Pull at 145 °F (63 °C)** — the recipe's verbatim instruction: *"Cook until the internal temperature is at least 145 F and the juices run clear"*, with its own tip that *"about 160 F is absolutely fine — they will not be dry"* and that searing on **to 165 F** is "very much OK" for this marbled cut. Probe inserted into the centre of the thickest chop. Final serving temperature: **145–165 °F (63–74 °C) as published by the source** (145 °F minimum, ~160 °F stated fine, 165 °F ceiling acceptable).

### 3. The Unmanly Chef — Korean Style Pork Shoulder Steaks
**Inspired by**: https://theunmanlychef.com/2017/01/10/korean-style-pork-shoulder-steaks/ (fetched 2026-10-26). The Unmanly Chef (US recipe site) — Korean-style preparation, attributed to its Korean tradition per this branch's convention.
**Serves**: not stated (1 lb of steaks) · **Prep**: not stated (+ 30 min–overnight marinate) · **Cook**: not stated (browning in batches) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 lb pork shoulder steaks
- 1 tbsp gochujang red pepper paste
- 1 grated Asian pear
- 1 tsp fresh grated ginger; 1 tsp garlic powder; 1 tsp onion powder
- 2 tbsp rice wine; 1–2 tbsp peanut oil

**Method**
1. Mix everything except peanut oil and rice wine; marinate the steaks **at least 30 minutes**, overnight ideal.
2. Heat the peanut oil in a large non-stick pan; add the steaks **in batches** so the pan is not crowded.
3. **Brown on each side**, reducing the heat if the sauce smokes (the pear's sugar is the risk).
4. Once cooked through, remove and deglaze the pan with the rice wine, reduce to a sauce and pour over.
5. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is "cooked through" plus brown. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)** for safe-and-juicy, or follow the pan's own 65–70 °C tolerance. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 4. GODT.no — Saftige nakkekoteletter med honning, sennep og nektarinsalat
**Inspired by**: https://www.godt.no/oppskrifter/kjoett/svin/8947/nakkekoteletter (fetched 2026-10-26). Lise Finckenhagen, GODT (Norwegian food publication).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not split out · **Cook**: 25 min total · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 thick nakkekoteletter (neck chops/steaks)
- 4 garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 tbsp coarse Dijon mustard; 2 tbsp liquid honey
- 3 nectarines, 150 g rocket, 2 balls fresh mozzarella, 1 lemon, parsley
- Oil and butter for frying; salt and black pepper

**Method**
1. Season the chops with salt and pepper; crush the garlic with the knife blade.
2. Use a large frying pan and fry over **high heat, about 4 minutes on each side**, depending on thickness; let the garlic fry along and add a knob of butter toward the end.
3. Stir the mustard and honey together; **turn the heat down** on the chops, pour over the mixture and let it simmer a few minutes over low heat.
4. The source publishes no internal temperature; doneness is time-plus-glaze. It adds that the chops can equally be fried over high heat or grilled in a grill pan. House guidance (not the source's): probe the centre — **Pull at 63 °C (145 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## What makes this method special

Pan frying gives this cut a steep, one-sided gradient: the face on the metal is far ahead of
the centre, so the method is a race between crust and core that the collar's fat makes
unusually forgiving — the same marbling that would keep a loin chop from browning works here
as an internal baste once it renders. The sources' shared physics shows in their sequencing:
every one of them manages heat *downward* after the first contact (halving the flame, cutting
the heat for the glaze, batching so the pan does not crash), which is the opposite of a grill's
management and is what keeps the sugar-bearing surfaces — German butter, Norwegian honey,
Korean pear — from carbonising before the middle reaches temperature. What the method buys
for a neck steak is speed with crust; what it costs is evenness, which is why every source
here either probes or times to a specific window rather than cooking by colour alone.

## Summary

Pan frying cooks a pork shoulder steak in about four minutes per side in a hot skillet, and
the collar's fat is what makes that work: the sources all pull their heat down after the
first sear so the sugars brown rather than burn, and the interior lands at 65 °C or above
where the fat has rendered and the meat is still juicy. Two of the four sources publish a
number — 65 °C in Germany, a 145 °F floor with 160–165 °F accepted in the US — the other
two judge by time and colour. Choose the pan when you want this cut in fifteen minutes with
a crust, and probe sideways from the edge because the steak's centre is the last thing to
arrive.
