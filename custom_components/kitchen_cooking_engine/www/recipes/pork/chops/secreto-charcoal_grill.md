# Secreto × Charcoal Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Charcoal is where the *secreto* earns its reputation as "the Wagyu of pork": the coarse seam
fat renders onto live coals and comes back as smoke, and the cut's thin geometry means the
whole cook is measured in flips rather than minutes on one side. The three fetched sources
share one rule and disagree on everything else. The rule is heat management — the coals must
be established and flameless before the meat goes on (whitish ash, grate raised well above the
fire), because dripping seam fat causes flare-ups that char the crust before the interior is
done. The disagreement is endpoint and cadence: the US charcoal source works at about 450 °F
and flips **every minute**, four to five minutes total, resting five minutes to a medium
finish; the Spanish butcher blog gives its Iberian pork a general "about 10 minutes per side"
over whitish coals at grate height and salts **after** cooking; the Dutch open-fire braai
source sears briefly over direct embers, finishes over indirect heat "to the desired doneness",
rests, and shaves hard cheese over the sliced meat. Only one of the three publishes an
internal temperature, and it is the kamado source: **65 °C (149 °F)** at the core, checked
with an instant-read thermometer.

## Styckesprofil

Kol är där *secreto* förtjänar sitt rykte som "grisens wagyu": det grova fettet renderas på
levande kol och kommer tillbaka som rök, och snittets tunna geometri gör att hela tillagningen
mäts i vändningar snarare än minuter på en sida. De tre hämtade källorna delar en regel och
är oeniga om allt annat. Regeln är värme­hanteringen — kolen måste vara etablerade och lågfria
innan köttet läggs på (vitaktig aska, galler högt över elden), eftersom droppande fett orsakar
flare-ups som bränner skorpan innan insidan är klar. Oenigheten gäller slutpunkt och takt: den
amerikanska kolkällan jobbar vid cirka 450 °F och vänder **varje minut**, fyra till fem minuter
totalt, fem minuters vila till medium; den spanska slaktarbloggen ger sitt iberiska gris "cirka
10 minuter per sida" över vitaktiga kol på gallerhöjd och saltar **efter**; den nederländska
öppen-eld-källan bränner kort över direkt glöd, blir klar över indirekt värme "till önskad
grad", vilar och rivar hårdost över det skivade köttet. Bara en av de tre anger innertemperatur,
och det är kamado-källan: **65 °C** i kärnan, kontrollerad med snabbläsande termometer.

## Source recipes

### 1. Fogo Charcoal — Secreto Iberico, the best piece of pork you will ever try (Sebastian Bussert, United States)
**Inspired by**: https://www.fogocharcoal.com/blogs/cook/secreto-iberico-the-best-piece-of-pork-you-will-ever-try (fetched 2026-10-26). Fogo Charcoal (US charcoal brand's recipe blog), recipe by Sebastian Bussert. US live-fire tradition.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 4–5 min · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- 4 pieces *secreto ibérico*, about 225–280 g each
- Salt
- Black pepper
- Olive oil

**Method**
1. Light the grill for **direct heat** and get it hot — around **450 °F (230 °C)**.
2. Season the secreto liberally with salt and pepper and rub with olive oil.
3. Place the meat over the direct heat and **flip it every minute**, keeping the cook to **4–5 minutes in total** — the point is a dark, crisp crust without drying the interior.
4. Take the meat off the coals and **rest 5 minutes** before slicing.
5. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; the endpoint is crust-and-flip cadence (the source's comparison note calls the finished cut a medium steak). House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest piece — **Pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Big Green Egg — Riso con chutney di peperoni e secreto iberico alla griglia (Italy)
**Inspired by**: https://www.biggreenegg.eu/it/inspiration/ricette/riso-chutney-peperoni-secreto-iberico-griglia (fetched 2026-10-26). Big Green Egg (kamado manufacturer's official recipe library), Italian-language. Italian kamado live-fire tradition.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min (65 min incl. rice) · **Cook**: ~30 min · **Rest**: not stated (a reheat step follows the rest)

**Ingredients**
- **Rub**: 1 tsp Madras curry powder; 1 tsp smoked paprika; 1 tsp onion powder; ½ tsp garlic powder; ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- **Secreto**: 400 g *secreto ibérico*
- **Rice**: 1 fresh chorizo sausage (about 100 g); 1 onion; 2 garlic cloves; 2 tbsp olive oil; 150 g bomba rice; 100 ml tomato juice; 200 ml chicken broth
- **To serve**: pepper chutney; a handful of rocket; flaky sea salt

**Method**
1. Light the charcoal in the Big Green Egg and heat it to **200 °C** with the cast-iron grid in place.
2. Mix all the rub ingredients and coat the *secreto ibérico* with it (the source suggests an overnight rub for a better result).
3. Lay the secreto and the chorizo on the grill. Cook the meat **about 3 minutes**; turn the secreto and chorizo and cook **2–3 minutes more, until the secreto reaches an internal temperature of 65 °C** — check with an instant-read thermometer.
4. Set the grilled secreto and chorizo aside. Swap the cast-iron grid for the stainless grid, hang the Dutch oven over it and preheat it with the lid closed. Dice the chorizo into 1 cm cubes; peel and finely chop the onion and garlic.
5. Sauté the olive oil, chorizo, onion and garlic in the Dutch oven for **about 3 minutes**; add the bomba rice and fry **3 minutes more**, stirring regularly and closing the lid after each action.
6. Deglaze with the tomato juice and chicken broth. Fit the convEGGtor, return the grid and the Dutch oven, close the lid and bring the kamado to **170 °C**; simmer **about 20 minutes** until the rice is cooked, adding a little water or broth if needed.
7. Remove the Dutch oven. Pepper the rested secreto, return it to the grill and reheat **about 3 minutes**. Slice it, salt with flaky sea salt, and serve with the rice, the pepper chutney and the rocket.
8. **Pull at 65 °C (149 °F)** (stated — the source gives 65 °C as the internal target at removal and states no carryover allowance). Final serving temperature: **65 °C / 149 °F** (stated).

### 3. Braaimaster — Blumenkohl mit Sanddornbeeren | Secreto Ibérico | Kartoffeln in Salzkruste (Netherlands; South African braai technique)
**Inspired by**: https://braaimaster.nl/de/rezepte/secreto-iberico-met-bloemkool/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Braaimaster (Dutch open-fire barbecue brand's official recipe library, German-language page). **Tradition note**: the apparatus and technique are South African *braai* (food hung and cooked over an open wood fire), but the plate itself is modern European (cauliflower, sea buckthorn, salt-crust potatoes, pecorino) — recorded as a Dutch open-fire recipe using braai technique, not passed off as a South African dish.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 60 min total · **Rest**: brief (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- **Cauliflower**: 150 g unsalted butter; 50 g sea buckthorn berry powder; 1 cauliflower
- **Secreto ibérico**: 1 *secreto ibérico*; 50 g celery salt; 100 g paprika powder; Pecorino, aged Manchego or another hard cheese of choice
- **Salt-crust potatoes**: 4 small potatoes per person; 200 g salt; 200 g flour; water; optional thyme, rosemary, shallot or onion, garlic
- **Equipment**: grill oven, meat hook, instant-read thermometer, fire gloves

**Method**
1. Melt the butter slowly and stir in the sea buckthorn berry powder until an aromatic butter forms. Brush the cauliflower generously with it, hang the cauliflower on a meat hook in the Braaimaster and let it cook slowly, turning regularly and alternating between direct heat and indirect embers so it does not burn.
2. Mix the celery salt and paprika powder into a spice blend. Rub the secreto **sparingly** with it — the spices should complement the meat, not cover it.
3. **Grill the secreto briefly over direct heat** — the fat melts and drips onto the coals, creating intense smoky aroma — then move the meat to an indirect-heat area and let it finish there **until the desired doneness is reached**.
4. Let the meat rest briefly; garnish it with finely grated Pecorino (or aged Manchego).
5. Potatoes: mix the salt and flour, add water gradually until a firm, non-sticky dough forms. Roll the dough into 0.5 cm sheets; wrap a portion of washed small potatoes with the chosen herbs in a completely sealed parcel. Grill the parcels at about **250 °C for 45–60 minutes**; the potatoes are done at an internal temperature of at least **90 °C**. Break the crust open before serving.
6. Slice the secreto thinly and serve with the potatoes and the whole cauliflower.
7. The source publishes **no internal temperature for the meat** — its endpoint is "the desired doneness" (the only number on the page is the potatoes' 90 °C). House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest part — **Pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fogo Charcoal (United States) | US direct-heat charcoal | 450 °F, flip every minute, 4–5 min total, 5 min rest | Not stated |
| Big Green Egg (Italy) | Italian kamado, direct + indirect | 200 °C direct, 3 min + 2–3 min; then reheat 3 min | **65 °C (149 °F)** internal (stated) |
| Braaimaster (Netherlands, braai technique) | Dutch open-fire, direct then indirect | brief direct sear, finish indirect "to desired doneness" | Not stated (90 °C is the potatoes') |

One of three sources publishes a number for the meat, and it is the kamado source's **65 °C
(149 °F)** core target — a medium-well reading that sits between the medium school in the
grill leaf (63 °C) and the Spanish well-done convention (70–72 °C). The other two publish
nothing: the US source's endpoint is a crust cadence (a flip a minute for four to five
minutes) and the braai source says only "to the desired doneness". No numerical range can
honestly be derived beyond the single stated 65 °C; the 60 °C house-guidance pulls in sources
1 and 3 are what would carry the meat to medium, and they are marked as house guidance. The
divergence that is observable without numbers is the cadence: the US source never lets the
meat sit on one side longer than sixty seconds, while the Spanish technique reference
preserved in the pan_sear leaf's corpus gives Iberian pork a general ten minutes per side
over coals — a completely different philosophy of the same fire. **Tie-break**: a single
measured source cannot be tied; **Applied target**: the stated 65 °C stands as this method's
target by default of being the only published value. **Temperature used in cooking_data.py:
65 °C (between `PORK_MEDIUM` and `PORK_WELL_DONE`, recorded as the kamado source's stated
core target)**. `recommended_doneness` for this method: **`medium`** — the single measured
value and both verbal sources' steak-style treatment cluster at medium-to-medium-well; the

## What makes this method special

Charcoal adds the one thing plancha and gas cannot: the *secreto*'s own fat returns as smoke.
The seams of this cut are coarse and abundant, so on live coals the dripping fat is a flavour
ingredient and a hazard at the same time — which is why all three fetched sources spend more
words on fire management than on the meat itself. The US source's counterintuitive move is the
flip-every-minute cadence: at 450 °F over direct coals, sixty-second flips build crust
repeatedly while keeping the thin interior far from the coals' peak intensity, avoiding the
one-side-charred, centre-dry result a single long sear produces on a 1–2 cm sheet. The kamado
source instead uses the dome: 200 °C direct for a few minutes, an internal target of 65 °C,
then — unusually — the meat rests while the rice cooks and goes back on the grill for three
minutes of reheat before slicing, a two-stage sequence that treats the cut like a component of
a composed dish. The braai source makes the fat's smoke explicit ("the fat melts and drips
onto the coals, creating intense smoky aroma"), searing briefly over direct embers and moving
to indirect heat to finish — the fire-side version of the Belgian gas source's indirect finish
in the grill leaf. The shared lesson across all three: with this cut, the fire is managed for
the fat, and the meat is judged by crust and rest.

## Summary

Charcoal cooking a *secreto* is fire management first and meat cookery second: establish
flameless coals, render the seam fat deliberately, and keep the thin sheet moving or away
from the hottest zone. The three fetched sources show three cadences — a flip every minute at
450 °F, a 200 °C kamado cook to a stated 65 °C core, and a brief direct sear finished over
indirect embers on an open fire — and only the kamado one publishes a temperature. The
method's signature is smoke from the cut's own fat, which is why the crust, not a probe
reading, is what the charcoal sources actually judge. Slice thin against the grain after a
short rest, and salt late if you salt at all during the cook.
