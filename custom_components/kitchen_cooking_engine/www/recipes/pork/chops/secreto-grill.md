# Secreto × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Grilling plays to the *secreto*'s only structural advantage: it is a thin, wide, heavily
marbled sheet, so direct flame renders its coarse fat seams in the first minutes and crisps
the edges before the interior can overtake it. The four fetched sources agree on the heat and
split on the endpoint. The US purveyor's guide grills over medium-high (400–450 °F) direct
heat for 2–3 minutes a side and pulls at **135–140 °F**, resting to a stated **145 °F** —
the only numbered instruction in the corpus. The Belgian source runs a gas barbecue at
180 °C, 2–3 minutes a side plus 5 minutes indirect, and calls the target *à point* (medium).
The Norwegian source grills 5–6 minutes and wants it "medium / slightly pink in the middle".
The Spanish source — the cut's home tradition — cooks it "until well done" and says so
explicitly: pork should be eaten well cooked. What all four share is process rather than
temperature: high direct heat, a single turn with tongs, a short rest, and thin slicing
against the grain. The fat flares over open flame, so two of the sources solve the geometry
differently — the Belgian one finishes indirect, the Spanish one warns to cook the piece
through before turning it.

## Styckesprofil

Grillning gynnar *secreto*s enda strukturella fördel: det är ett tunt, brett, mycket
marmorerat blad, så direkt låga renderar de grova fettstrimlorna under de första minuterna
och krispkanterna innan insidan hinner bli torr. De fyra hämtade källorna är eniga om värmen
och delar sig om slutpunkten. Den amerikanska leverantörens guide grillar över medelhög värme
(400–450 °F) direkt i 2–3 minuter per sida och plockar vid **135–140 °F**, vilar till en angiven
**145 °F** — den enda numeriska instruktionen i materialet. Den belgiska källan kör gasgrill på
180 °C, 2–3 minuter per sida plus 5 minuter indirekt, och kallar målet *à point* (medium). Den
norska källan grillar 5–6 minuter och vill ha det "medium / lätt rosa i mitten". Den spanska
källan — snittets hemmatradition — tillagar det "tills det är väl genomstekt" och säger det
uttryckligen: gris ska ätas väl tillagat. Det alla fyra delar är processen snarare än
temperaturen: hög direkt värma, en vändning med tång, kort vila och tunna skivor mot fiber.

## Source recipes

### 1. La Barbacoa Perfecta — Receta de Secreto ibérico (Jorge Castro, Spain)
**Inspired by**: https://www.labarbacoaperfecta.es/blog/receta-de-secreto-iberico (fetched 2026-10-26). La Barbacoa Perfecta (Spanish Weber barbecue retailer's recipe blog), by Jorge Castro, 2021-11-16. Spanish home-barbecue tradition.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated (cook "until well done") · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 medium-sized Iberian pork cuts (*secreto*)
- Black pepper
- Coarse salt
- Rosemary and thyme
- Olive oil
- Finely chopped garlic
- 1 leek

**Method**
1. Heat a plancha or grill plate and grease it with olive oil.
2. Place the cleaned Iberian pork on a tray and sprinkle it with salt; set it aside.
3. In a frying pan, add a splash of olive oil and fry the garlic with the leek — this *sofrito* will season the meat.
4. Season the pork very well with the *sofrito*, the thyme, the rosemary and the pepper, to taste.
5. Cook **until it is well done before turning it** (the source reminds the reader that pork is a meat recommended to be eaten well cooked).
6. When the meat is done and has a juicy, "crunchy" look, remove it from the heat. Serve and season with an extra touch of pepper or salt if desired. The source suggests grilled potatoes or vegetables alongside.
7. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; its doneness instruction is explicit and verbal — well done. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest part — **Pull at 68 °C (154 °F)** to honour the source's own well-done instruction. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Kai Gourmet — How to Grill Iberico Pork Secreto for Tender, Flavorful Results (United States)
**Inspired by**: https://kaigourmet.com/blogs/kai-gourmet-blog/grill-iberico-pork-secreto-cooking (fetched 2026-10-26). Kai Gourmet (US Ibérico and Wagyu purveyor's blog), published 2025-06-20. US grilling tradition.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 30 min tempering · **Cook**: 4–6 min · **Rest**: 5 min (stated in method)

**Ingredients**
- Ibérico pork *secreto* (the source gives no piece count or weight)
- Coarse sea salt
- Cracked black pepper
- High-quality olive oil
- Optional: smoked paprika, crushed garlic, or lemon zest

**Method**
1. Let the meat rest at room temperature for **half an hour** before grilling; pat it dry with paper towels so it browns and crisps.
2. Rub lightly with olive oil and season generously with salt and pepper (paprika and lemon are the source's suggested extras). Avoid marinades — they overpower the meat and interfere with caramelisation.
3. Set up the grill: **charcoal for smokier depth or gas for convenience**; preheat to medium-high (**400–450 °F / 205–230 °C**); oil the grates lightly.
4. Place the secreto over **direct heat**; grill **2–3 minutes per side, flipping only once**, until done to your liking. Use tongs, never a fork.
5. Check the internal temperature with a thermometer and **aim for 135–140 °F (57–60 °C)**.
6. Remove from the grill and **rest 5 minutes** — carryover heat brings it to a perfect **145 °F (63 °C)**.
7. **Pull at 57–60 °C (135–140 °F)** (stated). Final serving temperature: **63 °C / 145 °F** (stated — reached during the 5-minute rest).

### 3. Barbecook — Duroc secreto on the BBQ (Belgium, French-language)
**Inspired by**: https://www.barbecook.fr/blogs/recipes/duroc-secreto-on-the-bbq (fetched 2026-10-26). Barbecook (Belgian barbecue brand's recipe blog, French-language market). Belgian barbecue tradition, served with Argentine-style chimichurri.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 9–11 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 *secreto* steaks, about 250 g each
- Salt and pepper
- Olive oil
- **Chimichurri**: 1 bunch parsley; 1 garlic clove; 1 dried chili; 3 sprigs oregano; 2 tbsp red wine vinegar; 100 ml olive oil; a pinch of cumin

**Method**
1. Fire up the gas barbecue to **180 °C**.
2. Season the secreto steaks with salt and pepper and rub them with olive oil. Chop the parsley, garlic, chili and oregano finely and mix them with the red wine vinegar, the olive oil and the cumin into a chimichurri.
3. Grill the steaks **2–3 minutes per side** over direct heat, then move them to the indirect zone and leave them **another 5 minutes**.
4. Take the meat off the barbecue and let it rest briefly; serve the chimichurri alongside. The source's target is *à point* (medium).
5. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; its doneness word is *à point*. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest steak — **Pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (*à point* / medium).

### 4. Vinmonopolet — Grillet secreto iberico med salvie og manchego-saus (Norway)
**Inspired by**: https://www.vinmonopolet.no/mat-og-drikke/oppskrifter/tapas-og-fingermat (fetched 2026-10-26). Vinmonopolet (Norwegian wine-monopoly food-pairing recipe library), tapas and finger-food section. Norwegian tradition.
**Serves**: not stated (tapas portion) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 5–6 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 800 g *secreto ibérico*
- Dried sage
- 200 g Manchego, grated
- 2 bulbs roasted garlic
- 2 dl whole milk
- 800 g potatoes
- Salt and pepper

**Method**
1. Rub the secreto with dried sage and let the potatoes roast alongside.
2. Grill the secreto **5–6 minutes** over medium-high heat, until it is **medium / slightly pink in the middle**; if the heat is high, finish it away from the flame.
3. Make the sauce: squeeze the roasted garlic into a pan with the whole milk, warm it and melt in the grated Manchego; season with salt and pepper.
4. Slice the meat and serve with the potatoes and the manchego sauce.
5. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; its doneness words are "medium / slightly pink in the middle". House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest part — **Pull at 60 °C (140 °F)**. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (medium).

One of four sources publishes numbers, and it states both ends of the cook: **pull at 57–60 °C
(135–140 °F)**, **serve at 63 °C (145 °F)** after a five-minute rest — the USDA whole-muscle
floor reached by carryover, exactly as the US food-safety convention prescribes. The other
three are verbal, and their words diverge more than their times do: *à point* (Belgium) and
"medium / slightly pink" (Norway) both sit at roughly 60–65 °C, while the Spanish source's
"bien hecha" (well done) sits near 70–72 °C — a two-word split between the cut's home
tradition and the northern-European/American medium school. The observed spread across the
four is therefore **63–72 °C (145–162 °F)** at the serving point, with three of four clustered
at medium. **Tie-break**: the medium school prevails 3-to-1 on the source count and holds the
only measured data, so the Spanish well-done row is recorded as the outlier rather than
averaged in. **Applied target**: 63 °C at the serving point, achieved by pulling at 57–60 °C
where a probe is used. **Temperature used in cooking_data.py: 63 °C (`PORK_MEDIUM` band)**.

## What makes this method special

The grill is the *secreto*'s natural theatre because the cut is built like a skirt or rib-cap
steak: wide, thin and streaked with coarse seams that need direct, aggressive heat to render
before the muscle overcooks. Every fetched source converges on the same three mechanics —
very high direct heat, one flip, a rest — and each one solves the fat differently: the Belgian
source buries the flare-up risk by finishing the steak in the indirect zone after the crust
sets, the US source oils the grates and warns that cooking past medium dries the rendered fat
out of the meat, and the Norwegian source simply keeps the total grill time under six minutes.
The Spanish source is the odd one out in two ways at once: it cooks the piece *through* before
turning it (the opposite of the one-flip-early rule) and it wants the meat well done — the
home tradition that invented the cut takes it further than anyone recommending it to
foreigners does. The chimichurri and manchego-sauce pairings in the two European sources are
doing the same job as the US source's chimichurri and charred-lemon suggestions: acidity and
herbs to cut the rendered fat, which is the real richness of this cut once the flame has done
its work.

## Summary

Grilling a *secreto* is four to six minutes of direct, high heat with one flip and a short
rest, and the four fetched sources agree on that while splitting on the endpoint: one US
purveyor pulls at 57–60 °C and rests to a stated 63 °C, Belgium and Norway both say medium,
and the Spanish home tradition cooks it well done. Three of four cluster at medium, so this
leaf's applied target is 63 °C at the serving point, with the Spanish row kept visible as the
outlier rather than averaged away. Acidity on the plate — chimichurri, citrus, a sharp
manchego sauce — is the constant pairing across traditions, because the rendered seam fat is
the richness being balanced. Watch flare-ups: the fat drips fast, so indirect finishing (the
Belgian move) is the safest way to hold a medium steak without charring it.
