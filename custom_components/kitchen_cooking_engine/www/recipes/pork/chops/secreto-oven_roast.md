# Secreto × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

The oven is where the *secreto* stops being a steak and becomes a roast: the three fetched
sources all take thicker pieces than the plancha sheet of the pan_sear leaf — the two Spanish
tray roasts use 1 kg cut into 3–4 pieces (roughly 250–330 g each), and the Japanese source
butterfliesthe cut open and rebuilds it as a tied roulade — and then give it 50–60 minutes
of dry heat at 180–200 °C. That is a completely different endpoint from the two-minute
plancha cook: every source in this corpus lands the meat thoroughly well done, judging by
colour ("golden but not burnt") and by cutting the thickest piece, never by probe. The two
Spanish sources put the meat on a potato-and-onion bed that does the thermal work of a roasting
rack — it lifts the secreto off the tray, catches the rendered seam fat, and keeps the thin
belly edges from burning — and one of them deglazes the tray with beer to keep the roast
braising in its own drippings. The Japanese source instead stuffs the opened sheet with
julienned vegetables and jamón ibérico, ties it, and roasts it upright in geometry, deglazing
the tray with white wine at the end. No source in this leaf publishes an internal temperature.

## Source recipes

### 1. Recetas de ¡Escándalo! — Secreto ibérico de cerdo al horno con patatas asadas y cebolla, ¡de 10! (Spain)
**Inspired by**: https://www.recetasdeescandalo.com/secreto-iberico-de-cerdo-al-horno-con-patatas-asadas-y-cebolla-de-10/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Recetas de ¡Escándalo! (Spanish food blog). Spanish home-oven tradition.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min (majado) · **Cook**: 50–60 min · **Rest**: 5 min (page states 1 h 30 total)

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg *secreto de cerdo ibérico* (3–4 pieces)
- 4–6 medium potatoes; 2 onions
- 200 ml beer; dried oregano; black pepper; olive oil; salt
- **Majado** (mortar paste, quantities approximate per the source): 3 dried garlic cloves; 1 tbsp French mustard; ½ tsp ground black pepper; 1 tsp dried thyme; 1 tsp dried rosemary; 1 tsp salt; 1 tbsp lemon juice; 4 tbsp olive oil

**Method**
1. Take the secreto out of the fridge so it reaches room temperature before it meets the oven.
2. Make the *majado*: pound the garlic, pepper, salt, thyme and rosemary to a paste in a mortar; work in the lemon juice, then the mustard and the oil until creamy but pourable. It should taste strong and salty.
3. Brush the secreto pieces with the majado on both sides. Peel and slice the potatoes neither very thin nor very thick; julienne the onions and spread them over the potatoes (two layers max in a small tray for softer potatoes, a single layer in a large tray for more colour).
4. Preheat the oven to **180 °C, top-and-bottom heat with fan**. Pour the beer in among the potatoes; season the tray with salt, pepper, oregano and a drizzle of olive oil.
5. Lay the secreto pieces on the potatoes and roast on the middle shelf **30 minutes**; take the tray out, turn the pieces, and return it.
6. Roast **20–30 minutes more**, until the meat is golden but not burnt and the potatoes match. Watch that the tray never goes dry — add more beer from the side (so the herbs on top are not washed off).
7. Rest the tray **5 minutes** before serving. The source says the meat is done when it looks golden, and — for a first attempt — to cut into the thickest part to check.
8. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; the endpoint is colour and a cut-check. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest piece — **Pull at 68 °C (154 °F)** to honour the source's own well-done roast style. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 2. Recetas con Sazón (Susanna Brullet) — Secreto ibérico al horno con patatas (Spain)
**Inspired by**: https://recetasconsazon.com/receta/secreto-iberico-al-horno/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Recetas con Sazón (Spanish home-cooking blog), recipe by Susanna Brullet. Spanish home-oven tradition. **Tradition flag**: counted as the second Spanish source in this leaf — a duplication recorded here and deducted at cut level.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 1 h 5 min · **Rest**: a few minutes (page states 1 h 15 total)

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg *secreto ibérico*
- 4 medium white potatoes; 2 onions
- 1 glass of water; 8 garlic cloves, whole and unpeeled
- 1 tsp salt; 1 tsp ground black pepper; olive oil

**Method**
1. Wash and peel the potatoes and onions. Cube the potatoes, julienne the onions, and put everything in an oven tray with a drizzle of olive oil, salt, pepper and the whole unpeeled garlic cloves. Add a glass of cold water and stir.
2. Roast the tray in a preheated **180 °C oven (top-and-bottom heat)** for **30 minutes**.
3. Take the tray out, stir the potatoes, and lay the seasoned secreto on top of the bed. Return to the oven for **20 minutes**.
4. Take the meat out briefly, stir the potatoes again, turn the meat over, and roast **15 minutes more**.
5. If the top is not coloured enough, switch the oven to the **grill function for 5 minutes** to brown it.
6. Open the oven door, rest a few minutes, and serve. The source's own summary: "tender and juicy", crispy outside.
7. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; its FAQ gives the technique endpoints — high oven temperature plus a final grill function "to keep the juices inside" — and its stated reheat method is 150 °C under foil. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest piece — **Pull at 68 °C (154 °F)** to honour the source's own long-roast style. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

### 3. Suehiro-ya (iberikobuta.com) — Ibérico pork secreto stuffed with jamón ibérico, mushrooms and vegetables (Japan)
**Inspired by**: https://iberikobuta.com/hpgen/HPB/entries/41.html (fetched 2026-10-26). Suehiro-ya (スエヒロ家, Osaka Ibérico-pork and Kagoshima-buta specialist), official recipe library, Japanese-language. Japanese tradition — **Culinary Group A** source for this cut. **Form flag**: the secreto is butterflied open and rolled as a stuffed roulade tied with string — a rebuilt geometry rather than a flat roast; recorded as the Japanese presentation of the cut.
**Serves**: 2 (2 pieces, about 150 g each) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 1 h 45 stated total · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 Ibérico pork *secreto* pieces, about 150 g each
- ½ onion; 100 g seasonal mushrooms; 100 g jamón ibérico
- ½ carrot; 1 eggplant; ½ zucchini; 1 potato
- Salt; pepper; 50 ml white wine; olive oil

**Method**
1. Julienne the vegetables and clean and julienne the mushrooms. Heat a little olive oil in a pan and fry in order: onion first, then carrot, zucchini and eggplant, finally the mushrooms.
2. When everything is fried, add the shredded jamón ibérico. Finish the filling *al dente*; season with salt and pepper.
3. Cut the secreto open "like opening a book" (butterfly it flat).
4. Roll the fried vegetables inside the opened meat and tie it with kitchen string. Season with salt and pepper and roast it in a preheated **200 °C oven for 1 hour**.
5. Take the meat out; pour the white wine onto the hot roasting tray and boil it down into a sauce.
6. Julienne the potato and fry it in olive oil until golden-brown; drain.
7. Slice the meat, plate it, drizzle the tray sauce over it in a thread, and crown it with the fried potato.
8. The source publishes **no internal temperature**; the endpoint is the one-hour roast and the slicing. House guidance (not the source's): probe the thickest point of the tied roulade — **Pull at 68 °C (154 °F)**; a tied 150 g roulade held 1 hour at 200 °C is far past well done in any case. Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source**.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recetas de ¡Escándalo! (Spain) | Spanish tray roast, beer-deglazed | 180 °C fan, 30 + 20–30 min, turn at midpoint; "golden but not burnt"; cut-check; 5 min rest | Not stated |
| Recetas con Sazón (Spain) | Spanish tray roast, grill finish | 180 °C, 30 + 20 + 15 min; grill function 5 min to brown | Not stated |
| Suehiro-ya (Japan) | Japanese stuffed roulade roast | 200 °C, 1 hour; wine deglaze | Not stated |

Zero of three sources publishes an internal temperature, so **no numerical range exists in
this leaf** and none is inferred. The observable agreement is total: all three cook 50–60
minutes at 180–200 °C, all three judge by colour, and all three land the meat well done — this
is the one method in the cut's corpus where the whole-muscle `well_done` convention is not in
tension with any source, because no one is aiming for pink. The divergences are structural
rather than thermal: the Spanish pair varies only in what the tray holds (beer among the
potatoes versus a glass of water and a grill-function finish), while the Japanese source
changes the geometry itself — butterflied, stuffed with jamón and vegetables, tied, and
finished with a white-wine deglaze instead of a beer-enriched roasting liquor. The
house-guidance 68 °C pulls in all three rows are what would give this method its probe target;
they are marked as house guidance and are deliberately at the cut's well-done band because
that is what every source's own timing produces. `recommended_doneness` for this method:
**`well_done`** — the measured-evidence silence of the corpus is filled here by unanimous
long-cook timing, which is the strongest well-done support the cut has seen across its leaves.

## What makes this method special

Oven roasting is the *secreto*'s holiday form: the same cut that the plancha treats as a
two-minute steak is given an hour of moving hot air, and the seam fat that flares over charcoal
here renders slowly down into a tray of potatoes, onions and beer that the Spanish sources
clearly consider half the point of the dish. The potato bed is the quiet engineering in both
Spanish sources — it is a roasting rack you can eat, moderating the bottom heat so the thin
irregular edges of the sheet survive an hour at 180 °C, and it absorbs the rendered oleic fat
that makes Ibérico secreto worth roasting at all. The two finishing moves show the same
problem solved twice: Escándalo keeps the tray wet with beer so the meat braises gently in its
own drippings, Recetas con Sazón fires the grill function for five minutes to put back the
crispness that wet roasting costs. The Japanese roulade is the outlier that proves the cut's
range: once butterflied and stuffed, secreto behaves like a fine-dining roast, its fat seams
basting the jamón-and-mushroom filling from inside the tied roll, and the cook's own tray
sauce — white wine boiled down on the roasting sheet — replaces the Spanish potato liquor.

## Summary

Oven roasting a *secreto* means thicker pieces, a potato bed and an hour at 180–200 °C — a
Sunday roast from a cut the Spanish usually eat as a two-minute plancha steak. None of the
three sources probes the meat; all judge it golden and all land it well done, so this is the
one method where the cut's `well_done` default is confirmed rather than contested. Keep the
tray wet (beer, water or a wine deglaze) and finish either with a short grill blast for crust
or with the tray's own sauce. The Japanese stuffed roulade is the cut's most ambitious oven
form: butterflied, filled with jamón and vegetables, tied and roasted for an hour.
