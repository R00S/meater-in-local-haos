# Secreto × Sous Vide — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Sous vide is the *secreto*'s only fully measured method: all three fetched sources publish
their bath temperature and time, and none of them wants the cut well done. The spread is
**55–62 °C (131–144 °F) for 2–24 hours** — the US purveyor's cut-specific setting of 131 °F
for 2 hours, the Dutch blog's deliberate long cook of 57 °C for 24 hours ("the meat will be
fully pasteurized… pink pork is absolutely fine"), and the Italian food-school recipe's 62 °C
for 3 hours. Because these holds are long relative to the cut's 1–2 cm sheet geometry, the
bath setting *is* the core temperature: there is no meaningful gradient left to manage, which
is exactly why two of the three sources then insist on a hard finishing sear — one minute per
side in a screaming pan or on a grill — to put back the crust the bag cannot make. The
geometry of the cut is the hidden argument for the method: a thin, wide, heavily marbled sheet
is the hardest cut to cook evenly in a pan (its two-minute window) and the easiest to cook
evenly in a bath (no window at all), and the seam fat that flares over charcoal renders
gently into the bag instead. One of the three sources explicitly refuses the cut's traditional
endpoint: "we did not include information for well-done pork, as we do not recommend this for
Ibérico."

## Source recipes

### 1. Il Giornale del Cibo — Secreto iberico, cos'è e come si cucina: la ricetta del secreto iberico cotto a bassa temperatura (Gisella Salvati, Italy)
**Inspired by**: https://www.ilgiornaledelcibo.it/secreto-iberico-come-si-cucina/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Il Giornale del Cibo (Italian food media, "Scuola di cucina" column), by Gisella Salvati, published 2023-09-28. Italian home-precision-cooking tradition. **Article flag**: the page is a cut-guide article containing the full recipe ("La ricetta secreto iberico cotto a bassa temperatura") — the recipe below is the article's own, fully recorded; the same article's *brace/griglia* passage (ash-covered embers, "slightly rosa", mustard sauce) is preserved as a technique note in the charcoal_grill leaf's uncounted leads and is not double-counted here.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 3 h bath · **Cooling**: 1 h ice bath

**Ingredients**
- 800 g *secreto iberico*
- Fine salt, to taste

**Method**
1. Sprinkle the secreto on both sides with a pinch of fine salt.
2. Put it in a sous-vide bag and vacuum-seal it with a vacuum machine.
3. Fill a large pot with water and set the immersion circulator (*roner*) to **62 °C for 3 hours of cooking**. Once the water is at temperature, submerge the bag completely. (The source notes that without a circulator the same result can be had in a tall pot of water held at temperature and monitored with a kitchen thermometer.)
4. At the end of the cook, chill the bag: if you have no blast chiller, set it in a bowl of abundant water and ice **for one hour**.
5. Take the meat out of the bag and dry it thoroughly with absorbent paper.
6. Reheat a pan or griddle and cook the meat **a few minutes per side** on both sides.
7. Serve it sliced, with a soy sauce and a side of potatoes.
8. **Bath setting 62 °C (144 °F) for 3 hours** (stated — at this hold the bath is the core temperature; the source states no separate pull). Final serving temperature: **not stated by the source** (62 °C core at bag removal, then seared).

### 2. Campo Grande — The Ultimate Guide to Sous Vide Ibérico Pork (United States)
**Inspired by**: https://eatcampogrande.com/blogs/cooking/the-ultimate-guide-to-sous-vide-iberico-pork (fetched 2026-10-26). Campo Grande (US Ibérico-pork purveyor's blog). US precision-cooking tradition. **Source note**: the same company publishes a Spanish-language page ("Ibéricos al sous vide") giving 144 °F for secreto — recorded as a second page of the same source, not a separate tradition; the cut-specific setting below is the guide's English-language table.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 2 h bath · **Rest**: not needed (seared straight from the bag)

**Ingredients**
- Ibérico pork *secreto* (the guide gives no piece count or weight)
- Salt and pepper ("all you actually need"); optional additions: garlic butter, ginger and soy

**Method**
1. Season the secreto (salt and pepper at minimum) and seal it in a sous-vide bag with all air removed (food-saver or cryovac). If cooking from frozen, seal straight from the freezer and add an hour to the cook time.
2. Prepare the water bath with the immersion circulator and set the bag in the bath (properly sealed, the meat sinks).
3. **Secreto setting: 131 °F (55 °C) for 2 hours.** The guide's general small-cut table: rare 130 °F; medium-rare 130–140 °F; medium 140 °F; medium-well 150 °F — all for 1–4 hours. Explicitly: **no well-done guidance is given "as we do not recommend this for Ibérico."**
4. Remove the pork and pat it dry with paper towels. Finish with high heat — a grill, a screaming-hot pan **1 minute per side**, or a kitchen torch — for crispy edges and Maillard flavours. (The guide notes the sear is technically optional: the meat is fully cooked already.)
5. **Pull at 55 °C (131 °F)** (stated bath setting; at a 2-hour hold the bath is the core temperature). Final serving temperature: **55 °C / 131 °F plus sear** (stated setting; the guide's medium-rare school).

### 3. Stefan's Gourmet — Iberico Secreto Sous-Vide with Celeriac Fondant Sous-Vide (Netherlands)
**Inspired by**: https://stefangourmet.com/2013/10/13/iberico-secreto-sous-vide-with-celeriac-fondant-sous-vide/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Stefan's Gourmet (Dutch food blog), published 2013-10-13. Dutch home-precision-cooking tradition.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 24 h bath · **Rest**: not needed (seared straight from the bag)

**Ingredients**
- About 300 g Ibérico *secreto*
- 1 tbsp minced fresh sage; salt and freshly ground black pepper; extra virgin olive oil
- **To finish**: 60 ml of the wine served with the dish; clarified butter (or olive oil); celery leaves
- **Celeriac fondant** (served alongside): 1 celeriac; 4 celery stalks; olive oil; salt; white pepper; clarified butter (optional); and a pork stock (500 g ground pork, celeriac trimmings, garlic, onion, carrot, celery, fennel fronds)

**Method**
1. Rub the pork with salt, black pepper and olive oil; sprinkle with the sage and vacuum seal. Scald the bag briefly in boiling water to prevent a bad smell during the long cook.
2. **Cook sous-vide 24 hours at 57 °C (135 °F).** The source's own note: the meat will be fully pasteurized — "you may not be used to eating pork that is still pink, but that is absolutely fine as it is cooked for such a long time." (In this cook the bag juices were discarded so the sage stayed on the meat rather than in the sauce.)
3. Make the pork stock (brown the ground pork, deglaze, pressure-cook 2 h or simmer 4 h) and reduce it to about 180 ml of syrupy sauce; cook the celeriac and celery sous-vide **1 hour at 84 °C (183 °F)** and glaze the celeriac in clarified butter. (84 °C is the vegetable setting, not a meat temperature.)
4. Take the secreto out of the pouch and pat it dry. Brown it **very quickly over very high heat**, preferably in clarified butter. Remove the meat; deglaze the pan with the remaining stock and reduce to a syrupy sauce.
5. Slice the meat across the grain; serve with the sauce, the celeriac fondant and the celery stalks.
6. **Bath setting 57 °C (135 °F) for 24 hours** (stated). Final serving temperature: **57 °C / 135 °F plus sear** (stated setting).

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Tradition | Bath temperature | Time | Finish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Il Giornale del Cibo (Italy) | Italian home precision cooking | **62 °C (144 °F)** | 3 h | ice bath 1 h, then a few minutes per side in a hot pan |
| Campo Grande (United States) | US purveyor's precision guide | **131 °F (55 °C)** | 2 h | 1 min/side hard sear (or torch) |
| Stefan's Gourmet (Netherlands) | Dutch home precision cooking | **57 °C (135 °F)** | 24 h | very quick very-hot sear in clarified butter |

All three sources publish numbers, and the corpus converges on a bath band of **55–62 °C
(131–144 °F)** with holds of 2–24 hours — the strongest measured agreement of any method for
this cut. The three settings are not really in conflict: they are three points on one
precision-cooking axis, where the lower temperatures are the *rosa*/medium-rare school (the US
purveyor's 131 °F and the Dutch blog's 135 °F both explicitly want pink meat) and 62 °C is
the Italian home setting that lands nearer medium-medium-well. The time axis carries the
safety argument the temperature axis does not: the 24-hour hold at 57 °C is justified by the
source with pasteurization ("fully pasteurized… pink pork is absolutely fine"), and the US
guide closes the question with a policy statement — no well-done guidance for Ibérico at all.
**Tie-break**: three measured sources, all below or at the cut's `medium` band — none supports
`well_done`. **Applied target**: 55–62 °C bath with a hard finishing sear; the single most
defensible house setting is **57 °C (135 °F) for 2–24 h**, the middle of the band and the only
one carrying an explicit pasteurization justification. **Temperature used in the cut file:
55–62 °C, recorded as sous-vide bath settings (bath = core at these holds)**. `recommended_
doneness` for this method: **`medium`** — the measured band sits at the low edge of the cut's
`medium` doneness band (60–66 °C) and two of three sources name medium-rare; the cut-level
`well_done` is not proposed for change in this leaf alone (see Data applied).

## What makes this method special

The *secreto* is a worst case and a best case at once: a thin, wide, irregular sheet with
coarse fat seams is unforgiving in every high-heat method — the pan_sear leaf's corpus shows
the whole cook decided in a two-minute window, and the charcoal leaf manages flare-ups more
than meat — while in a bath its geometry becomes a gift, since a 1–2 cm sheet equilibrates to
the bath almost immediately and the cook is decided entirely by the setting the bag is held
at. That turns the cut's biggest argument (is it 55 °C rosa or 70 °C well done?) from a
timing skill into a dial setting, which is why this is the one method where all three sources
publish exact numbers: there is nothing left to describe but the temperature. The marbling
does the rest — Ibérico seam fat, rich in oleic acid, is deliberately left to render gently in
the bag rather than onto coals, and both European sources treat the pink result as the point
of buying this animal at all. The finishing sear is the one non-negotiable step shared by all
three: the bag cannot make a crust, and without the one-minute blast the cut's fat seams read
as greasy rather than crisp. The long-cook school (24 h at 57 °C) adds one more thing the
fast methods cannot offer: pasteurization as an explicit, cited safety argument for pink pork,
replacing the well-done convention with time-at-temperature instead of temperature alone.

## Summary

Sous vide is the *secreto*'s precision method: three sources, three published settings, one
band — 55–62 °C for 2–24 hours, followed in every case by a hard one-minute finishing sear.
The bath removes the cut's two-minute timing problem entirely, renders the Ibérico seam fat
gently, and (at 24 hours) makes a pasteurization case for pink pork that the well-done
convention never offered. The practical middle is 57 °C for 2–24 h with a dry, very hot final
sear; chill briefly first if a deep crust is wanted. This is also the leaf where the cut's
measured evidence now says rosa-to-medium three ways, which is why the whole-cut `well_done`
question is queued for one deliberate pass rather than changed piecemeal.
