# Tjocka Revben × Braise — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Braising is the method that turns the cut's biggest liability — a thick slab of belly meat bound to cracked rib bones by collagen and fat — into its biggest asset: gelatin. All four fetched traditions cook the ribs submerged or half-submerged in a flavour-carrying liquid until the meat yields to a fork, and three of the four then reduce the braising liquid into the sauce that finishes the dish, so nothing is thrown away. The cut suits the pot because the collagen-to-gelatin conversion both tenderises the meat and enriches the liquid, and because the rib bones release flavour into the sauce the whole time. The variation between traditions is mostly liquid choice and cut size: Chinese cooks cross-cut the rack into riblets and braise them in a wok for minutes-to-half-an-hour (bite-tender, sauce reduced to a glaze), while Italian, Mexican and Polish cooks keep larger pieces in a covered pot for one-and-a-half to three hours (fork-tender, saucy). Probe placement into the thickest rib meat tells little in a wet cook — the fork and the clock are the instruments every source actually uses. Carryover in a hot braising liquid is real but irrelevant: the meat is held in the liquid, not rested on a board.

## Styckesprofil

Bräsering förvandlar styckets svaghet — tjockt, kollagenrikt magkött med knäckta revben — till dess styrka: gelatin. Samtliga fyra hämtade traditioner lagar ribbenen halvt nedsänkta i smakbärande vätska tills köttet ger vek för gaffeln, och tre av fyra kokar in spadet till sås. Stycket passar grytan eftersom kollagenet både mörar köttet och tjocknar såsen, och eftersom benen avger smak hela tiden. Skillnaden mellan traditionerna är vätskan och styckets storlek: kinesiskt går spjället i bitar och bräseras i wok på minuter (med tuggmotstånd, såsen reducerad till glasyr), medan italienska, mexikanska och polska håller större bitar täckt i gryta i1,5–3 timmar (veka, såsrika). Sondtemperaturen säger lite i ett blötlag; gaffel och klocka är instrumenten.

## Source recipes

### 1. Chinese-American (The Woks of Life) — 1-2-3-4-5 Ribs
**Inspired by**: The Woks of Life (Leung family, US, Chinese tradition); https://thewoksoflife.com/1-2-3-4-5-ribs/ (fetched 2026-10-31). *Named for the 1-2-3-4-5 ratio of the sauce: Shaoxing wine, soy, black vinegar, sugar, water. Rated 4.78/5, 59 ratings.*
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 35–40 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1½ lb (680 g) pork ribs — "the meatier the better" — **cut into 1½- to 2-inch pieces** by the butcher
- 2 tablespoons oil, 4 slices ginger
- 1 tablespoon Shaoxing wine, 2 tablespoons light soy sauce, 3 tablespoons Chinese black vinegar, 4 tablespoons sugar, 5 tablespoons water

**Method**
1. Have the butcher cross-cut the rack into 1½–2 inch riblets; rinse and pat dry.
2. Preheat a wok until it smokes lightly; add the oil and ginger slices; over medium heat fry the ginger 1 minute. Add the ribs; turn heat to high and brown both sides, a few minutes.
3. Lower the heat; add the Shaoxing wine, light soy, black vinegar, sugar and water; stir well; bring to a boil.
4. Once boiling, cover and reduce to medium-low; **simmer 25 minutes** (20 if the ribs are less meaty), checking halfway that the liquid has not dried; reduce heat or add a little water if needed.
5. Remove the lid; with heat on high, continuously stir and toss the ribs to reduce the remaining liquid to about **¼ cup** so the ribs glisten. The source gives no probe step: this is a stovetop wok braise finished by feel — insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest riblet only if in doubt, and expect the 25-minute covered simmer plus reduction to be the real endpoint.
6. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its endpoint is the covered simmer plus sauce reduction with the meat still holding together, and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs.
7. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve immediately while the glaze is glossy.

### 2. Italian (Un Cuoco in Camicia, Giallozafferano blog) — Costine brasate al vino rosso
**Inspired by**: "un cuoco in camicia", blog.giallozafferano.it; https://blog.giallozafferano.it/uncuocoincamicia/costine-brasate-al-vino-rosso/ (fetched 2026-10-31). *Pork ribs braised in red wine with rosemary, served with homemade mashed potato. Rated 4.8/5, 67 ratings.*
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 30 min (incl. 10 min parboil) · **Cook**: 1 h 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 6 costine di maiale (pork ribs)
- 1 carota (carrot),1 cipolla rossa (red onion)
- red wine — the source page lists "300 cl vino rosso", evidently a typo for 300 ml; the method's coverage level (a braise, not a bath) confirms the smaller volume
- sale, pepe, rosmarino (rosemary), olio extravergine d'oliva
- For the purè: 400 g potatoes, 100 ml milk, salt, nutmeg, grated cheese, pepper

**Method**
1. "Sweat" the ribs about **10 minutes in boiling water**; drain and cut away the softened fat with a knife (the source's stated reason: costine are rich in fat).
2. In a large pot heat two tablespoons of olive oil; add the carrot pieces and red onion and fry a few minutes. Add the ribs and brown evenly over medium-high heat, turning frequently; add the rosemary sprig, a pinch of salt and grated pepper.
3. When well browned, add the red wine and let it cook until the alcohol aroma has gone; lower the heat to minimum and **cover with the lid**.
4. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest rib, away from bone. Braise about **1½ hours**, making sure the liquid does not dry out; add boiling water if needed.
5. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its stated endpoint is texture: *"la carne deve staccarsi dall'osso con la semplice sollecitazione della forchetta"* (the meat must leave the bone at the mere prompting of the fork), and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve over or beside the homemade mashed potato.

### 3. Mexican (La Piña en la Cocina, Rosie Torres) — Pork Ribs With Cactus in Salsa Verde
**Inspired by**: Rosie Torres, La Piña en la Cocina; https://pinaenlacocina.com/pork-ribs-with-cactus-in-salsa-verde/ (fetched 2026-10-31). *Uses "3 pounds pork spare ribs" — a bone-in cut identity match; braised in freshly blended tomatillo salsa verde with nopalitos. Rated 4/5.*
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 25 min · **Cook**: 1 h 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 lb pork **spare ribs**, cut into smaller sections
- Salt, pepper, granulated garlic to taste
- 3 tbsp avocado oil or pork lard
- 1 large white onion (half sliced, half left whole), 6 garlic cloves (3 whole, 3 minced)
- 12 large tomatillos, 3–4 jalapeños (stems removed), 3–4 serranos (stems removed)
- A handful of fresh cilantro (⅓ cup), 1 tsp cumin seeds, 1 tsp Mexican oregano, 3 bay leaves
- 3 cups cooked nopalitos (cactus paddles)

**Method**
1. Slice the ribs into smaller sections; season lightly with salt, pepper and granulated garlic; bring to room temperature.
2. Boil the tomatillos, jalapeños, serranos, half the onion and 3 whole garlic cloves just until the tomatillos turn from bright green to opaque olive; cover and remove from heat.
3. In a large heavy pot heat the oil or lard; **sear and brown** the seasoned pork in batches.
4. Transfer the boiled tomatillos, chiles, onion and garlic to a blender with 4 cups of the cooking water, the cilantro, salt and pepper; blend smooth.
5. To the browned pork add the remaining onion and garlic; sauté a few minutes; stir in the cumin seeds, oregano and bay leaves; pour in the blended salsa and scrape the fond from the bottom of the pot.
6. Bring to a boil, reduce to a simmer, **cover ¾ of the way and cook 35–45 minutes** until the pork is very tender. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest piece, away from bone, once simmering.
7. Fold in the cooked nopalitos; cover and continue **15 minutes**.
8. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its endpoint is "pork very tender" after the covered simmer, and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs. The source notes the same braise can be done in a pressure cooker in one-third of the time.
9. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve family-style with the cactus in the salsa.

### 4. Polish (CookINPolish) — Spare Ribs and Sauerkraut
**Inspired by**: CookINPolish; https://cookinpolish.com/spare-ribs-and-sauerkraut/ (fetched 2026-10-31). *Ribs browned, then baked covered in a Dutch oven between layers of sauerkraut with mushrooms and caraway; the sauerkraut braises in the rib juices.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 2 h 30 min covered + 30 min uncovered · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg spare ribs, cut into square pieces of 2 bones each
- 1 kg sauerkraut, 3 medium onions, a handful of dried forest mushrooms
- 1 tsp marjoram (majeranek), ½ tsp savory (cząber), 1 tsp ground caraway (kminek), ½ tsp ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp flour, oil or butter for browning
- Mushroom soaking water, reserved

**Method**
1. Cover the dried mushrooms with a cup of boiling water and let stand. Preheat the oven to **180 °C**.
2. Cut the spare ribs into 2-bone squares; salt and toss in flour; fry shortly in small amounts of oil or butter until golden; set aside.
3. Roughly cut and sauté the onions in the same pan; set aside. Cut the sauerkraut and fry it in the pan with the caraway, savory, marjoram and pepper; combine with the onions and the chopped mushrooms; keep the mushroom water.
4. In a Dutch oven place half the sauerkraut mixture on the bottom, lay the ribs on top, cover with the remaining sauerkraut and pour the mushroom water over the dish.
5. **Cover and bake 2½ hours**; then uncover and bake a further **half hour** so the sauerkraut gets a crisp top. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest rib, away from bone, before the lid goes on.
6. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its stated endpoint is texture: *"Spare ribs are tender and soft, they can be easily cut with a fork"* after the covered bake, and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs.
7. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve with fresh bread or hulled barley.

## What makes this method special

In a braise the cut and the sauce cook each other: collagen from the ribs and marrow-rich liquid from the cracked bones thicken the pot, while the liquid's flavour — wine, tomatillo, sauerkraut brine, black vinegar — works into the meat that direct heat could never reach through the thick belly layer. Compared with the oven and grill leaves for this same cut, braising is the only method where the fat that renders out is not lost but becomes part of the dish, and the only method where a tough end-cut and a tender end-cut converge to the same fork-tenderness. The physics is a long stay in the 70–90 °C zone with water present: proteins stay juicier than in dry heat, and the connective tissue fully converts without any surface to dry out. The cost is the crust — no bark, no lacquer — which is why three of the four traditions finish by reducing the braising liquid into a glaze or crisping something on top.

## Summary

Braising turns tjocka revben's collagen and fat into gelatin and sauce: riblets glazed in a wok for half an hour, ribs braised fork-tender in red wine, tomatillo salsa or sauerkraut for up to three hours. It suits the cut because the thick belly layer that fights dry heat surrenders completely to wet heat, and the rendered fat enriches the liquid instead of dripping away. The cook gains forgiveness and a built-in sauce; the sacrifice is any crust or smoke, which three of the four traditions compensate for by reducing the braising liquid. No fetched braise source publishes a doneness temperature — the traditions all read tenderness with a fork.
