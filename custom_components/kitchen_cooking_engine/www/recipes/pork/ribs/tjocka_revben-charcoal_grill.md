# Tjocka Revben × Charcoal Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Charcoal grilling gives these ribs two things at once: a dry, radiant heat that sets the surface, and smoke from the fuel itself, which the thick meat cover absorbs more readily than the thin retail rack does. All three sources set the charcoal to one side and cook the meat indirectly, which is the practical answer to this cut's uneven thickness — a thick front rack over direct coals dries at the thin end before the thick end is done. This method with this cut is documented in three traditions: German *Dicke Rippe* on briquettes (the only source that names this cut and states a core temperature), the South African *braai*, and Korean charcoal *돼지갈비*. Fuel is what separates this leaf from the `grill` leaf: those sources mostly name a gas or unspecified outdoor grill, while everything here names charcoal, briquettes or firewood. Carryover is small at these endpoints and is not measured by any source.

## Styckesprofil

Kolgrillning ger de här revbenen två saker samtidigt: en torr, strålande värme som sätter ytan, och röken från bränslet självt, som det tjocka köttlagret absorberar snabbare än det tynna detaljhandelsspjället. Alla tre källor lägger kolen åt ett håll och tillagar indirekt — det praktiska svaret på styckets ojämna tjocklek, eftersom ett tjockt främre spjäll över direkt glöd torkar i den tunna änden innan den tjocka är klar. Metoden med det här stycket är dokumenterad i tre traditioner: tysk *Dicke Rippe* på briketter (den enda källan som namnger stycket och anger kärntemperatur), den sydafrikanska *braai:n* och koreansk koldgrillad *돼지갈비*. Bränslet är det som skiljer det här bladet från `grill`-bladet: de källorna anger oftast gasol eller ospecificerad utegrill, medan allt här namnger kol, briketter eller ved. Eftervärmen är liten vid dessa ändpunkter och mäts inte.

## Source recipes

### 1. German (BBQ-County.de, Tobias) — Dicke Rippe gegrillt
**Inspired by**: Tobias, BBQ-County.de; https://www.bbq-county.de/rezepte/dicke-rippe-gegrillt/ (fetched 2026-11-01). ***The cut term is Dicke Rippe vom Schwein** — the German named counterpart for this cut — and this is the only fetched recipe anywhere in this leaf that both names the cut and prints a core temperature. Indirect, on charcoal briquettes with smoking wood.*
**Serves**: 1 portion (1 *Dicke Rippe*) · **Prep**: 8–10 h marinating · **Cook**: 50–70 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 Stück Dicke Rippe vom Schwein — 1 piece thick rib of pork
- 50 ml Senf — 50 g mustard
- 50 g Traeger Rub — 50 g commercial rub
- 50 ml Traeger Que BBQ Sauce — 50 ml barbecue sauce
- **Gewürzmischung zum selbst Mischen** (house spice mix): 3 tbsp *Pimenton de la Vera* (smoked paprika), 1 tbsp fine rock salt, 1 tbsp brown sugar, 2 tsp pepper (mixed), 1 tsp garlic granulate, 2 tsp onion granulate, 1 tsp oregano; chilli to taste
- **BBQ Sauce zum selbst Mischen**: 100 ml ketchup, 2 tbsp house spice mix, 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce, 1 tsp liquid smoke (or more to taste), 2 tbsp bourbon (optional)

**Method**
1. Spread the meat thinly with mustard, dust generously with the spice mix, wrap tightly in cling film (or vacuum-seal) and refrigerate **8–10 hours**, preferably overnight.
2. Set the grill up for **indirect cooking**: charcoal briquettes to one side, smoking wood on the coals, lid closed, chamber at **140–160 °C** (this is the *Gartemperatur*, the ambient chamber figure).
3. Place the ribs beside the coals and grill with the lid closed, turning **once at about half the cooking time** (50–70 minutes total).
4. When the meat reaches **70 °C** core, brush with the BBQ sauce; repeat at **75 °C** for a caramelised glaze. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest meat, away from bone, from the start.
5. Take the *Dicke Rippe* off the grill once the target core temperature is reached.
6. **Pull at 80–85 °C** (176–185 °F) — stated by the source as its *Kerntemperatur* line, with the glaze checkpoints at 70 and 75 °C as separate stated numbers.
7. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — served with BBQ beans, mac & cheese or Kentucky-style coleslaw.

### 2. South African (KarooBraai™) — Braaied Pork Spare Ribs
**Inspired by**: KarooBraai™ (named South African braai recipe site); https://karoobraai.com/pork-recipes/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *Cut term is generic "pork spareribs"; the page carries several recipes under one URL — the rib recipe is the "Braaied Pork Spare Ribs" block. Note: the 70 °C figure on this page belongs to a different recipe on the same page (pork loin chops) and is deliberately **not** applied to the ribs.*
**Serves**: not stated (1.5 kg ribs) · **Prep**: 2 h marinating · **Cook**: 25–30 min over coals · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1,5 kg pork spareribs, washed and trimmed
- 125 ml soy sauce
- 25 ml honey
- 15 ml sherry
- 1 clove garlic, crushed

**Method**
1. Lay the ribs in a shallow baking dish.
2. Combine the marinade ingredients and pour over the ribs (scale up the quantities if you need more).
3. Marinate **2 hours**, turning once.
4. Drain, reserving the marinade.
5. **Braai over moderate coals for about 25–30 minutes**, turning and basting often with the reserved marinade. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest piece, away from bone, when the ribs go on.
6. At about 80% done the source optionally cuts the ribs and tosses them in the remaining marinade, then returns them to the grid to finish, so the sauce coats the cut faces.
7. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** The page prints no internal temperature for the ribs; "moderate coals" is the only heat description, and the 70 °C on the page belongs to the loin-chop recipe.
8. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source**.

### 3. Korean (Naver blog, 시골댁 안주인) — 캠핑 바베큐 숯불 양념 돼지갈비구이
**Inspired by**: 시골댁 안주인 (named Naver blogger); https://m.blog.naver.com/leehumdinger/224311503872 (fetched 2026-11-01). *Written in Korean; a home cook's camping cookout account with photographs. Cut term is 돼지갈비 for grilling (구이용), bought pre-cut — a generic rib term, not a front/back specification. No times or temperatures are printed; the source works entirely by fire behaviour, which is itself the data point for this method.*
**Serves**: not stated (600 g ribs, two racks) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated (over coals, turned when smoke rises) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 돼지갈비 600 g (구이용) — 600 g pork ribs, cut for grilling, described as fatty with large bones
- 시판용 매운 양념장 — commercial spicy marinade, used for a second grilling after the ribs are cooked
- (charcoal: *번개탄* charcoal briquettes plus *장작* firewood, lit in advance)

**Method**
1. Rinse the ribs under running water; the source notes the grilling-cut pieces already carry scoring marks.
2. Build the charcoal in advance from briquettes and firewood, and set the grate over it.
3. Put the ribs on and, **when the smoke rises, turn them front and back repeatedly** until cooked; cut into eating-sized pieces with scissors as you go, which the source says speeds the cooking.
4. The source's stated rule on seasoning: *돼지갈비가 익지 않은 상태에서 양념을 묻혀서 구우면 고기가 익기전에 겉면만 탈 수 있어서 완젼 익힌 후 양념을 묻혀서 한번만 더 구우면 딱 좋아요* — if the marinade goes on before the meat is cooked the outside burns before the inside is done, so grill it through first, then marinate and grill once more.
5. It also notes the bones need longer than the meat.
6. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest piece, away from bone, once the ribs are on — an instruction added for this leaf, not something the source states.
7. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** The source gives no time, no chamber temperature and no core temperature; doneness is judged by look and by the cut pieces cooking through.
8. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — eaten with rice, lettuce wraps and, for the author, a beer.
