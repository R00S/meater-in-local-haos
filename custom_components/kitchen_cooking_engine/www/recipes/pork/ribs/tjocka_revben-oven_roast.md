# Tjocka Revben × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven roasting is the domestic default for tjocka revben in its home tradition: Svenskt Kött's cut page prescribes the whole slab on a rack in a roasting pan, bone side up, and its own recipe turns that into a two-stage roast (hot start, then long gentle baking, then a final crisping of separated ribs). The cut's thick, uneven meat layer and cracked bones suit the oven well — a closed roasting pan holds the moisture the fat and collagen need, while the final uncovered phase drives off surface water and concentrates the glaze. Because the slab varies from thin sternum end to thick belly end, the oven's steady ambient heat cooks the rack more evenly than direct fire can. Probe placement: between two rib bones into the thickest muscle of the belly-side meat layer, never touching bone or the cracked bone ends. Carryover is small at these endpoints (the meat is already fully rendered) — sources treat ribs as ready when the collagen has converted, not when a number is hit; the one fetched source that states an internal temperature gives 80 °C.

## Styckesprofil

Ugnsbakning är hemmetoden för tjocka revben i hemlandet: hela spjället på galler i långpanna, bensidan upp. Recepten arbetar i steg — en het inledning, sedan lång låg tillagning, avslutningsvis spritsning och glasering på delade ribben. Det ojämna, feta köttlagret gynnas av ugnens jämnma värme: sluten form håller fukten som behövs för att kollagenet ska smälta, sista steget utan lock koncentrerar ytan. Sond mellan två ben in i tjockaste köttmassan, borta från de knäckta benändarna. Innertemperatur enligt den enda hämtade källa som anger ett värde: **80 °C**; övriga källor bedömer mörhet på känsla och tid.

## Source recipes

### 1. Swedish (Svenskt Kött) — Tjocka revbensspjäll i ugn
**Inspired by**: Svenskt Kött ( Swedish meat industry association ); https://svensktkott.se/recept/grillade-revbensspjll/ (fetched 2026-10-31). *The national meat board's own recipe for this exact cut; oven with a homemade grill sauce; states an internal temperature of 80 °C. Rated 4/5, 22 ratings.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: ca 1 h 15 min · **Rest**: none stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg revbensspjäll (tjocka revbensspjäll)
- Salt and white pepper, to rub
- **Grillsås**: 500 g krossade tomater (crushed tomatoes), 1 finhackad gul lök (yellow onion), rapsolja (rapeseed oil) for frying, 1 msk koncentrerad kalvfond (veal stock concentrate), 2 msk tomatpuré (tomato paste), 2 tsk tamarisoja (tamarind soy sauce), 2 tsk dijonsenap (Dijon mustard), 1 tsk malen svartpeppar (black pepper), 2 tsk torkad oregano (dried oregano)
- To serve: baked potato, grilled corn

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to **225 °C**. Rub the meat with salt and white pepper. Lay the slab in a roasting dish **meaty side down**.
2. Make the grill sauce: fry the onion in rapeseed oil in a saucepan; add tomatoes and stock concentrate; stir in tomato paste, soy, mustard, pepper and oregano. Bring to a boil, lower heat, simmer uncovered about 10 minutes.
3. Brush the meat with sauce, pour a little water into the roasting pan, insert the MEATER probe from the side between two rib bones into the thickest part of the meat layer, away from the cracked bone ends, and put the slab in the oven.
4. Bake about **20 minutes**, then turn the slab and brush the meaty side with sauce. Bake a further about **45 minutes**.
5. Remove the slab to a board and cut it into individual ribs between the bones; return the ribs standing on their sides in the dish and fry about **7 minutes per side**, adding a little water if the dish runs dry.
6. **Pull at 80 °C** (176 °F) — the source's stated internal temperature: *"De ska ha en innertemperatur på 80°, då är de klara."*
7. Final serving temperature: **80 °C / 176 °F** (done; serve immediately with the remaining sauce, baked potato and grilled corn).

### 2. German (ChefKoch.de, "Bio-Bine") — Spareribs aus dem Backofen
**Inspired by**: ChefKoch.de user "Bio-Bine"; https://www.chefkoch.de/rezepte/3114391464375683/Spareribs-aus-dem-Backofen.html (fetched 2026-10-31). *Italo-Western BBQ marinade; foil-sealed at 150 °C, then grilled at 250 °C. Rated 4.62/5, 65 ratings.*
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 30 min · **Cook**: 2 h 5 min · **Rest**: 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg Spareribs (fresh, from the butcher), cut into 2–4 bone portions
- **Marinade**: 3 EL Tomatenketchup, 1 EL Waldhonig (forest honey), 1 EL Barbecuesauce (smoky, e.g. Heinz), 1.5 TL Sojasauce, 1.5 EL heller Balsamico, 1 EL naturtrüber Apfelsaft (cloudy apple juice), 1 small onion (diced), 1.5 TL scharfer Senf (sharp mustard), 1.5 TL brauner Zucker, 0.5 TL Knoblauchgranulat, 0.5 TL edelsüßes Paprikapulver, 1 TL Salz, 1 TL bunter Pfeffer, 1 TL each rosemary, thyme, oregano, basilikum (chopped/rebbed), chilli flakes to taste

**Method**
1. Wash the ribs, pat dry, cut into 2–4 bone portions; dice the onion.
2. Stir all marinade ingredients in a saucepan, bring to the boil and simmer about 10 minutes until slightly creamy. Cool quickly in a water bath.
3. Heat the oven to **150 °C** top/bottom heat. Line a baking sheet with foil; brush the rib portions all over with marinade; lay **bone side down** on the foil and cover tightly with a second layer of foil, sealing the edges.
4. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest portion, away from bone. Bake on the middle rack about **1.5 hours** at 150 °C.
5. Remove the top foil; move the sheet to the top rack and grill at **250 °C** for **10–15 minutes** until the marinade turns dark and crusty on the meat.
6. Return the sheet to the middle rack and let the ribs rest about **10 minutes** under the grill function at ca 100 °C.
7. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its endpoints are the dark glazed surface and the foil phase's 1.5 h at 150 °C, and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs, used here as the probe instruction.
8. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve hot with salads, baguette or roast potatoes.

### 3. Australian (RecipeTin Eats, Nagi Maehashi) — Oven Pork Ribs with Barbecue Sauce
**Inspired by**: Nagi Maehashi, RecipeTin Eats (AU); https://www.recipetineats.com/oven-baked-barbecue-pork-ribs/ (fetched 2026-10-31). *Cider-braised under foil at 160 °C, crust at 180 °C, then repeated sauce glazing. Rated 4.98/5, 290 ratings.*
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 2 h 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2–2.5 kg (4–5 lb) pork ribs (2 racks)
- **Rub**: 2.5 tsp paprika, 2 tsp garlic powder, 1.5 tsp onion powder, 1 tsp cayenne pepper, 1.5 tsp dried thyme, 1.5 tsp dried oregano, 1.5 tsp salt, 0.5 tsp black pepper
- ¾ cup sweet apple cider (hard cider) or apple juice
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- **Barbecue sauce**: ½ cup apple cider vinegar, 1½ cups tomato ketchup, ½ cup water, 1½ tbsp molasses, ⅓ cup brown sugar, 2 tsp mustard powder, 1½ tsp garlic powder, 2 tsp Worcestershire sauce, 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp Tabasco or cayenne (optional)

**Method**
1. Rub the ribs on both sides (most on the meaty side); rest 20 minutes or overnight.
2. Preheat the oven to **160 °C**. Lay the racks on a tray in a single layer; pour the cider underneath the ribs (not over the rub); cover tightly with foil.
3. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest part of the rack, away from bone. Bake **1 hour 30 minutes** until the meat is pretty tender.
4. Remove; increase oven to **180 °C**; remove foil; drizzle with olive oil; return **15 minutes** until the rub forms a crust.
5. Transfer to a foil- and paper-lined tray, pour any tray juices over, flip so the bonier side is up, slather with barbecue sauce and bake **10 minutes**.
6. Turn meaty side up, slather with sauce, bake **5 minutes**; repeat 2–3 more times until a thick glaze builds.
7. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its endpoint is "meat pretty tender" after the covered phase plus glaze build, and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs.
8. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — cut into rib portions and serve with the remaining sauce.

### 4. Russian (Edimdoma, "Апрель") — Свиные ребрышки со специями и яблочным соком
**Inspired by**: Edimdoma.ru author "Апрель"; https://www.edimdoma.ru/retsepty/58048-svinye-rebryshki-so-spetsiyami (fetched 2026-10-31). *Dry spice rub with apple juice; stated for oven or grill; oven version 160 °C × 1.5 h. Rated 5/5, 34 ratings.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min + 1 h marinating · **Cook**: 1 h 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg свиные ребра (pork ribs), racks halved if needed
- **Spice rub**: 1 ст. л. соль (salt), 2 ст. л. паприка (paprika), 1 ст. л. перец чили молотый (ground chilli), 1 ст. л. чесночная специя (garlic spice), 1 ст. л. тмин молотый (ground cumin), 1 ст. л. сахар (sugar), кайенский перец (cayenne) to taste, 1 ч. л. сухая горчица (dry mustard; use 1 tbsp if prepared mustard)
- ½ стакан сок яблочный (apple juice)
- соус барбекю (barbecue sauce), optional, to serve

**Method**
1. Mix the spices. Cut the racks in half if needed.
2. Coat both sides of the ribs with the spice mix; sprinkle with the apple juice; cover and refrigerate **1 hour or more**.
3. Preheat the oven to **160 °C**. Lay the ribs on a tray; insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest part, away from bone.
4. Roast **1 h 30 min** (the source's stated oven time; the same recipe can be grilled over coals, spritzing with apple juice). Optionally brush with barbecue sauce near the end — the author skipped it.
5. **Pull at 88–93 °C** (190–199 °F) — the source states no internal temperature; its endpoint is the 1.5 h at 160 °C with spice crust set, and this pull band is the ToR's house band for collagen-converted ribs.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — cut the racks apart and serve.

The full observed range from fetched sources is a single explicit figure: **80 °C** (Svenskt Kött); no other oven source for this cut states an internal temperature, so the range cannot be widened from recipe data. The wider guidance picture disagrees: Svenskt Kött's own cut page gives **70 °C** as the well-done internal temperature for tjocka revben, and the Grilltips low-and-slow guidance (see the smoker leaf) starts rendering fat only around 90 °C — a documented Swedish disagreement about where "done" sits, preserved rather than merged. ## What makes this method special

The oven's enclosed, radiant heat turns tjocka revben into a self-basting environment: fat renders off the thick belly layer and collects in the pan, while the covered or foil-sealed phases keep that moisture around the meat, so collagen converts without the surface drying out — a real risk on a cut whose top meat layer can be twice as thick as its sternum end. The two-stage pattern common to all four sources (gentle long bake, then a short high-heat finish on separated ribs or uncovered foil) exists because of this cut's geometry: the slab needs slow even heat to render, but the individual ribs need direct heat to lacquer. Compared with smoking, the oven delivers no smoke flavour and relies on glaze and fond for surface complexity; compared with braising, it keeps the rub crust intact. Carryover is minimal — the endpoints are already at full collagen conversion, and the sources rest the meat briefly only to redistribute juices, not to climb in temperature.

## Summary

Oven roasting renders tjocka revben's thick, fatty belly meat into tender, pull-apart ribs under a lacquered crust: a slow covered or sealed bake converts the collagen, then a short high-heat finish on separated ribs caramelises the glaze. It suits the cut because the closed pan protects the uneven slab while it renders, something direct fire struggles with. The cook gains an almost entirely hands-off method with forgiving timing; the sacrifice is smoke flavour, which glaze has to replace. All four sources agree on the method's shape and none of them except the Swedish meat board states a doneness temperature, which is why the Swedish 70–80 °C figures are the only numeric anchors in this leaf.
