# Tjocka Revben × Pressure Cooker — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Tjocka revben are meat-covered front pork ribs, sold bone-in. A pressure cooker uses moist heat under pressure, followed in this recipe by an oven glaze; the source specifies the cut but not the internal temperature. The ribs can be whole and cracked or cut into pieces. Carryover after pressure release and oven finishing is not measured by the recipe.

## Source recipes

### 1. Swedish (Åse, 56kilo) — Honung- och ananasglaserade tjocka revben
**Inspired by**: https://www.56kilo.se/honung-och-ananasglaserade-tjocka-revben/ (read 2026-11-01). The source explicitly names tjocka revben and allows whole cracked ribs.
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: at least 1 h marinating · **Cook**: pressure time disputed below, plus browning and oven finish · **Rest**: cool slightly

**Ingredients**
- 1.5–2 kg pork ribs, salt and pepper
- ½ dl oil, 1 dl pineapple juice, ½ dl soy sauce, ½ dl honey
- ½ dl brown sugar, ½ dl sweet chilli sauce, ½ dl ketchup, 1 tbsp HP sauce
- 2 grated garlic cloves, 2 tbsp thinly sliced fresh ginger, 2 chopped red chillies

**Method**
1. Mix marinade ingredients and marinate ribs for at least one hour, preferably overnight in the refrigerator.
2. Brown ribs in a little oil; place in the pressure cooker with the sauce.
3. Close the lid and valve and use MEAT/STEW and TURBO. The recipe card says **15 minutes before TURBO**, whereas the introduction says **20 minutes before TURBO**, reducing time by 40%; the author also answers a reader that a pressure cooker takes 15 minutes. This conflict is unresolved, and appliance settings differ; consult the cooker's instructions.
4. Let pressure release naturally for 10 minutes, then release the remainder according to the appliance instructions. Remove the ribs; reduce the sauce, thickening if needed.
5. Brush the ribs with sauce and finish in a 225 °C oven until coloured, brushing again partway through. After opening the cooker, a probe can be inserted into the thickest meat away from bone to check temperature; the source gives no numeric pull target.
6. Slice between the bones and brush with remaining glaze. **Pull temperature: not stated. Final serving temperature: not stated.**

### 2. Japanese (Just One Cookbook, Namiko Hirasawa Chen) — Instant Pot honey spare ribs
**Inspired by**: Namiko Hirasawa Chen, Just One Cookbook; https://www.justonecookbook.com/instant-pot-honey-spare-ribs/ (read 2026-11-01). *Japanese home cooking adapted to a pressure cooker; the page states a preference for thick, meaty spare ribs over back ribs, which is the cut claim this leaf needs.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 20 min high pressure + 30 min natural release · **Rest**: in the pot

**Ingredients**
- 3 lb spare ribs, full rack cut into 1-inch cubes
- 1 tsp kosher salt; black pepper
- ½ yellow onion (113 g); 1 tsp grated ginger; 1 garlic clove; 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 tbsp sake
- 4 tbsp mirin; 3 tbsp sugar; 1 tbsp unseasoned rice vinegar; 4 tbsp soy sauce; 4 tbsp honey

**Method**
1. Salt and pepper the ribs. Mince the onion, grate the ginger and mince the garlic.
2. Sauté the ribs in oil in batches until browned; wipe excess oil from the pot.
3. Sweat the onion, deglaze with the sake, then add the ginger and garlic.
4. Add the ribs with the mirin, sugar and vinegar, then the soy sauce and honey.
5. Pressure cook on **High for 20 minutes** (25–30 minutes if the pieces are larger than 1 inch), then let the pressure release naturally for **30 minutes**.
6. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest piece, away from bone, after opening the pot; **the source states no target temperature** — its endpoint is the timed pressure cook plus natural release.
7. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve from the pot with the reduced sauce.

### 3. Levantine-spiced (frightful kitchen) — Pomegranate and sumac pork ribs
**Inspired by**: frightful kitchen; https://frightfulkitchen.wordpress.com/2016/04/21/pomegranate-sumac-pork-ribs/ (read 2026-11-01). *Pomegranate molasses, sumac and cinnamon under pressure, then an oven glaze; the author notes pork is unusual in the region and suggests beef or lamb ribs as the local equivalent.*
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 20–25 min under pressure + 20–30 min oven · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- ½ rack pork ribs
- 3 tbsp pomegranate molasses; 1 tbsp sumac; 2 tsp ground cinnamon
- A handful of dried cranberries (optional); 5 garlic cloves (2 chopped, 3 whole)
- 1 tsp sugar; salt; 10 baby potatoes (optional); a knob of butter
- **Glaze**: 2 tbsp pomegranate molasses; 2 tsp sumac

**Method**
1. Put the ribs, pomegranate molasses, sumac, cinnamon, cranberries, garlic, sugar, salt and potatoes in the pressure cooker; add cold water to just cover.
2. Bring to pressure (about 5 minutes), then cook on low **20–25 minutes**.
3. Release the pressure per the cooker's manual and transfer the ribs and potatoes to an ovenproof dish.
4. Brush with the glaze and cook in a preheated oven **20–30 minutes**, basting, until it does not dry out.
5. Sieve the cooking liquid back into the cooker and reduce uncovered about 20 minutes; whisk in the butter.
6. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest meat after the pressure stage; **the source states no internal temperature and no oven setting**.
7. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — spoon the reduced sauce over.
