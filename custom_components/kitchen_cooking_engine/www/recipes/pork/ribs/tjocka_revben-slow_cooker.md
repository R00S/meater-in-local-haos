## Cut profile

Slow cooking applies long, enclosed moist heat to this bone-in, meat-covered front-rib cut. The Swedish Christmas source finishes the ribs under the oven grill for colour; the British recipe uses meaty pork ribs but does not specify which part of the rack, so it is an anatomical lead rather than proof of an exact match. Check doneness in the thick meat away from bone; the sources give times, not measured internal endpoints. Carryover after the cooking and finishing stages is not documented.

## Source recipes

### 1. British (BBC Good Food, Good Food team) — Slow cooker ribs
**Inspired by**: BBC Good Food; https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/slow-cooker-ribs (archived copy fetched 2026-10-31). *Low slow-cooker cook in spiced stock, then an oven or barbecue crisping finish. Rated 5/5, 70 ratings.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 8–9 h low + 20–30 min finish · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1½ kg **meaty pork ribs**
- 1 bottle barbecue sauce (350 g approx), of which 4 tbsp go into the cooker and the rest is used for basting
- 2 pork stock cubes, 2 bay leaves, 1 tsp coriander seed, 1 tsp mustard seed, 1 tsp peppercorn
- Water to cover (roughly 1.5 l)

**Method**
1. Put 4 tbsp of the barbecue sauce together with all the other ingredients into the slow cooker; top up with enough water to cover (roughly 1.5 l).
2. **Slow cook on Low for 8–9 hours** until very tender but not completely falling off the bone. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest part, away from bone, once the ribs are submerged — the source works to time, not to a temperature.
3. Heat the oven to **220 °C** (200 °C fan / gas 7). Lift the ribs out with tongs — handle carefully, the meat is very tender — baste with the remaining barbecue sauce and lay on a foil-lined tray.
4. Cook **20–30 minutes** until the outside starts to crisp; alternatively finish on the barbecue, turning from time to time until piping hot throughout.
5. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** Its endpoint is very tender but not completely falling off the bone, followed by crisping.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve after the crisping finish.

### 2. Swedish (Linda, Lindas Mathörna) — Julens godaste revbensspjäll
**Inspired by**: https://lindasmathorna.se/juligaste-revbenspjallen-i-slowcookern/ (read 2026-11-01). The source specifies *tjocka revben* and identifies the recipe as from Linda's slow-cooking cookbook.
**Serves**: 6–8 on a Christmas buffet · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: low 7–8 h or high 5–6 h, plus 10 min finish · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 kg tjocka revben
- 1.5 tbsp ground ginger, 0.5 tbsp coriander, 1 tbsp garlic powder, 1 tsp onion powder
- 0.5 dl chilli sauce, 2 dl apple juice, 0.5 tbsp salt, black pepper to taste
- Glaze: 1 garlic clove, 1 tbsp tomato purée, 2 tbsp Japanese soy sauce, 1 tbsp rapeseed oil, 2 tbsp honey, 2 tsp grated ginger, 1 tsp cinnamon

**Method**
1. Mix dry spices, salt and pepper; rub on the ribs and place in the slow cooker.
2. Pour apple juice and chilli sauce over the ribs. Cook on low 7–8 hours or high 5–6 hours.
3. Heat oven grill to 250 °C. Bring the glaze ingredients briefly to a boil, then brush onto ribs in an oven dish.
4. Grill in the oven for 10 minutes for surface colour. After cooking, insert a probe into the thickest meat, avoiding bone and fat pockets, if checking temperature. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.**
5. **Final serving temperature: not stated by source.** The source does not give a rest time.

### 3. Chinese tradition (Caroline's Cooking) — Chinese spare ribs in the slow cooker
**Inspired by**: Caroline's Cooking; https://www.carolinescooking.com/chinese-spare-ribs-slow-cooker/ (read 2026-11-01). *Char siu-style hoisin, soy and honey ribs cooked as individual ribs in the slow cooker, then broiled; the source prefers St Louis-style ribs as "probably best suited". No internal temperature is stated.*
**Serves**: 2–3 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 6–7 h · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 lb St Louis-style pork ribs, about half a rack
- 2 tbsp hoisin sauce; 2 tbsp honey; 1 tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp rice wine
- 1 tsp Chinese five spice powder; ½ tsp grated ginger; ½ tsp grated garlic

**Method**
1. Cut the ribs between the bones into individual ribs and lay them in a single layer in the slow cooker.
2. Mix the hoisin, honey, soy, rice wine, five spice, ginger and garlic and pour over; turn to coat, leaving a thin layer of sauce and a small puddle around the ribs.
3. Cook on **low for 6–7 hours**, until tender but not falling off the bone.
4. Insert a probe from the side into the thickest rib, away from bone, if checking; the source gives no target.
5. Reduce the sauce in a small pan and pour a little over the ribs; broil a couple of minutes to caramelise.
6. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve with the remaining sauce.

### 5. American (101 Cooking For Two, Dan Mikesell) — Slow Cooker Baby Back Ribs
**Inspired by**: Dan Mikesell ("DrDan"), 101 Cooking For Two; https://www.101cookingfortwo.com/crock-pot-baby-back-ribs/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *Named home-cook author with a tested, timed method. Cut term: baby back ribs — the loin-side rack — while the page itself notes "Spare ribs or St. Louis ribs also work, but may need trimming to fit"; stored as a comparator on that basis.*
**Serves**: not stated (1 rack, 3–4 lb) · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 7–8 h LOW or 3–4 h HIGH, then broil · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 rack baby back ribs, 3–4 pounds (spare ribs or St. Louis ribs also work, per the source)
- Pork dry rub — the source's simple version or a favourite
- BBQ sauce — for brushing before broiling
- Liquid smoke, optional — the source recommends Wright's brand

**Method**
1. Peel the membrane off the bone side, cut the rack in half so it fits, add the liquid smoke if using, and rub all over.
2. Stand the ribs around the sides of the crock pot, **meat side facing the centre**.
3. Cook **7–8 hours on LOW** or **3–4 hours on HIGH**, until fork-tender; the source allows 1–2 hours more for two racks or a tightly packed pot. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest meat, away from bone, at load time.
4. Finish under the broiler with BBQ sauce for a sticky, caramelised crust. The source's fallback, if the pot will not get hot enough, is a **300 °F (150 °C)** oven finish — an appliance figure.
5. **Pull at 93–96 °C** (200–205 °F) — stated by the source as the usual doneness ("usually 200°–205°F"), with **88 °C (190 °F)** given as the minimum target and the instruction to check the thickest meat without touching bone.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — sauce, broil, serve.
