## Cut profile

Sous vide is the one method for tjocka revben where the Swedish meat board's own cut page names the
technique explicitly (its tillagningssätt list includes *Sous-vide*), yet the fetched recipe corpus
is the thinnest of all the methods on this cut — modernist rib cooking gravitates to baby backs and
St. Louis racks, and the sources that do exist disagree radically about temperature: one cooks
cross-cut rib pieces at 75 °C for five hours in the dim-sum style, the other holds whole racks at
65 °C for 36 hours for a Southern-style bark-and-glaze finish. Both work because a sealed bath controls the water temperature for long cooking with limited evaporation — the failure mode the oven and grill must constantly manage. Food safety depends on the full time–temperature process and thickness; a bath setting by itself does not establish pasteurisation. Probe placement is per the ToR's sous vide
exception: no probe during the sealed cook — the circulator temperature is the cook target, and the
bag liquid or a post-bath check verifies. Carryover does not apply: the bag is opened at the target
and finished by sear, broil, smoke or oven, which is where any residual climb happens.

## Styckesprofil

Sous vide är den enda metod där den svenska köttbranschens egen styckessida namnger tekniken
uttryckligen (*Sous-vide*), men den hämtade reseptkorpusen är tunnast av alla metoder på detta
stycke. Källorna motsäger sig radikalt om temperatur: ett tillagar korsskurna ribbitar vid 75 °C i
fem timmar i dim sum-stil, det andra håller hela spjäll vid 65 °C i36 timmar för BBQ-avslut. Båda
fungerar eftersom ett förseglat bad håller det tjocka magköttlagret på exakt temperatur hur länge
som helst utan ytattorkning. Sond placering enligt ToRs sous vide-undantag: ingen sond under det
förseglade badet — cirkulatorns temperatur är målet. Eftervärmen gäller inte; påsningen öppnas vid
målet och avslutas med sotning, grillpanna, rök eller ugn.

## Source recipes

### 1. Chinese-American (Anova Culinary, Carolyn Jung) — Sous Vide Chinese Black Bean Spare Ribs
**Inspired by**: Carolyn Jung (James Beard Award-winning Bay Area food journalist), Anova Culinary recipe community; https://recipes.anovaculinary.com/recipe/sous-vide-chinese-black-bean-spare-ribs (fetched 2026-10-31). *Dim-sum black bean spare ribs; the source explicitly recommends St. Louis ribs ("their abundance of fat will be juicier than baby backs") cut into segments. Rated 40 votes.*
**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 5 h at 75 °C · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2½ lb pork ribs, **cut into 1-inch pieces** (source's stated preference: St. Louis style, for fat)
- 6 tbsp salted black beans, rinsed
- 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
- 3–5 Thai chiles or 1–2 jalapeños, stems removed and minced
- 1½ tbsp minced fresh ginger, 5 garlic cloves peeled and minced
- 1 tbsp cornstarch, 1 tbsp mirin (Japanese rice wine), 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tsp granulated sugar

**Method**
1. Set the sous vide bath to **75 °C (167 °F)** — the circulator temperature is the cook target; no probe is inserted during the sealed cook (ToR sous vide exception).
2. Smash the black beans to a paste; stir in the soy sauce, chiles, ginger, garlic, cornstarch, mirin, sesame oil and sugar; coat the rib pieces well.
3. Seal in a zipper-lock or vacuum bag (water immersion or moist setting); cook **5 hours**.
4. **Pull at 75 °C** (167 °F) — stated by the source as the bath temperature; the bag is opened at the target.
5. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** (75 °C is the bath setting, not a measured serving temperature) — pour the ribs and their sauce straight into a serving bowl; the source's stated serving cue is the brothy sauce begging for steamed rice.

### 2. Modernist BBQ, "JC" (Anova Culinary, Shanghai & England) — JC's Perfect 36h BBQ Pork Ribs
**Inspired by**: JC (based in Shanghai & England), Anova Culinary recipe community; https://recipes.anovaculinary.com/recipe/jcs-tender-bbq-pork-ribs (fetched 2026-10-31). *36-hour whole-rack cook; the source's own time study: "At 24 hours the meat has a little too much bite while by 48 the racks are all falling apart,36 hours seems perfect." States Baby Back or St. Louis cut. Rated 4.8/5, 34 votes.*
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 40 min · **Cook**: 36 h at 65 °C + finishing · **Rest**: ice bath, then up to 2 days refrigerated in bag

**Ingredients**
- Baby Back ribs **or St. Louis Style ribs**, one rack per bag (the source's stated options; St. Louis is a trimmed spare-rib-derived comparator, not a proven exact match to the Swedish cut)
- **Rub**: 30 g paprika, 150 g soft brown sugar, 5 g black pepper, 40 g kosher salt, 15 g chilli powder, 15 g onion powder, 15 g garlic powder
- Yellow mustard or a little olive oil, to make the rub stick
- Honey, for the finishing glaze (or the reduced bag liquid, or favourite BBQ sauce)

**Method**
1. Mix the rub ingredients. Coat the ribs with yellow mustard or a little olive oil, then add the rub generously to both sides and all edges.
2. Bag the ribs, one rack per bag (the source bags a second rack as insurance for such a long cook).
3. **Sous vide for 36 hours at 65 °C** — the circulator temperature is the cook target; no probe during the sealed cook (ToR sous vide exception).
4. Remove and chill in an ice bath (or cold water); once cooled, use immediately or keep in the bag in the fridge up to 2 days.
5. To finish: pour the bag liquid into a saucepan and simmer down to a thick glaze; paint the ribs with it; oven at **125 °C for 30 minutes** straight on the shelf; add honey to the sauce, paint again, back in the oven **30 minutes**.
6. **Pull at 65 °C** (149 °F) — stated by the source as the bath temperature for the full 36-hour hold; the finishing oven adds surface heat only.
7. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — flip bone side up, cut between the ribs and serve.
