# Tjocka Revben × Steam — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Steaming cooks these ribs in vapour at atmospheric pressure, in a covered pot or basket sitting above the water, so the meat is heated by condensing steam rather than by fat or braising liquid — moisture stays inside the cut, and the collagen in the thick front meat cover softens without the cooking liquid diluting the seasoning. This method meets this cut in Chinese cooking, where the front rack is explicitly the piece to steam: one source below is written for 猪前排 (front ribs) and says so, and the naming research for this cut records 前排 as the first choice for steaming and for children. Four traditions are represented — Chinese, Japanese, Thai and Vietnamese — each with its own steamer and its own cut word, and each judges doneness by time and by how the meat releases from the bone rather than by a probe. The sources do not measure carryover; a plate lifted from a steamer cools quickly, so carryover should be small, but no source quantifies it.

## Styckesprofil

Ånga lagar de här revbenen med vattenånga i en täckt korg ovanför vattnet — köttet får värme genom kondensation, så fuktigheten stannar i stycket och kollagenet i det tjocka köttlagret mjuknar utan att kokbuljongen spädar ut kryddningen. Metoden och stycket möts i det kinesiska köket, där främre spjäll är just det man ångar: en av källorna är skriven för 猪前排 (främre revben), och namnresearchen för det här stycket anger 前排 som förstahandsval vid ångning. Fyra traditioner är representerade — kinesisk, japansk, thailändsk och vietnamesisk — och alla avgör mognaden efter tid och efter hur köttet släpper från benet, inte efter sond. Källorna mäter inte eftervärme; en tallrik ur ångaren svalnar snabbt, så eftervärmen bör vara liten, men ingen källa anger något.

## Source recipes

### 1. Chinese (美食天下, 空客) — 蒜蓉豆豉蒸排骨
**Inspired by**: 空客, 美食天下; https://home.meishichina.com/recipe-53197.html (fetched 2026-11-01). *Written for 猪前排 (front ribs) by name — the cut term used is 「猪前排 一根」. Sourced in Chinese on a Chinese recipe site; the page carries the author's own note that the pan juices belong over plain rice.*
**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: ca 40 min steaming (plus 3 min blanching) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 猪前排 一根 — one front rack of pork ribs (front rib, the exact term of this cut)
- 豆豉 — fermented black beans, to taste (source states 适量)
- 蒜头 3片 — 3 slices/cloves garlic
- 姜 一片 — 1 slice ginger
- 花生油 — peanut oil, to taste (适量; source warns not to use much, as the beans and ribs already carry fat)
- 蚝油 — oyster sauce, to taste (适量)
- 生抽 — light soy sauce, to taste (适量)
- 花椒粉 — ground Sichuan pepper, to taste (适量)

**Method**
1. Bring a pot of water to the boil. Wash the ribs and blanch them **3 minutes** in the boiling water; drain and plate them.
2. Chop the fermented black beans together with the rinsed garlic and ginger.
3. Heat a little oil in a pan — deliberately little, since beans and ribs already carry fat — and fry the bean, garlic and ginger mixture until fragrant.
4. Add the oyster sauce, soy sauce and pepper and cook briefly, stirring.
5. Tip the fried sauce straight over the ribs and mix thoroughly so every piece is coated.
6. Bring the steamer to the boil, set the plate in, and steam **40 minutes**. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest front-rib piece, away from bone, at the start of steaming.
7. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** The endpoint is the stated time; the source prints no core temperature and none is inferred.
8. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — the author's serving note is to pour the juices over plain rice.

### 2. Japanese (LEE 2026年5月号, ワタナベマキ) — スペアリブとアスパラガスの塩麹蒸し
**Inspired by**: ワタナベマキ (Watanabe Maki), LEE 2026年5月号, recipe pages 3561353; https://lee.hpplus.jp/recipe/3561353/ (fetched 2026-11-01). *A named cookbook-author recipe from a Japanese food magazine (author of 『ほったらかしでおいしい！せいろでおかず蒸し』, Gakken). Searer (せいろ) steaming; the cut term is generic 豚スペアリブ.*
**Serves**: 2–3 · **Prep**: 20 min (plus marinating) · **Cook**: 23 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 豚スペアリブ 6個 (500–600 g) — 6 pieces pork spare ribs
- アスパラガス 6本 — 6 asparagus spears
- 塩麹 大さじ2と1/2 — 2½ tbsp *shio-koji* (salt-fermented rice malt paste)
- レモン汁 大さじ1 — 1 tbsp lemon juice
- レモン（国産）の皮 少々 — a little lemon zest

**Method**
1. Pat the pork dry with kitchen paper, rub the shio-koji all over it, and leave **20 minutes**.
2. Trim the tough root end from the asparagus and halve the spears lengthwise, splitting the thick ones.
3. Line a seiro/steamer basket with oven sheet and set it over a pot of boiling water; lid on, **high heat, steam 20 minutes**. Add the asparagus at the edge and steam **3 minutes** more. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest rib piece, away from bone, at the start.
4. Take off the heat, squeeze the lemon juice over, and scatter the zest.
5. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** The source's stated endpoint is time; no core temperature appears, and the author's note on the method is that shio-koji breaks down the pork proteins for a softer texture.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve warm with the lemon.

### 3. Thai (ครัวบ้านพิม, พิม) — ซี่โครงหมูนึ่งเต้าเจี้ยว
**Inspired by**: พิม (Pim), ครัวบ้านพิม (Krua Ban Pim); https://www.pim.in.th/side-dish-by-pork/651-2013-06-07-04-51-02 (fetched 2026-11-01). *A named Thai home-cooking blogger, writing in Thai; the source's own cut term is ซี่โครงอ่อนหมู, described on the page as "โครงอ่อนที่สวยมาก เนื้อเยอะ" — soft ribs, lots of meat.*
**Serves**: not stated (400 g ribs) · **Prep**: 1 h 30 min marinating · **Cook**: ca 1 h 05 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- ซี่โครงอ่อนหมู หั่นเป็นชิ้น 400 กรัม — 400 g soft pork ribs, cut into pieces
- เต้าเจี้ยวดำ แบบเป็นเม็ด (น้ำและเนื้อรวมกัน) 5 ชต. — 5 tbsp black salted soybeans, beans and liquid
- กระเทียมสับ 2 ชต. — 2 tsp minced garlic
- ขิงแก่สับ 2 ชต. — 2 tsp minced mature ginger
- น้ำตาลทรายขาว 1+1/2 ชช. — 1½ tsp white sugar
- น้ำมันงา 1+1/2 ชต. — 1½ tbsp sesame oil
- น้ำเปล่า 1/4 ถ้วย — ¼ cup water
- ขึ้นฉ่าย 1 ต้นเล็ก — 1 small celery/celery-leaf stalk

**Method**
1. Mix the minced ginger, garlic, sugar, sesame oil, black fermented soybeans and water in a bowl until the sugar dissolves; taste the bean liquid first — if it is very salty, 4 tbsp of beans is enough.
2. Add the cleaned, cut ribs and toss to coat; marinate in the refrigerator **1 hour 30 minutes**.
3. Take the ribs out and let them lose their chill. Steam **about 1 hour**, counted from the water boiling, over **medium heat** — the source explicitly says medium is enough and high heat is not needed. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest piece, away from bone, at the start.
4. For a softer result the source steams **30 minutes longer**; for a shortcut it offers boiling/braising on low heat instead, noting that steaming keeps the bone and the overall flavour gentler.
5. Open the lid, scatter the celery, and steam **5 minutes** more.
6. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** Doneness is by time and texture; no core temperature is printed.
7. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — serve with plain rice or rice congee.

### 4. Vietnamese (Bếp Trưởng Á Âu, Nguyễn Richard) — Sườn lợn hấp hạt kê
**Inspired by**: Nguyễn Richard (bếp phó), Bếp Trưởng Á Âu; https://beptruong.edu.vn/cach-lam-suon-hap (fetched 2026-11-01). *A Vietnamese culinary-school site, written in Vietnamese; this is variant 1 of four steamed-rib recipes on the page. The cut term is generic sườn heo / sườn non, with no front/back statement.*
**Serves**: not stated (300 g ribs) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 20–30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Sườn heo 300 gr — 300 g pork ribs
- Dầu hào 1 muỗng canh — 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- Đường trắng 1 muỗng cà phê — 1 tsp white sugar
- Nước tương 1 muỗng canh — 1 tbsp soy sauce
- Sốt cà chua 1 muỗng canh — 1 tbsp tomato sauce/ketchup
- Tiêu 1/2 muỗng cà phê — ½ tsp ground pepper
- Gừng băm 1 muỗng cà phê — 1 tsp minced ginger
- Hạt kê 50 gr — 50 g millet (*hạt kê*, hulled millet, coating the meat as in 粉蒸 style)

**Method**
1. Soak the millet in water **20–30 minutes**, then drain in a sieve.
2. Wash the ribs, pat dry, cut into bite-sized pieces.
3. Toss the ribs with all the seasonings, then add the millet and mix until the grains cling.
4. Bring a pot of water to the boil, set the bowl of ribs in, and steam **20–30 minutes until cooked**. Insert the MEATER probe from the side into the thickest piece, away from bone, at the start.
5. **Pull temperature: not stated by source.** The source states only that the ribs are cooked; no core temperature appears.
6. Final serving temperature: **not stated by source** — eat with hot rice.
