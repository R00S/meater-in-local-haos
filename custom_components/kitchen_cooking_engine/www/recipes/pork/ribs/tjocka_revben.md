# Tjocka Revben — Cut Overview

## Cut profile

Tjocka revben is the Swedish cut from the front of the pork rib cage, with substantial meat attached, sold bone-in and, for this entry, without skin. It is often sold *knäckt* (cracked to help portion it). Swedish sources distinguish it from posterior *tunna revben* and upper, loin-side *kamben*; similar English names alone do not establish equivalence. See [Svenskt Kött's glossary](https://svensktkott.se/om-kott/styckdetaljer/amerikanska-styckningsdetaljer-pa-svenska/) and [cut page](https://svensktkott.se/om-kott/styckdetaljer/gris/revbensspjall/).

### Cutting lines and comparable cuts

- US full spare ribs come from the belly side; the [South Dakota State University fabrication guide](https://extension.sdstate.edu/pork-carcass-fabrication-primal-and-retail-cuts) explains how cutting deeper leaves meatier ribs and less bacon. [Grilltips](https://grilltips.se/grilltips/kottguider/svenska-tunna-revben) says a US full rack includes the Swedish thick-rib area, trimmed differently. That is a practitioner's comparison, not proof that every US spare-rib or St. Louis rack has the same front portion and meat cover. Ask for the front, meat-covered, bone-in portion and check the actual cut; cooking time need not transfer unchanged.
- The Boston butt and picnic shoulder can be used for pulled pork ([SDSU](https://extension.sdstate.edu/pork-carcass-fabrication-primal-and-retail-cuts)); their cutting lines differ from the Swedish front-rib cut. The source does not establish exactly where the meat covering tjocka revben ends up in every US breakdown. Conversely, [Anders's carnitas](https://ikoketmedanders.se/carnitas-pa-tjocka-revben-i-crock-pot/) explicitly pulls Swedish thick ribs after long cooking. Neither observation proves identical butchery.
- Danish [revelsben](https://www.danishcrownprofessional.com/da-dk/produkter/bryst/revelsben-1-bens-i-skiver/) is sold as skinless bone-in breast roast in one-bone slices. German butcher [Dirk Ludwig](https://www.der-ludwig.de/grill-bbq/bbq-spareribs/spareribs-brustspitze/) describes *Brustspitzribs* / *Dicke Rippe* as the lower rib section. Both are anatomically useful leads; neither source proves an exact Swedish cutting-line match. Verify skin, bone and front location on individual recipes.
- Beef **prime rib** is a beef rib roast, not a translation of this pork cut ([Beef Checkoff](https://www.beefitswhatsfordinner.com/cuts/cut/2851/rib-primal)). Pork baby back is from the loin, while St. Louis is a trimmed spare-rib preparation; neither name alone proves the thick Swedish cut.

### The same cut idea in other languages

US butchery is the weakest comparison because it has no named item here. German-speaking, Danish and Finnish butchery splits the carcass closer to the Swedish pattern, and each has a *named* retail item for this piece — better evidence than an English label:

- **German — *Dicke Rippe*, also sold as *Brustspitze* / *Schweinebrust*.** The German cut scheme numbers item 5 as "Brust(spitze) / Dicke Rippe", between *Kamm* and *Stielkotelett*, with *Schweinebauch* a separate item 9; the article notes that "den Teil der Rippenenden in unterschiedlichen Zuschnitten auch Dicke Rippe" is how the rib ends are named ([Teilstücke des Schweinefleischs](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teilst%C3%BCcke_des_Schweinefleischs)). A Bavarian butcher-meister describes *Brustspitzribs* as coming from "der sogenannten Dicken Rippe des Schweins", with more connective tissue and cartilage, and prescribes long cooking ([Dirk Ludwig](https://www.der-ludwig.de/grill-bbq/bbq-spareribs/spareribs-brustspitze/)). This is the closest named match found in any language so far.
- **Austrian — *Schweinebrust*.** The same source records that in Austria item 5, the rib section, is called *Schweinebrust* ([Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teilst%C3%BCcke_des_Schweinefleischs)) — a rib-bearing breast cut, not boneless brisket-type meat.
- **Danish — *revelsben*.** Danish Crown sells it as slices "skåret af dansk stegestykke uden svær og med ben, så der er et ben i hver skive", 26.8 g fat per 100 g, roasted at 180 °C ([Danish Crown Professional](https://www.danishcrownprofessional.com/da-dk/produkter/bryst/revelsben-1-bens-i-skiver/)). Same anatomical zone, presented as cross-sawn single-bone slices rather than a slab.
- **Finnish — *luinen kylki*.** A Finnish BBQ guide separates it from ribs explicitly: "Nämä eivät ole 'ribsejä', vaan luullista kylkeä. Luiden päällä on kyllä samaa lihaa kuin sparessakin mutta siihen on jätetty paksummalti muutakin" — the same meat as spare ribs with more left on the bone, needing a longer cook ([Poppamies](https://poppamies.fi/reseptit/ribsien-valmistustekniikat-simppelisti/)). That description matches this cut's defining property, the thick meat cover on the bone.
- **Polish — *żeberka paski* and *żeberka grube*.** Polish fabrication describes the belly as containing "the split sternum, the middle and lower rib sections and their cartilaginous ends", with a narrow-cut belly yielding *żeberka paski* and the forequarter yielding *żeberka grube*, an item of "wybitnie kulinarne" use ([mieso.com.pl](https://mieso.com.pl/aktualnosci/technologia-rozbioru-poltusz-a-przeznaczenie-miesa-wieprzowego/)); a Polish culinary textbook describes *żeberka* as long, thin rib meat with cartilage taken from the thoracic section ([zpe.gov.pl](https://zpe.gov.pl/a/usytuowanie-elementow-kulinarnych-w-tuszy-zwierzat-rzeznych/D1Wx6hydi)). Which Polish item corresponds to tjocka revben is not settled by these sources.
- **Norwegian — *ribbe* is not established as this cut.** Norwegian *ribbe* is recorded as "en betegnelse på sidekjøttet på et dyr" ([no.wikipedia](https://no.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbe)), i.e. side meat generally; none of the sources read give a bone-and-meat specification matching this cut, so the Norwegian Christmas rib is treated here as a different, side-based item rather than a match.

No source states that any of these items is identical to the Swedish cut, and Swedish retail practice itself varies. What they establish is narrower and useful: a rib-end or breast piece carrying a thick meat cover is a normal **named** cut in the German-speaking, Danish and Polish schemes, where US butchery instead routes that tissue into the shoulder, the belly and the loin. Recipes in those languages are therefore better research targets for this cut than English spare-rib recipes.
### The same cut idea in Asia, Africa, the Caribbean and South America

The same question pays off unevenly here. In East Asia the front rib carrying a thick meat layer and cartilage is a **named** cut with its own cooking tradition; in most of Africa, Indonesia, Brazil and Spanish America the word for "ribs" is generic and the piece is not separately named.

- **Chinese — *小排* / *前排*, the closest non-European match.** The market word 排骨 is an umbrella ("在菜市场里，排骨往往是一个统称"), and cutting a whole rack front to back yields named sections including *猪前排* and *猪小排*: "猪小排则是猪前排有肋骨部位，带肋骨5根~6根，去脊椎，硬胸骨，呈'A'字形的部位肉" — front ribs, spine off, hard sternum kept ([Xinhua reprinting 科普中国](http://www.zj.xinhuanet.com/20260509/76c8958b00bf4ae4abe1f76dc5145b42/c.html)). A mainland culinary reference adds the defining property: "小排的肉层比较厚，并带有白色软骨" — a thick meat layer with white cartilage ([meishichina](https://www.meishichina.com/YuanLiao/ZhuXiaoPai/tiyan/)). *猪肋排*, by contrast, is the mid-side rib "从猪背脊下面、五花肉上面" — the spare-rib analogue. **Caveats:** Chinese usage is not uniform; a Taiwanese industry page puts 肋排 in the back/loin primal and *腩排* ("帶有白色軟骨，肉層比較厚") in the belly ([Taïsugar](https://www.taisugar.com.tw/monthly/CPN.aspx?ms=1366&p=13384168&s=13384205)), while a Taiwan butchery explainer reverses that placement ([foodsandsation](https://www.foodsandsation.com.tw/pages/ribs)). *软骨排* is a vendor term with no authority behind it.
- **Japanese — *肩スペアリブ*.** A major processor defines bone-in belly as spare ribs: "骨つきのバラ肉を「スペアリブ」と言います。肋骨とそのまわりの赤身肉のこと" ([Nippon Ham](https://www.nipponham.co.jp/recipes/meat/knowhow/pork/01.html)); a producer splits the term into *肩スペアリブ* and *バラスペアリブ*, and the shoulder one is the thick-meat item: "肉に厚みがあり、食べごたえのあるジューシーな肉質…日本のスーパーに並ぶスペアリブは肩スペアリブの場合が多い" ([HyLife Pork](https://hylifepork.com/tips/sparerib_and_backrib/)). *バックリブ* is the loin-side rib with less fat. So Japanese names this piece by rib *position* (shoulder end) rather than by meat thickness.
- **Korean — *쪽갈비*.** Korean divides the rack by rib number: front ribs #1–#5 as 갈비, with *등갈비* as baby back from about #5 and *마구리* as rib tips ([Kimchimari's cut chart](https://kimchimari.com/know-your-pork-cut/)). A Korean butchery explainer describes *쪽갈비* as the lower, belly-adjacent rib cut that comes away with belly meat attached, against the leaner *등갈비* ([Naver blog](https://m.blog.naver.com/cc1125cc/224382012605)); that page is blog-level and the boundary is given as rib 5 *or* 6, so the exact line is not settled. *갈비* alone is simply "rib".
- **Vietnamese — *sườn non*.** Vietnamese separates thick from bony ribs by quality rather than position: "Sườn non… là phần xương sườn nhỏ, xương dẹt, nhiều thịt, ít xương, thường có sụn" against *sườn già*, which has bigger, harder bones and less meat ([vi.wikipedia](https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C6%B0%E1%BB%9Dn_(%E1%BA%A9m_th%E1%BB%B1c)). The same page renders *sườn non* as English "rib tips", and **neither page states whether it sits at the front** — so it is a thick-meat rib, not a located front-rib cut.
- **Thai — *ซี่โครงหมู* is generic and the polarity is reversed.** A Thai pork-cut explainer puts the small, dense rib "ใกล้กับกระดูกสันหลัง" (near the spine) as *ซี่โครงหมูอ่อน*, and the bone-in rib "ตัดมาจากท้องหมูหรือซี่โครงล่าง" with less meat and more fat as the cheaper item ([Sanook](https://www.sanook.com/women/247113/)). That is the opposite of calling the meaty front rib the premium cut, so Thai does not supply a name for this piece.
- **Indonesian — *iga babi* is generic.** Indonesian Wikipedia's article is a stub: ribs, meat and bone "dipotong menjadi beberapa bagian", with no front/back, meat-thickness or belly-versus-loin distinction ([id.wikipedia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_babi)). **Filipino** rib terminology could not be verified at all — searching returned no usable source, so nothing is claimed.
- **South Africa — *Thick Rib* / *Dikrib*, distinct from *Belly* / *Lies* and *Breast* / *Bors*.** A bilingual trade cut list names "Dikrib/ Thick Rib", "Lies/ Belly", "Bors/ Breast" and "Ribbetjie/ Rib or Spare rib" as separate lines, and the industry guide states "Thick rib chops are cut close to the neck end of the carcass" while "The breast is where spare ribs are cut from" ([southafrica.co.za quoting *The Complete Guide to South African Pork*](https://southafrica.co.za/versatility-of-pork-meat.html)). This is the only African tradition found that names the front rib separately — but it sells it as **chops**, not as a slab, and the Afrikaans *ribbetjie* usually means a rib slab generally (often lamb).
- **Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, Zimbabwe — generic only.** Retailers and processors use plain English "ribs", "spare ribs" and "back ribs" (Colcom lists spare and back ribs side by side); no indigenous named front-rib cut surfaced, and pork is a minority meat in most of these markets.
- **Angola and Mozambique — *entrecosto*** ("between-ribs"), with *costeleta* for a rib chop; Brazilian-influenced usage says *costela*. No separate name for a thick front-rib layer.
- **Brazil — *costela* is generic; *costela de barriga* is the belly rib.** A major processor states that *costela de barriga*, "também chamada de spare ribs", is taken from "as costelas mais baixas do porco, na região do abdômen" and is "maior e mais carnudo" than baby back ([Seara](https://www.seara.com.br/materia/costela-suina-tipos-caracteristicas-e-dicas-de-preparo/)). So in Brazilian usage the *meatier* rib is the belly one, and the front rib has no separate name; the ABPA export list keeps *Barriga* and *Costela* as distinct categories but names only ([brazilianpork.com.br](https://brazilianpork.com.br/industria-suina/produtos/)).
- **Spanish-speaking South America and the Caribbean — generic, with unverified leads.** Spanish *costilla*/*costillar* is the rib cage generally; the Colombian national cut manual lists "Costilla de pecho", "Costilla campesina" and "Costilla San Luis" among its costilla cuts, but only the contents list could be read, so **none of those definitions is verified**. Jamaican butchery follows the British-derived primal scheme (neck end, shoulder, loin, belly, chump) with "spare ribs" as the only rib term ([Jamaica Observer](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2010/02/03/back-to-basics-butchery-101-knowing-your-meat/)). Argentina, Chile, Peru, Mexico and Trinidad remain unverified.

**What this changes:** the front rib with a thick meat cover is a named, separately cooked cut in **Chinese (*小排*/*前排*), Japanese (*肩スペアリブ*), Korean (*쪽갈비*) and South African (*Thick Rib*)** butchery. Chinese is the strongest lead, because it names the piece *and* cooks it constantly — braised, steamed and deep-fried in cross-cut pieces, which matches this cut's bone-in, meat-heavy structure far better than American spare-rib recipes. Africa south of the Sahara, Indonesia, Brazil, Spanish America and the Caribbean mostly say "ribs" and leave the piece unnamed, so those traditions are useful for cooking technique but not for cut identification.

**Temperature behaviour (verified 2026-10-31):** the fetched Swedish guidance for this exact cut disagrees with itself, and the disagreement is recorded rather than merged. Svenskt Kött's cut page states the well-done internal temperature is **70 °C** and that traditional Swedish cooking is high heat with sage or dried ginger for a resistant texture, or pre-boil in broth/salted water (~45 min, to 70 °C) then glaze, or slow roast/grill under lid until "saftiga, eftergivna och hopfallande". Its own oven recipe pulls at **80 °C**. The Swedish BBQ school, represented by Grilltips.se's tjocka-revben method, states the cut is *"egentligen klara redan vid75 °C, men det är först vid90 °C som fettet smälter"* and cooks to **90–95 °C** with a documented comment-thread disagreement (pull at 70 °C vs 90–93 °C), while American low-and-slow guidance (Meathead) works at a 107 °C pit and accepts ~93 °C (200 °F) probe readings only as a collagen-conversion signal, preferring the bend test. These are different cooking and texture goals; the source’s description of fat melting at 90 °C should not be read as an exclusive physical melting point or as a universal guarantee of texture. USDA-style safe guidance for pork (63 °C /145 °F) sits below every culinary endpoint recorded for this cut, as it does for the sibling rib cuts.

The in-language pass added three methods that had had no leaf at all — **steam** (Chinese 猪前排 steaming is the first choice named for the front rack), **air fryer** (Chinese, German, Brazilian and Korean recipes, all appliance set-points and no core temperatures) and **charcoal grill** (kept separate from `grill`, which holds gas and unspecified outdoor grills; the German charcoal source is the only one that both names *Dicke Rippe* and states a core temperature). ## Styckesprofil

Tjocka revben är den främre delen av grisens revbenkorg: de tjocka framre revbenen med ett kraftigt lager magkött kvar, ben-in och **utan hud**, oftast "knäckta" så spjället portjoneras lätt (Svenskt Kött; SV Wikipedia). Det är ett eget stycke, skilt från det som sitter bakom: de bakre revbenen blir tunna revben ("även kallade 'spare ribs'"), medan kamben/baby back sitter uppe mot länden. Amerikanska spare ribs kommer från sidan, men styckningsgräns och mängden kvarlämnat kött kan skilja sig; de är inte automatiskt samma detalj. Strukturen är fett, kollagen och knäckta ben: mörhet3/3, marmorering3/3 på Svenskt Köts egen skala. Erstattningsdetalj: bogblad.

**Temperaturbeteende (verifierat2026-10-31):** den svenska vägledningen för detta stycke säger emot sig själv, och motsägelsen registreras i stället för att slås samman. Svenskt Kött anger70 °C som genomstekt innertemperatur (traditionellt hög värme med salvia/torkad ingefära, eller förkok45 min i buljong till70 °C före glasering, eller långsam stekning tills köttet "hopfallande"); deras eget ugnssrek drar vid80 °C. Den svenska BBQ-skolan (Grilltips) anger att stycket är "klara redan vid75 °C, men först vid90 °C smälter fettet" och lagar till90–95 °C, med dokumenterad kommentarsstrid (dra vid70 °C kontra90–93 °C), medan amerikansk låg-och-långsam-vägledning (Meathead) arbetar vid107 °C grop och accepterar~93 °C som kollagensignal hellre än termometer. Källorna beskriver olika tillagningsmål; uppgiften om fett vid 90 °C är inte en generell smältpunkt eller en garanti för en viss konsistens. USDA-säkerhet för fläsk (63 °C /145 °F) ligger under alla kulinariska ändpunkter för detta stycke, precis som för syskonstycena.
