## Cut profile

Braising is the older, wetter half of European ham cookery. A cured joint — gammon in Britain,
Kassler in Germany, ham hocks in either — is simmered in cider, beer or stock for one to three
hours until a fork slides in, then either served from the pot or finished with a glaze in a hot
oven. Where the oven roast works by radiant heat on a scored, glazed surface, the braise works by
liquid: it equalises salt, softens the cured muscle, and produces a cooking liquor that becomes the
gravy. Ham takes to it well because curing has already done the tenderising that other braising
cuts need hours of collagen breakdown for — which is also why the times here (1–3.5 hours) are
shorter than a pork shoulder's.

None of the five fetched sources publishes an internal temperature: every one uses time plus a
tenderness cue, and the two German sources give none at all beyond "turn it now and then".

**Tradition note:** all five are British or German, i.e. one culinary group. The leaf records that
plainly instead of treating five pages as five traditions.

## Source recipes

### 1. Glazed Gammon — James Mackenzie
**Inspired by**: Great British Chefs; https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/glazed-gammon-recipe (fetched 2026-10-24). UK; raw cured boned and rolled gammon.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 24 hours soaking · **Cook**: 45–60 minutes simmer plus about 20 minutes in the oven · **Rest**: cools in the liquid

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg boned and rolled gammon
- 1 litre scrumpy cider; 1 litre apple juice
- 2 star anise; 1 cinnamon stick; 2 bay leaves; 2 cloves; 150 g sugar
- Glaze: 2 tbsp English mustard; 1 tbsp honey; 1 tbsp cider vinegar
- Waldorf salad, to serve

**Method**
1. Soak the gammon in water for 24 hours; drain.
2. Put it in a pan with the cider, half the apple juice and the spices; top up with water if needed.
3. Bring to the boil, skim, then simmer 45–60 minutes "until just cooked". Cool somewhat in the liquid.
4. Heat the oven to 190 °C. Mix the glaze, drain the gammon, brush with glaze and bake about 20 minutes, until glazed.
5. Reduce the sugar, remaining apple juice and a ladle of cooking liquid to a light caramel for the apple syrup; serve with the Waldorf salad.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until just cooked", judged through the simmer time.

### 2. Cider-sloshed Gammon — Abel & Cole
**Inspired by**: Abel & Cole; https://www.abelandcole.co.uk/recipes/cider-sloshed-gammon (fetched 2026-10-24). UK; raw cured whole gammon.

**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: overnight cooling optional · **Cook**: 1 hour simmer plus 30–45 minutes in the oven · **Rest**: 30 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 1–1.2 kg whole gammon
- 1 litre cider or apple juice
- 6 fresh bay leaves; 12 whole cloves; 1 cinnamon stick; 12 black peppercorns
- 1–2 tbsp honey; 1–2 tbsp plain white flour

**Method**
1. Rinse the gammon, cover with water in a lidded pot, bring to the boil, drain and rinse both gammon and pan.
2. Return the gammon with half the cider and enough water to cover, add half the bay and spices, bring to the boil and simmer 1 hour.
3. Cool, ideally overnight, then drain.
4. Heat the oven to 200 °C and score the fat. Put the gammon in a roasting dish with the remaining cider, bay and spices and roast 30–45 minutes, until the fat colours and crisps.
5. Baste with honey and return 5–10 minutes. Rest 30 minutes; strain the cider for gravy.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature; the cues are "the fat has coloured and crisped up" and a resting period.

### 3. Kassler Pot Roast (Kasslerbraten) — MyBestGermanRecipes
**Inspired by**: MyBestGermanRecipes; https://mybestgermanrecipes.com/kassler-roast-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Germany; smoked cured Kassler pork loin, one unsliced piece.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: about 2 hours · **Rest**: kept warm

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg Kassler (smoked cured pork loin), one piece
- 1–2 tbsp clarified butter; 4 medium onions
- 500 ml malt beer; 500 ml beef broth; salt and pepper
- Sauerkraut and potatoes, to serve

**Method**
1. Pat the meat dry, slice the onions and peel the potatoes.
2. Brown the meat on all sides in clarified butter and remove it.
3. Brown the onions in the same pan, return the meat and add the malt beer and broth.
4. Cover and simmer about 2 hours, turning now and then.
5. Keep the roast warm in a low oven, purée the juices for gravy and serve with sauerkraut and potatoes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature; the only control is the 2-hour covered simmer.

### 4. GDR Kasselerbraten (braised smoked pork) — Janine Henschel
**Inspired by**: DDR Museum; https://www.ddr-museum.de/en/blog/2016/gdr-recipe-pork-roast (fetched 2026-10-24). Germany; smoked pork back or neck.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: about 2 hours braised, or about 1.5 hours at 200 °C in the oven · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1–1.5 kg smoked pork, back or neck
- 2 large onions; clarified butter
- 500 ml meat broth; red wine or malt beer, as desired; salt and pepper
- Sauerkraut and potatoes, to serve

**Method**
1. Wash and dry the pork; chop the onions.
2. Brown the pork on all sides in clarified butter and remove it; fry the onions until translucent.
3. Add the stock and bring to the boil, with red wine or malt beer if wanted.
4. Return the meat, cover and "let the smoked pork stew for about 2 hours, turning the meat from time to time."
5. Purée the sauce, season and thicken; serve with sauerkraut and potatoes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. The page also gives an oven alternative — about 200 °C for about 1.5 hours.

### 5. Braised Ham Hock with Maple Mustard Glaze — Lucy Parissi
**Inspired by**: Supergolden Bakes; https://www.supergoldenbakes.com/braised-ham-hock/ (fetched 2026-10-24). UK; cured smoked or unsmoked gammon hocks (a fresh hock also works per the notes).

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2 hours soaking · **Cook**: 3–3.5 hours in the oven, or 2.5–3 hours on the stove, or 8 hours low in a slow cooker · **Rest**: 5 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 2 large ham/gammon hocks, about 1.2–1.5 kg each
- 2 carrots; 1 onion; 2 celery stalks; 3 garlic cloves
- 8 whole cloves; 6 allspice berries; 2 tsp black peppercorns; 2 bay leaves
- 1 litre cider or stock; water to almost cover
- Glaze: 3 tbsp wholegrain mustard; 3 tbsp maple syrup; 3 tbsp light brown sugar; 1 tbsp cider vinegar; 1 tbsp bourbon (optional)

**Method**
1. Soak the hocks in cold water in the fridge 2 hours (overnight for traditionally cured hocks); drain and pat dry.
2. Put the hocks, vegetables, aromatics and liquid in a Dutch oven or pot, with water to almost cover.
3. Oven: bring to a simmer, cover and braise at 325 °F/160 °C for 3–3.5 hours. Stovetop: simmer covered 2.5–3 hours, turning once.
4. Check the tenderness cue, lift out the hocks, cool 10–15 minutes and peel away the rind.
5. Strain and reduce the braising liquid with the vegetables for gravy.
6. Simmer the glaze ingredients 3–5 minutes until syrupy; brush over the hocks and roast at 425 °F/220 °C for 10–15 minutes, glazing again halfway. Rest 5 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: the hocks are ready when "a fork slides in with no resistance."

## Evidence limits

Five fetched sources, all British or German (one culinary group). **No source publishes an internal
temperature** — braised-ham practice in this set is entirely time-and-fork. The two German recipes
are smoked cured loin rather than leg, so they document the method's behaviour on cured pork
generally rather than on a whole ham.

## What makes this method special

The braise is the only ham method where the cooking liquid is a product in its own right: cider,
beer or stock takes up salt and cured flavour and becomes gravy, which is why British and German
practice both build the meal around it. It also handles the cut's awkward formats — hocks, bone-in
pieces, joints too salty to roast — better than an oven does, because the liquid equalises salt
while it cooks. What it cannot do is produce the lacquered crust that defines baked ham, so four of
the five sources add a hot oven or grill finish.
