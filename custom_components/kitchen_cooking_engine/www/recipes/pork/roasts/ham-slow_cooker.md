## Cut profile

The slow cooker is the modern British answer to a whole gammon joint. A 1.2–2 kg cured, rolled
joint goes into a crock with water, cider or cola and cooks 4–8 hours at low power; the long moist
heat draws out salt, keeps the dense cured muscle tender, and leaves the joint ready for a fast
glaze under the grill or in a hot oven. The method exists because cured ham is already tender in a
way raw pork leg is not — there are no collagen problems to solve, only an even heat-through — so
the slow cooker's weakness (no browning) can be repaired at the end in 7–15 minutes.

Two of the four fetched sources give an internal target: 68 °C/155 °F and 71 °C/160 °F. Both are
above the 63 °C USDA line for whole-muscle pork and inside the Swedish 72 °C practice recorded in
[ham-oven_roast.md](./ham-oven_roast.md), which makes the slow cooker the cut's best-documented
method for raw/cured joints after the oven.

**Tradition note:** all four sources are British gammon recipes. Their agreement is therefore not
cross-tradition evidence, and this is recorded as a limitation rather than presented as consensus.

## Source recipes

### 1. Slow Cooker Honey Roast Ham — Justine Pattison
**Inspired by**: BBC Food; https://www.bbc.co.uk/food/recipes/slow-cooker_roast_ham_25878 (fetched 2026-10-24). UK; raw cured gammon, smoked or unsmoked.

**Serves**: 6–8 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 6–8 hours on low plus 12–15 minutes in the oven · **Rest**: 10 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 1.6 kg boneless, rolled smoked or unsmoked gammon
- 1 onion, peeled and quartered
- 1 tbsp English mustard; 1 tbsp clear honey; 2 tbsp demerara sugar

**Method**
1. Put the gammon, onion and 100 ml water in the slow cooker; cover and cook on low 6–8 hours, "or until thoroughly cooked."
2. Lift out the gammon and leave 10 minutes, until cool enough to handle.
3. Heat the oven to 220 °C (200 °C fan). Remove the string and rind, leaving a thin fat layer, and score the fat.
4. Put the ham fat-side up in a foil-lined tin, mix the mustard, honey and sugar, spread over the fat.
5. Bake 12–15 minutes, until the fat is browned. Serve warm or cold.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature. Published cue: "until thoroughly cooked", with browning as the finish test.

### 2. Slow Cooker Cola Ham — Ella Tarn
**Inspired by**: BBC Food; https://www.bbc.co.uk/food/recipes/slow_cooker_cola_ham_08734 (fetched 2026-10-24). UK; raw cured unsmoked gammon.

**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 7 hours on high plus 7–8 minutes under the grill · **Rest**: 20 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 2 kg unsmoked boneless gammon joint
- 1 onion, roughly chopped
- 2 litres cola
- 50 g runny honey; 1 tbsp wholegrain mustard

**Method**
1. Put the gammon and onion in the slow cooker and add enough cola to just cover the ham. Cover and cook on high 7 hours.
2. Remove the ham and discard the onion and cola. Heat the grill to high; remove the skin, leave the fat and score it in diamonds.
3. Mix the honey and mustard and brush half over the fat. Grill 7–8 minutes, basting with the rest, until golden and sticky.
4. Rest loosely covered with foil 20 minutes before carving.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step and no internal temperature; the source's controls are 7 hours on high and a sticky glaze.

### 3. Slow Cooker Gammon in Cider — Sarah James
**Inspired by**: Tales From The Kitchen Shed; https://www.talesfromthekitchenshed.com/2023/10/slow-cooker-gammon-in-cider/ (fetched 2026-10-24). UK; raw cured gammon, boneless and unsmoked preferred.

**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 10 minutes · **Cook**: 4–6 hours on high or 6–8 hours on low, plus 30 minutes in the oven · **Rest**: 15 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 1 onion; 1 carrot; 1 celery stick
- 2 kg boneless unsmoked gammon joint
- 2 tsp black peppercorns; 2 bay leaves; ½ tsp allspice berries; 1 cm cinnamon stick
- 500 ml cider; 1 litre water
- Glaze: 2 tbsp French mustard; 4 tbsp maple syrup

**Method**
1. Cut the vegetables into large pieces and lay them in the slow cooker as a trivet; set the gammon on top.
2. Add the spices, cider and water. Cover and cook on high 4–6 hours or low 6–8 hours.
3. Remove the ham and vegetables; cool the ham 10–15 minutes. Reserve the braising liquid if wanted.
4. Heat the oven to 200 °C (180 °C fan). Remove the rind, leave about 1 cm of fat and score it in diamonds.
5. Set the ham on the vegetables in a foil-lined tray, brush with half the glaze, roast 15 minutes, glaze again and roast another 15 minutes.
6. Rest loosely covered 15 minutes before carving.

**Probe and endpoint**: the page states "The temperature should be at least 71 °C / 160 °F" and gives a knife cue — the blade "comes out easily without resistance." No probe placement is described.

### 4. Slow Cooker Gammon (Honey Glazed Ham) — Julia Frey
**Inspired by**: Vikalinka; https://vikalinka.com/slow-cooker-gammon-honey-glazed-ham/ (fetched 2026-10-24). UK-based author; raw cured smoked gammon.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 5 minutes · **Cook**: 4–5 hours on high or 6–7 hours on low, plus 15 minutes in the oven · **Rest**: 10 minutes

**Ingredients**
- 1.2 kg boneless smoked gammon joint
- 2 bay leaves; 1 tsp black peppercorns
- 440 ml dry apple cider; 500 ml water
- Glaze: 2 tbsp honey; 1 tbsp wholegrain mustard; 1 tbsp brown sugar

**Method**
1. Put the cider, water, bay, peppercorns and gammon in the slow cooker, adding water so the liquid mostly covers the joint.
2. Cook on high 4–5 hours or low 6–7 hours. "Check the centre with an instant-read thermometer."
3. Remove the ham, cool slightly. Heat the oven to 200 °C and mix the glaze.
4. Remove the string and skin, leave a thin fat layer, score in diamonds and brush with glaze.
5. Bake on a rack over a roasting pan 15 minutes, until the glaze caramelises. Rest at least 10 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: explicit target — "It should read 68C/155F", checked with an instant-read thermometer in the centre of the joint.

## Evidence limits

Four fetched sources, all British gammon recipes, so the agreement between them reflects one
tradition rather than independent confirmation. Two publish an internal target (68 °C/155 °F,
71 °C/160 °F); the other two rely on time and a physical cue. No source describes where in the joint
the probe should sit, and none covers a bone-in ham in a slow cooker.

## What makes this method special

Ham is one of the few cuts where a slow cooker is a genuine technique rather than a compromise: the
meat is already cured and tender, so the appliance's even, moist heat does what a long oven roast
does without the risk of drying the outside. The trade is crust — every source finishes the joint
under a grill or in a 200–220 °C oven for 7–30 minutes to caramelise a glaze, which is the step that
makes the result recognisably baked ham. Salt is the other variable: two sources cook the joint in
plain water or cola specifically to pull salt out, so the liquid is doing chemistry as well as heat
transfer.

## Summary

Slow-cooking a gammon joint is a 4–8 hour moist cook at low power followed by a fast glaze; two of
four British sources give an internal target of 68–71 °C. The method is well documented but
single-tradition, and no source covers a bone-in joint.
