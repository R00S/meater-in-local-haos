## Cut profile

The air fryer is a compact high-velocity convection oven, and for bone-in, skin-on thighs
that means rapid rendering of the skin from above while the basket's confined airflow browns
the sides. The fetched recipes all exploit the same cut-specific behaviour: the bone-in thigh
is thick enough to need a two-stage cook (skin down first to render, then skin up to crisp),
and dark meat tolerates the aggressive finishing heat that would dry out breast. Basket
arrangement and preheating vary by source, and so — dramatically — does the stated endpoint:
the four fetched sources range from "juices run clear" with no temperature at all to a
195 °F (91 °C) target aimed at tandoori-style tender texture.

## Source recipes

### 1. Emily Teel (reviewed by Suzy Karadsheh) / The Mediterranean Dish — Air Fryer Chicken Thighs with Ras el Hanout
**Inspired by**: https://www.themediterraneandish.com/air-fryer-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). The Mediterranean Dish; North African / Moroccan influence (ras el hanout). The page specifies **2 pounds (4 to 6) bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs**; boneless, skinless may be substituted but are not the specified version.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated (marinade ≥15 min) · **Cook**: 10 min + 6–8 min (+ optional 2–3 min) · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 pounds (4 to 6) bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
- 1 teaspoon extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 large lemon, zested and juiced (about 3 tablespoons juice)
- 2 garlic cloves, grated
- 1 tablespoon ras el hanout
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper

**Method**
1. In a large bowl, combine the olive oil, lemon zest and juice, grated garlic, ras el hanout, salt and pepper.
2. Pat the thighs dry. Add them one at a time, turning to coat. Marinate at least 15 minutes, or up to 24 hours in the refrigerator.
3. Preheat the air fryer to **380°F for 5 minutes**.
4. Arrange the thighs **skin-side down in a single layer**, leaving space between them. Cook **10 minutes**.
5. Turn the thighs **skin-side up** and cook another **6–8 minutes**, until the internal temperature reaches **165°F**.
6. Optional: raise the air fryer to **400°F** and cook **2–3 minutes** more for extra-crisp skin.
7. Transfer to a plate and rest **5 minutes** before serving.

**Probe and endpoint**: insert an instant-read thermometer into the thickest part of the thigh, near the bone but not touching it — **Pull at 74°C** (165°F). (The page's tips also give 370°F for air fryers that run hot and 400°F for models that run cool.)
Final serving temperature: **74°C / 165°F** (the source's stated endpoint; rest 5 minutes, no carryover figure given).

### 2. umejan / Cookpad Japan — ノンフライヤーでローストチキンレッグ (Roast chicken leg in a non-fryer / air fryer)
**Inspired by**: https://cookpad.com/jp/recipes/25294042 (fetched 2026-10-24). umejan, Cookpad Japan; Japanese home cooking. The ingredient is **骨付き鶏もも肉** (bone-in chicken thigh/leg); the method places it skin-side up.

**Serves**: 1 · **Prep**: not stated (overnight refrigeration + 1 h tempering) · **Cook**: 20 min (+3–5 min if needed) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 bone-in chicken thigh/leg (骨付き鶏もも肉)
- 1 potato
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- Grated garlic, to taste
- Herb salt, to taste
- Dried rosemary, as desired

**Method**
1. Cut along the bone of the bone-in meat and open it out.
2. Put all ingredients except the potato in a plastic bag. Massage well so the seasoning coats the whole piece of meat.
3. Press out the air, tie the bag, and refrigerate overnight. Take the chicken out 1 hour before cooking to return it to room temperature.
4. Wash the potato, remove any sprouts and cut it into four pieces (peeling is not necessary).
5. Line the air-fryer basket with cooking paper and place the chicken **skin-side up**. Sprinkle rosemary on the meat if desired.
6. Put the potato in the bag that held the chicken, massage lightly to coat it, and arrange the potato around the meat; sprinkle lightly with herb salt.
7. Lift the ingredients out with the cooking paper. Return the basket to the air fryer and preheat at **200°C for 3 minutes**.
8. After preheating, return the ingredients with the cooking paper to the basket. Cook at **200°C for 20 minutes**; if not done, cook another 3–5 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated**.

### 3. Samuel Goldsmith / BBC Good Food — Air fryer chicken thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.bbcgoodfood.com/recipes/air-fryer-chicken-thighs (recipe text verified via the accessible BBC Good Food Middle East mirror https://www.bbcgoodfoodme.com/recipes/air-fryer-chicken-thighs/, fetched 2026-10-24; the main .com page exposes only a subscription notice). Samuel Goldsmith, BBC Good Food; UK tradition. Ingredient line: **"4 chicken thighs, bone in"**.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2 min · **Cook**: 25 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- 1/2 teaspoon mixed herbs
- 1/2 teaspoon garlic granules (optional)
- 4 chicken thighs, bone in
- 1 teaspoon olive oil
- 1/2 teaspoon salt (specified in the method)
- 1/2 teaspoon ground black pepper (specified in the method)

**Method**
1. Combine the paprika, herbs and garlic granules (if using) with the salt and black pepper. Scatter the spice mixture onto a plate. Rub the thighs with oil, then coat them in the spice mix.
2. Put the thighs in the air-fryer basket **skin-side down** and cook **10 minutes at 180C**. Turn them over and cook a further **10–15 minutes**, until cooked through and the skin is crispy.
3. Pierce the thickest part with a knife to check that the juices run clear. Remove the thighs from the air fryer immediately to stop the skin softening.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; the doneness check is visual — pierce the thickest part, juices run clear. **no internal temperature stated**.

### 4. Debby / A Feast for the Eyes — Air Fryer Tandoori Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://afeastfortheeyes.net/2025/06/air-fryer-tandoori-chicken.html (fetched 2026-10-24). Debby, A Feast for the Eyes; Indian tandoori tradition applied to the air fryer. The page describes **bone-in, skin-on thighs**; the recipe card specifies four 5-ounce bone-in thighs, trimmed.

**Serves**: 2 (4 thighs) · **Prep**: 5 min (+10 min marinating) · **Cook**: 20–30 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tablespoon grated fresh ginger
- 1 1/2 teaspoons garam masala
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon chili powder
- 1 teaspoon vegetable oil
- 1/2 teaspoon salt total (1/4 teaspoon in the spice mixture + salt to taste for the sauce)
- 1/2 teaspoon pepper total (1/4 teaspoon in the spice mixture + pepper to taste for the sauce)
- 1/2 cup plain whole-milk yogurt, divided
- 4 teaspoons lime juice, divided
- 4 bone-in chicken thighs, 5 ounces each, trimmed

**Method**
1. Combine the garlic, ginger, garam masala, cumin, chili powder, oil, 1/4 teaspoon salt and 1/4 teaspoon pepper. Microwave about 30 seconds, until fragrant.
2. Cool slightly. Stir in 1/4 cup yogurt and 1 tablespoon lime juice.
3. Pat the chicken dry. Poke the skin side 10–15 times with a metal skewer.
4. Toss the chicken in the yogurt-spice mixture and marinate 10 minutes.
5. Mix the remaining 1/4 cup yogurt and 1 teaspoon lime juice; season with salt and pepper to taste and set aside.
6. Remove the chicken from the marinade, letting excess drip off. Arrange **skin-side down**, evenly spaced, in the air-fryer basket (the page's air fryer was preheated about 10 minutes).
7. Set the air fryer to **400°F**. Cook until browned and the skin looks crisp, about 10–15 minutes, depending on the air fryer.
8. Carefully flip with tongs. Cook 10–15 minutes longer, until browned and crisp and the thickest part registers **195°F** (rotate the chicken halfway through). Stated total cook range 20–30 minutes.
9. Transfer to a platter, tent loosely with foil and rest 5 minutes. Serve with the reserved yogurt-lime sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: check the thickest part with a meat thermometer — **Pull at 91°C** (195°F), the source's stated air-fryer doneness target (the page's separate oven method gives 175°F for legs/thighs). Note: 195 °F sits *above* the cut file's `dark_meat_optimal` band maximum (85 °C / 185 °F) and is recorded as published, not adjusted.
Final serving temperature: **91°C / 195°F** (the source's stated endpoint; rest 5 minutes tented, no carryover figure given).

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| North African / Moroccan (The Mediterranean Dish) | "internal temperature reaches 165°F" | 74 °C / 165 °F |
| Japanese (Cookpad JP / umejan) | Not stated — 200 °C, 20 min + visual check | Not stated |
| UK (BBC Good Food / Samuel Goldsmith) | "juices run clear" when pierced | Not stated |
| Indian tandoori (A Feast for the Eyes) | "thickest part registers 195°F" | 91 °C / 195 °F |

The full observed range across the fetched sources is 74–91 °C (165–195 °F) where numbers are
stated, with two of four sources silent (time and visual endpoints). The two numeric sources
diverge sharply and the divergence is the finding: the Moroccan-seasoned source pulls at the
USDA safe minimum (`safe` band), while the tandoori-style source deliberately overshoots the
`dark_meat_optimal` band's 85 °C / 185 °F ceiling to reach 91 °C / 195 °F — a texture goal
(tandoori-style tender, fully rendered dark meat) rather than a safety claim. The Japanese and
British sources show the older visual convention (clear juices, crisp skin) that predates
home-probe use. The doneness key best representing the broadest common use remains
`dark_meat_optimal` (target 79 °C / 175 °F), which brackets the USDA floor below and sits
inside the span between the two numeric sources; note that the strong tandoori tradition sits
outside that band on the high side.

## What makes this method special

The air fryer's confined high-velocity airflow renders skin-on thigh skin far faster than a
conventional oven — the sources cook at 180–200 °C (360–400 °F) equivalent and get crisp skin
in 20–30 minutes — while the basket's small volume means radiant reflection also browns the
sides. Every fetched source flips the thigh mid-cook (skin down first in three of four), which
protects the skin from scorching while the bone-side heats through. The cut's tolerance for
high finishing heat is why the tandoori source can target 195 °F without drying: the collagen
and intramuscular fat of the bone-in thigh keep it succulent well past the breast-drying
threshold. The main failure mode the sources guard against is crowding (single layer, space
between pieces) and softening skin after cooking (remove immediately).

## Summary

Air-frying bone-in thighs delivers oven-roast-quality crisp skin in half the time, using a skin-down-to-skin-up flip and high heat the dark meat absorbs without drying. It suits the cut exceptionally well — the fetched sources treat it as the convenient weeknight equivalent of roasting. The temperature record is the collection's most divergent: 165 °F and 195 °F targets sit far apart, so cooks must choose their texture goal deliberately.
