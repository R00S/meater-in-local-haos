# Chicken Thigh (Bone-in) × Boil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Boiling bone-in thighs appears in recipe titles and instructions, but many soup recipes bring liquid to a boil and then reduce to a simmer; those are better categorized as simmering. One named-author chicken-stock soup explicitly directs a “simmering rolling boil” for a long cook, while a second bone-in thigh recipe says “allow to boil” after reducing the heat without describing a rolling boil. This evidence supports recording the method boundary, not treating every soup as a sustained-boil recipe.

## Source recipes and method evidence

### 1. Ally / Ally’s Kitchen — Bone Stock Chicken Soup
**Inspired by**: https://allyskitchen.com/bone-stock-chicken-soup/ (fetched 2026-10-24). American home cooking; ingredient line: “3 lbs. chicken quarters, thighs and legs” (mixed bone-in parts, includes thighs).

**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 1.5 h plus 30 min bone cook and 30–45 min noodles · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 lb chicken quarters, thighs and legs
- 32 oz chicken broth plus 4 cups water
- 1 onion, cubed; 3 celery stalks with leaves; 3 large carrots
- 2 tsp sea salt; 2 tsp coarse ground pepper
- 1 (12 oz) box cream of chicken soup; 3 cups medium egg noodles
- 2 tbsp dried parsley or 1/4 cup fresh chopped parsley

**Method**
1. Put chicken, broth, water, onion, celery, carrots, salt and pepper in a stock pot; cover and bring to a boil.
2. Reduce heat to medium-high or medium, “keeping a simmering rolling boil”; leave lid slightly askew. Cook about 1.5 hours.
3. Remove chicken, pull meat from bones, return bones to pot and cook another 30 minutes; remove bones.
4. Strain vegetables, mash them and return to stock. Add cream of chicken soup and noodles; cover and cook 30–45 minutes until noodles are tender. Return desired chicken meat, add parsley and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The source uses “simmering rolling boil” and meat tender enough to shred.

### 2. Jessica Burgess / Fantabulosity — How to Boil Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://fantabulosity.com/how-to-boil-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). American method page. Exact cut described in method: “bone-in chicken thighs”; the page demonstrates with bone-in thighs and optional vegetables.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 20 min for thawed; 40–45 min for frozen · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- Bone-in chicken thighs, quantity not stated in extracted method
- Water to cover by about 1 inch
- Salt and black pepper
- Optional: 3 carrots, 1 tbsp minced garlic, 3 rosemary sprigs

**Method**
1. Put thighs in a large pot; season with salt and black pepper. Add optional carrots, garlic and rosemary.
2. Add water to about 1 inch above the chicken; heat on high until boiling.
3. When boiling, reduce heat to medium, cover and “allow to boil for 20 minutes,” or until the thickest part reaches **165°F**. Frozen thighs may take 40–45 minutes to reach the same stated internal temperature.
4. Remove from water and serve or use in another recipe. Optional broil finish is a separate step for browning skin.

**Probe and endpoint**: thermometer in the thickest part; source says **165°F internal**. It does not specify whether reduced-medium heat means a sustained rolling boil; boil intensity is ambiguous.

### 3. Sarah Hill / Real Food with Sarah — Bone-In Chicken Soup (boundary comparison)
**Inspired by**: https://www.realfoodwithsarah.com/orzo-chicken-soup-with-bone-in-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). American home cooking; ingredient line says “2 pounds bone in chicken thighs, (typically 4 thighs).” This recipe is **not counted as a boil source**; included to document the common boil-then-simmer sequence.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 40 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 2 lb bone-in chicken thighs; 1 yellow onion; 2 carrots; 2 celery stalks; 1 zucchini; 2 garlic cloves
- 1/2 tsp each salt, black pepper, turmeric and ginger; 1 bay leaf; thyme and rosemary
- 4 cups broth; 3/4 cup orzo; juice of 1/2 lemon

**Method**
1. Brown thighs in oil 2–3 minutes per side; remove and remove skin.
2. Soften vegetables; add spices, chicken and broth.
3. Cover, bring to a boil, then reduce heat and simmer about 20 minutes.
4. Remove chicken, shred, and return with orzo; simmer until pasta cooks. Finish with lemon.

**Probe and endpoint**: **no internal temperature stated**; page's method explicitly transitions from boil to simmer.

## Method disposition

The fetched evidence shows that sustained boil is less clearly distinct than simmer/poach for this cut. The Ally recipe uses “simmering rolling boil,” and Fantabulosity says “allow to boil” after reducing to medium, but neither defines the actual liquid agitation precisely; common soup instructions explicitly switch to simmer. Keep `boil` as a documented lead, not a confidently established distinct method across traditions. No internal temperature is inferred.

## Summary

Boiled chicken thighs have named-author recipe evidence, but the distinction from simmering is ambiguous in current pages. A soup recipe's initial boil is not by itself evidence of sustained boiling; the specific phrase “simmering rolling boil” and one temperature-bearing tutorial are recorded as published. **PARTIAL RESEARCH**: one true soup source, one method page with ambiguous reduced-heat boil, and a comparison source excluded from the boil count.
