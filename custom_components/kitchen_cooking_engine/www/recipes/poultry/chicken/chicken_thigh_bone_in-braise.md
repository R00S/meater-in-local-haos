## Cut profile

Braising is the bone-in thigh's home method across the world's cuisines: sear the skin to build
fond and fat-render, then finish the piece gently in a measured amount of liquid so the thigh's
collagen converts to gelatin without the muscle drying. The bone-in, skin-on format is what
makes these dishes work — the femur holds the thigh together through 20–60 minutes of moist
heat, and the skin (softened by the braise in most traditions) still contributes body to the
sauce. All four fetched sources use the same architecture — sear, aromatics, deglaze, covered
finish — while diverging in cuisine and in whether anyone measures the endpoint: two state
internal temperatures (both US-page Caribbean/African-diaspora sources) and two cook by time
and feel alone.

## Source recipes

### 1. Audrey Le Goff / Pardon Your French — Braised Chicken Thighs with Garlic and Onion
**Inspired by**: https://www.pardonyourfrench.com/braised-chicken-thighs-with-garlic-and-onion/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Audrey Le Goff, Pardon Your French; French tradition. The page specifies **"6 Chicken thighs, skin-on, bone-in."**

**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 10 min (+2 h salting) · **Cook**: 55 min · **Rest**: not stated (seared chicken rests under foil while the aromatics cook)

**Ingredients**
- 6 chicken thighs, skin-on, bone-in
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp fresh ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp (15ml) extra virgin olive oil
- 7–8 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 tbsp (28.5g) unsalted butter
- 1 large onion, peeled and sliced in half-rounds
- 6 garlic cloves, peeled (3 diced, 3 left whole)
- 1/3 cup dry white wine

**Method**
1. Two hours before cooking, rinse the chicken thighs and pat them dry. Season on both sides with salt and pepper; set aside at room temperature.
2. Preheat the oven to **350F (180C)**, rack in the middle.
3. Heat an oven-safe skillet over **medium** heat with the olive oil. When sizzling, place the chicken **skin-side down** and add the thyme sprigs. Cook **uncovered 8–10 minutes**, until golden to golden brown. Flip and cook 6–7 minutes more. Transfer the chicken and thyme to a plate and cover with foil; leave the drippings in the pan.
4. Add the butter. When foaming, add the onion and garlic. Cook about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion is lightly caramelized.
5. Nestle the chicken back on top of the onion. Pour in the white wine and transfer the pan to the oven for **30 minutes, uncovered**. Serve over pasta or rice.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is 30 minutes in the oven until done.

### 2. Hyosun Ro / Korean Bapsang — Jjimdak (Korean Braised Chicken)
**Inspired by**: https://www.koreanbapsang.com/jjimdakdakjjim-korean-style-braised/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Hyosun Ro, Korean Bapsang; Korean tradition (Andong jjimdak). The page uses a whole chicken cut up (2.5–3 pounds of cut pieces) and notes pre-cut parts may be used; bone-in pieces are the format.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 medium-sized chicken, cut up (2.5 to 3 pounds of cut pieces)
- 3 ounces sweet potato starch noodles (dangmyeon, 당면)
- 2 medium white or yukon gold potatoes (about 10 ounces)
- 1 medium carrot
- 1/2 medium onion
- 3–4 mushroom caps (rehydrated dried shiitake, or fresh shiitake, white, or baby bella)
- 2 scallions
- 2–3 dried whole red chili peppers (4 to 5 small ones)
- 1–2 fresh chili peppers or jalapeños (optional)
- 2 tablespoons minced garlic
- 1 teaspoon grated ginger

*Braising liquid*
- 1/2 cup soy sauce
- 2 tablespoons oyster sauce (or more soy sauce)
- 2 tablespoons rice wine (mirim or mirin)
- 2 tablespoons dark brown sugar (or regular sugar)
- 3–4 tablespoons corn syrup, Korean jocheong (rice syrup), Oligodang (oligo syrup), or 2–3 tablespoons honey, adjusted to taste
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 tablespoon sesame oil
- 1 teaspoon sesame seeds
- 3 cups water (added separately to the chicken)

**Method**
1. Soak the starch noodles in warm water about 30 minutes while preparing the other ingredients. Drain well before using.
2. Clean the chicken and cut it into small pieces. Trim off excess fat.
3. Cut the potatoes into chunks (about 1 1/2 inch) and soak them in water. Cut the carrot, mushrooms and onion into bite-sized pieces.
4. Put the chicken in a large pot and pour the 3 cups water over it (reduce the water if not using noodles). Add the braising liquid ingredients **except the sesame oil and sesame seeds**.
5. Bring to a boil over **high heat, uncovered**, and continue boiling about 10 minutes. Skim off the foam.
6. Add the potatoes, carrot, mushrooms, onion, dried red chili peppers, garlic and ginger. Cover and cook **8–10 minutes**.
7. Gently mix in the fresh chili peppers (or jalapeños), scallions and drained noodles. Cook **uncovered** another **3 minutes**. Stir in the sesame oil and garnish with sesame seeds.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is the timed boil-and-simmer sequence.

### 3. Imma / Immaculate Bites — Jamaican Brown Stew Chicken
**Inspired by**: https://www.africanbites.com/jamaican-browned-chicken-stew/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Imma, Immaculate Bites; Jamaican/Caribbean tradition (the page labels the recipe Caribbean). The page uses "1 3½–5 pound (1.75–2.5k) chicken, cut into 10 pieces" — bone-in pieces; it does not explicitly say bone-in, and the site's slow-cooker variant of the same dish explicitly calls for bone-in thighs.

**Serves**: 10 · **Prep**: 15 min (+1 h marinating) · **Cook**: 35 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
*Chicken marinade*
- 1 3½–5 pound (1.75–2.5k) chicken, cut into 10 pieces
- Salt and black pepper, to taste
- 1 teaspoon (2g) chicken bouillon powder
- 1–2 teaspoons (2–4g) minced ginger
- 1 teaspoon (5g) minced garlic
- 1/2 teaspoon (1g) white pepper
- 1 teaspoon (2g) fresh thyme (1/4–1/2 teaspoon dried)
- 1/2 teaspoon (2g) paprika
- 2 green onions, finely sliced

*Brown stew*
- 2 tablespoons (30ml) canola oil (more if needed)
- 1 medium onion, diced (about 1 1/4 cups)
- 2 teaspoons (10g) minced garlic
- 1 teaspoon (2g) fresh thyme (1/4–1/2 teaspoon dried)
- 1 teaspoon (5g) hot sauce (or chili sauce)
- 1 1/2 teaspoons (5g) paprika
- 1 tablespoon (12g) brown sugar
- 1–2 teaspoons (5–10ml) browning sauce
- 2–3 tablespoons (30–45g) ketchup
- 2 small bell peppers, sliced (red or green)
- 1–2 cups (236–470ml) chicken broth (or water)

**Method**
1. Pat the chicken dry. Season with salt and pepper; add the bouillon powder, ginger, garlic, white pepper, thyme, paprika and green onions. Mix to coat, cover, and marinate in the fridge about an hour or overnight.
2. Remove the excess marinade and reserve it. Heat a large pan with about 2 tablespoons oil. Brown the chicken **3–4 minutes** until golden; remove and set aside. Drain the excess oil, leaving 2–3 tablespoons.
3. Add the onion, garlic, thyme, hot sauce, sugar, browning sauce, ketchup, bell peppers and salt to taste. Stir **2–3 minutes** until the onions are translucent, then add the reserved marinade.
4. Deglaze with the chicken broth or water. Bring to a boil and return the browned chicken to the pan.
5. Cover and cook about **25 minutes**, until cooked through — the page specifies "no pink showing" — and the sauce thickens slightly. Adjust seasoning and sauce consistency.
6. Turn off the stove and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is "no pink showing" after ~25 minutes covered.

### 4. Imma / Immaculate Bites — Yassa Chicken (Poulet au Yassa)
**Inspired by**: https://www.africanbites.com/yassa-chickenpoulet-au-yassa/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Imma, Immaculate Bites; Senegalese tradition (the page describes yassa as originating in the Casamance region of Senegal). The page states: "Feel free to swap a whole chicken for bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs, breasts, or legs" — its skillet sear-plus-simmer method applies to bone-in thighs.

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 15 min (+2 h marinating) · **Cook**: 50 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2½–3 pounds (1–1½kg) skin-on chicken, cut into pieces (thighs, legs, or breast)
- Salt and black pepper, to taste (¾–1 teaspoon salt per pound is the page's average)
- 1 tablespoon (15g) grated ginger
- 4 cloves garlic, minced, divided (half for the chicken, half for the sauce)
- 1 tablespoon (4g) minced parsley (optional)
- 1 tablespoon (12g) chicken bouillon powder, divided
- 2 lemons (one for the chicken, one for the sauce)
- 1 tablespoon (15g) neutral-flavored cooking oil (canola, sunflower seed or avocado)
- 2 large white onions, thinly sliced
- 1–2 bay leaves
- 2 tablespoons (30g) Dijon mustard (whole-grain or stone-ground)
- 1 cup (240ml) chicken stock (or 1 cup water + 1 tsp bouillon powder)
- 1 Scotch bonnet pepper (or cayenne, adjusted to taste)
- 1 cup (120g) pitted whole green olives

**Method**
1. Trim excess fat from the chicken pieces and pat dry. Season with salt, pepper, ginger, garlic, parsley and bouillon powder. Squeeze the juice of one lemon over the chicken and mix well.
2. Put the chicken in a zip-lock bag or glass bowl. Marinate in the refrigerator 2 hours or up to overnight.
3. Drain the chicken, reserving the marinade for the sauce.
4. Preheat a frying pan and add the oil. Place the chicken **skin-side down and sear 5 minutes**; flip and sear another **5 minutes**. Remove and set aside. (The page gives grilling as an alternative; its skillet method is the pan-sear.)
5. Heat a saucepan or skillet over medium heat with cooking oil. Sauté the sliced onions and remaining garlic until the onions are translucent.
6. Add the bay leaves, mustard, broth, reserved marinade, Scotch bonnet and green olives. Adjust seasoning. Cook about **5 minutes**.
7. Squeeze in the juice of the second lemon. Add the chicken and bring to a **simmer over low heat**, spooning sauce over the chicken.
8. Cover and cook **18–20 minutes**, until the chicken reaches the stated internal temperature (an oven finish is noted as possible if the skillet is oven-proof, but no oven temperature or time is given).
9. Adjust seasoning, remove the bay leaves, arrange the chicken on a plate, drizzle with sauce and serve over rice or couscous.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step, but the page gives numeric doneness temperatures — **Pull at 74°C** (165°F) as the doneness temperature, and **75°C / 80°C (175°F)** "gives more tender results if using just thighs."
Final serving temperature: **74–80°C / 165–175°F** (as stated by the source; the higher target is explicitly the thighs-only preference).

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| French (Pardon Your French) | Not stated — 30 min oven finish "until done" | Not stated |
| Korean (Korean Bapsang, jjimdak) | Not stated — timed boil + covered simmer | Not stated |
| Jamaican (Immaculate Bites, brown stew) | "no pink showing" | Not stated |
| Senegalese (Immaculate Bites, yassa) | "165°F … 175°F gives more tender results if using just thighs" | 74 °C / 165 °F; **75–80 °C / 175 °F for thighs** |

The full observed range where temperatures are stated is 74–80 °C (165–175 °F), and the only
source that measures gives two targets: the USDA safe floor as the general doneness figure,
and 75–80 °C (175 °F) as the explicitly thigh-preferred, more-tender target. That single
stated preference maps precisely onto the cut file's `dark_meat_optimal` band (74–85 °C,
target 79 °C / 175 °F) — the braise method is where dark-meat collagen rendering is the
express goal, so this is the expected landing zone. The variation in the collection is mostly
non-numeric: the French and Korean sources trust a fixed braise time, and the Jamaican source
trusts "no pink showing", which is the older visual convention. What drives the variation is
that braised thighs are never the plated centrepiece the way grilled ones are — they are
sauce-carriers, so cooks track sauce reduction rather than meat temperature. On current
evidence `dark_meat_optimal` (79 °C / 175 °F) is the best representation for this method,
with the Senegalese source as its explicit published backing.

## What makes this method special

The braise gives the bone-in thigh the one thing dry heat cannot: a collagen-conversion window.
After the sear builds fond and renders skin fat, the covered moist finish holds the thigh near
simmer so its connective tissue melts into gelatin — the sauce gains body and the meat gains
the "more tender" texture the Senegalese source targets at 175 °F. The bone helps here too:
it moderates the temperature rise at the joint end, letting the whole thigh finish evenly in
liquid that would overcook a boneless piece. The cost is skin: braised skin softens, and the
fetched sources either accept that (French, Korean), brown hard first (Jamaican, Senegalese),
or build the sauce from the drippings so nothing is lost.

## Summary

Braising is the bone-in thigh's most natural match: sear for flavour, then let the thigh's collagen melt into the sauce over a gentle covered finish. The four fetched sources span French, Korean, Jamaican and Senegalese kitchens and agree on architecture while two of them explicitly prefer dark meat taken to 175 °F for tenderness. This is the method where the cut's optimal doneness is published rather than assumed — and the only one in this collection where a source states a thigh-specific temperature preference.
