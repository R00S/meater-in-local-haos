## Cut profile

Grilling a bone-in, skin-on thigh is a two-zone problem: the skin needs direct heat to render
and blister, but the thick bone-side muscle needs time and gentler heat to reach the centre
without charring the exterior. All four fetched sources solve it with structure — a hot side
for the initial skin-down sear and a cooler side (or a covered grill) for the finish, or a
whole-grill medium heat with frequent turning — and all four marinate or dry-brine first,
because the grill's dry convective heat punishes underseasoned dark meat. The bone-in format
is standard in grilling traditions worldwide because the femur keeps the thigh intact over the
fire and shields the joint meat from overcooking; the fetched sources span US barbecue, Indian
tandoori-style, Greek and Jamaican jerk treatment.

## Source recipes

### 1. Michael Symon / Food Network Magazine — Grilled Chicken Thighs with Blackberry BBQ Sauce
**Inspired by**: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/michael-symon/grilled-chicken-thighs-with-blackberry-bbq-sauce-5209285 (fetched 2026-10-24). Michael Symon, Food Network Magazine; US barbecue tradition. Ingredient line: **"8 skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs."**

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated (seasoned "several hours, preferably overnight") · **Cook**: 2 min hot side + 15–20 min cool side (sauce: 2 h) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 tablespoon ground coriander
- 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
- 1 tablespoon kosher salt
- 8 skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs
- Olive oil, for brushing (amount not stated)

*Blackberry BBQ sauce (1/2 recipe used)*
- 3 pints fresh blackberries
- 12 ounces dark beer
- 1 cup balsamic vinegar
- 1 cup red wine vinegar
- 1/2 cup packed light brown sugar
- 1 onion, sliced
- 1 clove garlic, minced
- 1 habanero pepper, slit
- 1 tablespoon ground chipotle chile powder
- 1 tablespoon finely ground coffee
- 1 tablespoon ground coriander
- 1 tablespoon ground cumin

**Method**
1. Combine the coriander, paprika and salt. Pat the thighs dry, season both sides, and refrigerate in a gallon-size zip-top bag for several hours, preferably overnight.
2. Prepare a **lump-charcoal grill with two heat zones: high and low**.
3. Brush the thighs with olive oil. Grill **skin-side down on the hot side, covered, for 2 minutes**.
4. Move them **skin-side up to the low side**. Cover and cook 15–20 minutes, until the stated internal temperature is reached. Baste occasionally with half the sauce during the final 10 minutes.
5. Remove from the grill and serve with the remaining sauce.
6. For the sauce: combine its ingredients in a large saucepan and cook over **medium-low** heat, stirring occasionally, for 2 hours; purée and strain.

**Probe and endpoint**: the source gives a numeric internal endpoint — **Pull at 71°C** (160°F) — quoted as published. Note this is **below** the USDA poultry minimum of 74 °C / 165 °F and is recorded as the source's divergence, not as guidance.
Final serving temperature: **71°C / 160°F** (as stated by the source; no rest or carryover figure given).

### 2. Melissa Gaman / Food Network Kitchen — Tandoori-Style Grilled Chicken
**Inspired by**: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/tandoori-style-grilled-chicken-12937932 (fetched 2026-10-24). Melissa Gaman for Food Network Kitchen; Indian tandoori tradition applied to the grill. Ingredient line: **"8 pieces skin-on, bone-in chicken drumsticks and/or small thighs (2 1/2 to 2 3/4 pounds)."**

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated (+8 h marinating) · **Cook**: 25–30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 1/2 cups whole-milk yogurt (not Greek)
- 4 teaspoons garam masala
- 1 tablespoon ground turmeric
- 3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice, plus lemon wedges for serving
- 2 tablespoons vegetable oil, plus more for the grill
- 1 tablespoon grated fresh ginger
- 1 clove garlic, grated
- Kosher salt and freshly ground pepper
- 8 pieces skin-on, bone-in chicken drumsticks and/or small thighs (2 1/2 to 2 3/4 pounds)
- 2 pieces naan
- 1/4 seedless cucumber, grated
- 3 tablespoons chopped fresh cilantro, plus leaves for topping

**Method**
1. Whisk 1 cup yogurt with the garam masala, turmeric, 2 tablespoons lemon juice, oil, ginger, garlic, 1/2 teaspoon salt and a few grinds of pepper. Add the chicken and rub to coat. Cover and refrigerate at least 8 hours or up to 1 day.
2. Preheat the grill to **medium** and lightly oil the grates.
3. Grill the chicken **skin-side down, turning occasionally**, until cooked through, **25–30 minutes**. Add the naan for the final 2–3 minutes to warm and lightly toast.
4. Mix the remaining 1/2 cup yogurt with the cucumber, cilantro and remaining 1 tablespoon lemon juice; season with salt and pepper. Serve the chicken with the sauce, naan, lemon wedges and cilantro leaves.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is "cooked through" after 25–30 minutes.

### 3. Food Network Kitchen — Grilled Chicken Thighs with Israeli Couscous Salad ("a Greek treatment")
**Inspired by**: https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/grilled-chicken-thighs-with-israeli-couscous-salad-3362752 (fetched 2026-10-24). Food Network Kitchen; the page describes the chicken as receiving "a Greek treatment." Ingredient line: **"6 large bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 2 pounds)."**

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 5 min (+5 min inactive) · **Cook**: 45 min · **Rest**: at least 5 min

**Ingredients**
*Chicken*
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for oiling the grill grates
- 1 tablespoon dried oregano
- 6 large bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 2 pounds)
- 3 large cloves garlic, grated
- 2 lemons
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper (quantity not stated)

*Couscous*
- 1/3 cup sliced almonds
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 2 cups Israeli couscous
- 2 teaspoons minced ginger
- 2 large cloves garlic, grated
- 1/4 cup golden raisins
- 2/3 cup crumbled feta
- 1/3 cup pitted kalamata olives, halved
- 1/3 cup roughly chopped fresh parsley
- (method adds: 2 1/4 cups water and 1 teaspoon salt for cooking the couscous)

**Method**
1. Put the olive oil, oregano, chicken and garlic in a large resealable bag. Finely grate the zest of 1 lemon into it and add the juice of both lemons. Seal, massage until coated, and marinate at room temperature while making the couscous.
2. Prepare the grill for **medium-high** heat.
3. Toast the almonds in a skillet over medium-high heat; set aside. Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a saucepan over medium heat; toast the couscous about 3 minutes, add the ginger and garlic and cook about 1 minute. Add 2 1/4 cups water, the raisins and 1 teaspoon salt. Bring to a boil, cover, reduce heat to low and cook about 12 minutes, until tender and the water is absorbed.
4. Remove the chicken from the marinade. Oil the grill grates, season the chicken liberally with salt and pepper, and grill **skin-side down about 3 minutes** until lightly charred and releasing from the grill. Rotate about 90 degrees and cook another 3 minutes; flip and cook 5 minutes more.
5. Cover the grill and cook about **10 minutes more**, until an instant-read thermometer in the deepest part of each thigh reads **165 degrees F**. Transfer to a platter and rest at least 5 minutes.
6. Finish the couscous with the remaining 3 tablespoons oil, almonds, feta, olives, parsley, 1/2 teaspoon salt and pepper. Add the zest of 1 lemon and the juice of both lemons; toss. Serve with the thighs.

**Probe and endpoint**: insert an instant-read thermometer into the deepest part of each thigh — **Pull at 74°C** (165°F).
Final serving temperature: **74°C / 165°F** (as stated by the source; rest at least 5 minutes, no carryover figure given).

### 4. Stephanie Kay / Stephanie Kay Nutrition — Grilled Jamaican Jerk Chicken
**Inspired by**: https://kaynutrition.com/grilled-jerk-chicken/ (fetched 2026-10-25). Stephanie Kay, Stephanie Kay Nutrition; Jamaican/Caribbean tradition (Group C). The author states that classic jerk "calls for bone-in, skin-on cuts of chicken as they provide the deepest and richest flavors." Ingredient line: **"8 pieces skin-on, bone-in, chicken thighs and drumsticks (about 2.5lbs)."**

**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 2 h (incl. 1–2 h marinating) · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 8 pieces skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs and drumsticks (about 2.5 lbs)
- 1 small onion, roughly chopped
- 2 scallions, ends removed and roughly chopped
- 2 cloves garlic
- 1–2 scotch bonnets or habanero peppers, stem and seeds removed
- 1 1/2 teaspoon ground allspice
- 1/2 tablespoon dried thyme
- 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
- 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
- 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1/2 teaspoon sea salt
- 3 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 lime, juiced
- Olive oil, for the grill
- Scallions and limes to serve

**Method**
1. Combine all the ingredients except the chicken in a blender or food processor and blend on high until a thick marinade forms.
2. Place the chicken pieces in a large bowl or bag, cover with marinade and refrigerate a minimum of 1–2 hours, preferably overnight.
3. Take the chicken out of the fridge at least 30 minutes before cooking so it comes to room temperature.
4. Preheat a grill to **medium-high heat** and lightly brush with oil to stop the chicken sticking.
5. Place the chicken **skin-side down** over **medium heat** and cook **25 to 35 minutes, turning often, until cooked through (internal temperature of 165°F)**.
6. Transfer to a plate and rest **10 minutes**. Dress with scallions and extra lime wedges to serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: the source states a numeric internal endpoint — **"until cooked through (internal temperature of 165°F)"** — **Pull at 74°C** (165°F).
Final serving temperature: **74°C / 165°F** (as stated by the source; 10-minute rest, no carryover figure given).

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| US barbecue (Michael Symon / Food Network) | "until the stated internal temperature is reached" — 160 °F | 71 °C / 160 °F |
| Indian tandoori-style (Food Network Kitchen) | "cooked through" — time-based, 25–30 min | Not stated |
| Greek treatment (Food Network Kitchen) | "instant-read … reads 165 degrees F" | 74 °C / 165 °F |
| Jamaican jerk (Stephanie Kay) | "cooked through (internal temperature of 165°F)" | 74 °C / 165 °F |

The full observed range across the fetched sources where a temperature is stated is
**71–74 °C (160–165 °F)**, with one source (Indian tandoori-style) silent on temperature and
relying on time. The variation is small in degrees but meaningful in policy: the US barbecue
source pulls at 71 °C / 160 °F — below the USDA poultry minimum — while the Greek-treatment and
Jamaican jerk sources both pull at exactly 74 °C / 165 °F, the safe line, with a probe (Greek) or
a stated target (Jamaican). Three of the four numeric targets land on the `safe` band and one sits
under it; none expresses the higher `dark_meat_optimal` (79 °C / 175 °F) that this collection's
braise, air-fryer and smoker sources show. The divergence here is grill craft — the barbecue source
assumes carryover and gives no rest instruction at all, while the Jamaican source rests 10 minutes
and still names 165 °F as the pull point — rather than texture philosophy. On current evidence
`safe` (74 °C / 165 °F) is the best-represented target for this method, but the barbecue tradition
sitting below it is a strong, deliberate practice worth noting.

## What makes this method special

Grilling is the only method that simultaneously subjects the skin to intense radiant and
convective heat while leaving the cook full control of heat zoning — which is exactly what a
bone-in thigh needs, since the skin-side wants 200 °C+ contact and the bone-side centre wants
gentle time. The fetched sources all use two-zone or covered structures, or a whole-grill medium
heat with frequent turning, to exploit this, and all marinate first (yogurt, lemon-herb, dry rub,
or a wet jerk paste of allspice and scotch bonnet) because the fire's dry heat concentrates
seasoning on the surface. Flare-ups from rendering subcutaneous fat are the cut-specific hazard,
which is why the sources sear briefly over the hot zone and finish covered over the cool one, or
turn often over a single medium fire. The Jamaican source adds a distinct variable: the jerk
tradition's pimento-wood smoke and its bone-in requirement are about the fat and marrow carrying
flavour into the meat, not just heat transfer.

## Summary

Grilling suits bone-in thighs well: the two-zone fire crisps the skin while the covered finish
walks the thick bone-side muscle to temperature. The fetched sources show a real disagreement at
the endpoint — US barbecue practice pulls at 160 °F, Greek-style and Jamaican jerk both at
165 °F, and the tandoori-style source trusts time entirely. The method's texture is the
collection's best after pan-frying, and the bone-in format is chosen deliberately in the jerk and
barbecue traditions for the fat and marrow flavour the bone carries.
