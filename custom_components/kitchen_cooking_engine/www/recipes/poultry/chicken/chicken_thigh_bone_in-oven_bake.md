# Chicken Thigh (Bone-in) × Oven Bake — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Oven-baked bone-in thighs use steady dry oven heat, usually on a sheet pan or rack, without a stovetop sear. Several sources add a brief broiler finish to crisp skin; that finishing step is noted but the main cook remains oven baking. Bone-in thighs cook longer than boneless pieces, and the collected recipes use either a thermometer endpoint or visual cues.

## Source recipes

### 1. Holly / Spend With Pennies — Crispy Baked Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.spendwithpennies.com/baked-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). US home cooking; source says it uses bone-in thighs, 6 oz each, and may optionally broil at the end.

**Ingredients**
- Bone-in chicken thighs; olive oil; poultry or Italian seasoning; salt to taste

**Method**
1. Pat thighs dry, drizzle with oil and season generously.
2. Bake at **425°F** on a rack if available until internal temperature reaches **165°F**; the page notes the recipe is for 6 oz thighs and larger pieces take longer.
3. Broil briefly afterward if desired to crisp the skin.

**Probe and endpoint**: the page specifies 165°F internal and recommends a meat thermometer; it does not give a precise probe-location sentence.

### 2. Nigella Lawson — Chicken, Sausage, Sage and Onion Traybake
**Inspired by**: https://www.nigella.com/recipes/chicken-sausage-sage-and-onion-traybake (fetched 2026-10-24). British recipe; the author explicitly says jointed chicken may be replaced with “10 thigh pieces, bone in and skin on.”

**Ingredients**
- 1 large onion; 125 ml olive oil; 2 tsp English mustard; 1 tbsp dried sage; black pepper
- 1 lemon; 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 kg jointed chicken (or 10 bone-in, skin-on thigh pieces); 12 sausages
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh sage (or chives/watercress) to serve

**Method**
1. Cut onion into eighths; combine in a bag with olive oil, mustard, dried sage, pepper, lemon juice and husks cut into eighths, and Worcestershire sauce. Add chicken and marinate overnight or up to 2 days.
2. Preheat oven to **220°C / 425°F** (200°C fan). Bring chicken to room temperature in marinade.
3. Arrange chicken skin-side up with marinade in a deep roasting tin; tuck sausages around it and sprinkle fresh sage over.
4. Bake **1 h 15 min**; turn sausages halfway through. The author advises checking earlier with a fan oven.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe instruction; **no internal temperature stated**.

### 3. Justin Winn / Real Simple Good — Crispy Baked Bone-In Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://realsimplegood.com/oven-baked-bone-in-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). US recipe; ingredient line says “3 lbs bone in chicken thighs, (about 6 thighs).”

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 30–40 min at 400°F (25–35 min at 425°F) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 3 lb bone-in chicken thighs (about 6); 2 tbsp olive oil
- Seasoning: 1 tsp garlic powder; 1 tsp smoked paprika; 1 tsp mustard powder; 1/2 tsp sea salt; 1/2 tsp thyme

**Method**
1. Preheat oven to 400°F and line a sheet pan or baking dish.
2. Mix seasoning. Pat thighs dry, coat with olive oil and season on both sides, leaving skin-side up.
3. Bake 30–40 minutes depending on size. The page states internal temperature should be at least 165°F; it says thighs may be cooked to 175–185°F for softer, more tender texture.
4. Optionally broil the last few minutes to crisp skin, watching carefully.

**Probe and endpoint**: source says at least 165°F internal and explicitly gives 175–185°F as a juicier texture option; probe placement not specified.

## Temperature consensus

The collected recipe pages state a 165°F safety endpoint or visual/time endpoints; Real Simple Good also publishes a 175–185°F texture preference for thighs. This is not a cross-tradition consensus: the temperature-bearing sources are US-based. The broiler finish in two recipes remains an optional secondary stage rather than the primary bake method.

## Summary

Oven baking bone-in thighs is documented by a 425°F sheet-pan recipe, a British traybake explicitly permitting bone-in thigh pieces, and a 400°F recipe that reports both the safe endpoint and a higher thigh-texture range. **PARTIAL RESEARCH**: only three sources, and only two source traditions, so the collection gate remains unmet.
