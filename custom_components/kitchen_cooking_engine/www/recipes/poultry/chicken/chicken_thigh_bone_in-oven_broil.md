# Chicken Thigh (Bone-in) × Oven Broil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Broiling places bone-in thighs close to a high radiant heat source, browning and crisping the skin without a prolonged oven roast. Because the skin can char before the thick joint meat finishes, the recipes use a set distance and turn the thighs or monitor internal temperature. The documented times and endpoints vary; internal targets are quoted only when the recipe page states them.

## Source recipes

### 1. Laura Denby / Kelley French / Taste of Home Test Kitchen — Crispy Garlic-Broiled Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.tasteofhome.com/recipes/crispy-garlic-broiled-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). US recipe by Kelley French; ingredient line: “8 bone-in chicken thighs (about 3 pounds).”

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 20 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1/3 cup melted butter; 1/4 cup reduced-sodium soy sauce; 7 minced garlic cloves; 1/2 tsp pepper
- 8 bone-in chicken thighs (about 3 lb)

**Method**
1. Mix butter, soy sauce, garlic and pepper; reserve 1/4 cup for basting. Coat chicken in remaining mixture.
2. Place chicken skin-side down on a broiler pan, **4–6 inches from heat**.
3. Broil 10–15 minutes per side, basting occasionally during the last 10 minutes, until a thermometer reads **170–175°F**.

**Probe and endpoint**: thermometer endpoint **170–175°F internal**; page gives no exact placement beyond checking temperature.

### 2. Deb / Deb’s Daily Dish — Marinated Broiled Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://debsdailydish.com/marinated-broiled-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). US recipe; card ingredient line says “4 bone-in, skin on chicken thighs (about 2 pounds).”

**Serves**: not stated in fetched text · **Prep**: 30 min minimum marinade · **Cook**: about 20 min (largest thighs may take 25 min) · **Rest**: about 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 2 lb)
- Juice of 1 lemon; 1/3 cup olive oil; 1/2 tsp salt; 1/2 tsp pepper
- 2 thyme sprigs; 2 sage leaves; 4 garlic cloves; 2 tsp granulated sugar or substitute; 1 tsp lemon pepper

**Method**
1. Mix marinade ingredients; coat chicken and refrigerate at least 30 minutes.
2. Drain and discard marinade; pat thighs dry. Preheat broiler and lightly oil an oven-safe skillet or broiler pan.
3. Place thighs skin-side down, 5–6 inches from broiler. Broil 10 minutes, rotate pan and flip; baste with pan juices.
4. Broil another 10 minutes; check largest thigh with thermometer for at least **165°F**. The author says large pieces may require about 25 minutes total.
5. Rest about 5 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: source says meat thermometer and **at least 165°F internal**, measured in largest thigh; no more precise placement given.

### 3. Brandon / Kitchen Konfidence — Broiled Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.kitchenkonfidence.com/2026/09/broiled-chicken-thighs-recipe (fetched 2026-10-24). US recipe. Ingredient line permits “6 chicken thighs (bone-in or boneless, skin-on or skinless, whatever you’ve got),” so bone-in is explicitly suitable but not required.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2 min · **Cook**: 16 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 6 chicken thighs (bone-in or boneless; skin-on or skinless)
- Olive oil; kosher salt; freshly ground black pepper; optional seasoning blend or marinade

**Method**
1. Place oven rack 5–6 inches below broiler and preheat to high. Line a rimmed sheet with foil.
2. Drizzle thighs with olive oil; season with salt and pepper and space on tray.
3. Broil until charred in spots and cooked through, internal temperature **165°F**, about 8 minutes per side.
4. Rest 5 minutes.

**Probe and endpoint**: internal endpoint 165°F; page does not state more precise probe placement.

## Temperature consensus

The three fetched US pages state endpoints of 165°F and 170–175°F; two use a thermometer and the third gives temperature as the cooked-through endpoint. All are from one national tradition, so this is not a cross-tradition consensus. The high-heat geometry and varying thigh sizes explain the source-reported time spread; no source temperature is modified or inferred.

## Summary

Oven broiling is documented as a separate top-heat method for bone-in thighs, with 165°F and 170–175°F endpoints published by US recipe sources.
