## Cut profile

Pan-frying a bone-in, skin-on thigh is a crust-building method: the skin goes into a hot pan
and renders its subcutaneous fat against direct conductive heat while the muscle above it
stays insulated. Because the femur slows heat penetration to the centre of the thickest
part, pure stovetop pan-frying to doneness is slow, and the fetched recipes all compensate
in some way — a brief covered steam, a covered finish in stock, or an oven finish. That
pattern is itself the finding for this cut: pan-frying bone-in thighs is typically a
sear-plus-gentle-finish method, not a full dry-heat cook. No fetched source measures the
pan temperature; they speak in heat levels and minutes per side.

## Source recipes

### 1. Daddy Lau / Made With Lau — Dad's Pan-Fried Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.madewithlau.com/recipes/pan-fried-chicken-thighs (fetched 2026-10-24). Daddy Lau, Made With Lau; Chinese-American (Hong Kong diner-style). The page states deboning and skin-trimming are optional — bone-in is permitted — and instructs slicing thicker meat so it cooks evenly if the bone stays in.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 25 min · **Cook**: 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 17 oz chicken thigh (bone-in optional per the page)
- 1/2 red onion
- 1/2 white onion
- 3 garlic cloves
- 3 tbsp cornstarch
- 2 tbsp oil

*Marinade*
- 1 tbsp oyster sauce
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp cooking wine (Shaoxing or other)
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1/2 tsp white pepper
- 2 tbsp cornstarch
- 1/2 oz ginger

*To finish*
- 1 tbsp cooking wine
- 1/2 tsp garlic salt

**Method**
1. Combine the oyster sauce, soy sauce, 1 tbsp cooking wine, salt, sugar, white pepper and 2 tbsp cornstarch. Mince the ginger, add it and stir.
2. Slice both onions; mince the garlic.
3. Deboning and trimming the skin and excess fat are optional; if leaving the bone in, slice the thicker meat so it cooks evenly.
4. Pat the chicken dry, coat it in the marinade and leave it about 10 minutes. Spread 3 tbsp cornstarch on a plate and lightly coat both sides of each thigh; shake off excess.
5. Heat a skillet or flat-bottomed wok over **high heat**. Add the oil once hot. When shimmering, turn to **medium-low** and lay the chicken in **skin-side down**. Cook about **2–3 minutes**, until golden.
6. Flip and cook the other side about **5–6 minutes**, until golden. Flip skin-side down again and cook **1–2 minutes** to crisp the skin.
7. Push the chicken to the sides of the pan. Add the minced garlic to the centre and cook about 10 seconds, until fragrant. Add the onions and cook about 20 seconds.
8. Pour in the remaining 1 tbsp cooking wine and immediately cover. Turn the heat to **high** and cook covered **30–40 seconds**.
9. Uncover, sprinkle with 1/2 tsp garlic salt, mix briefly and serve over the onions.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step in the recipe; the page's FAQ recommends a thermometer and gives **Pull at 74°C** (165°F) at the thickest part.
Final serving temperature: **74°C / 165°F** (the source's stated endpoint; no separate serving figure or carryover given).

### 2. Julie Maestre / Cooked by Julie — Pan Seared Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.cookedbyjulie.com/pan-seared-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Julie Maestre, Cooked by Julie; the page labels its cuisine American. Recipe card specifies **bone-in, skin-on** thighs.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 24 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on
- 2 tbsp canola or vegetable oil
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp onion powder
- 1/2 tsp Italian seasoning
- Salt, to taste
- 1/2 lemon
- 2 garlic cloves, finely minced
- 1 cup chicken stock
- 1 tbsp butter

**Method**
1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over **medium-high** heat.
2. Mix the paprika, garlic powder, onion powder and Italian seasoning.
3. Season under the skin with salt and the spice blend; season the skin and the other side with salt only.
4. Cook **skin-side down for 7 minutes**, flip, and cook another **7 minutes**. Remove and set aside.
5. Discard the excess oil. Add the butter and garlic; cook 15 seconds.
6. Add the chicken stock and lemon juice, check the seasoning and bring to a simmer.
7. Return the chicken to the skillet, cover, and cook **10 minutes** or until fully cooked (the page suggests adding about 1/2 cup stock if the sauce thickens too much).

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step in the recipe; the page directs cooking until the internal temperature reaches **Pull at 74°C** (165°F).
Final serving temperature: **74°C / 165°F** (the source's stated endpoint; no separate serving figure given).

### 3. LapSha (Мария) / EdimDoma — Куриные бедра с хрустящей ароматной корочкой (Chicken thighs with crispy aromatic crust)
**Inspired by**: https://www.edimdoma.ru/retsepty/37270-kurinye-bedra-s-hrustyaschey-aromatnoy-korochkoy.amp (fetched 2026-10-24). LapSha (Мария), EdimDoma; Russian home-cooking tradition. The page says "chicken thighs" without specifying bone status; the author trimmed excess fat and some skin, so a skin-on finished cut is not established.

**Serves**: metadata says 1 · **Prep**: not stated (marinade ≥30 min) · **Cook**: ~10 min per side in the pan, then 35–45 min in the oven · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 large chicken thighs
- 3 large garlic cloves
- 1 red onion
- 1 tsp turmeric
- Chili pepper, to taste (author used a pinch "on the tip of a knife")
- 100 g light sour cream
- Salt
- 3 tbsp breadcrumbs
- 3 tbsp four-grain flakes
- 1 tbsp bran
- 1/2 tsp salt (for the crumb mix)
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1 tsp dried basil
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- Vegetable oil, or the fat trimmed from the chicken, for frying (no quantity stated)

**Method**
1. Crush the garlic. Peel and finely chop the onion; mix with turmeric and chili.
2. Wash the thighs. Trim excess fat and a little skin if desired. Salt the thighs, add them to the onion-garlic mix and stir.
3. Add the sour cream and mix so the marinade coats each piece. Marinate **at least 30 minutes**.
4. Put the flakes, breadcrumbs, bran, herbs and 1/2 tsp salt in a blender, coffee grinder or mortar.
5. Grind to a uniform crumb.
6. Heat a frying pan and add vegetable oil or the trimmed chicken fat.
7. Coat each piece in the crumb mixture, pressing crumbs onto the top as well.
8. Fry over **medium heat** on both sides until golden, **about 10 minutes per side**. (The oven heats to 200°C during this step.)
9. Transfer the browned thighs to a baking sheet or dish and bake **35–45 minutes** at **200°C**, depending on size.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated**.

### 4. Aliya LeeKong — North African-Spiced Chicken Thighs with Oranges, Marcona Almonds & Summer Vegetables
**Inspired by**: http://www.aliyaleekong.com/blog/north-african-spiced-chicken-thighs-with-oranges-marcona-almonds-summer-vegetables (fetched 2026-10-25). Aliya LeeKong; North African (ras el hanout) tradition — Group C. Ingredient line: **"2 lbs chicken thighs, skin-on with bone (4 to 6 thighs)."**

**Serves**: 4 to 6 · **Prep**: not stated (+15 min salting for the vegetables) · **Cook**: 3–4 min + 2 min sear, then 20–25 min oven finish · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 or 5 zucchini or summer squash, julienned
- 1½–1¾ tsp salt
- 2 lbs chicken thighs, skin-on with bone (4 to 6 thighs)
- 2 tsp paprika; 2 tsp ground toasted cumin; ½ tsp cinnamon; ¼ tsp turmeric; ½ tsp ground ginger; 2 tsp ground black pepper; ¼ tsp cayenne; pinch saffron
- 1 tsp unsalted butter; 2 tsp vegetable oil
- 1 onion, julienned or finely chopped; 2 garlic cloves, minced
- ¼ cup raw unsalted marcona almonds, toasted
- Small bunch of thyme
- 5 or 6 thin orange slices (about 1/4 of an orange); 1 tsp orange zest
- Salt to taste

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **400 °F** with the rack on the top shelf.
2. Julienne the zucchini, toss with ½–¾ tsp salt and let sit 15 minutes, then wring out the moisture.
3. Bring the thighs to room temperature. Mix the paprika, cumin, cinnamon, turmeric, ginger, pepper, cayenne, saffron and 1 tsp salt; season the thighs on both sides.
4. Heat a large pan over **medium-high**. Add the butter and oil and lay the thighs **skin-side down**. Cook **3–4 minutes** until the skin is crisp, keeping the heat from climbing so the spices do not burn. Flip and cook **2 minutes** more. Remove to a plate.
5. Reduce to medium, pour off the excess fat, add the onion and garlic with salt and cook a minute. Off the heat, toss in the zucchini, almonds and thyme. Layer the orange slices around the pan and set the thighs on top; sprinkle with orange zest.
6. Bake on the top rack **20–25 minutes, until the juices run clear out of the chicken.**

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step. **no internal temperature stated** — the endpoint is "until the juices run clear"; the author notes the thighs can be crisped again briefly in a sauté pan if the vegetables release moisture.

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Preferred doneness name | Final serving temp |
|-----------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Chinese-American (Made With Lau) | "165°F at the thickest part" (FAQ thermometer guidance) | 74 °C / 165 °F |
| American (Cooked by Julie) | "until internal temperature reaches 165 degrees F" | 74 °C / 165 °F |
| Russian (EdimDoma) | Not stated — golden crust in pan, then 35–45 min oven finish | Not stated |
| North African (Aliya LeeKong) | Not stated — "until the juices run clear", then oven finish | Not stated |

The observed range across the fetched sources where a temperature is stated is a single value —
**74 °C / 165 °F** — with two silent sources. Both stating sources land exactly on the USDA safe
line (`safe` doneness band, 74 °C / 165 °F), and neither expresses the higher dark-meat target that
other methods in this collection show (the braise leaf's Senegalese source pulls thighs at
75 °C/80 °C equivalent and the air-fryer leaf records a 195 °F endpoint). The variation here is
therefore not in stated numbers but in technique: every fetched source protects the centre of the
bone-in cut with a gentle covered or oven finish rather than trusting dry pan heat alone. The two
silent sources both use the classic visual test — golden crust, then "juices run clear" or a long
oven finish — which is practice for full doneness, not a measured figure. `recommended_doneness`
for this method on current evidence: `safe` (74 °C / 165 °F) is the only documented target, though
it is likely that unmeasured home practice for dark meat sits higher.

## What makes this method special

Pan-frying puts the thigh's skin in direct contact with hot metal, rendering fat and driving
Maillard browning that no oven or air-fryer match for depth of crust. The bone then becomes
the limiting factor: heat must conduct around the femur to the centre, so the fetched recipes
all add a short moist or oven phase to finish the interior without burning the crust — a
covered splash of wine, a covered simmer in stock, or a 200 °C oven. The method therefore
produces this cut's best crust at the price of an extra finishing step, and the pan drippings
(fond) it leaves behind are the starting point for the pan sauces several sources build
directly in the same skillet.

## Summary

Pan-frying gives bone-in thighs their deepest crust, but the bone forces every fetched recipe to finish gently — covered steam, covered stock or oven — to cook the centre through. Both sources that state a target say 165 °F, while the Russian and North African sources stay silent after a pan-and-oven sequence and trust a visual test. The method suits the cut well when paired with its finishing step; the temperature record is too thin to claim anything beyond the USDA floor.
