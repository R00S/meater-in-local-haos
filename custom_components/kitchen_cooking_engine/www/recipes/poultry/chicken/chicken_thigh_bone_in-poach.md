# Chicken Thigh (Bone-in) × Poach — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Poaching submerges chicken in hot liquid held below a simmer or lets it finish off heat, with less agitation than a soup simmer. Bone-in thighs can be poached whole or deboned while retaining the bones in the broth; skin and dark meat tolerate the moist method, and aromatics flavour both meat and cooking liquid. The sources vary between residual-heat cooking and a very gentle simmer; only one gives an internal endpoint.

## Source recipes

### 1. Connie Veneracion / Umami Days — Poached Chicken Hainanese-Style
**Inspired by**: https://cooking.umamidays.com/poached-chicken-hainanese-style/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Hainanese-style; exact cut: “3 large bone-in chicken thighs with the back portion attached.” The author explicitly says, “It’s poach, not simmer,” and calls for very little surface agitation.

**Serves**: 3 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: 10 min

**Ingredients**
- 3 large bone-in chicken thighs with back portion attached; 3 tbsp rock salt; 2 tbsp chopped ginger; 2 tbsp chopped scallions
- 4 cups chicken bone broth; 1/4 cup Shao xing rice wine; 1 two-inch knob ginger; 4–5 garlic cloves; 6 scallion stalks; salt to taste
- 1 tbsp sesame seed oil; cooked rice; 1 head broccoli; 1 cucumber

**Method**
1. Rub chicken with rock salt, chopped ginger and scallions; rinse and drain.
2. Put broth, rice wine, sliced ginger, garlic and scallions in a deep pan. Bring to a boil and boil uncovered 5 minutes; season to taste.
3. Submerge thighs. When the liquid returns to a boil, reduce heat until there is very little surface agitation; cover and poach 10 minutes.
4. Turn off heat and leave covered another 10 minutes in residual heat.
5. Remove chicken, brush skin with sesame oil and rest 10 minutes. Reheat liquid and cook broccoli in it 5 minutes; serve with rice and cucumber.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The endpoint is 10 minutes poaching plus 10 minutes residual heat.

### 2. Pailin Chongchitnant / Hot Thai Kitchen — Thai Grandma’s Twice Cooked Chicken
**Inspired by**: https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/gai-kem-v2/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Thai family recipe with Chinese-Thai ceremonial context. Ingredient line: “4 bone-in chicken thighs, 1 small breast (670g total weight).” The poached chicken is later stir-fried; this source covers the poaching stage, not a final poached-only dish.

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: about 20–30 min poaching · **Rest**: cooling to room temperature, time not stated

**Ingredients**
- Poaching: 4 bone-in chicken thighs; 1 small breast (670 g total); 3 garlic cloves; 8 ginger slices; lemongrass tops if available; 1/2 tsp salt
- Stir-fry: 6 garlic cloves; 4 ginger slices; 1 tbsp + 1 tsp chopped palm sugar; 1 1/2–2 tbsp soy sauce; salt as needed; a dash black soy sauce; 1/4 tsp black pepper; optional chopped cilantro
- Optional sauce: 2 tbsp chopped ginger; 2 garlic cloves; 2 Thai chilies; 2 tbsp chopped cilantro stems; 2 tbsp soy sauce; 2 tbsp lime juice; 1/2 tsp sugar

**Method**
1. For optional dipping sauce, pound ginger, garlic, chilies and cilantro stems; mix with soy sauce, lime juice and sugar.
2. Put chicken, ginger, garlic and optional lemongrass in a pot; cover with cold water and stir in salt.
3. Heat to a very gentle simmer with steam and only a few gentle bubbles; do not boil. The author states poaching-water temperature **175–185°F (80–85°C)**.
4. Poach about 20–30 minutes, remove and cool; tear into bite-size pieces.
5. Stir-fry garlic and ginger, add palm sugar, then chicken, sauces and pepper. Toss until liquid is absorbed and the chicken caramelizes; serve with rice.

**Probe and endpoint**: the published 175–185°F is the **poaching-water temperature**, not internal chicken temperature; **no internal temperature stated**.

### 3. Tiffy Cooks — Scallion Ginger Poached Chicken (With Soup!)
**Inspired by**: https://tiffycooks.com/scallion-ginger-poached-chicken-with-soup/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Singaporean/Taiwanese-inspired; ingredient line: “8 chicken thighs skin-on, deboned but keep the bones!”

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 20 min total (12–15 min poaching plus optional 10–15 min broth simmer after chicken removal) · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 8 skin-on chicken thighs, deboned but bones retained; bones from thighs
- 3 green onions; 5 garlic cloves; 3 slices fresh ginger; 1/4 tsp white pepper; 1.5 tbsp soy sauce; 5 cups hot chicken stock (1.2 L); 1 tsp sesame oil for brushing
- Ginger-scallion oil: 3 green-onion stalks; 1 tbsp grated ginger; 1/4 tsp salt; 2 tbsp neutral oil
- Kai dong sauce: 1 1/2 tbsp Four Season Scallion Sauce; 1 tbsp Golden Shallot Oil; 1 1/2 tbsp chicken stock

**Method**
1. Debone the thighs, retaining the bones. Put bones in a pot and layer thighs skin-side up; add green onion, garlic, ginger, white pepper and soy sauce.
2. Add hot chicken stock. Bring to a gentle simmer, not a rapid boil, and cook 12–15 minutes until tender and cooked through.
3. Remove thighs and brush with sesame oil. Optionally continue simmering bones 10–15 minutes for broth.
4. Pour heated neutral oil over chopped green onion, ginger and salt to make the ginger-scallion oil. Mix the kai dong sauce with stock.
5. Slice chicken and serve with the sauces; broth may be used as soup.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The page gives 12–15 minutes and “tender and cooked through.”

### 4. Laura Fuentes / Laura Fuentes — Perfectly Poached Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://www.laurafuentes.com/poached-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). General American technique; the card itself is boneless, but the author explicitly says the method can use “skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs.”

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2 min · **Cook**: 25 min card time (12–15 min after heating water, according to directions) · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 1–1.5 lb chicken thighs (recipe card specifies boneless, skinless)
- 4 cups cold water; 1 tsp salt; 2 tsp peppercorns; 1 tsp dried thyme, optional; chosen herbs/spices

**Method**
1. Place thighs in a pot and cover with cold water by 2 inches; season with salt, peppercorns and optional thyme/herbs.
2. Bring water and chicken to a low boil; flip chicken pieces.
3. Cover, turn off heat and leave in hot water 12–15 minutes. The page says the internal temperature should reach **175°F**.
4. Remove chicken and rest 5 minutes before slicing or shredding.

**Probe and endpoint**: the author states 175°F internal temperature. Since the recipe card is boneless, this source is counted only as explicit guidance that the technique can be used with bone-in thighs, not as a directly cooked bone-in batch.

## Method distinction and endpoint

Poaching and simmering overlap in practice, but are distinguishable in these pages: Umami Days explicitly says “poach, not simmer” and directs very little agitation; Laura Fuentes turns off heat after bringing the water up; Hot Thai Kitchen specifies very gentle 175–185°F water. Sarah Gavigan's OBSESSED post (https://iamobsessed.substack.com/p/poached-chicken) offers a Japanese *tori no mizutaki* framework with bone-in thighs and a bare-simmer instruction, but the author explicitly says it is not an exact recipe; it is retained here as a method-discovery lead and is **not counted** among the four source recipes. By contrast, the simmer leaf records recipes that keep a soup or broth at a reduced simmer for an extended period. No general internal-temperature consensus is drawn from these heterogeneous sources; numeric values are quoted only for the water bath (Hot Thai Kitchen) or the Laura Fuentes page's stated internal endpoint.

## Summary

Five fetched recipe/technique pages establish poaching as a distinct method lead for bone-in chicken thighs. The Japanese Mizutaki recipe and Hainanese-style recipe both explicitly specify bone-in thighs; the Thai recipe also lists bone-in thighs but poaches before stir-frying. The Tiffy Cooks thighs are deboned with the bones retained, while the Laura Fuentes card is boneless, so neither counts as a direct intact bone-in batch. **PARTIAL RESEARCH**: even counting the three direct bone-in preparations, source/tradition diversity does not meet the 4–6-source gate; source endpoints remain exactly as published.
