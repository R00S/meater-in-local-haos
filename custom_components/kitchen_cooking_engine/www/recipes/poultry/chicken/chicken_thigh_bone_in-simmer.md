# Chicken Thigh (Bone-in) × Simmer — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Bone-in thighs simmered in soup or broth cook by gentle convection rather than direct pan contact. The femur and dark meat tolerate extended moist heat and enrich the cooking liquid, while low agitation helps keep the pieces intact. The soup sources bring broth up before reducing to a simmer; the stovetop source maintains a covered gentle simmer and finishes with a brief reduction and sear. Four sources use time or tenderness, while the Tea for Turmeric recipe also publishes a 165°F internal endpoint.

## Source recipes

### 1. Nagi / RecipeTin Eats — Vietnamese Chicken Pho (Pho Ga)
**Inspired by**: https://www.recipetineats.com/vietnamese-chicken-pho-soup-pho-ga/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Vietnamese tradition; recipe says “1.5 kg / 3 lb chicken thighs, bone in skin on.”

**Serves**: 4–6 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 1 h 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1 tbsp vegetable or canola oil
- 2 onions, halved
- 5 cm / 2 in ginger, sliced
- 2 L / 2 qt water
- 1.5 kg / 3 lb bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
- 1 small bunch coriander/cilantro
- 5 star anise pods; 1 cinnamon stick; 4 cloves
- 2 tsp fennel seeds; 2 tsp coriander seeds
- 8 tsp fish sauce; 6 tsp white sugar; 3/4 tsp cooking/kosher salt (or 1/2 tsp table salt)
- 360 g / 13 oz dried thin rice noodles (or 600 g fresh); 2 green onion stems
- Toppings: 3 cups bean sprouts; 1 small bunch each Thai basil, mint and coriander; 2 limes; hoisin and Sriracha; optional sliced red chillies

**Method**
1. Char onion and ginger in oil in a large pot, about 2 minutes per side.
2. Add water and broth ingredients except salt; bring to a simmer, then reduce to a very gentle simmer with the lid ajar.
3. Simmer 1.5 hours, skimming scum once or twice. Remove chicken, strain broth, and adjust volume to 1.5 L as the source directs.
4. Add salt and keep broth warm. Remove chicken meat from bones and discard skin and bones.
5. Prepare noodles separately; serve with chicken, broth, green onions and toppings.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. Time and gentle simmer are the published endpoint.

### 2. Lisa Johnson / Cooking with Curls — Caldo de Pollo
**Inspired by**: https://cookingwithcurls.com/2024/01/10/caldo-de-pollo-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Mexican tradition; recipe ingredient line says “3 pounds skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs.”

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 1 h 15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 8 cups filtered water; 3 lb skin-on, bone-in chicken thighs
- 3 celery stalks; 1 large white onion; 4 large garlic cloves; 3 large carrots for broth
- 5 sprigs Italian parsley; 5 peppercorns; 2 bay leaves; 1/2 tsp dried marjoram; 1/2 tsp dried thyme
- 4 zucchini; 4 large carrots; 2 ears corn; 1 tbsp coarse sea salt
- 2 limes; 1/4 cup chopped cilantro; cooked Mexican or white rice, about 1/2–1 cup per serving

**Method**
1. Put chicken, water, celery, garlic, onion, marjoram, oregano, peppercorns, parsley and bay leaves in a pot; cover and bring to a boil.
2. Reduce heat and simmer partially covered for 50 minutes. Skim foam/fat; reserve broth and chicken, discard other solids.
3. Cool chicken enough to handle; remove meat from bones and shred or chop (the source also permits serving pieces whole).
4. Add sliced carrots, zucchini, corn and salt to the broth; cover and simmer 20–30 minutes until tender.
5. Serve over rice with lime and cilantro; tortillas and avocado are suggested accompaniments.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The page's endpoint is 50 minutes for chicken and tender vegetables.

### 3. Katya / Little Broken — Carrabba’s Copycat Chicken Soup
**Inspired by**: https://www.littlebroken.com/sicilian-chicken-soup/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Italian-American/Carrabba’s-inspired; ingredient line: “4 bone-in-skin-on chicken thighs, 2 pounds.”

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: not stated as a single total in the fetched text; chicken simmer about 30 min, pasta 13–15 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (2 lb)
- 1 medium onion; 3 celery ribs; 3 medium carrots; 1 large yellow bell pepper; 2 medium russet potatoes
- 4 garlic cloves; 1 (14.5 oz) can no-salt diced tomatoes; 2 bay leaves; 1 tbsp kosher salt
- 1 (32 oz) carton low-sodium chicken stock; 4 cups water (or stock)
- 1/2 cup dry ditalini pasta; 1/2 cup chopped fresh Italian parsley; black pepper to taste

**Method**
1. Combine chicken, vegetables, tomatoes, bay leaves, garlic, stock and water in a soup pot; bring to a boil.
2. Reduce heat and simmer partially covered about 30 minutes, until chicken is tender; remove chicken.
3. Add pasta and simmer partially covered 13–15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until tender.
4. Shred chicken, discard skin and bones, return meat to soup, discard bay leaves, stir in parsley and serve.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The page says cook until tender.

### 4. Izzah Cheema / Tea for Turmeric — Bone-In Chicken Thighs (Stovetop)
**Inspired by**: https://www.teaforturmeric.com/bone-in-chicken-thigh-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Izzah Cheema; Pakistani/South Asian home-cooking context. Recipe card explicitly lists “2 lb bone-in, skinless chicken thighs”; the author notes thighs are shown in the photos and split leg quarters in the video.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: optional marination up to 2 h at room temperature or 24 h refrigerated · **Cook**: about 25 min gentle simmer, then brief reduction/sear · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 lb bone-in, skinless chicken thighs
- 2 1/2 tbsp distilled white vinegar; 2 tbsp olive or neutral oil; 1 tbsp cumin seeds
- 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt (or 1 1/4 tsp fine sea/table salt); 1 tsp crushed red chili flakes, plus more to taste; 3/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp ketchup

**Method**
1. Optionally marinate the chicken with all ingredients except ketchup, up to 2 hours at room temperature or 24 hours refrigerated.
2. Heat a wide lidded pan over medium heat. Add chicken and marinade, mix, and spread in a single layer.
3. Reduce heat to low or low-medium to maintain a gentle simmer. Cover and cook, tossing halfway through, about 25 minutes until fully cooked.
4. Increase heat to high; boil the remaining liquid until mostly evaporated, about 5–6 minutes. Stir in ketchup and sauté, stirring only as necessary, about 2 minutes until the chicken lightly sears; turn off heat when moisture has evaporated.

**Probe and endpoint**: source recommends a thermometer at the thickest point, **165°F / 75°C**; this is below the recipe's final sauté stage. The cut-specific leaf records the published figure without treating the later sear as an alternative safety endpoint.

### 5. Sarah Hill / Real Food with Sarah — Bone-In Chicken Soup
**Inspired by**: https://www.realfoodwithsarah.com/orzo-chicken-soup-with-bone-in-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). American home cooking; ingredient line says “2 pounds bone in chicken thighs, (typically 4 thighs).”

**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 40 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 tbsp olive oil; 2 lb bone-in chicken thighs (typically 4)
- 1 yellow onion; 2 carrots; 2 celery stalks; 1 zucchini; 2 garlic cloves
- 1/2 tsp each salt, black pepper, turmeric and ginger
- 1 bay leaf; 1–2 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 tsp dried); 1–2 sprigs fresh rosemary (or 1 tsp dried)
- 4 cups chicken broth; 3/4 cup orzo; juice of 1/2 lemon

**Method**
1. Brown thighs in olive oil 2–3 minutes per side; remove, remove skin and set aside.
2. Soften vegetables; stir in garlic and seasonings.
3. Return chicken with herbs and bay leaf, add broth, cover and simmer about 20 minutes.
4. Remove chicken, discard bones and shred meat. Return meat with orzo and simmer until pasta is cooked; turn off heat and add lemon juice.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The page uses the timed simmer and cooked orzo as its endpoint.

## Temperature consensus

Four of the five fetched recipes use time or qualitative tenderness without an internal temperature; Tea for Turmeric separately recommends 165°F / 75°C at the thickest point before its final liquid reduction and light sear. No numeric target is inferred for the other sources. The soup recipes use an initial boil followed by reduced simmer, while the stovetop recipe maintains a low covered simmer; this leaf documents sustained simmering rather than the separate enum method `boil`.

## Summary

Five fetched recipes confirm sustained simmering of bone-in thigh pieces across Vietnamese pho, Mexican caldo, Italian-American and American soups, plus a Pakistani/South Asian stovetop preparation. Tea for Turmeric gives a 165°F / 75°C internal endpoint; the four soup recipes give time or tenderness endpoints only. **PARTIAL RESEARCH**: this is a method-discovery collection, not a complete 4–6-source temperature-divergence analysis.
