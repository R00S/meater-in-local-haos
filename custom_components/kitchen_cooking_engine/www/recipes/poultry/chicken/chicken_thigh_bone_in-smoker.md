## Cut profile

Smoking bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs is an American barbecue practice: the thighs are
rolled or tucked into uniform bundles so the thin flap of meat around the femur does not
overcook while the thick centre slowly comes up to temperature. Low smoker heat
(around 120–135 °C / 250–275 °F) renders subcutaneous fat slowly and keeps the skin
leathery rather than crisp — barbecue practice therefore finishes the thighs hot or sauced,
or accepts soft skin. The bone-in format is preferred in barbecue because the femur
protects the meat from drying during the long exposure to dry convective heat and smoke.

This leaf records one fetched American barbecue source. Smoking traditions for bone-in
thighs also exist elsewhere (e.g. European poultry smoking), but no other qualifying
recipe was fetched this session, so this is partial research.

## Source recipes

### 1. Susie Bulloch / Hey Grill Hey — Smoked Chicken Thighs
**Inspired by**: https://heygrillhey.com/smoked-chicken-thighs/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Susie Bulloch, Hey Grill Hey; US backyard-barbecue tradition. The source explicitly calls for a reliable meat thermometer and cooking to temperature, not time.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 45–75 min (recipe card states 1 h) · **Rest**: 5–10 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 pounds bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
- 2 tablespoons Hey Grill Hey Sweet Rub (linked spice-rub recipe)
- 1/2 cup Hey Grill Hey Whiskey Peach BBQ Sauce (linked sauce recipe; any BBQ sauce may be substituted)
- Olive oil, to drizzle (amount not stated)

**Method**
1. Preheat the smoker to **275°F** (135°C).
2. Trim excess skin or thin chicken around the bone. Roll the thighs tightly into uniform pieces and drizzle all sides with olive oil.
3. Season all sides with the Sweet Rub, covering the thighs.
4. Place the thighs on the smoker **skin-side up**, close the lid, and smoke until the internal temperature reads **165°F** (74°C) — the source estimates 45–75 minutes depending on thigh size and smoker heat.
5. Baste the thighs with the Whiskey Peach BBQ Sauce.
6. Continue smoking until the thighs reach **170–175°F** (77–79°C).
7. Remove from the smoker and rest **5–10 minutes**. Serve hot with extra sauce if desired.

**Probe and endpoint**: the source instructs using a reliable meat thermometer and gives the two-stage internal endpoint: **Pull at 74°C** (165°F) for basting, then continue to **170–175°F** (77–79°C) as the final doneness target.
Final serving temperature: **77–79°C / 170–175°F** (as stated by the source; no separate post-rest figure given).

One fetched recipe establishes the method, but a single source cannot produce a cross-tradition consensus. Within this one source the two-stage endpoint is notable: the thighs are sauced at the USDA minimum (74 °C / 165 °F) and then taken to 77–79 °C / 170–175 °F, sitting inside the cut's `dark_meat_optimal` band (74–85 °C) and above the USDA safe line. The observed endpoint is higher than the USDA minimum by design — dark-meat collagen rendering is the stated texture goal of the extended cook. No divergence data exists yet; with more sources the likely axis of variation is smoker temperature (225–275 °F practice) and whether the skin is finished hot.

## What makes this method special

Smoking surrounds the thigh with low-temperature convective air and wood smoke for 45–75 minutes, so the subcutaneous fat renders gradually while smoke compounds (guaiacol, syringol) deposit on the moist surface. Unlike oven roasting, the skin never sees enough direct heat to crisp, which is why this source rolls the thighs and finishes them sauced rather than expecting crackling. The bone-in cut benefits from the long gentle cook: collagen converts steadily without the outer muscle drying, and the femur buffers the thickest part of the thigh. The one fetched source nonetheless ends at 170–175 °F — above the poultry minimum — because pulled pork-style dark-meat texture is the goal.

## Summary

Smoking gives bone-in thighs a slow, gentle render and deep smoke flavour at the cost of crisp skin, which barbecue practice compensates for with a sauce finish. It suits the cut's collagen and fat well, and the fetched source deliberately overshoots the poultry minimum for tender dark meat. The method is well matched to thighs, but this leaf rests on one US source and needs more traditions before any temperature claim is general.
