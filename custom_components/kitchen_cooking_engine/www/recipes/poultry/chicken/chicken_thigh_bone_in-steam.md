# Chicken Thigh (Bone-in) × Steam — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Steaming cooks the thigh above boiling water without submerging it, allowing the piece to retain its juices and drip them into a dish or broth below. Bone-in, skin-on thighs are explicitly used or permitted in the fetched Hainanese-style, Chinese, and Hawaiian home recipes. The sources' cooking times vary with whether thighs are whole, cut or deboned, and none states an internal temperature in the retrieved recipe text.

## Source recipes

### 1. Ms Shi and Mr He — Hainanese Chicken Rice (Steamed Chicken)
**Inspired by**: https://msshiandmrhe.com/hainanese-chicken-rice/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Chinese/Hainanese-style; the recipe explicitly permits “4 boneless/bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs.”

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 30 min · **Rest**: cool several minutes, then refrigerate (duration not stated)

**Ingredients**
- Chicken: 4 skin-on chicken thighs (boneless or bone-in); 1/2 tsp salt; 1/4 tsp turmeric; 4 ginger slices; 1 tbsp cooking wine, optional
- Dipping sauce: 1/2 cup chopped scallions; 2 tbsp minced ginger; 1/2 tsp salt; 1 tsp sugar; optional 1/2 tsp Sichuan peppercorns, 2 tbsp chopped cilantro and 2 Thai chilies; 2 tbsp vegetable oil; 2 tbsp soy sauce
- Rice: 2 cups jasmine rice; 2 1/5 cups chicken broth; 1/2 tsp salt

**Method**
1. Season thighs on both sides with salt and turmeric.
2. Put a small bowl upside down in a pasta bowl; arrange thighs around it and put a ginger slice on each. Add optional cooking wine. Set bowl on a steamer or rack in a pot with cold water.
3. Steam over medium-high heat for **30 minutes**, until fully cooked. Cool briefly; remove bones if using bone-in thighs, slice, cover and refrigerate.
4. Use the collected chicken juices as broth; cook rice with broth and salt.
5. For sauce, combine scallions, ginger, salt, sugar, optional peppercorns/cilantro/chilies; pour over 2 tbsp smoking-hot oil, then add soy sauce. Serve chicken with rice and sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The source says steam 30 minutes until fully cooked.

### 2. Bima / Spicygo — Steamed Chicken Leg Chinese Style
**Inspired by**: https://spicygo.com/the-art-of-simplicity-steamed-chicken-leg-chinese-style/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Chinese cuisine; ingredient line: “1 no. Chicken thigh, bone-in, skin-on.” The source uses a par-boil followed by steaming; filed here for the dominant final steam stage, with the two-stage method preserved.

**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 10 min par-boil + about 30 min steam · **Rest**: chill, duration not stated

**Ingredients**
- 715 ml water; 1 bone-in, skin-on chicken thigh; 15 ml Chinese rice wine
- 1/2 spring onion stalk, cut into 2-cm lengths; 20 g sliced ginger
- Garnish: 1 spring onion green, julienned; 1/2 tomato; 2 lettuce leaves
- Seasoning: 15 ml light soy sauce; 15 ml sesame oil

**Method**
1. Bring water to a boil, add thigh and boil 10 minutes; remove to a plate.
2. Season parboiled chicken with rice wine, spring onion and ginger.
3. Place chicken on a tray and steam about 30 minutes.
4. While warm, rub with soy sauce and sesame oil; chill.
5. Remove bone, cut meat into 2-cm pieces and garnish with tomato, lettuce and spring onion.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. Page gives a 30-minute steam endpoint.

### 3. Elaine / China Sichuan Food — Chinese Steamed Chicken
**Inspired by**: https://www.chinasichuanfood.com/chinese-steamed-chicken-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Chinese cuisine; exact recipe uses “Half of a whole chicken, around 1 and 1/2 pound,” not a thigh-only cut. The author specifically describes younger whole chicken as suitable for steaming. **Not counted as direct bone-in-thigh evidence**; retained as a whole-bird method comparison only.

**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min (+30 min marinade) · **Cook**: about 20 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 1/2 whole chicken, about 1.5 lb; 1 tbsp ginger shreds; 1 tbsp white-part green onion shreds
- Marinade: 1 tbsp light soy sauce; 1 tsp Chinese cooking wine; 1/2 tsp salt
- Dipping sauce: 1 tbsp oyster sauce; 1 tbsp light soy sauce; 4 tbsp water; 1 garlic clove; 1 tbsp chopped green onions; 1 tbsp sesame oil

**Method**
1. Clean chicken; rub marinade over both sides. Top with ginger and green onion and marinate at least 30 minutes.
2. Place in steamer; steam over high heat around 20 minutes until soft and a chopstick inserts easily.
3. Heat sesame oil, fry garlic and green onion until aromatic, add oyster sauce, soy sauce and water; bring to a boil for dipping sauce.
4. Cut chicken into chunks and serve with sauce.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. This source uses a whole chicken, not a thigh, so it is excluded from the cut-specific source count.

### 4. Onolicious Hawaiʻi — Steamed Ginger Chicken
**Inspired by**: https://onolicioushawaii.com/steamed-ginger-chicken/ (fetched 2026-10-24). Hawaiʻi home cooking with Chinese pantry influences. Recipe card uses chicken legs; author says the recipe can also be made with chicken thighs but does not state bone status. **Not counted as direct bone-in-thigh evidence.**

**Serves**: not stated · **Prep**: 30 min–1 day marinade · **Cook**: 20 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 2 chicken legs; 1 tbsp soy sauce; 1 tbsp oyster sauce; 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 1 two-inch knob ginger, peeled and julienned; optional 1 tbsp goji berries; 1 green onion, sliced

**Method**
1. Pat chicken legs dry. Mix soy sauce, oyster sauce and sesame oil; coat chicken and marinate at least 30 minutes.
2. Put half the ginger in a shallow steaming dish, place chicken on top, then add remaining ginger and optional goji berries.
3. Steam 20 minutes; check with chopstick for tenderness. Garnish with green onion and serve with rice.

**Probe and endpoint**: no probe step; **no internal temperature stated**. The recipe permits thighs but does not specify bone-in.

## Temperature consensus

Two directly qualifying recipes use bone-in thighs and give time/qualitative endpoints only: 30 minutes for Hainanese-style thighs and about 30 minutes of steaming after a 10-minute par-boil for the Chinese-style leg. The whole-chicken and unspecified-bone thigh-leg pages are leads, not strict cut matches. No internal temperature is stated in the qualifying recipes.

## Summary

Steaming is confirmed for bone-in thighs by two fetched recipes, with two additional adjacent leads that do not clearly establish the target cut.
