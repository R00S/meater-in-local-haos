# Goose Thigh × Braise — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Goose thigh is the one cut in this project where a **high** internal temperature is not a
mistake. The muscle is dense, heavily worked and cross-hatched with collagen, and it carries a
thick subcutaneous fat layer that has to render before the skin can crisp. Braising is how the
German-speaking kitchen solves both problems at once: sear the skin hard, set the legs on a
bed of Suppengrün in a Bräter, add wine or fond, and let them sit at 150–180 °C for an hour to
an hour and a half. The collagen turns to gelatine, the fat renders into the sauce, and the legs
come out at **75–85 °C** — a temperature that would ruin a chicken breast and is exactly right
here.

The four fetched German records agree on the shape of the cook and disagree, usefully, on the
number. Two of them publish a band: **75–79 °C for pink (rosa) and 80–85 °C for fully cooked
(durch)**, and separately "gar at 80 °C". Both sit far above the 74 °C USDA poultry minimum,
which is the point — for this cut, 74 °C is safe but still chewy, and 80 °C+ is what makes the
thigh tender. The low-temperature variants (80 °C oven for two hours) exist for the same reason.

## Source recipes

### 1. eat.de (Germany) — Garzeit der Gänsekeule
**Inspired by**: https://eat.de/magazin/garzeit-gaensekeule/ (fetched 2026-10-26). eat.de (German food magazine), by Marlene, updated 2026-06-22. German Festtagsküche tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 2–4 · **Cook**: ~60 min braise, or ~2 h low-temperature

**Ingredients**
- 2 goose legs (*Gänsekeulen*), salted and peppered
- Butterschmalz, goose fat or a high-smoke-point oil for searing
- Soup vegetables (*Suppengrün*), 1 onion, dried bay leaves
- Wine or vegetable stock to deglaze

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **180 °C**.
2. Sear the seasoned legs **about 5 minutes on all sides** in a Bräter; brown the soup vegetables briefly in the same fat.
3. Set the legs on the vegetables, pour in the liquid, and braise **about 60 minutes at 180 °C**, **turning twice** and finishing skin-side up so the skin crisps.
4. **Low-temperature variant**: preheat to **80 °C**, sear the legs about 10 minutes on all sides, set them skin-side up on a rack over a drip tray and cook **a good 2 hours**; the last quarter hour at 220 °C gives a crust.
5. Check with a core-temperature probe: **rosa 75–79 °C, durch 80–85 °C.** If it is short, give it a few more minutes.

*Internal temperature: **the cut's explicit band**, 75–79 °C pink and 80–85 °C through. This is the
most useful record in the corpus for this cut.*

### 2. Die Frau am Grill (Germany) — Gänsekeulen mit Rotkohl und Knödel
**Inspired by**: https://die-frau-am-grill.de/gaensekeulen-rezept/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Die Frau am Grill (German recipe site), 5/5 from 10 ratings. German Sunday-roast tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Cook**: ~1.5 h braise + crisping

**Ingredients**
- 4 goose legs (one 300–500 g leg per person)
- Onions, garlic, Suppengrün · 1 orange · red wine · poultry stock
- Juniper berries, bay leaves, cloves, parsley, mugwort (*Beifuß*)
- Butterschmalz

**Method**
1. Build the base: brown the onions in Butterschmalz, add the soup vegetables and garlic, deglaze with red wine and top up with poultry stock.
2. Put the spices, herbs and orange slices into the liquid, then lay the goose legs in **skin-side up**.
3. **Braise slowly for about 1.5 hours at 160 °C** so meat and sauce can take on flavour.
4. Pour off the braising liquid and keep it warm; **crisp the legs at high heat** and serve with the sauce.
5. The page's own check: **"Gänsekeulen sind bei einer Kerntemperatur von 80 Grad gar"** — use a meat thermometer, because times vary leg to leg.

*Internal temperature: **80 °C stated as the doneness point**, matching eat.de's upper band. The
page also names the two failure modes for this cut — searing too hot, or braising too briefly —
which both produce tough skin and tough meat.*

### 3. NDR (Norddeutscher Rundfunk, Germany) — Gänsebraten zubereiten
**Inspired by**: https://www.ndr.de/ratgeber/kochen/warenkunde/Gaensebraten-zubereiten-So-wird-die-Gans-knusprig-und-zart,gans6.html (fetched 2026-10-26). NDR, the northern German public broadcaster's cooking guide. German/north-German tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: varies · **Cook**: ~1.5 h for legs; 3–4 h for a whole bird

**Ingredients / elements**
- Goose legs (or a whole goose, 3.5–6 kg)
- Salt, pepper · apples, onions, marjoram, mugwort or thyme for a whole bird
- Water in the drip tray; red or port wine for the sauce; cold butter or cornstarch to bind

**Method**
1. For **individual legs**: "kurz anbraten und dann in einem Topf oder im Ofen geschmort" — sear briefly, then braise in a pot or the oven. **That takes about one and a half hours.** The legs can also be braised with vegetables first and grilled crisp in the oven just before serving.
2. For a whole goose: 180–200 °C, roughly one hour per kilo, basting repeatedly and pricking the skin below the legs so the fat runs.
3. **The meat must be cooked through — it should reach a core temperature of at least 80 °C.** A roasting thermometer is the way to check; the bird is done when clear juice runs from the leg and the meat loosens from the bone.
4. **Low-temperature method**: 1 hour at 180–220 °C, then about **7 hours at 80 °C** for a 4 kg goose.
5. In the last 10–15 minutes raise the oven to 230–250 °C and brush the skin with salt water or honey for crackling.

*Internal temperature: **at least 80 °C**, from a public broadcaster's test-kitchen guide — the
strongest authority in this leaf. Note the constructive detail that the leg is done when it
loosens from the bone, which is the physical sign of collagen conversion at that temperature.*

### 4. Tastybits (Germany) — Knusprige Gänsekeule aus dem Backofen
**Inspired by**: https://www.tastybits.de/rezepte/fleisch-gefluegel/knusprige-gaensekeule-aus-dem-backofen-mit-leckerer-sauce/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Tastybits.de, recipe by Jannik Degner, 4.9/5 from 25 ratings. German Festtagsküche tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Total**: 2 h 30 min

**Ingredients**
- 4 goose legs, about 500 g each
- 2 carrots · 100 g celeriac · 1 onion · 1 tbsp tomato purée · 2 tsp icing sugar
- 100 ml port + 100 ml red wine (or 200 ml alcohol-free wine / 100 ml grape juice + dark balsamic)
- 500 ml goose fond or lightly salted water
- 1 cinnamon stick · 1 star anise · 1 bay leaf · 2 cloves · a piece of orange peel · 4 allspice berries · black pepper · fresh thyme · 2 tbsp dried mugwort
- 1–2 tsp cornstarch

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **150 °C fan**.
2. Score and salt the legs, then sear **skin-side down 2–3 minutes and 1–2 minutes on the other side** in a large Bräter; pour off most of the rendered fat (keep it for the red cabbage and potatoes).
3. Brown the vegetables 3–5 minutes in the remaining fat, stir in the tomato purée and roast 2 minutes, then add the wine, fond, spices and herbs and bring to a simmer.
4. Return the legs to the Bräter, cover, and braise at **150 °C** — the whole dish takes **2 h 30 min** including the finish.
5. Lift the legs out, strain and skim the sauce, thicken with cornstarch, and **crisp the skin in the oven or in a pan** just before serving. The legs can be cooked the day before and crisped at the last minute.

*Internal temperature: not published in the fetched text; the source times the cook instead. Its
distinctive contribution is the **day-ahead** workflow — braise, chill, then crisp — which is a
practical argument for the high-temperature finish on this cut.*

## Temperature summary

| Source | Published target | Cook | Tradition |
|--------|------------------|------|-----------|
| eat.de | **75–79 °C rosa · 80–85 °C durch** | 180 °C, ~60 min (or 80 °C, 2 h) | German |
| Die Frau am Grill | **80 °C gar** | 160 °C, ~1.5 h, then crisp | German |
| NDR | **at least 80 °C** | legs ~1.5 h; low-temp 1 h + 7 h at 80 °C | German (public broadcaster) |
| Tastybits | **none published** | 150 °C fan, 2 h 30 min total | German |

**Conclusion for this leaf:** three of four sources publish a number and they agree closely —
**75–85 °C, with 80 °C as the practical done point** — which is 6 °C above the 74 °C USDA poultry
minimum and is the band this cut should carry. The convergent technique is sear-then-braise at
150–180 °C for roughly an hour to ninety minutes, finishing hot for the skin. All four sources are
German, so this leaf adds no new culinary group to the cut.
