# Goose Thigh × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Roasting splits from braising on one practical question: does the leg sit in liquid or not? In
this leaf the legs sit on a bed of fruit or bones with only water or nothing under them, skin up,
and the oven does the work by rendering fat through the skin rather than by exchanging flavour
with a sauce. That is why the temperatures here are lower and the times longer than the braise
leaf — 150 °C for two to three hours is common — and why every source in this leaf spends its
words on **skin**: pricking, scoring, drying, salting, basting, and a final blast of heat.

Goose thigh is forgiving of a long roast in a way no other poultry cut in this project is. Its fat
layer bastes it from underneath, and its collagen needs time, so the danger is not overcooking the
muscle but serving flabby skin. The one numeric target published in this leaf is for the **breast**
of the same bird (55 °C in the Raymond Blanc record), which is a reminder that legs and breast are
cooked separately for exactly this reason.

## Source recipes

### 1. Raymond Blanc (UK) — Roast goose
**Inspired by**: https://www.raymondblanc.com/recipes/roast-goose/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Raymond Blanc (Le Manoir aux Quat'Saisons, two Michelin stars). British/French restaurant tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 8 · **Prep**: 30 min · **Cook**: 150 min

**Ingredients (legs element)**
- Legs removed from a 4.5–5 kg goose, plus the chopped wings and neck
- 1 bay leaf, 2 sprigs thyme, 1 garlic clove, coarse salt, black pepper
- 2 tbsp goose fat · 100 g onion · 60 g carrot · 20 g celery
- 300 ml hot water · 200 ml port · 100 ml Madeira (optional)

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **150 °C**. Rub the leg flesh with bay, thyme, garlic, salt and pepper.
2. Colour the wings and neck in goose fat for 5 minutes, add the vegetables and brown 3 minutes more.
3. **Lay the legs skin-side up in the roasting tray on the coloured bones and vegetables, and cover the tray tightly with foil. Roast 1 hour.**
4. Raise the oven to **230 °C**, uncover, sit the goose crown on top of the legs and roast **30 minutes**.
5. Turn down to **150 °C**, add hot water, bay and thyme, and cook **30–35 minutes more, or until the breast reaches 55 °C**, basting every 10 minutes.
6. Remove the crown, wrap in foil and rest 30 minutes — **continuing to cook the legs during that time**. Then lift the legs out to rest.
7. Deglaze the tray with the port and Madeira, reduce, strain, and finish the jus.

*Internal temperature: the **breast** target is given as 55 °C; **no leg temperature is
published**. The chef's own explanation of why the legs come off the bird is the value of this
record: "if you were to roast the bird whole, then the breast would be over-cooked, and the legs
would be tough."*

### 2. Polish Your Kitchen (Poland) — Polish Roasted Goose Legs (Gęsie Nogi)
**Inspired by**: https://www.polishyourkitchen.com/polish-roasted-goose-legs-gesie-nogi/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Polish Your Kitchen (Anna Hurning). Polish tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 1 leg per person · **Prep**: 10 min + overnight · **Cook**: 2.5–3 h

**Ingredients**
- Goose legs · salt, pepper · fresh thyme or marjoram
- About ½ apple and ½ pear per leg

**Method**
1. Wash and dry the legs, season with salt, pepper and herbs, and **leave in the fridge overnight**.
2. Preheat the oven to **150 °C (300 °F)**.
3. Quarter the apples and pears, deseed, and lay them in the bottom of a roasting pan; set the legs on top of the fruit.
4. **Roast 2.5 hours, basting occasionally** (up to 3 hours for larger legs).
5. Serve with potato dumplings (*kopytka* or *kluski śląskie*) and a leek or beet salad.
6. Strain the fruit out and keep the rendered fat in a jar in the fridge for frying.

*Internal temperature: not published. The source's own explanation of the mechanism is worth
recording — "long roasting allows the fat to soak into the tough meat, making it super tender" —
and the fruit bed is doing the basting that a sauce would do in a braise.*

### 3. Ester kocht (Germany) — Gänsekeule im Backofen ohne Anbraten
**Inspired by**: https://www.esterkocht.com/gaensekeule-im-backofen-ohne-anbraten/ (fetched 2026-10-26). Ester kocht (German home-cooking blog), 4.91 from 21 votes. Franconian Sunday-roast tradition — **Culinary Group D** source.
**Serves**: 6 · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 2 h 15 min

**Ingredients**
- 6 goose legs · 500 ml water (plus more for the sauce)
- 1 onion · 1 carrot · 3 tsp salt · freshly ground pepper

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to **200 °C** top/bottom heat. Dice the onion and carrot.
2. Pat the legs dry and **prick each one several times with a sharp knife** so the salt penetrates.
3. Rub well with salt (about 2 tsp) and pepper; lay **skin-side up** on a baking tray, pour about 500 ml water around them and scatter the vegetables in.
4. Roast on the middle shelf **about 1 hour**, then turn the legs and roast **30 minutes** more.
5. Reduce to **180 °C** and roast **30 minutes** without turning.
6. Turn the legs skin-side up again, salt, switch the oven to the **grill setting at 200 °C** and grill **15 minutes, until the skin is golden**. Watch it closely so it does not burn.
7. Make the sauce from the tray juices with about 250 ml water, skimming the fat; no need to blend or thicken.

*Internal temperature: not published — this is the no-sear, water-in-the-tray method, and it is the
only record in the leaf that gets crisp skin entirely from a final grill stage rather than from
searing first.*

### 4. Dive Bomb Industries (US) — Brining and Roasting Goose Legs
**Inspired by**: https://www.divebombindustries.com/blogs/news/brining-and-roasting-goose-legs-for-a-flavorful-holiday-dish (fetched 2026-10-26). Dive Bomb Industries (US waterfowl-hunting company blog), published 2025-01-27. North American wild-game tradition — **Culinary Group B** source.
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 4–24 h brine · **Cook**: per pound

**Ingredients (brine)**
- ½ cup kosher salt · ¼ cup brown sugar · 4 cups water · 2 garlic cloves, smashed
- 1 tbsp black peppercorns · 2 sprigs thyme · 1 bay leaf · 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar

**Method**
1. Simmer the brine until salt and sugar dissolve; cool completely.
2. Submerge the legs and refrigerate **at least 4 hours, ideally 12–24 hours**.
3. Preheat the oven to **325 °F (165 °C)**. Pat the legs dry — dry skin is what crisps — rub with olive oil and season with thyme, rosemary and garlic powder.
4. **Score the skin** in a crisscross pattern without cutting into the meat, so the fat renders.
5. Roast, basting every 20–30 minutes with the rendered fat, until the skin is golden and crisp.
6. The page also gives an alternative high-heat method (425 °F / 220 °C preheated pan) and notes the fat can be poured off and used for vegetables.

*Internal temperature: not published for the legs in the fetched text. The contribution here is the
**brine** — 4 to 24 hours in salt, sugar and vinegar — which is the North American answer to the
gamey, dense leg, and the only record in either goose-thigh leaf that cures before roasting.*

## Temperature summary

| Source | Published target | Roast | Tradition |
|--------|------------------|-------|-----------|
| Raymond Blanc (UK) | **breast 55 °C**; no leg figure | 150 °C covered 1 h → 230 °C 30 min → 150 °C 30–35 min | British/French |
| Polish Your Kitchen (PL) | **none** | 150 °C, 2.5–3 h on a fruit bed | Polish |
| Ester kocht (DE) | **none** | 200 °C 1 h + 30 min turned + 180 °C 30 min + grill 15 min | German |
| Dive Bomb (US) | **none** | brine 4–24 h, 325 °F (165 °C), basted | US |

**Conclusion for this leaf:** no source publishes a leg temperature for the roast method, so this
leaf adds **technique, not numbers** — the numbers for this cut live in the `braise` leaf
(75–85 °C, 80 °C practical). What the roast records converge on is that the leg wants either a
long low roast on a non-stick bed (fruit, bones) or a shorter hot roast with a grill finish, and
that the skin must be pricked, dried and salted before it goes in.
