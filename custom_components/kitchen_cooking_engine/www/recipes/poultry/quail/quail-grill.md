# Quail × Grill — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Quail are small (100–200 g), lean game birds whose thin breast meat and thin bones make direct radiant heat both the fastest and the riskiest method: a charcoal fire browns the skin in minutes, but the window between juicy and dry is narrow because there is little fat and no collagen buffer. Grilling suits the cut when the birds are spatchcocked or butterflied (as in the American Southwestern preparation below) or grilled whole over embers (as in the Turkish tradition), because flattening evens out the extreme breast-to-leg geometry of a 150 g bird. The fetched sources use visual and textural endpoints — skin colour, leg joints loosening — and one states an internal temperature; no pull value is invented where a source is silent. Carryover on a small split bird is small.

## Styckesprofil

Vaktel är en liten (100–200 g) mager spel fågel där tunt bröstkött och tunna ben gör direkt strålvärme både snabbast och riskfylldast: glöd bränner skinnet på minuter men fönstret mellan saftigt och torrt är smalt. Grillning passar fågeln när den är fjärilsöppnad (amerikansk sydvästberedning) eller hel över glöd (turkisk tradition), eftersom utplattning utjämnar fågelns extremt olika bröst- och lårgeometri. Källorna använder visuella och taktila slutpunkter — skinnfärg, att lårlederna lossnar — och en anger innertemperatur.

## Source recipes

### 1. Hank Shaw — BBQ Quail, Southwest Style
**Inspired by**: https://honest-food.net/bbq-quail-southwest-style-recipe/ (fetched 2026-10-23). American wild-game author Hank Shaw (Hunt, Gather, Cook); recipe designed for Quail Forever.
**Serves**: 4 (8 quail) · **Prep**: 20 min · **Cook**: 35 min (+30 min sauce simmer)

**Ingredients**
- 8 quail, backbones removed and flattened
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- Sauce: 4 tbsp unsalted butter or lard
- ½ onion, grated
- 2 shots tequila
- ½ cup honey, mesquite bean syrup or prickly pear syrup
- ¼ cup cider vinegar
- ½ cup chicken stock
- 1 tbsp chopped sage
- 1–2 tsp ground dried hot chiles (or hot sauce to taste)
- Salt, to taste

**Method**
1. Make the sauce: heat butter or lard over medium heat, sauté the grated onion 3–4 minutes until translucent, add the remaining sauce ingredients and simmer 30 minutes or more; blend if desired.
2. Flatten the quail: cut out the backbones with kitchen shears, press flat (optionally snip out ribs and saber bones); salt and toss in the oil.
3. Grill over a two-zone fire (one cool zone with no coals): lay the quail breast-up on the cool side, lid down, 10 minutes. Paint with sauce, repeat twice more (30 minutes total).
4. **Pull at 65–68 °C** (150–155 °F) — the source's stated thermometer check in the thickest part of the breast; the alternative endpoint is leg joints that want to come apart but do not fall apart. Up to 20 additional minutes may be needed depending on fire heat.
5. Turn, paint the cooked side, grill breast-side 2 minutes over the lit zone to char. Serve with the remaining sauce. The source states no final serving temperature after resting.

### 2. Nefis Yemek Tarifleri — Mangalda Bıldırcın
**Inspired by**: https://www.nefisyemektarifleri.com/mangalda-bildircin/ (fetched 2026-10-23). Turkish community recipe platform; contributor recipe with "Ben de Yaptım" verification count.
**Serves**: 1–2 (2 quail) · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 25 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 quail
- Salt
- Red pepper (pul biber)
- Dried thyme
- Dried mint

**Method**
1. Pluck, clean and wash the quail; let them drain.
2. Toss with the salt, red pepper, thyme and mint.
3. Grill over charcoal (mangal) until done. The source gives no time breakdown, internal temperature, or explicit pull point; do not infer one.
4. Serve immediately. The source states no final serving temperature.

## Temperature consensus

| Source | Stated doneness evidence | Internal/pull temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Hank Shaw (US) | 30 min two-zone grilling with sauce; leg-joint texture | 150–155 °F (65–68 °C) breast |
| Nefis Yemek Tarifleri (TR) | Grilled over charcoal "until done" | Not stated |

The US source is the only one of the two with a thermometer endpoint: 65–68 °C breast, which sits **below** the 74 °C USDA poultry line recorded for quail — a divergence between chef practice and food-safety guidance worth preserving rather than resolving. The Turkish source relies entirely on the cook's judgment. Two sources (Group B, Group C) do not meet the 4–6-source, four-tradition gate, so no `recommended_doneness` change follows from this leaf.

## What makes this method special

Direct radiant heat crisps quail skin faster than any other method and adds smoke-adjacent flavour from rendered fat dripping on coals, but the lean 150 g body has almost no thermal reserve — two-zone grilling (Shaw) exists precisely to decouple browning from through-cooking, and the Turkish whole-bird version leans on constant attention instead. The spatchcock geometry matters: it equalises breast and leg distances to the fire so one bird finishes together. Shaw's sauce glazes during the cook, protecting the surface while building lacquer.

## Summary

Grilling quail gives crisp lacquered skin and the fastest cook, at the cost of the narrowest drying margin of any method. One US source pulls the breast at 65–68 °C and one Turkish source trusts the eye; the leaf records the method's shape and the safety-guidance divergence but cannot yet support a temperature recommendation.
