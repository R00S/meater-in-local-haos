# Quail × Oven Roast — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Quail (Japanese quail *Coturnix japonica* and common quail *Coturnix coturnix*) are tiny game birds of 100–200 g served one to two per person, with thin, easily pierced breast meat and thin bones. Oven roasting is the most universally documented quail method: the small body roasts through in 20–50 minutes depending on stuffing, glaze and oven temperature, while high heat (180–200 °C in the sources below) browns a skin with very little subcutinal fat to render. The method suits the cut because a whole bird can be plated individually and the oven frees the cook from the minute-by-minute attention a grill demands; the risk is drying, since the lean breast crosses from done to dry quickly. None of the fetched sources states an internal pull temperature — endpoints are glaze colour, clear juices, or stated times — so no pull value is inferred here. Carryover for a 100–200 g bird is modest; the Turkish source rests the bird 5 minutes.

## Styckesprofil

Vaktel (*Coturnix japonica* / *Coturnix coturnix*) är en liten spel fågel på 100–200 g med tunt bröstkött och tunna ben. Ugnstekning är den mest universellt dokumenterade metoden: fågeln blir klar på 20–50 minuter beroende på fyllning och glasyr, medan hög värme (180–200 °C i källorna) ger yta på ett skinn med lite underhudsfett. Metoden passar eftersom hel fågel serveras individuellt och ugnen frigör kocken från ständig uppsikt; risken är att den magra fågeln torkar. Ingen källa anger innertemperatur — slutpunkterna är glasyrfärg, klar saft eller angiven tid.

## Source recipes

### 1. Валерия / Edimdoma — Маринованные перепела в вине, запечённые с овощами и беконом
**Inspired by**: https://www.edimdoma.ru/retsepty/71289-marinovannye-v-vine-perepela-zapechennye-s-ovoschami-i-bekonom (fetched 2026-10-23). Russian home-cooking platform; recipe by author "Валерия".
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 2 h (incl. 1 h wine marinade) · **Cook**: about 30 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 quail
- 500 ml semi-dry red wine
- 2 tbsp vinegar
- 7 sprigs fresh thyme
- 6 potatoes
- 200 g broccoli
- 100 g carrot
- 1 onion
- 200 g bacon
- Salt and pepper, to taste
- Vegetable oil, for greasing

**Method**
1. Singe, wash and split the quail along the breast.
2. Mix the wine, vinegar, thyme leaves and salt; marinate the quail in the refrigerator 1 hour.
3. Slice potatoes and carrots, dice the onion, cut the bacon into strips; combine with the broccoli, season.
4. Spread the vegetables in an oiled baking dish, lay the quail back-up on top, and roast in a 180–200 °C oven about 15 minutes. Brush with vegetable oil and roast another 15 minutes.
5. Check doneness by piercing the thickest part — clear juice without pink tint is the stated endpoint. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time. Serve in the baking dish.

### 2. Liliana Fuchs / Directo al Paladar — Codornices asadas glaseadas al whisky
**Inspired by**: https://www.directoalpaladar.com/recetas-de-carnes-y-aves/codornices-asadas-con-glaseado-de-naranja-sirope-de-arce-y-whisky (fetched 2026-10-23). Spanish food media; author Liliana Fuchs.
**Serves**: 2 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: about 30 min · **Rest**: a few minutes (wrapped in foil)

**Ingredients**
- 2 quail
- 1 orange
- 1 mandarin
- 20 ml maple syrup
- 5 ml soy sauce
- 10 ml Dijon mustard
- 30 ml whisky
- 2 ml Worcestershire sauce
- A pinch of cocoa powder
- Dried rosemary, to taste
- Fresh chives
- Black pepper, salt, extra-virgin olive oil

**Method**
1. Preheat the oven to 200 °C and line a dish with aluminium foil. Clean the quail; stuff each with mandarin segments and chives; tie the legs.
2. Brush with olive oil, season, and add rosemary.
3. Whisk 2 tbsp orange juice with the soy sauce, mustard, whisky, maple syrup, Worcestershire and cocoa; brush the quail.
4. Roast 10 minutes, then turn, re-glaze, and continue 10 minutes, lowering the heat if they colour too much. Finish the glaze and roast to about 30 minutes total.
5. Wrap in the foil and rest a few minutes. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 3. Renata Briano / GialloZafferano blog — Quaglie al forno con pancetta e patate
**Inspired by**: https://blog.giallozafferano.it/renatabriano/quaglie-al-forno-con-pancetta-e-patate/ (fetched 2026-10-23). Italian food blog by Renata Briano (naturalist and Ligurian cooking writer).
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 40–50 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 4 quail
- 1 sprig rosemary
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 tbsp juniper berries
- 600 g potatoes
- 150 g pancetta
- Extra-virgin olive oil, to taste
- Salt and pepper, to taste

**Method**
1. Clean the quail; singe any feather stubs over a flame. Peel and dice the potatoes; parboil in salted water about 4 minutes.
2. Season the cavities with salt, pepper, juniper berries, rosemary needles and a piece of garlic; massage the skin with salt for crispness.
3. Wrap each quail in pancetta, closing the wings; tie the legs with string.
4. Combine potatoes and quail in an oiled baking dish, season with oil and salt, add the bay leaves and roast at 180 °C fan for 40–50 minutes, turning occasionally; raise to 200 °C for the final 5 minutes.
5. Serve hot. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 4. 美君厨房 / Xiachufang — 蜜汁烤鹌鹑（烤箱版）(Honey-glazed roast quail, oven version)
**Inspired by**: https://m.xiachufang.com/recipe/105846255/ (fetched 2026-10-23). Chinese recipe platform; author 美君厨房.
**Serves**: 3 (10 quail) · **Prep**: marinade 2 h+ · **Cook**: 50 min · **Rest**: not stated

**Ingredients**
- 10 quail
- 1 bowl light soy sauce
- 5 tbsp oyster sauce
- 3 tbsp sugar
- 2 tbsp cooking wine
- Ginger slices and minced garlic, to taste
- 1 tbsp ground cumin
- Glaze: 1 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp oyster sauce, 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp ground cumin
- Chilli powder and sesame seeds, optional

**Method**
1. Remove heads, tails and feet; toss the quail with all marinade ingredients and refrigerate at least 2 hours.
2. Preheat the oven (top and bottom heat) to 180 °C; lay the quail on the rack, lower shelf, and roast 20 minutes to set the shape (no glaze yet).
3. Mix the glaze. Brush both sides of the half-roasted quail.
4. Return cavity-up for 20 minutes at 180 °C.
5. Brush again, position back-up, and roast 10 minutes at 180–200 °C, watching to avoid scorching. Total 50 minutes; the constant glazing keeps the skin moist rather than crisp. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

### 5. Zeynep Ayar / Yemek.com — Fırında Bıldırcın
**Inspired by**: https://yemek.com/bildircin-nasil-pisirilir/ (fetched 2026-10-23). Turkish food media method guide (oven, pan and grill variants); editorial by Zeynep Ayar.
**Serves**: 1 (2 quail) · **Prep**: not stated · **Cook**: 20–25 min · **Rest**: 5 min

**Ingredients**
- 2 cleaned quail
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 crushed garlic clove
- 1 tsp butter
- Salt
- Black pepper
- Dried thyme or rosemary

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 200 °C and preheat thoroughly — the source stresses preheating because quail cooks fast.
2. Pat the quail dry; moisture prevents browning.
3. Massage the birds with olive oil, garlic, salt, pepper and thyme; place in a baking dish and top each with a knob of butter.
4. Roast 20–25 minutes, until lightly browned on top.
5. Rest 5 minutes before serving so the juices redistribute. The source gives no internal temperature or explicit pull point; do not infer one from the stated time.

## What makes this method special

Roasting surrounds the small bird with dry convective heat that browns a skin with minimal fat, while the short cook window makes glazing (honey, maple, wine) the practical lever for colour and surface moisture — two of the sources roast in three glazing stages precisely to protect the lean breast. Stuffed and bacon-wrapped variants (Russian, Italian) trade skin crispness for basting and insulation, extending the cook to 40–50 minutes without drying the meat. Because the whole bird is under 200 g, thermal gradients are small and carryover minimal; the failure mode is overshoot, not under-resting.
