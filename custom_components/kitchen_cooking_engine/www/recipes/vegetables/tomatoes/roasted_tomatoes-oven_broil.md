# Roasted Tomatoes × Oven Broil — Recipe Temperature Research

## Cut profile

Broiling is the only tomato method in this file that heats from one side only. The halves sit cut-side up
8–15 cm below a glowing element, and the cooking event is almost entirely at the surface: the breadcrumb or
cheese topping browns by radiant heat, the skin on the underside blisters, and the interior warms by
conduction over minutes rather than hours. That is why no tradition times this method by internal
temperature — there is no safe-temperature question to answer (a tomato is eaten raw), and the useful
signal is visual, the moment the topping turns gold before it turns black.

The structure that matters is the tomato's own: a thin outer skin over a thick pericarp wall, with the
seed-bearing locules and their gel in the middle. Radiant heat drives water out of the exposed cut face
fast, concentrating sugars in the pericarp and collapsing the locule gel; the skin, which is far less
permeable, traps steam on the underside and is what lifts and splits when the half is left too long. A beefsteak
half needs the full 10–15 minutes; a plum half 8–12; a cherry tomato 4–6 and it will burst.

Probe placement is impractical and no fetched source attempts it: the mass is too small and the gradients
inside a 3 cm half are larger than the tolerance. The substitutes that the sources actually use are time,
rack height and appearance, and this leaf records them as the evidence they are.

## Styckesprofil

Grillning i ugn är den enda tomatmetoden i den här filen som värmer från ett håll. Halvorna ligger med
snittytan uppåt 8–15 cm under ett glödande element, och det som händer är nästan helt en ythändelse:
paneringen eller osten bryns av strålningsvärme, skalet på undersidan blåsas upp och insidan värms genom
ledning på minuter i stället för timmar. Därför anger ingen tradition någon innertemperatur för metoden —
det finns ingen säkerhetsfråga att svara på (en tomat äts rå), och den användbara signalen är visuell:
ögonblicket då paneringen blir gyllene innan den blir svart.

Strukturen som avgör är tomatens egen: ett tunt yttre skal över en tjock perikarpvägg, med de fröbärande
facken och deras gel i mitten. Strålningsvärme driver ut vatten ur den blottade snittytan snabbt,
koncentrerar socker i perikarpen och får fackens gel att kollapsa; skalet, som är mycket mindre
genomsläppligt, håller kvar ånga på undersidan och är det som reser sig och spricker om halvan ligger för
länge. En bifftomat behöver hela 10–15 minuter, en plommonhalva 8–12, en körsbärstomat 4–6 och då brister den.

Sond är opraktisk och ingen av de hämtade källorna försöker: massan är för liten och gradienterna i en 3 cm
halva är större än toleransen. Det källorna faktiskt använder är tid, gallerhöjd och utseende, och det är
vad det här bladet redovisar som evidens.

## Source recipes

### 1. Parmesan Broiled Tomatoes (inspired by Mary Younkin)
**Inspired by**: Mary Younkin, vegetablerecipes.com/broiled-tomatoes/ (fetched 2026-10-27; US, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 5–6 min · **Rest**: none

**Ingredients**
- 4 ripe tomatoes (beefsteak or similar), halved horizontally
- 40 g (½ cup) freshly grated Parmesan
- 2 g (½ tsp) salt
- 1 g (¼ tsp) freshly ground black pepper
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) olive oil

**Method**
1. Set the oven rack about 15 cm (6 in) from the broiling element and preheat the broiler.
2. Oil a large baking sheet, place the tomato halves cut-side up on it and lightly oil them.
3. Mix the cheese, salt and pepper and sprinkle over the cut faces.
4. Broil, watching closely so they do not burn, until the tomatoes soften a little and the tops brown — about 5–6 minutes.
5. Serve warm. **No internal temperature is published**; doneness is the softened tomato under a browned crust.

### 2. Italian Broiled Tomatoes (inspired by Art of Natural Living)
**Inspired by**: artofnaturalliving.com/italian-broiled-tomatoes/ (fetched 2026-10-27; Italian-American, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 10 min · **Cook**: 10–20 min · **Rest**: none

**Ingredients**
- 4 medium tomatoes, cored and halved
- Salt, to taste
- 2 cloves garlic, minced (or ½ tsp garlic powder)
- 5 g (2 tsp) dried oregano (or 2 tbsp fresh, chopped)
- 55 g (2 oz) grated provolone or Parmesan
- 30 ml (2 tbsp) olive oil

**Method**
1. Core and halve the tomatoes.
2. Salt the cut sides, then top with garlic and oregano.
3. Sprinkle about 15 g (½ oz) of grated cheese on each half and drizzle with olive oil.
4. Broil on low until the cheese is melted and the tomato is slightly softened and warm inside — or bake covered for about 15–20 minutes. A minute or two more under the broiler adds colour if needed.
5. **No internal temperature is published.** The dual instruction (broil-on-low *or* bake covered 15–20 min) is itself the finding: this source treats the two as interchangeable for the same visual endpoint.

---

### 3. Broiled Parmesan Tomatoes (inspired by Paula Deen)
**Inspired by**: pauladeen.com/recipe/broiled-parmesan-tomatoes/ (fetched 2026-10-27; US Southern, Group B)
**Serves**: 16 · **Prep**: 5 min · **Cook**: 10–15 min · **Rest**: none

**Ingredients**
- 3 × 411 g (14½ oz) tins whole Italian plum tomatoes, drained
- 113 g (½ cup) butter
- 100 g (1 cup) freshly grated Parmesan
- Salt and black pepper, to taste

**Method**
1. Put the drained tomatoes in a 23 × 33 cm (13 × 9 in) casserole.
2. Season with salt and pepper and top each tomato with a pat of butter.
3. Sprinkle the Parmesan generously over the top.
4. Broil 10–15 minutes, until the tomatoes are heated through and the cheese is bubbly.
5. **No internal temperature is published**, and the recipe uses tinned tomatoes, which changes the starting water content: they heat through faster than fresh halves and the endpoint is "bubbly", not "soft".

---

### 4. Pomodori gratinati al forno (inspired by Lulù, Allacciate il grembiule)
**Inspired by**: blog.giallozafferano.it/allacciateilgrembiule/pomodori-gratinati-al-forno/ (fetched 2026-10-27; Italian, Group B)
**Serves**: 4 · **Prep**: 15 min · **Cook**: 15–20 min · **Rest**: none

**Ingredients**
- 4 vine tomatoes
- 40 g (4 tbsp) dry breadcrumbs
- 12 g (2 tbsp) Parmigiano Reggiano
- 30 ml (2 tbsp) extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- 1 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
- 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
- Salt

**Method**
1. Wash the tomatoes, remove the stalks and halve them horizontally.
2. Score the cut faces lightly with a knife point, without cutting through, and dry them with kitchen paper.
3. Mix the breadcrumbs, Parmesan, garlic, parsley and a pinch of salt, then stir in two tablespoons of oil.
4. Lay the halves on a lined tray, spaced apart and scored side up, press the crumb mixture onto them and into the cuts.
5. Bake in a fan oven at 200 °C for 15–20 minutes, until cooked and golden on top. **For the last 5 minutes switch on the grill.**
6. **No internal temperature is published.** The Italian version reaches the same endpoint by oven heat and adds the grill only as a 5-minute finishing pass, which is the clearest statement in the set of what the broiler is actually for.

## Temperature consensus

| Tradition | Source | Heat | Published internal target | Endpoint given instead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US | Mary Younkin | broiler, rack at 15 cm | none | 5–6 min, softened and browned |
| Italian-American | Art of Natural Living | broiler on low, or 175 °C oven | none | cheese melted, tomato warm inside |
| US Southern | Paula Deen | broiler | none | 10–15 min, heated through, bubbly |
| Italian | Lulù (giallozafferano) | 200 °C fan + 5 min grill | none | golden surface, 15–20 min |

**Not one of the four publishes an internal temperature, and none probes.** This is the leaf's real finding:
broiling is a surface method with a visual endpoint, and the hub's `tender` target of 95 °C and
`caramelized` target of 160 °C are **inherited guidance with no source behind them in this leaf** — the same
class of unsupported number that the temperature criteria exist to catch. The only numbers the sources agree
on are the ones that describe the oven and the clock: 15 cm from the element, broil on low or high, 5–15
minutes depending on tomato size and whether it is tinned, and a 5-minute grill pass at the end.

## What this method does to this cut

Broiling dries and browns the exposed face while the skin side steams, so the same tomato ends up with a
concentrated, slightly chewy top and a juicy base — the opposite gradient to a slow oven roast, which drives
water out evenly from all sides. It is the fastest way to make a tomato taste cooked, and the least
forgiving: the window between "cheese browned" and "topping black" is about two minutes at 15 cm.

## Summary

Four broiled-tomato sources were fetched — two US, one Italian-American, one Italian — and not one publishes
an internal temperature or probes the fruit. The method is a surface event with a visual endpoint, which makes
the hub's 95 °C and 160 °C doneness targets inherited numbers rather than researched ones.
